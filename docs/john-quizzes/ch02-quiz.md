# John 2 quiz: who Jesus shows himself to be

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John
> **Covers:** John 2:1-25 (Unit 3, "Sign 1 and the temple," Lessons 1-4), plus the claims from John 1 that chapter 2 puts on display
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 21 multiple-choice questions at 2 points each, 2 short written answers at 4 points each.
> **Total:** 50 points, about 40 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz, so keep your ESV open. Many of the answer choices are things people often say about this chapter. Your job is to find the answer the text itself gives. The choices use plain words, with no quotation marks or verse references, so find the verse named in each question and read it before you choose. The multiple-choice questions are worth 2 points each. The two written answers at the end are worth 4 points each and are graded by hand.

## Part 1: the first sign (John 2:1-11)

**1. John ends the Cana story by telling you what the sign accomplished (2:11). According to John, what is the point of Cana?**
- A. Jesus cares about the small, everyday problems in our lives, even something like running out of wine.
- B. Jesus blessed marriage by coming to a wedding and providing for it.
- C. Jesus showed his glory, who he really is, and his disciples believed in him. ✓
- D. The wine was a picture of the blood Jesus would shed for us.

*Feedback: John 2:11. Some of the other three are true in themselves. Jesus did come to a wedding, and he does care about ordinary trouble. His blood was shed for us, and the church has long connected the cup of the Lord's Supper with it (Matthew 26:28). In class we followed Cana down the road to the cross, where the wine is his blood, and that's a true connection. None of them is the point John names in 2:11. He tells you his point outright: the sign showed Jesus' glory, and his disciples believed in him. Read a true statement in its own place, and don't let it crowd out what the author actually said here.*

**2. What's the difference between a sign and a magic trick? Which answer matches what John says a sign is for (2:11; 20:31)?**
- A. The only difference is that a sign is real and a magic trick is fake.
- B. A magic trick points at itself and hides how it's done, while a sign points past itself to the one who did it and is meant to be understood. ✓
- C. A sign is a miracle so big that nobody can deny God is real, and a magic trick can always be explained by someone who knows how.
- D. A sign is God showing someone what to do next, like an answer to prayer, and a magic trick is only for entertainment.

*Feedback: John 2:11 and 20:31 (one of our review verses this unit). A sign is real and a trick is an illusion, and John says more than that: a sign is given so you can see who Jesus is. A trick keeps your eyes on the performance. A sign sends your eyes to the person, "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God."*

**3. Jesus answers his mother, "Woman, what does this have to do with me? My hour has not yet come" (2:4). What does his answer show? (Compare 13:1.)**
- A. His life runs on his Father's timetable, and that timetable is headed for his hour: the cross and his return to the Father. ✓
- B. He had planned to wait before his first miracle. His mother asked, so he changed his plans.
- C. He was telling her that a wedding party was beneath him, since he had come for more serious things.
- D. He was short with his mother, which shows that even Jesus had moments of frustration.

*Feedback: John 2:4-5, 12:23, 13:1, and 19:26-27. "Woman" was a respectful way to address a woman, and he goes on to make the best wine of the feast, so the wedding mattered to him. His question does set real distance: his mother's request now stands under his Father's hour. Mary hears it and turns to the servants: "Do whatever he tells you" (2:5). Jesus acts after his mother speaks, and the only reason John gives in 2:4 is his hour. John never says he changed his plans. That hour is the cross and his return to the Father, when "his hour had come to depart out of this world to the Father" (13:1; see 12:23). At the cross he speaks to her the same way: "Woman, behold, your son!" (19:26).*

**4. John points out that the six stone jars were "for the Jewish rites of purification" (2:6). Why does that detail matter? (Compare 1:17.)**
- A. Jesus takes the dirty water of our lives and turns it into something good.
- B. Six is the number of man, one short of seven, so the jars show that human effort always falls short of God.
- C. Jesus was showing that all religious rituals are empty, and what matters is a personal relationship with him.
- D. Jars kept for ceremonial washing now hold the best wine of the feast. What those washings looked toward has arrived in Jesus. ✓

*Feedback: John 2:6-7, 1:17, and 1:29, with Hebrews 1:3. These stone jars held water for ceremonial washing, like washing hands and cups before a meal. The servants fill them with water (2:7), so there's no dirty wash water here, and John never gives the number six a meaning. Jesus keeps the jars, has them filled to the brim, and makes them hold the best wine at the feast. People kept those jars because they needed to be clean before God, and that need was real. What the washings looked toward has arrived: "grace and truth came through Jesus Christ" (1:17). Jesus brings cleansing before God, as the Lamb of God "who takes away the sin of the world" (1:29), and he made "purification for sins" at the cross (Hebrews 1:3). He brings the joy of the feast too, and that's what the wine shows.*

**5. What does turning water into wine (2:7-9) show about who Jesus is? (Compare 1:3.)**
- A. He's a prophet like Elisha, who filled a widow's jars with oil by God's power.
- B. He's the Creator. Everything was made through him, so he can make wine out of water on the spot. ✓
- C. He's a good and kind man who used his gifts to meet people's needs.
- D. He became God's Son when the Spirit came down on him at his baptism, and that's where his power came from.

*Feedback: John 1:1-3 and 2:7-9, with 2 Kings 4:2-6, John 1:32-34, and Acts 10:38. Elisha acted as God's servant. Jesus makes wine the way he made everything, because "all things were made through him." The Spirit did come down on Jesus and remain on him (1:32-33), and God anointed him "with the Holy Spirit and with power" (Acts 10:38). The baptism is where John the Baptist recognized who Jesus already was: "this is the Son of God" (1:34). He was the Son and the Creator "in the beginning" (1:1-3).*

**6. At Cana Jesus "manifested his glory" (2:11). According to 1:14, what kind of glory is this?**
- A. Reflected glory, like the shine on Moses' face after he had been talking with God.
- B. The glory of a man so full of the Spirit that God's power shone through him.
- C. His own glory, the glory of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth. ✓
- D. The glory of fame. After Cana, people all over Galilee knew his name.

*Feedback: John 1:14 (a review verse this unit) and 2:11, with Exodus 34:29. Moses' face reflected a glory that came from outside him. Jesus shows his own glory, "glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth." That's God the Son, seen in our flesh.*

**7. The jars held twenty or thirty gallons each, and the servants "filled them up to the brim" (2:6-7). Jesus made well over a hundred gallons of wine, and the master of the feast says, "you have kept the good wine until now" (2:10). What does John want you to see in that? (Compare Isaiah 25:6.)**
- A. Good wine kept until now, poured out far past anyone's need, is a sign of the feast God promised through the prophets. ✓
- B. God saves the best for last, so if you're patient and keep trusting him, your best days are still ahead of you.
- C. Jesus was protecting the bride and groom from the shame of running out of wine at their own wedding.
- D. God wants his people to enjoy an abundant life of health, success, and plenty, and Cana proves it.

*Feedback: John 2:10 with Isaiah 25:6-8; Amos 9:13; Joel 3:18, and John 12:23-24. A jar or two would have saved the family's reputation. Jesus has six filled to the brim. Isaiah promised a mountain feast of "well-aged wine" (he names the aged wine twice, 25:6), and Amos and Joel promised a day when "the mountains shall drip sweet wine." Cana tastes of that promised feast, arriving early at a village wedding in Galilee. It also shows the "fullness" John named in 1:16: "from his fullness we have all received, grace upon grace." Option B turns the sign into a promise about your future, and option D turns it into a promise of health and plenty. John makes it a sign about who Jesus is. On the same mountain as that feast, Isaiah says, "He will swallow up death forever" (25:8), and Jesus swallows it by dying: the hour his signs point to is a grain of wheat that "falls into the earth and dies" (12:23-24).*

**8. The disciples were already following Jesus in chapter 1, yet after Cana John says, "his disciples believed in him" (2:11). What does this show?**
- A. They weren't really believers until they saw a miracle with their own eyes.
- B. Real faith is a one-time decision, so this must be when they finally made theirs.
- C. Seeing miracles is the best way to get strong faith, so we should ask God for them.
- D. Faith grows: Jesus promised Nathanael he would see greater things, and seeing his glory deepened their trust. ✓

*Feedback: John 1:49-50 and 2:11. They had already followed him and called him "the Son of God" (1:49). Jesus told Nathanael, "You will see greater things than these" (1:50). At Cana they began to see those greater things, and their faith grew. Faith has a beginning, and it keeps growing. John shows it growing again in 2:22.*

## Part 2: Jesus in his Father's house (John 2:12-17)

**9. Jesus drives out the sellers and says, "Do not make my Father's house a house of trade" (2:16). What is Jesus claiming about himself?**
- A. He's standing up for the poor. The leaders were cheating them, and he put a stop to it.
- B. He's God's own Son, with the right to clean house in his Father's house. ✓
- C. He's a prophet like Jeremiah, calling Israel back to true worship.
- D. He's teaching that church buildings are sacred, so there shouldn't be any business in them.

*Feedback: John 2:16, with 4:44, 5:18, Mark 11:17, and Hebrews 3:5-6. John names no cheating and no victims in 2:14-16. Jesus' stated objection is that they have made his Father's house "a house of trade." Jesus is a prophet, and he said so himself: "a prophet has no honor in his own hometown" (4:44). The other Gospels quote Jeremiah 7:11 when they tell of him clearing the temple. As we saw in class, the market also crowded outsiders out of the one court where they could come near God, in a house meant to be "a house of prayer for all the nations" (Mark 11:17). In 2:16, though, Jesus makes a claim about himself that no prophet could make: it's "my Father's house." John shows how his enemies heard that kind of talk: "he was even calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God" (5:18). Hebrews says it plainly: "Moses was faithful in all God's house as a servant... but Christ is faithful over God's house as a son" (Hebrews 3:5-6). John 1 already told you who he is: "the only Son from the Father" (1:14) and "the Son of God" (1:49).*

**10. Jesus made "a whip of cords" and drove them all out (2:15). What does this show?**
- A. He acted on purpose, out of zeal for his Father's worship. ✓
- B. Even Jesus lost his temper sometimes, which shows he was fully human.
- C. Jesus was a revolutionary who used force against a corrupt religious system.
- D. Anger is fine for Christians, as long as it's about something that really matters.

*Feedback: John 2:15-17, with Mark 3:5, Hebrews 4:15, Ephesians 4:26, and James 1:20. John says Jesus made the whip himself before he drove them out, so this was a chosen act. The disciples saw zeal for his Father's house (2:17), and Question 11 shows where that zeal led him. Jesus felt real anger ("he looked around at them with anger, grieved at their hardness of heart," Mark 3:5), and he was tempted in every way "yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15). Being fully human never meant losing his temper. Ephesians 4:26 does say, "Be angry and do not sin," and its test for anger is sin: "do not let the sun go down on your anger." James adds that "the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God" (1:20). John 2 is teaching you about Jesus and his zeal.*

**11. The disciples remembered, "Zeal for your house will consume me" (2:17). What does that line mean? (Compare 2:19-21.)**
- A. It's a call for us to be on fire for God and passionate about church the way Jesus was.
- B. Jesus had the zeal of a Zealot, like the group that wanted to drive the Romans out of Israel.
- C. It shows how far the temple had fallen, and why God would later let the Romans destroy it.
- D. His zeal for his Father's house would eat him up and cost him his life at the cross. ✓

*Feedback: John 2:17 and 2:19-21, with Psalm 69:9. "Consume" means to eat up. Psalm 69:9 says zeal "has consumed me," and John writes "will consume me," looking ahead. The zeal is his before it's ever ours. It eats him up at the cross, when they destroy "this temple" (2:19), "the temple of his body" (2:21).*

## Part 3: Jesus the true temple (John 2:18-22)

**12. After the temple is cleared, the Jewish leaders ask, "What sign do you show us for doing these things?" (2:18). Why did they demand a sign?**
- A. They were honest skeptics who would have believed if he had done a miracle on the spot.
- B. They were jealous that the crowds were starting to follow him instead of them.
- C. He had taken authority over the temple, and they wanted proof he had the right to. ✓
- D. They wanted a miracle to watch for entertainment, the way Herod later did.

*Feedback: John 2:18. They understood that clearing the temple was a claim to authority over it, so they asked for proof. Signs were how God backed up the one he sent: Moses was given signs "that they may believe that the LORD... has appeared to you" (Exodus 4:5), and Paul says "Jews demand signs" (1 Corinthians 1:22). Matthew 27:18 does say the leaders later handed Jesus over "out of envy," but John 2 names no motive of that kind. More signs wouldn't have changed their minds: "Though he had done so many signs before them, they still did not believe in him" (12:37).*

**13. Jesus says, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up" (2:19). What was he talking about?**
- A. The church, since the church is the body of Christ.
- B. The temple building. The Romans would destroy it about forty years later.
- C. His own body, which would be killed and then raised on the third day. ✓
- D. A threat to tear the temple building down himself.

*Feedback: John 2:19-21, with 10:18. John settles it in his own words: "he was speaking about the temple of his body." Paul calls the church Christ's body (1 Corinthians 12:27), and here in John 2 the body is the one they'll kill and he'll raise. Notice who does the raising: "I will raise it up." Only God raises the dead, and Jesus claims the work as his own: "I have authority to take it up again" (10:18).*

**14. Read John 1:14 and 1:51, then put them together with "the temple of his body" (2:21). Where does a person meet God now?**
- A. In heaven, when we finally see him face to face after we die.
- B. In Jesus himself. He's where God lives with people, the true temple the others pointed to. ✓
- C. Through a priest or pastor, who stands between you and God.
- D. Anywhere you feel close to him, like out in nature or during worship music.

*Feedback: John 1:14, 1:51, and 2:21, with Genesis 28:12-17 and Revelation 21:22; 22:4. "Dwelt among us" (1:14) means the Word pitched his tent among us, the way God lived among Israel in the tabernacle. In 1:51 Jesus says heaven will open and angels will go up and down "on the Son of Man," like the ladder Jacob saw at the place he called "the house of God" (Genesis 28:17). The tabernacle, Jacob's house of God, and the temple all pointed ahead to a person. We will see God's face in the new creation (Revelation 22:4), and even there the meeting place is the Lamb: "I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb" (Revelation 21:22). John says the Word already "dwelt among us," so you meet God in Jesus now. The place to meet God has a face and a name.*

**15. After Jesus was raised from the dead, "his disciples remembered that he had said this, and they believed the Scripture and the word that Jesus had spoken" (2:22). What does this show?**
- A. Real faith means believing without any evidence at all.
- B. The disciples understood everything he said, because they were with him every day.
- C. The disciples made up a meaning for his words years later to explain his death.
- D. His rising made his words clear, and they believed them. ✓

*Feedback: John 2:22, with 14:26. The disciples didn't understand the saying when they first heard it. They believed after the evidence came: a risen Jesus who had told them this would happen. Jesus had also promised that the Holy Spirit would "bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you" (14:26). Their remembering was the Spirit's work, and the meaning they remembered was Jesus' own.*

**16. Twice John says the disciples "remembered" (2:17 and 2:22). In 2:17 they remember a Scripture; in 2:22 they remember Jesus' word and then believe the Scripture. What does that show about how the Old Testament relates to Jesus?**
- A. The Old Testament points to Jesus, so the disciples found him in the Psalms and, after he rose, believed the Scripture. ✓
- B. Every Old Testament verse is secretly about Jesus if you look hard enough.
- C. The disciples went looking for old verses that seemed to fit what they already believed.
- D. The Old Testament is the story of Israel, and the New Testament is the story of Jesus.

*Feedback: John 1:45, 2:17 (quoting Psalm 69:9), 2:22, and 14:26, with Luke 24:27, 45. Philip already said it in chapter 1: "We have found him of whom Moses in the Law and also the prophets wrote." In 2:17 the disciples remember a psalm about zeal for God's house. In 2:22, after Jesus rose, they remember his word and "believed the Scripture." The whole Old Testament points to Jesus (Luke 24:27). Luke shows the risen Jesus doing exactly this: he "opened their minds to understand the Scriptures" (24:45), and John tells you the Spirit would bring his words back to their minds (14:26). They found him in plain words, read in the light of his rising, with no hidden code needed.*

## Part 4: Jesus who knows the heart (John 2:23-25)

**17. "Many believed in his name when they saw the signs that he was doing," yet "Jesus on his part did not entrust himself to them" (2:23-24). Why not?**
- A. Their faith wasn't strong enough yet, and he was waiting for it to grow.
- B. He was keeping his identity quiet until his hour came.
- C. He was protecting himself from people who might turn on him later.
- D. He knew all people and what was in them. ✓

*Feedback: John 2:24-25. John gives the reason himself: "because he knew all people" and "he himself knew what was in man." Faith does grow (Question 8), and here John gives a different reason for Jesus holding back: he knew them. John doesn't tell us what Jesus saw in them. He tells us Jesus could see it.*

**18. The crowd "believed in his name" (2:23), the very words John used in 1:12. According to 1:12-13, what is true of those God makes his children?**
- A. They decided by their own will to make themselves children of God.
- B. They received him, and they were born of God, not of blood or human will. ✓
- C. They believed more sincerely than the Passover crowd did.
- D. They grew up in families of believers who taught them the faith.

*Feedback: John 1:12-13 (1:12 is a review verse this unit), 2:24-25, and 3:2-3, with 2 Timothy 3:15. Verse 12 names what God's children do: they receive him and believe in his name. That believing is truly theirs. Verse 13 names where their new birth comes from. John rules out every human source: family line ("blood"), natural desire ("the will of the flesh"), and a man's will ("the will of man"). A believing family is still a gift God often uses: Timothy knew the Scriptures from childhood, and they were "able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus" (2 Timothy 3:15). A family can't give the new birth, though. God's children are born "of God," and the Word they received is the one who "gave the right to become children of God." John doesn't stop to dissect the Passover crowd's faith or call it fake. He tells you Jesus could read it (2:24-25), and then he shows you the next man on stage. Nicodemus believes because of the signs ("no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him," 3:2), and Jesus tells him, "unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God" (3:3).*

**19. John says Jesus "needed no one to bear witness about man, for he himself knew what was in man" (2:25). What is John claiming? (Compare 1 Kings 8:39.)**
- A. Jesus knows every human heart himself, the way only God does. ✓
- B. Like a prophet, he was sometimes given a glimpse into one person's heart when God chose to show him.
- C. Jesus had seen enough of people's fickle loyalty to expect the worst from them.
- D. Jesus was an unusually wise man with a deep understanding of human nature.

*Feedback: John 2:24-25 and 1:1, with 2 Kings 5:26, 1 Kings 8:39, Jeremiah 17:10, and Revelation 2:23. A prophet might be shown one person's secret now and then, the way Elisha knew what his servant had done. John says much more about Jesus: he "knew all people," he "needed no one to bear witness about man," and "he himself knew what was in man." Solomon prayed to God, "you, you only, know the hearts of all the children of mankind." The Word who "was God" (1:1) knows every heart. The risen Jesus takes God's own words from Jeremiah 17:10 for himself: "I am he who searches mind and heart" (Revelation 2:23). Jesus is also truly a man, and John shows that too (4:6; 11:35). He is one person, fully God and fully man.*

**20. Jesus' knowledge of hearts shows up twice: with Nathanael (1:47-48) and with the Passover crowd (2:24-25). What do the two scenes show together?**
- A. Nathanael earned Jesus' trust by being honest, and the crowd lost it by being shallow.
- B. The same Jesus sees every heart fully, whether he is welcoming Nathanael or holding himself back from the crowd. ✓
- C. Jesus knew about Nathanael only because Philip had told him ahead of time.
- D. Jesus trusted people who came to him on their own and held back from people who came because of miracles.

*Feedback: John 1:46-50 and 2:24-25. Jesus says, "Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you" (1:48). He knew Nathanael before Nathanael had done anything toward him, and Nathanael came because Philip called him. The disciples also believed after a sign (2:11), and Jesus stayed with them, so what John stresses in 2:24-25 is what Jesus knew. The knowledge that saw "an Israelite indeed, in whom there is no deceit" (1:47) also saw through the Passover crowd. Being fully known turned the skeptic who asked, "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" (1:46) into a worshiper: "Rabbi, you are the Son of God!" (1:49).*

**21. Read 2:24-25 alongside 4:29 and 21:17. Being fully known by Jesus is both bad news and good news. Which pair matches what the text means for you?**
- A. Bad news: he sees every mistake I make. Good news: he knows my heart, so he knows I mean well deep down.
- B. Bad news: he's always watching and keeping a record. Good news: if I stay on my best behavior, I'll have nothing to hide.
- C. Bad news: he knows my sin and secrets. Good news: he knows my deepest needs too, and still offers himself to me. ✓
- D. Bad news: that Passover crowd had shallow faith. Good news: people who grow up in church don't have that problem.

*Feedback: John 2:24-25, with 4:29, 21:17, 1:12, Jeremiah 17:9-10, and Hebrews 4:13-16. Nobody gets to hide behind good intentions or good behavior, and nobody has to. Option A trusts the heart itself, and Scripture won't: "The heart is deceitful above all things... I the LORD search the heart" (Jeremiah 17:9-10). John 2:24 shows the hard side of being fully known: Jesus held himself back from a crowd drawn by signs. The rest of John shows the other side. The Samaritan woman runs to town saying, "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did" (4:29), and Peter, restored, says, "Lord, you know everything" (21:17). To all who receive him, he gives the right to become children of God (1:12). Hebrews puts both sides in one breath: "all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account... Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace" (Hebrews 4:13, 16). He knows the worst and the deepest in you, and knowing all of it, he still holds himself out to be received.*

## Part 5: written answers (4 points each, graded by hand)

**22. Choose one thing John says about Jesus in chapter 1 that the sign at Cana puts on display. Write (1) the claim from John 1 with its verse, (2) the detail from John 2:1-11 that displays it, with its verse, and how it displays it, and (3) what it shows you about who Jesus is.**

*Feedback: A full answer pairs a claim from John 1 with a detail from Cana, gives a verse for each, explains how the detail puts the claim on display, and says what that shows about who Jesus is.*

Rubric: 1 point for an accurate claim from John 1 with a correct verse, 1 point for a matching detail from 2:1-11 with a correct verse, 1 point for explaining how the detail displays the claim, and 1 point for a clear statement of what it shows about who Jesus is.

Accept any accurate pairing the student can explain. The common ones:
- "All things were made through him" (1:3) and the water made wine (2:7-9): he is the Creator.
- "We have seen his glory" (1:14) and "manifested his glory" (2:11): God's own glory, shown in the Son.
- "From his fullness we have all received, grace upon grace" (1:16) and jars filled to the brim with good wine kept until now (2:6-7, 10): he gives in overflowing abundance, an echo of the feast God promised (Isaiah 25:6).
- "The law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ" (1:17) and the purification jars becoming wine (2:6-9): what the old washings looked toward has arrived in him, cleansing before God and the joy of the feast.
- "You will see greater things than these" (1:50) and the disciples seeing his glory and believing (2:11): he keeps his word to the ones who follow him.

Model answer: John 1:3 says, "All things were made through him." At Cana the servants fill the jars with water, and the master of the feast tastes "the water now become wine" (2:9). The Word through whom all things were made takes what he made and changes it without a touch or a prayer, with only an instruction to the servants. The sign shows that Jesus is the Creator himself, God at a wedding.

**23. Jesus says, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up" (2:19). Write (1) what the Jewish leaders thought he meant (2:20) and what he actually meant (2:21), (2) when the disciples understood the saying and what they believed then (2:22), (3) in your own words, how John 1:14 connects to Jesus being the temple, and (4) what that shows you about who Jesus is.**

*Feedback: A full answer explains that the leaders heard a claim about the building while Jesus meant his body, says that the disciples understood after he was raised and believed the Scripture and his word, connects "dwelt among us" (1:14) to Jesus as the place where God lives with his people, and says what that shows about who Jesus is.*

Rubric: 1 point for the leaders' misunderstanding and Jesus' meaning (the building that took forty-six years, 2:20; the temple of his body, 2:21). 1 point for when the disciples understood (after he was raised from the dead) and what they believed (the Scripture and the word Jesus had spoken), 2:22. 1 point for connecting 1:14 to the temple (the Word "dwelt" or pitched his tent among us, as God dwelt in the tabernacle and temple). 1 point for a clear statement, in the student's own words, of what this shows about who Jesus is (he is where God lives with people and where we meet God).

Model answer: The leaders thought he meant the temple building, which had "taken forty-six years to build" (2:20), but John says "he was speaking about the temple of his body" (2:21). The disciples understood only after he was raised from the dead, when they remembered what he said and "believed the Scripture and the word that Jesus had spoken" (2:22). John 1:14 says the Word "dwelt among us," the way God lived among Israel in the tabernacle. So the temple was pointing ahead to Jesus himself. He is where God lives with people, and he is where we meet God.

## Teacher notes

**Answer choices and references.** No answer choice carries a verse reference or quotation marks, and the instructions tell students so. Correct answers put John's words in plain language, so students have to read the verse to see which choice says what John says. Every quotation is in the feedback, where it confirms what the student found in the text. Right answers vary in length and in the number of sentences. The key is balanced: A five times, B six, C five, D five.

**How the wrong answers work.** Every wrong answer is something people actually believe about this chapter, worded the way they say it. None of them is a straw man, and several are preached from pulpits. Several are true somewhere else in Scripture or elsewhere in the story, including Q1 A, B, and D; Q7 C; Q9 C; Q12 B; and Q13 A. Q10 D and Q16 D are half true. Three wrong answers give Jesus a high place and still miss the verse (Q1 D, Q13 A, Q16 B), so a student who always picks the highest view of Christ without reading will lose points. The feedback acknowledges what is true before showing what John 2 says. Going over the quiz lets students see which of their beliefs came from the page and which came from tradition, church culture, or their own instincts. Where the beliefs come from:

**Memory-verse alignment.** Unit 3 is a review week with no new verse, and the three under review are John 20:31, 1:14, and 1:12. The quiz leans on exactly those three: 20:31 anchors Q2, 1:14 anchors Q6 and feeds Q14 and Q23, and 1:12 anchors Q18 and Q21. Going over the quiz is a review session for the memory work, so you can point that out to students.

**Grading.** Grade the written answers for meaning and accurate references, the way you grade memory verses for sense. Content is graded and belief stays invitational: both written questions ask what John says about Jesus, so any student can earn full marks without being asked to profess anything. Q23 part (4) asks what the temple connection shows about Jesus, so give the fourth point for a clear statement even if it repeats Q14's wording in a new sentence. Question 21 names 4:29 and 21:17 in its stem, so students can answer it from the text with no class notes, and its feedback lands on grace, so it never works as a fear test. This matches the reading guide's note on Lesson 4, which says the cure for a shallow faith is coming to him with nothing in your hands.

**Timing.** Twenty-one open-Bible questions, several with long stems (Q7 especially), plus two written answers make 40 minutes tight for slower readers. Q14 sends students to read 1:14 and 1:51 themselves, and Q3, Q4, Q5, Q11, and Q19 each name a second verse to compare, which adds a little time. Watch the time the first time you give it.

**Handle with care when you go over it.** Question 3's option B and Question 14's option C touch Marian and priestly traditions some students' families hold. Question 1's option D touches the Lord's Supper, which families hold in different ways. The questions ask what John's text says, and the feedback stays on the text. Question 7 leaves the alcohol question alone on purpose. On Question 19, a sharp student may bring up 5:19 ("the Son can do nothing of his own accord") or Mark 13:32. Both show Jesus' true humanity and his dependence on the Father. John 2:24-25 shows what belongs to him as God: knowing every heart himself. The church has always confessed both of one person (Chalcedon). A student may also say "the Spirit showed him," and that's true too: John Owen taught that the Spirit furnished Christ's human nature with its wisdom and knowledge, and the Spirit was given to him "without measure" (3:34; Isaiah 11:2-3). What sinks option B is its scope: "sometimes" and "one person's," against John's "all people" and "he himself." On Question 14, option A describes what the church confesses about believers after death: their souls "behold the face of God, in light and glory" (Westminster Confession 32.1; Revelation 22:4). The word "now" in the stem is what makes it the wrong answer here, so say that plainly when you go over it.

**For going over the quiz.** On Q4, three passages cut from the student feedback are worth raising in class. Mark 7:3-4 says the Jews kept these washings by "holding to the tradition of the elders," customs added on top of the law's purity rules. John 3:25 shows the theme coming back, when an argument "over purification" sends John the Baptist's disciples to him and he points them to Jesus. And 15:3 shows cleansing through his word: "Already you are clean because of the word that I have spoken to you." If a student defends option B, treat it with respect: allegorical readings of the six jars are old and sincere, and the lesson is that John gives the number no meaning. On Q6, some translations render Hebrews 1:3 "the reflection of God's glory" (NRSV), so a sharp student may push back. The ESV's "radiance" and the Nicene Creed's "Light from Light" mark the difference from Moses, whose glory "was being brought to an end" (2 Corinthians 3:7). On Q9, Israel called God Father as a people, and the Davidic king could pray, "You are my Father" (Psalm 89:26). What makes "my Father's house" a claim no prophet could make is how John's enemies heard it (5:18) and how Hebrews 3:5-6 reads the house: Moses a servant in it, Christ a son over it. On Q11, have students compare Psalm 69:9 ("has consumed me") with John 2:17 ("will consume me"). The disciples' remembering turns the psalm toward what was still ahead for Jesus. The New Testament reads Psalm 69 of Jesus' suffering more than once: 69:4 in John 15:25 ("They hated me without a cause"), 69:21 in the sour wine of John 19:28-29, and 69:9 in Romans 15:3. That's the textual ground for "cost him his life at the cross." Paul reads the verse of Christ and makes Christ's self-giving the ground for ours (Romans 15:1-3), which is the order of the feedback: the zeal is his before it's ever ours. On Q12, the leaders' question was lawful in form, since Israel was told to test a prophet's word (Deuteronomy 18:21-22). John's charge against them comes later (12:37). Saying so in class keeps the care you've taken all year not to cast the Jewish people as villains. On Q13, the wedding is "on the third day" (2:1) and the riddle promises "in three days" (2:19). Some readers hear a hint of the resurrection in the pairing. The more common reading counts the third day forward from the days John marks in 1:29-43, so offer the link as a possibility for discussion. On Q15, a student may defend option A with 20:29: "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed." Read on to 20:30-31. The blessing belongs to people who believe the eyewitnesses' written account of the signs, so their faith rests on testimony handed down, and John wrote his book to give it. On option C, hostile witnesses repeated a version of the saying at his trial (Mark 14:58), so it was in circulation before Easter. On Q16, John gives no time for the remembering in 2:17, and many commentators place it at the temple scene itself. The remembering in 2:22 comes after the resurrection, and Luke 24:27 and 24:45 make a good pair with it: all the Scriptures concern him, and he is the one who opens minds to see it. On Q18, for the disciple tier: "Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ has been born of God" (1 John 5:1). The new birth lies beneath faith, and that's how the Reformed tradition reads 1:13. On Q21, if you want the comfort in John's own voice: "whenever our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart, and he knows everything" (1 John 3:20).
