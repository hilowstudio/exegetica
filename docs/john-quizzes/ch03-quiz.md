# John 3 quiz: born from above, and the Son lifted up

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John
> **Covers:** John 3:1-36 (Unit 4, "The new birth," Lessons 1-4, and Unit 5, "He must increase," Lessons 1-2)
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 21 multiple-choice questions at 2 points each, 2 short written answers at 4 points each.
> **Total:** 50 points, about 40 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz, so keep your ESV open. Many of the answer choices are things people often say about this chapter. Your job is to find the answer the text itself gives. The choices use plain words, with no quotation marks or verse references, so find the verse named in each question and read it before you choose. The multiple-choice questions are worth 2 points each. The two written answers at the end are worth 4 points each and are graded by hand.

## Part 1: the night visit and the new birth (John 3:1-8)

**1. Nicodemus is "a man of the Pharisees," "a ruler of the Jews," and in 3:10 Jesus calls him "the teacher of Israel." He opens by calling Jesus "a teacher come from God" (3:2). What is Jesus telling him in 3:3?**
- A. Even the most respected teacher in Israel needs a birth that comes from God before he can see God's kingdom. ✓
- B. The Pharisees were hypocrites, and Nicodemus had to leave them before he could follow Jesus.
- C. Nicodemus was close, and he only needed to add faith in Jesus to the good life he was already living.
- D. Calling Jesus a teacher from God was a good start, and seeing more of his signs would finish the job.

*Feedback: John 3:1-3, with 2:23-25 and 1:13. John never calls Nicodemus a hypocrite. He came to Jesus, and he'll show up again (7:50-51; 19:39). He believes because of the signs: "no one can do these signs that you do unless God is with him" (3:2). That's the same signs-faith John described just before, in 2:23-25. Jesus doesn't tell him to add something to his religion or to watch for more signs. He tells him he needs a birth: "unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God." The best religious résumé in the room can't get him in. In 3:7 the "you" in "You must be born again" is plural in Greek, so Jesus widens it past Nicodemus to everyone.*

**2. The word translated "again" in 3:3 can also mean "from above." Nicodemus asks, "Can he enter a second time into his mother's womb and be born?" (3:4). What does Jesus mean, and why is Nicodemus confused?**
- A. Jesus means a second chance to start life over, and Nicodemus thought he was too old for a fresh start.
- B. Jesus means being born into a new life after you die, and Nicodemus thought he meant this life.
- C. Jesus means a powerful emotional experience, and Nicodemus was too much of a thinker to understand it.
- D. Jesus means a birth that comes down from God, and Nicodemus heard only the meaning of being born a second time. ✓

*Feedback: John 3:3-4, 3:31, and 1:13. John uses the same word in 3:31: "He who comes from above is above all." Jesus means a birth whose source is God. Nicodemus hears only "again" and pictures going back into the womb, and that mix-up is what lets Jesus press his point. You didn't arrange your first birth, and you can't arrange this one. John said it in chapter 1: God's children are "born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God" (1:13). So "born again" isn't the name of one kind of Christian. It names what God does in every believer.*

**3. Jesus says, "unless one is born of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter the kingdom of God" (3:5). Then he asks, "Are you the teacher of Israel and yet you do not understand these things?" (3:10). Read Ezekiel 36:25-27. What do water and the Spirit point to?**
- A. Cleaning up your habits first, and then God fills you with his Spirit as a reward.
- B. A brand-new teaching that no one could have understood until Jesus explained it.
- C. God's promise to wash his people clean and put his own Spirit within them, one new birth that God gives. ✓
- D. A refreshed feeling, like cool water, that the Spirit gives you during worship.

*Feedback: John 3:5 and 3:10, with Ezekiel 36:25-27. God promised in one breath, "I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean," and "I will put my Spirit within you." Cleansing and a new heart are two sides of one work of God. Jesus scolds "the teacher of Israel" for not knowing "these things," which points to a promise Nicodemus already had in his Bible, so the new birth was no brand-new teaching (option B). It's God's old promise arriving. Option A makes the new birth a self-improvement plan with the Spirit as the prize, and option D makes it a feeling. Ezekiel says God does the washing, God gives the new heart, and God puts his Spirit within.*

**4. "The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit" (3:8). The same Greek word means both wind and Spirit. Jesus compares the wind to "everyone who is born of the Spirit." What is he saying about a person born of the Spirit?**
- A. They become free spirits, unpredictable and led wherever they feel moved to go.
- B. Their new life is real and can be noticed, like the wind's sound, but its source can't be traced to anything they did. It comes from God. ✓
- C. They can always point to the exact day and way it happened, the way you'd mark a date on a calendar.
- D. Their new life is invisible, a private feeling inside that nobody else can notice.

*Feedback: John 3:8, with 1:13 and 3:21. Jesus' comparison is about the person: "So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit." Like the wind, "you hear its sound." The new life is real, and it shows. The one who comes to the light lets it "be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God" (3:21), so option D misses it. And like the wind, "you do not know where it comes from." The new birth can't be traced back to family, effort, or a decision someone engineered. John said so in chapter 1: God's children are born "not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God" (1:13). Some believers do remember a moment, and that's a gift. What Jesus says is that the source is hidden with God. Option A borrows the wind's freedom for the person. The wind in this picture "blows where it wishes," and the freedom belongs to God. That's good news: you aren't left to produce your own new birth.*

## Part 2: the Son of Man lifted up (John 3:9-15)

**5. Nicodemus asks, "How can these things be?" (3:9), and Jesus answers with a story from Numbers. Read Numbers 21:4-9. What did the bitten Israelites have to do to live?**
- A. Confess their sin, pray, and offer a sacrifice for forgiveness.
- B. Look at the bronze serpent God told Moses to set on a pole. ✓
- C. Kill the snakes in the camp and keep away from them.
- D. Prove they were truly sorry by changing the way they lived.

*Feedback: Numbers 21:6-9. The people did confess, "We have sinned," and Moses prayed for them (21:7). That was right. But God didn't take the snakes away. He gave a cure: "everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live." So "if a serpent bit anyone, he would look at the bronze serpent and live" (21:9). No medicine, no climbing, no work. Just a look at what God provided.*

**6. Jesus says, "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (3:14). What is he talking about? (Compare 12:32-33.)**
- A. His ascension into heaven after he rose from the dead.
- B. People lifting up his name in worship and praise.
- C. His death on the cross, where he'd be raised up so dying people could look to him and live. ✓
- D. His becoming famous, so that people all over the world would look up to him.

*Feedback: John 3:14-15, with 12:32-33 and 8:28. John settles it himself. When Jesus says "when I am lifted up from the earth," John adds, "He said this to show by what kind of death he was going to die" (12:33). The cross is the pole. "Lifted up" also means exalted, so John uses one word for two heights: Jesus is raised up on the wood and raised up in glory in the same act. Praising him is good, and he did ascend, but in 3:14 the lifting up is the cross.*

**7. The snakebites were God's judgment on Israel's sin, and the bite meant death (Numbers 21:5-6). Jesus says the Son of Man must be lifted up "as Moses lifted up the serpent" (3:14). What does the bronze serpent show about how people today are saved? (Compare 3:18 and 3:36.)**
- A. Like the bitten Israelites, we're under God's judgment for sin and can't save ourselves. God has provided one rescue outside us, the Son lifted up, and we look to him and let go of every other hope. ✓
- B. God helps those who help themselves. Do everything you can about your sin, and Jesus will make up the rest.
- C. If you're truly sorry enough for your sin, God will see how sorry you are and heal you.
- D. Jesus is one of several ways God has provided, so anyone who sincerely looks to God will find life.

*Feedback: Numbers 21:5-9 and John 3:14-15, with 3:18, 3:36, and Acts 4:12. The people "spoke against God," and "the LORD sent fiery serpents among the people, and they bit the people, so that many people of Israel died" (Numbers 21:5-6). The bite was judgment, and it meant death. The cure wasn't medicine, sucking out the venom, cutting off the limb, or being sorry enough. The people did confess, "We have sinned" (21:7), and that was right, but the cure was God's one provision: "everyone who is bitten, when he sees it, shall live" (21:8). Jesus says that's us. The one who does not believe "is condemned already" (3:18), and "the wrath of God remains on him" (3:36). We can't save ourselves, and God has provided one rescue outside us: the Son lifted up. "There is salvation in no one else" (Acts 4:12). We look to him and give up every other hope.*

**8. In 3:14-15, faith is pictured as a look: "that whoever believes in him may have eternal life." In class we called it the empty-handed look of faith. In what way is the gospel free, and in what way is it costly?**
- A. It's free, so you can keep trusting everything you already lean on and simply add Jesus to it.
- B. It's costly, so you have to bring God enough good works and sacrifice to be worth saving.
- C. It's free to anyone who looks sincerely enough, because the strength of your faith is what saves you.
- D. It's free, because you can't offer anything in exchange for it. It's costly, because you have to set down everything else you look to for life and righteousness. ✓

*Feedback: John 3:14-15, with Numbers 21:9, Isaiah 55:1, and Philippians 3:7-9. The bitten Israelites came to the pole with nothing in their hands. They couldn't pay for the cure, and they didn't fix the bite. They looked at what God provided and lived. That's the free side: "he who has no money, come, buy and eat!... without money and without price" (Isaiah 55:1). You can't look at the pole while you keep trusting your own remedy, though. To take hold of Christ you have to set down everything else you'd look to for life and for righteousness, the way Paul did: "whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ... not having a righteousness of my own" (Philippians 3:7, 9). Option A keeps the free part and skips the cost. Option B turns the cost into a price. Option C makes your sincerity the thing that saves, and the healing came from the one on the pole.*

## Part 3: God so loved the world (John 3:16-17)

**9. John 3:16 comes right after 3:14, where the Son of Man is lifted up. In that light, what does "God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son" mean?**
- A. God loved the world so much that we could never measure it.
- B. God loved like this: he gave his Son to be lifted up on the cross. ✓
- C. God loves everyone so much that he accepts everyone, no matter what.
- D. God loved the world because people are valuable and worth saving.

*Feedback: John 3:14-16, with Romans 8:32 and Romans 5:8. "So loved" tells you the way God loved, and verse 14 just showed it: the Son lifted up. It's true that God's love is beyond measuring, so option A is true and still misses what "so" points to here. "Gave" is what a Father does when he hands over his Son: "He who did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all" (Romans 8:32). And God didn't love the world because it was lovable: "God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8).*

**10. Who is "the world" God loved in 3:16? (Look ahead to 3:19 and back to 1:10.)**
- A. The good people who were already trying to follow God.
- B. The earth God made: nature, the animals, and the planet.
- C. Israel, the nation God had chosen as his own.
- D. People in the dark who had turned away from God, and God loved them anyway. ✓

*Feedback: John 3:16 and 3:19, with 1:10 and 4:42. In 3:19, "the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light." In 1:10, "the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him." That's the world God loved: people with their backs turned. God made the earth and cares for it, and Israel was his chosen people, but John's word "world" here means people, and more than one nation. The Samaritans call Jesus "the Savior of the world" (4:42). God loved that world as it was, so nobody has to clean up first.*

**11. Read 3:16-17. Who receives eternal life, and why did God send his Son?**
- A. Everyone receives eternal life in the end, because God sent his Son to save and never to condemn.
- B. People who live good enough lives receive eternal life, and the Son came to show them how.
- C. Whoever believes in him receives eternal life, and God sent his Son so the world might be saved through him. ✓
- D. People raised in the faith receive eternal life, and the Son came to judge the people outside it.

*Feedback: John 3:16-18. The offer is wide and free: "whoever believes in him." Anyone at all can believe. The danger is real too: "should not perish." Verse 17 is the headline over everything hard that follows: "God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him." Option A takes verse 17 and drops verse 18, which says the one who doesn't believe "is condemned already." Belief is the way in, and the way is open to whoever believes.*

## Part 4: light and darkness (John 3:18-21)

**12. "Whoever believes in him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe is condemned already" (3:18). Is this verse about the future or about right now?**
- A. The future. It's a verdict God will announce at the end of time, so nobody's standing is known until then.
- B. Right now. It names a person's present standing, and believing in the Son moves you out of condemnation. ✓
- C. Neither. It was a warning for the leaders who were rejecting Jesus in his own day.
- D. It's a threat, meant to scare people into believing before it's too late.

*Feedback: John 3:18, with 5:24 and 3:14. "Is not condemned" and "is condemned already" are both present tense. Jesus says the same thing in 5:24: whoever believes "has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life." The verse is honest about the danger, and it holds the door open in the same breath. It isn't a scare tactic. Fear can show you a problem, and it never gives new life. Only the Son lifted up draws people (3:14).*

**13. "And this is the judgment: the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil" (3:19). What is the judgment?**
- A. God searching out every sinner so he can punish them.
- B. God weighing your good deeds against your bad ones.
- C. The guilty feeling you get when you've done something wrong.
- D. The light came, and people loved the darkness and turned away from it. ✓

*Feedback: John 3:17-20. God didn't come hunting people down. He sent his Son to save (3:17). The judgment is that the light came and people chose the dark. That keeps both things true: God is kind, because he came to save, and refusing the light is a real choice that people are responsible for. Verse 20 gives the reason people stay away: "lest his works should be exposed." The verdict has nothing to do with a scale of good and bad deeds or with feelings of guilt. It's about what people do with the light.*

**14. "But whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God" (3:21). What does the end of that verse show about the person who comes to the light?**
- A. Even their coming to the light was God at work in them. ✓
- B. They had cleaned up their life first, so they were ready to come to the light.
- C. They were good people all along who never needed to change.
- D. They had earned God's approval by doing what was true.

*Feedback: John 3:20-21, with 3:8 and 1:13. Coming to the light means letting your real self be seen, the very thing the person in 3:20 is afraid of. The light came to save (3:17), so being seen by it is safe. Then John adds that the person's works "have been carried out in God." Even the coming was God's work in them. The chapter ends where it began: the wind blows where it wishes (3:8), and God does the saving. Coming to the light isn't the entry fee you pay before grace. It's what the new birth looks like.*

## Part 5: the friend of the bridegroom (John 3:22-30)

**15. John's disciples come to him about Jesus and say, "look, he is baptizing, and all are going to him" (3:26). John answers, "A person cannot receive even one thing unless it is given him from heaven" (3:27). What is John telling them?**
- A. Success doesn't really matter, so they should stop caring about how many people come.
- B. They should pray harder, so God would send his followers back to him.
- C. Everything anyone has, a following included, is God's gift, so the people going to Jesus were God's gift to his Son, and John had nothing to defend. ✓
- D. He was admitting that God had never really sent him in the first place.

*Feedback: John 3:26-28, with 1:13 and 3:8. The disciples want John to defend his shrinking crowd. John answers on a deeper level. If every good thing is a gift from heaven, then John's crowd was a gift too, and the people crossing over to Jesus are heaven's gift to Jesus. He has nothing to grab and nothing to protect. And he was sent: "I am not the Christ, but I have been sent before him" (3:28). This is the same truth Jesus taught Nicodemus with the wind (3:8): new life and new followers come from heaven.*

**16. John calls himself "the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him," and says, "Therefore this joy of mine is now complete" (3:29). What is the friend of the bridegroom's job, and why is John's joy complete?**
- A. He's the best man, who brings the bride and groom together and rejoices when the groom comes for his bride. John's joy is full because Jesus has come. ✓
- B. He's a rival for the bride who has lost her, and John is choosing to give her up graciously.
- C. He's a servant at the feast, and John is glad because his hard work is finally over.
- D. He's the groom's closest friend, and John is happy because he'll get to share in Jesus' fame.

*Feedback: John 3:28-29, with 1:20 and 1:23. At a wedding the friend of the bridegroom arranged things, looked after the bride, and brought the two together. His work was done when he heard the bridegroom's voice, and at that sound his joy was full. John's joy fills up at the very moment his crowd leaves, because the one he was sent to point to has come. John knew who he was all along: "I am not the Christ" (1:20), "the voice of one crying out in the wilderness" (1:23). Now the voice goes quiet so the bridegroom's voice can be heard.*

**17. John says, "The one who has the bride is the bridegroom" (3:29). Back at Cana, Jesus supplied the wine at a wedding (2:1-11), the groom's own job. According to the wedding thread through the Bible, who is the bride? (See Isaiah 54:5, Ephesians 5:25-27, and Revelation 19:7-9.)**
- A. Each believer on their own, in a private romance with Jesus.
- B. John the Baptist's followers, who were now leaving John to follow Jesus.
- C. God's people, the church, whom Christ loved and gave himself up for. ✓
- D. The nation of Israel, and no one else.

*Feedback: John 2:9-11 and 3:29, with Isaiah 54:5, Hosea 2:19, Ephesians 5:25-27, and Revelation 19:7-9. God told Israel, "your Maker is your husband" (Isaiah 54:5), and "I will betroth you to me forever" (Hosea 2:19). Jesus is that bridegroom come in person, and his bride is his people: "Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her" (Ephesians 5:25). The thread ends at "the marriage supper of the Lamb" (Revelation 19:9). The bride is one people, Jew and Gentile together in Christ, so option D draws the circle too small. Each believer belongs to Christ, and the Bible's picture is a whole people as one bride. The followers crossing over to Jesus in 3:26 were part of that people coming to him.*

**18. "He must increase, but I must decrease" (3:30). What does John's decrease show?**
- A. Christians should think of themselves as worthless, so that Jesus can be everything.
- B. John was losing confidence after his ministry shrank, so he gave up.
- C. We should all work hard at being humble like John, and if we're humble enough, God will reward us.
- D. John's place before God was settled, so he could gladly step back for Jesus to be seen. His decrease was joy. ✓

*Feedback: John 3:27-30. John's lowliness comes from a secure place before God. He knows exactly who he is (3:28), so a shrinking crowd can't shake him, and he says his joy is "complete" (3:29). That's confidence. Option A turns it into self-hatred, and John was glad, not crushed. Option C turns it into a task you perform to earn a reward. Humility this glad grows from knowing you belong to Christ, and Christ is its source. The word "must" carries God's settled will: Jesus increasing was always the plan.*

## Part 6: the One from above (John 3:31-36)

**19. From 3:31 on, John the Gospel writer is speaking (John the Baptist's words end at 3:30). Read 3:31-32. Where does Jesus come from, and why can you trust what he says?**
- A. He comes from above, from heaven, so he tells what he has seen and heard himself. ✓
- B. He grew up with devout parents and studied the Scriptures carefully.
- C. He was the greatest of the prophets and received visions like the prophets before him.
- D. He was a good man whose life matched his words, so people could trust him.

*Feedback: John 3:31-32, with 3:13, 1:1, and 1:18. "He who comes from above is above all... He bears witness to what he has seen and heard." Jesus told Nicodemus the same thing: "No one has ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven, the Son of Man" (3:13). The Prologue said it first: "No one has ever seen God; God the only Son, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (1:18). Everyone else, even John the Baptist, speaks "from the earth." Jesus speaks as an eyewitness of heaven.*

**20. Read 3:36. It describes two roads. Which answer matches what John says?**
- A. Believe and you'll receive eternal life when you die. Refuse, and you'll be punished later if you don't change.
- B. Keep God's commands and you earn life. Break them and you lose it.
- C. Believe in the Son and you have eternal life now. Refuse to obey the Son and you won't see life, and God's wrath stays on you. ✓
- D. Believe and you have life, and whether you obey the Son afterward makes no difference.

*Feedback: John 3:36, with 5:24 and 3:16. "Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life." The verb is present tense, so life with God starts now, the day you trust him. On the other road John writes "whoever does not obey the Son," where you might expect "does not believe." He treats trusting the Son and obeying the Son as one movement toward him, so faith and following can't be split apart (option D), and life is a gift to receive, not a wage earned by keeping rules (option B). This is the message of 3:16 said again: believe in the Son and have life.*

**21. 3:36 ends, "the wrath of God remains on him." What does "remains" mean, and how does the first half of the verse answer it?**
- A. God gets angry at people for a while, but his anger cools off if you give it time.
- B. Wrath is God's settled response to sin, and it already rests on a person outside of Christ. The Son came to lift it, and whoever believes in him has life. ✓
- C. Wrath belongs to the Old Testament, and Jesus replaced it with love.
- D. Wrath stays on Christians who keep sinning after they believe.

*Feedback: John 3:36 and 3:14-16, with Ephesians 2:3-5. God's wrath isn't a temper. It's his settled, right response to sin, and "remains" means it's already resting on a person and stays there until they come to the Son. Paul says the same: we "were by nature children of wrath, like the rest of mankind. But God, being rich in mercy... made us alive together with Christ" (Ephesians 2:3-5). Remember the bronze serpent: the venom was real, and the serpent lifted up healed it (3:14). Wrath is real, and the cross is where Jesus lifts it. That's why the verse leads with the gift: "Whoever believes in the Son has eternal life." And the Son the Father loves, who holds "all things" in his hand (3:35), holds the life he gives.*

## Part 7: written answers (4 points each, graded by hand)

**22. Jesus says, "as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life" (3:14-15). Write (1) what was happening in Numbers 21 and what the bitten Israelites had to do to live, with a verse, (2) what Jesus says will happen to him and what the "pole" is, with a verse, (3) how the look is a picture of faith, and (4) what this shows you about who Jesus is.**

*Feedback: A full answer retells the bronze serpent with a verse from Numbers 21, says that Jesus would be lifted up on the cross with a verse from John, explains the empty-handed look of faith (the bitten brought nothing and looked at what God provided), and says what that shows about who Jesus is.*

Rubric: 1 point for the Numbers 21 story and the look, with a correct verse (the people spoke against God, the LORD sent fiery serpents, many died, and whoever was bitten looked at the bronze serpent on the pole and lived, 21:6-9). 1 point for the cross as the pole, with a correct verse (3:14, or 12:32-33). 1 point for the empty-handed look of faith: you bring nothing in exchange and set down everything else you'd look to, and you trust the one God provided. Give the point for either half stated clearly. 1 point for a clear statement of what it shows about who Jesus is (he is God's one cure for people dying of sin, lifted up in our place).

Model answer: In Numbers 21 the people spoke against God, and the LORD sent snakes that bit them, so that many died. God told Moses to put a bronze serpent on a pole, and "if a serpent bit anyone, he would look at the bronze serpent and live" (Numbers 21:9). Jesus says the Son of Man "must... be lifted up" the same way (3:14), and 12:33 says he meant the kind of death he would die, so the cross is the pole. The bitten didn't fix the bite or pay for the cure. They came empty-handed and looked at what God provided, and faith is like that: I can't offer Jesus anything for life, and I have to let go of everything else I'd trust in to take hold of him. It shows that Jesus is God's cure for sin, the one lifted up in our place so that whoever looks to him lives.

**23. John 3:16 and 3:36 say the same message in two different ways. Write (1) what 3:16 says God did and why, (2) the two roads in 3:36 and what each person has, (3) one thing the two verses share, and (4) what they show you about who Jesus is.**

*Feedback: A full answer says that God gave his only Son so that whoever believes would not perish but have eternal life, lays out both roads of 3:36, names something the two verses have in common, and says what that shows about who Jesus is.*

Rubric: 1 point for 3:16 (God loved the world and gave his only Son, so that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life). 1 point for both roads of 3:36 (whoever believes in the Son has eternal life; whoever does not obey the Son shall not see life, and the wrath of God remains on him). 1 point for a real shared point (believing in the Son as the way to life; eternal life as the gift; a real danger on the other side; the Son sent from the Father). 1 point for a clear statement of what the verses show about who Jesus is (the only Son, the Father's gift, the one in whom life is found).

Model answer: John 3:16 says God loved the world in this way: he gave his only Son, so that whoever believes in him "should not perish but have eternal life." John 3:36 gives two roads. Whoever believes in the Son "has eternal life," and whoever does not obey the Son won't see life, and "the wrath of God remains on him." Both verses say that believing in the Son is the way to life, and both are honest that there's real danger without him. They show that Jesus is God's only Son, the gift the Father gave, and that life is found in him alone.

## Teacher notes

**Answer choices and references.** No answer choice carries a verse reference or quotation marks, and the instructions tell students so. Correct answers put John's words in plain language, so students have to read the verse to see which choice says what John says. Every quotation is in the feedback, where it confirms what the student found in the text. Right answers vary in length. The key is balanced: A five times, B five, C six, D five. Forms shuffles the options, so the balance matters for the printed copy.

**How the wrong answers work.** Every wrong answer is something people actually believe about this chapter, worded the way they say it. Several are true somewhere else in Scripture, or true in part, including Q5 A (they did confess and Moses did pray), Q6 A and B (he did ascend, and he is to be praised), Q9 A (God's love is beyond measure), Q10 B and C (God made the earth, and Israel was his chosen people), and Q17 A and B. The feedback says what's true before showing what John 3 says. Where the beliefs come from:

**Memory-verse alignment.** Unit 4 plants 3:16, and Unit 5 is review-only. The quiz doesn't ask students to recite 3:16, since the memory work is graded on its own line and the quiz is open Bible. It does lean on 3:16 in Q9, Q10, Q11, and Q23, so going over the quiz is a review session for the verse. John 3:30 is the optional off-line stretch for Unit 5; Q18 teaches it and doesn't ask anyone to recite it.

**Grading.** Grade the written answers for meaning and accurate references, the way you grade memory verses for sense. Content is graded and belief stays invitational: both written questions ask what John says, so any student can earn full marks without being asked to profess anything. Q12, Q13, and Q21 cover condemnation and wrath, and their feedback leads with the rescue, so none of them works as a fear test. That matches the unit rulings: fear diagnoses and never regenerates, and only the Son lifted up draws.

**Timing.** Twenty-one open-Bible questions with several long stems (Q1, Q15, Q16, Q17), plus two written answers, make 40 minutes tight. Q3 and Q5 send students to Ezekiel 36 and Numbers 21, and Q17 names three passages, which adds time. Watch the time the first time you give it.

**Handle with care when you go over it.** The quiz leaves the baptism readings of 3:5 alone on purpose, so no option or feedback line takes them up. Q4 option C: some students will have a date they remember, so say plainly that a remembered moment is a gift and the verse's point is that the source is hidden with God. Q7 option D will meet students with friends or family of other faiths; keep the feedback on the text's one provision (Numbers 21:8; Acts 4:12) and away from anyone's people. Q7 option C names sorrow, which the Israelites rightly had (21:7); the point is that sorrow was never the cure. Q10 option C: Israel was God's chosen people, and the point is that John's "world" reaches past one nation, never that Israel is left out. Keep the care you've taken all year not to cast the Jewish people as villains: Nicodemus is "a ruler of the Jews," and he's a seeker, not a foil (Q1 option B is the cartoon). On Q17, option D is wrong because of the word "only." Old Testament Israel is the bride in Hosea and Isaiah, and the New Testament bride includes believing Israel. On Q20, a sharp student may ask whether obeying earns life. The answer is in the verse's own pairing: John sets "does not obey" opposite "believes," so obeying here is the shape of believing, and life is had by believing.

**For going over the quiz.** On Q1, bring Nicodemus back once more as the bookend: he comes by night (3:2), defends Jesus before the council (7:50-51), and by 19:39 he helps bury Jesus in the open with "about seventy-five pounds" of spices. Let the trajectory preach without overclaiming his conversion. On Q2, the same word anothen shows up in 19:11 ("from above"), which a disciple-tier student can look up. On Q4, the "Truly, truly" at 3:3, 5, and 11 is Jesus staking his authority on what follows. On Q6, 8:28 is the third "lifted up" text, and it's worth reading aloud: "When you have lifted up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he." On Q19, the speaker change at 3:31 is one sentence and no more. The ESV, NIV, and NET all close the Baptist's words at 3:30. On Q21, 3:33 ("sets his seal to this, that God is true") and 1 John 5:10 are the disciple-tier flip side: to refuse the Son is to call God a liar. Handle it gently.

**Coverage.** Every question maps to a content objective from the six lessons. Unit 4 Lessons 1 and 4 and Unit 5 Lesson 2 are tested by multiple choice; Unit 4 Lesson 2 also has Q22, and Unit 4 Lesson 3 and Unit 5 Lesson 2 share Q23. Not quizzed: reciting 3:16 (graded on the memory line), 3:13 and 3:33-35 beyond their use in feedback (stretch-tier), and the speaker question as its own item (it's in Q19's stem).
