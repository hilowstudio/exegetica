# John 1 quiz: the Word and the first witnesses

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John  
> **Covers:** John 1:1-51 (Unit 1 Lesson 1 through Unit 2 Lesson 4)  
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 21 multiple-choice questions at 2 points each, 2 short written answers at 4 points each.  
> **Total:** 50 points, about 30 to 35 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz, so keep your ESV open. Every question gives you a verse reference. Look it up and read it before you answer. The multiple-choice questions are worth 2 points each. The two written answers at the end are worth 4 points each and are graded by hand.

## Part 1: the Word (John 1:1-18)

**1. Read John 1:1. What three things does John say about the Word?**
- A. The Word was made first, the Word worked for God, and the Word became a god.
- B. The Word already existed in the beginning, the Word was with God, and the Word was God. ✓
- C. The Word was light, the Word was life, and the Word became flesh.
- D. The Word was with Moses, the Word gave the law, and the Word was a prophet.

*Feedback: John 1:1. He already existed when the beginning began, he's a distinct person who was with God, and he is God himself. The one this whole book is about is God.*

**2. Some people read 1:1 as "the Word was a god," meaning a lesser, created being. How does 1:3 show that can't be right?**
- A. Everything that was made was made through him, so he can't be one of the things that was made. ✓
- B. The word "God" in 1:1 starts with a capital letter, so it has to mean the real God.
- C. John the Baptist said the Word was God, and John was a prophet.
- D. Anyone who is "with God" has to be God.

*Feedback: John 1:3 says, "All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." The maker of everything can't be one of the things he made. John closes the book the same way, when Thomas calls Jesus "My Lord and my God!" (20:28).*

**3. John 1:5 says, "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." What does this mean?**
- A. The light and the darkness are equally strong, and the fight could go either way.
- B. The darkness will win in the end, so the light has to hide.
- C. Jesus' light keeps shining in a dark world, and the darkness can't put it out. ✓
- D. The light only shines in places where there's no darkness.

*Feedback: John 1:5. The light is shining right now, in a world that really is dark, and the darkness hasn't beaten it. The light has a name: Jesus.*

**4. John 1:6 introduces "a man sent from God, whose name was John." Who is he, and why was he sent (1:6-8)?**
- A. The John who wrote this Gospel, sent to be the light of the world
- B. John the Baptist, sent to be the light for Israel
- C. John the disciple, sent to write down Jesus' miracles
- D. John the Baptist, sent to bear witness about the light so that all might believe through him ✓

*Feedback: John 1:6-8. This is John the Baptist, a different man from the John who wrote the book. His whole job was to point people to the light, and the light is Jesus.*

**5. John 1:10-11 names two groups who did not receive the Word. Who are they?**
- A. The Romans and the Greeks
- B. The world, which was made through him, and his own people ✓
- C. The priests and the Levites
- D. The disciples and John the Baptist

*Feedback: John 1:10-11. The world he made didn't know him, and his own people turned him away. John is honest about the rejection before he tells you that some did receive him (1:12).*

**6. John 1:12 says that to all who received Jesus and believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God. According to 1:13, who causes that new birth?**
- A. Being born into the right family ("blood")
- B. A person's own natural desire ("the will of the flesh")
- C. A person's own decision ("the will of man")
- D. God ✓

*Feedback: John 1:13. John rules out family line, natural desire, and human decision, and he credits God: "but of God." You come to Jesus and believe, and God is the one who gives the new birth. You'll hear Jesus say the same thing to Nicodemus in John 3.*

**7. John 1:14 says, "And the Word became flesh." What does "became flesh" mean?**
- A. The Word became a real, fully human person while staying fully God. ✓
- B. The Word only looked human, the way an actor wears a costume.
- C. The Word stopped being God so he could become a man.
- D. The Word became a message about God that people could read.

*Feedback: John 1:14. The God of 1:1 became a real man you could see and touch, and he stayed fully God. He came close enough to be seen, heard, and even killed.*

**8. The word behind "dwelt" in 1:14 means to pitch a tent. What Old Testament picture is John pointing to?**
- A. The tent where Abraham met three visitors
- B. The tents the Israelites camped in for forty years in the wilderness
- C. The tabernacle, the tent God's glory filled so he could live among his people ✓
- D. The tents shepherds lived in while they watched their flocks

*Feedback: John 1:14. In Exodus, God's glory filled the tabernacle so he could live in the middle of his people (Exodus 40:34-38). Jesus is that tent in person. God moved into the neighborhood in a body.*

**9. Read John 1:17: "For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ." What does this mean?**
- A. The law of Moses was bad, so Jesus threw it out.
- B. The God of the Old Testament was harsh, and Jesus showed people a kinder God.
- C. The same God gave both. The law was a good gift, and Jesus brought grace and truth in person and in full. ✓
- D. Moses and Jesus taught exactly the same thing in exactly the same way.

*Feedback: John 1:16-17. One God, one gift after another: "grace upon grace." What Moses served in shadow, Jesus brought in person.*

**10. John 1:18 begins, "No one has ever seen God." According to the rest of the verse, how can anyone know what God is like?**
- A. Jesus, who is at the Father's side, has made him known. ✓
- B. People can study the stars and nature closely.
- C. People can keep the law of Moses perfectly.
- D. Prophets can see him in dreams and visions.

*Feedback: John 1:18. Jesus, at the Father's side, has made God known. If you want to know what God is like, look hard at Jesus. That's why we're spending the whole year in this book.*

## Part 2: the witnesses and the first disciples (John 1:19-51)

**11. The priests and Levites ask John the Baptist, "Who are you?" (1:19-23). He tells them he isn't the Christ, Elijah, or the Prophet. What does he say he is?**
- A. The light of the world
- B. The Lamb of God
- C. The one who baptizes with the Holy Spirit
- D. "The voice of one crying out in the wilderness, 'Make straight the way of the Lord'" ✓

*Feedback: John 1:23. He calls himself a voice, quoting the prophet Isaiah. A voice carries a message and then fades out. The other three answers all describe Jesus.*

**12. In 1:26 John tells the men questioning him, "Among you stands one you do not know." What did we learn this means?**
- A. An angel was hiding in the crowd.
- B. Jesus the Messiah was already standing among them, and the religious leaders didn't recognize him. ✓
- C. Elijah had secretly come back.
- D. John was talking about himself.

*Feedback: John 1:26-27. The Messiah was standing right there, and the people who knew the Bible best missed him. That's 1:11 coming true: "his own people did not receive him."*

**13. The next day John sees Jesus coming and says, "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (1:29). Why does calling Jesus a lamb point ahead to the cross?**
- A. A lamb was a sacrifice that died in someone's place, like the Passover lamb, and like the servant in Isaiah 53 who "bore the sin of many." ✓
- B. Lambs are gentle, so John meant Jesus would be a gentle teacher.
- C. Jesus was a shepherd who took care of lambs.
- D. Lambs were a sign of springtime and new beginnings.

*Feedback: John 1:29. On the first day of Jesus' public life, before a single miracle, John announces how he'll die. The cross was the reason he came.*

**14. The words "takes away" in 1:29 mean to lift up and carry off. What does that picture?**
- A. Sin gets covered over, but it's still there underneath.
- B. God decides to look the other way.
- C. Sin gets a little smaller every year.
- D. Sin is lifted off you and carried away, gone for good. ✓

*Feedback: John 1:29. It's a real removal, the way trash gets carried out of the house for good. The Lamb takes away the sin of the world, and that includes yours.*

**15. John says twice, "I myself did not know him" (1:31, 33). How did he come to recognize Jesus as the Son of God (1:32-34)?**
- A. He studied the prophets until he figured it out.
- B. God showed him: he saw the Spirit come down like a dove and remain on Jesus. ✓
- C. Jesus told John who he was.
- D. The crowd pointed Jesus out to him.

*Feedback: John 1:31-34. Even the greatest witness needed God to show him who Jesus was. Recognizing Jesus is a gift God gives, and that's good news if you don't think of yourself as a naturally spiritual person.*

**16. In 1:38 Jesus speaks his first words in John's Gospel. What does he ask, and what does he tell the two disciples in 1:39?**
- A. "Who do you say that I am?" and "Follow me."
- B. "Do you love me?" and "Feed my sheep."
- C. "What are you seeking?" and "Come and you will see." ✓
- D. "What are you seeking?" and "Go and tell your brothers."

*Feedback: John 1:38-39. Jesus opens with a question and an invitation to come find out for yourself. The two disciples spent the rest of the day with him.*

**17. After spending the day with Jesus, what's the first thing Andrew does (1:40-42)?**
- A. He finds his brother Simon, tells him "We have found the Messiah," and brings him to Jesus. ✓
- B. He goes back to tell John the Baptist.
- C. He reports to the priests in Jerusalem.
- D. He goes home to think it over.

*Feedback: John 1:40-42. A man who was pointed to Jesus turns and points his own brother to him. That's how the witness keeps moving: one person telling one other person.*

**18. In 1:42 Jesus renames Simon "Cephas" (Peter), which means rock. When does Jesus give him this new name?**
- A. After Peter proved he could lead the others
- B. After Jesus rose from the dead
- C. Right away, before Peter had done anything to earn it ✓
- D. When Peter asked Jesus for a new name

*Feedback: John 1:42. Jesus names Peter on the spot, before any success or any failure. The identity Jesus gives is a gift, and he's the one who keeps it.*

**19. Nathanael scoffs, "Can anything good come out of Nazareth?" Philip answers, "Come and see" (1:46). What changes Nathanael from a scoffer into someone who says, "Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!" (1:47-49)?**
- A. Jesus does a miracle in front of him.
- B. Philip wins the argument by quoting Moses.
- C. Jesus shows he already knows him: "When you were under the fig tree, I saw you." ✓
- D. John the Baptist tells Nathanael that Jesus is the Messiah.

*Feedback: John 1:47-49. Jesus knew Nathanael before they ever met and named something true and good in him. Being fully known by Jesus, and still wanted, turned a scoffer into a worshiper.*

**20. In 1:51 Jesus says Nathanael "will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending on the Son of Man." This reaches back to Jacob's dream of a ladder between heaven and earth (Genesis 28). What is Jesus claiming?**
- A. He'll build a real ladder up to heaven someday.
- B. Nathanael will become an angel.
- C. Angels will do Jesus' work for him while he's on earth.
- D. He is the ladder, the place where heaven and earth meet and God comes down to be with people. ✓

*Feedback: John 1:51. Jacob woke up from that dream and called the place "the house of God" (Genesis 28:17). Jesus says the house of God is now a person. To reach God, you come to Jesus.*

**21. Across John 1, Jesus is called the Lamb of God, Rabbi, Messiah, Son of God, King of Israel, and Son of Man. What did we learn these names are doing?**
- A. Each name describes a different person in the chapter.
- B. They're different angles on one person, all showing who Jesus is. ✓
- C. They're titles the disciples got wrong and later dropped.
- D. They're a list of names to memorize for the test.

*Feedback: John 1. Many names, one person. They're building toward what Thomas says near the end of the book: "My Lord and my God!" (20:28).*

## Part 3: written answers (4 points each, graded by hand)

**22. John 1:12 says, "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God." Choose one scene: Andrew and Peter (1:35-42) or Philip and Nathanael (1:43-51). Show where each part of 1:12 happens in that scene: (1) receiving Jesus, (2) believing in his name, and (3) being given a place in God's family. Give a verse reference for each.**

*Feedback: A full answer shows all three parts of 1:12 in one scene, each with a verse: receiving Jesus (following him, coming, staying), believing in his name (a confession like "We have found the Messiah"), and being given a place (Simon's new name, or Nathanael called "an Israelite indeed").*

Rubric: 1 point for each of the three parts tied to a real moment in the scene, plus 1 point for verse references on all three.

Model answer (Andrew and Peter): The two disciples receive Jesus when they follow him, come, and stay with him that day (1:37-39), and Andrew brings Simon to him (1:42). Andrew believes in his name when he says, "We have found the Messiah" (1:41). Jesus gives Simon a place when he names him Cephas on the spot, a new identity he didn't earn (1:42).

Model answer (Philip and Nathanael): Philip receives Jesus when Jesus says "Follow me" (1:43), and Nathanael comes to see him (1:46-47). Nathanael believes in his name: "Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are the King of Israel!" (1:49). Jesus gives Nathanael a place when he calls him "an Israelite indeed" and promises he'll see heaven opened (1:47, 51).

**23. According to John 1, who is Jesus? Answer in two or three sentences. Use at least two names or titles John gives him in this chapter, with verse references, and say what each one tells you about him.**

*Feedback: A full answer says plainly who Jesus is and uses two titles from John 1 with verse references, like the Word (1:1), the Lamb of God (1:29), the Son of God (1:34, 49), or the Son of Man (1:51), and explains what at least one of them shows about him.*

Rubric: 1 point for a clear, accurate statement of who Jesus is, 1 point for each of two titles with a correct verse reference, and 1 point for explaining what at least one title shows about him.

Accept any accurate title: the Word (1:1, 14), the light (1:4-9), the only Son at the Father's side (1:14, 18), the Lamb of God (1:29, 36), the Son of God (1:34, 49), Rabbi (1:38, 49), the Messiah (1:41), the one Moses and the prophets wrote about (1:45), the King of Israel (1:49), the Son of Man (1:51).

Model answer: John says Jesus is God who became a man so we could see him. He's the Word (1:1), who was God and made everything, and he's the Lamb of God (1:29), who came to die in our place and carry away the sin of the world.

## Teacher notes

**Grading.** Grade the written answers for meaning and accurate references, the way you grade memory verses for sense. Content is graded and belief stays invitational: both written questions ask what John says about Jesus, so any student can earn full marks without being asked to profess anything.

**Two ESV wording checks, confirmed against the Crossway text:**
- 1:39 reads "Come and you will see." "Come and see" is Philip's line in 1:46. The lessons used "come and see" for both, so Question 16 quotes 1:39 exactly.
- Newer ESV printings of 1:18 read "God the only Son, who is at the Father's side," and older ones read "the only God." Question 10 avoids both phrases so it works with either printing in your classroom set.
