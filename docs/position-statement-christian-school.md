# Our Position Statement on the Wise & Ethical Use of AI in Christian Education

*Hi Low Studio LLC. Adapted for Christian schools from our statement on Christian home education.*

As we stand in this new technological age, we are called to be wise stewards of the tools God has allowed humanity to create. Large Language Models ("LLMs", commonly referred to as "Artificial Intelligence"), in their power and potential, present both great opportunities and serious risks for the Christian school and the families it serves. We acknowledge that discerning Christian parents and teachers may be working through how this new technology may shape our classrooms and our households. Therefore, with the Scriptures as our guide and the flourishing of our students as our aim, we put forward the following principles for the wise and ethical use of AI in the sacred spheres of Christian schooling, worship, and discipleship.

## Part I: Principles for Proper Stewardship

We affirm that human technological innovation is an expression of the Imago Dei and can be a gift of common grace when properly stewarded. In the school, AI's proper role is that of a tool or a servant, always under the dominion and discernment of the teacher, who serves under the authority of parents and the school's leadership. Proper uses are characterized by human agency, clear boundaries, and a commitment to tasks that are impersonal and supplementary.

### AI as a Research Assistant in the Classroom

AI can be prudently used to supplement a teacher-led curriculum. It may serve as a powerful encyclopedia to explain a scientific concept, provide historical context for a piece of literature, or generate practice problems for a math lesson. In this role, it is a tool for gathering information, which the teacher must then vet, interpret, and teach through the lens of a biblical worldview.

### AI as a Creative Spark for Discipleship

AI can assist teachers in preparing for discipleship moments. A teacher might ask it to generate ethical scenarios for class discussion ("What would you do if...?"), create a list of questions to discuss after reading a chapter of Scripture, or find the lyrics to a hymn for chapel. The AI provides the raw material; the teacher provides the wisdom, application, and relational context.

### AI as an Administrative Helper

AI can be used for impersonal tasks that support the school's mission, such as organizing a class schedule, drafting a rubric, or finding resources for a lesson plan. This is a legitimate use of technology to redeem time for the more important, non-delegable tasks of love and instruction.

### AI in the Hands of Students

A student may use AI only for a purpose a teacher has set, and only as a reference work: to find a source, check a fact, or see where a passage is discussed. What it returns is a starting point, which the student must check against the text itself. It is never the student's thinking, and work submitted as a student's own must be the student's own. Students and their parents will be told plainly what such a tool keeps, who can read it, and what it sends elsewhere.

In all these uses, the governing principle is that teachers and students should regard and utilize AI as a machine that serves the God-given responsibility of parents and of the teachers they entrust, not a partner that shares that responsibility.

## Part II: Prohibitions and Dangers

AI is categorically disqualified from occupying any role that requires a soul, a personal relationship with God, a shepherd's heart, or the love of a parent or teacher. To cede these roles to a machine is a major theological error that neglects the very heart of the family and the school.

### AI Must Never Be the Teacher

The responsibility of teaching and training a child is a sacred, relational duty given to parents (Deut. 6:7, Eph. 6:4) and entrusted in part to the teachers who serve them. The "friction" and struggle of learning, for both teacher and student, is a God-ordained process of spiritual and intellectual formation. To outsource teaching to a "frictionless" AI is to short-circuit this formation, stunt the teacher's growth, and rob the student of an education rooted in love and a unified worldview. AI can provide well-structured content, but it cannot impart experiential wisdom. Just as with a textbook, AI is a tool for the teacher to utilize, not a replacement for the teacher.

### AI Must Never Lead Worship

Chapel, classroom prayer, and family worship are not a transfer of information; they are the "logic on fire," as D. Martyn Lloyd-Jones called it, of a people communing with their Creator. Prayer, devotion, and exhortation are acts that flow from a heart that knows and loves God. As E. M. Bounds wrote, "The Holy Ghost does not flow through methods, but through men. He does not come on machinery, but on men." To have an AI lead a prayer or orchestrate a devotion to God is to invite a dead formalism into the heart of the school and the home.

### AI Must Never Be a Companion

The greatest danger of AI is the subtle, slow erosion of relationships that leads to mutually assured boredom, where we find our own family members, classmates, and teachers boring compared to an artificial companion. Allowing a student (or a teacher or parent) to turn to an AI for companionship, comfort, or relational and spiritual counsel is to actively surrender them to a manipulative technology that creates false trust bonds and preys on the God-given desire to be known and loved. It is to betray our humanity and teach our students that a machine is a worthy substitute for a person. This is a lie that leads to a wasteland of lovelessness.

## Part III: The Ultimate Goal

Why do we insist on these boundaries? Because the Christian home is to be a sanctuary of embodied love and a primary outpost for the kingdom of God, and the Christian school serves that home as a community of the same love. In an age of endless distraction, the most counter-cultural and Christ-like gift we can give our students is our attention. The heart of the gospel is that the omniscient God of the universe is mindful of us, pouring out His careful attention upon His children.

Our mandate as teachers, in partnership with parents, is to reflect this divine attentiveness to the students in our care. By rejecting the false promises of AI and choosing the glorious impingement of real, messy, embodied school and family life, we are doing more than setting rules. We are building a community where students can flourish in the knowledge that they are seen, known, and loved. First, by their Creator, and then by the parents and teachers He has appointed to shepherd them.
