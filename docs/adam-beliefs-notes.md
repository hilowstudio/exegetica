# Adam Vega — Belief Study: Running Notes

**Method:** Reading all 136 sermon transcripts in full (28 batches of 5), in chronological order. For each sermon I record its core convictions and note recurring emphases as they surface. These notes are the raw material; the synthesis ("What Adam Vega believed most passionately") will be written at the end from this file.

**Corpus:** 136 sermons · 4.71M characters
---

## Recurring themes tally (updated as I read)

- **Penal substitutionary atonement / imputed righteousness** — "we stand right before God SOLELY on the basis of the work done on our behalf in Christ." Christ took our payment; we get his righteousness. [S1, S2]
- **Grace vs. works / anti-moralism** — self-cleaning and consequence-avoidance are NOT worship; you can do "holy things" and be a "dirty rotten sinner in your heart." [S1]
- **"Getting the order right" — indicatives before imperatives** — obedience flows FROM secured favor, never to earn/keep it. "We talk about it a lot at Mercy's Door." Structure of Philippians proves it (gospel first, then instructions). [S2, S4]
- **Assurance / security of the believer** — "there's nothing you can do to mess it up"; freedom from proving yourself ("we're like Rudy trying to prove God made the right call" — rebuked). [S2]
- **Total depravity + monergism** — "he walked into a morgue and said 'I'll take that corpse'"; dead in sin (Eph 2), we contribute nothing. [S2]
- **Unconditional election / predestination** — chosen "before the foundation of the world," before birth, before any deeds; adoption is "Plan A, not a backup plan," utterly orchestrated. [S2]
- **The Fatherhood of God / adoption as the heart of the gospel** — J.I. Packer: "everything distinctively Christian is summed up in the knowledge of the fatherhood of God." [S2]
- **Union with Christ** — adopted as Christ's brothers → therefore the Father's sons; "the two can't be separated." [S2]
- **Sin is primarily vertical (against God)** — "against you and you only have I sinned." [S1]
- **Repentance = sprinting TO God, not away** — the test of really believing the gospel is what you do when you sin. [S1]
- **Corporate/missional salvation** — "saved not just out of something but INTO something"; give the gospel away; God is "saving a people unto himself." [S1]
- **The Holy Spirit as agent of adoption, assurance, and power** — Spirit testifies "Abba Father" (Rom 8); can't say "Jesus is Lord" apart from the Spirit. [S1, S2, S3]
- **Suffering theology: "cross first, then glory"** — suffering perfects our worship (a "weight of glory," 2 Cor 4); creation groans for our revealing (Rom 8); communion/access to the Father available NOW, not just in glory; "a cap on despair." Rejects prosperity assumptions AND the "God isn't good/powerful" objection. [S2, S3]
- **God's sovereignty over suffering/creation** — God "subjected creation to futility… in hope"; suffering has a start and an end cap; it's not random. [S3]
- **Christ-centered, expository, verse-by-verse** method; heavy Reformed sources (Packer, A.W. Pink, Keller, Joni Eareckson Tada). Personal, confessional vulnerability (absent father, adopting stepson Jack, son Boaz in NICU).

## Per-batch notes

### Batch 1 (Sermons 1–5)
- **S1 Open My Lips (Ps 51):** Believing the gospel is *active*, not mental assent — three tests: (1) ongoing confession vs. hiding, (2) giving the gospel away, (3) freedom in worship despite forgiven sin. All sin is primarily against God. Grace not self-cleaning. Broken/contrite heart is the "right sacrifice." A.W. Pink: grief-over-sin (not sinlessness) marks a child of God vs. an "empty professor."
- **S2 Adopted by God (Acts 9 / Paul):** Fatherhood of God + adoption = heart of the gospel. Total depravity ("morgue/corpse"), unconditional election before the foundation of the world, unbreakable security, Spirit-given assurance ("Abba Father"). "Getting the order right." Deeply personal (his own absent father, adopting Jack).
- **S3 Glory in Suffering (Rom 8:18–25):** No condemnation is the ground. Glorification perfects worship; suffering is instrumental, purposeful, God-ordained (subjected creation "in hope"), with a start and end cap. "Welcome, my slave — produce in me the glory God intends." Communion with the Father available now. (Joni Eareckson Tada, Keller.)
- **S4 Contentment in Christ (Phil 4:10–23):** Contentment in Christ alone, not circumstances (Paul joyful in prison). *Explicitly attacks the prosperity gospel* ("garbage") AND the inverse pride-in-poverty; both plenty and want equally threaten contentment; the answer is Christ's sufficiency, not Stoic self-sufficiency ("Christ-reliance, not self-reliance"). "Every need" met = ultimate/eternal need in Christ, not material. We follow Christ INTO suffering, not out of it ("not moving from better to better to heaven"). Structure of Philippians = gospel first, then imperatives.
- **S5 The Last Will Be First (Mk 10:13–31, rich young ruler):** High Christology — Jesus is "good with the total goodness of God, or not good at all." Grace vs. merit: the ruler counted wealth + law-keeping as righteousness; "conservative values"/self-discipline can be a damning "lesser righteousness." Salvation is FREE but COSTLY — "the demand is as total as the offer is free"; must lay everything down under Christ's lordship. Faith ≠ mental assent — must "come under his lordship." (Spurgeon, Chris Tomlin, John Trainer.)

**New themes added to tally:** (14) **Cost of discipleship / lordship** — salvation free yet total-claim; count the cost; "Christ will have all or nothing." (15) **Explicit anti-prosperity-gospel** and rejection of Stoic self-sufficiency. (16) **Faith is more than mental assent** — repeated test ("actively believing" vs. "agreeing with the mechanics"). (17) **High Christology** — Jesus as fully God.

### Batch 2 (Sermons 6–10)
- **S6 The King's Peaceful Entry (Mk 11:1–11, Palm Sunday):** Jesus deliberately fulfills OT prophecy (Daniel 9's timeline, Zechariah 9's donkey) — Christ the great **prophecy-fulfiller / promise-keeper**. "Liar-lunatic-Lord" apologetic: "he's either God or a lunatic." Attacks the modern **relativized / build-your-own Jesus** as idolatry. Two comings: suffering Messiah (donkey/Lamb) → conquering Messiah (white horse/Lion, Rev 19), ending in judgment and the eradication of sin.
- **S7 The Power of the Church (Col 1:9–20):** Heavy stress on the believer's **passivity/monergism** — "be filled," "be strengthened," "he qualified you"; the church's power is Christ's, not its own strategy ("our strategies apart from him are useless"). **Christ's supremacy/preeminence** (image of invisible God, firstborn, creator/owner/sustainer, Head of the body) — "in everything he might be preeminent." The **story is not about us; it's about the glory of the Son** — "the fastest way to feel distant from the power of the gospel is to make yourself the central glorified character." Church = a Spirit-indwelt **reconciled people**, hands/feet directed by the Head (not a building/program). Prayer as first power (Christ our interceding High Priest, Heb 7).

**New themes to tally:** (18) **Christ's supremacy/preeminence + God-centeredness** — God's chief end is his own glory; the story isn't about us (Edwards/Piper-flavored). (19) **Radical passivity / monergism** in salvation *and* sanctification. (20) **Church = reconciled people, Christ's body directed by the Head**, not building/strategy/programs. (21) **Prophecy fulfillment / Christ the promise-keeper**; the "liar-lunatic-Lord" apologetic against a customized Jesus.

- **S8 The Purpose of the Church (Acts 2:36–47):** The Acts-2 church-life (devotion to teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, prayers, radical generosity/all-things-in-common) is **normative, not merely descriptive/prescriptive** — the "visceral, instinctual reaction to the gospel." Diagnosis when church life is cold: *not* "you lack discipline" but "you've forgotten something about the gospel" — again the anti-moralism reflex. Ongoing repentance (Ephesus "you have abandoned the love you had at first"). The Lord — not our strategy — "added to their number… those who were being saved" (I planted, Apollos watered, God gave growth). Confessional/vulnerable (his own cooled first-love). Anti-anti-intellectualism (the Spirit makes doctrine *accessible*, not off-limits).
- **S9 Your Kingdom Come (Matt 6:9–13):** Kingdom = God's **kingship/lordship**, not primarily a realm or a people ("embodied within the King himself"). Three petitions in "your kingdom come": (1) redeem the lost — monergism, "we can't make people born again"; (2) refine his children — increasing submission/lordship over every faculty; (3) return & restore all things — eschatological hope, "come put the final dagger in the prince of darkness." Sharp **anti-autonomy / anti-"captain of my own ship"** worldview: three false views (I'm king / others are king / all is random) all rejected — "there is a King, and it's not you, and it's God… he's not in the White House." Comfort in sovereignty over nations (Nebuchadnezzar, Isaiah's whistle). Political de-catastrophizing rooted in the kingdom-not-of-this-world.
- **S10 Preparing for Burial (Mark 14:1–11):** The load-bearing question of the whole Mark series: **do you love Jesus himself, or the benefits of Jesus?** ("Jesus plus \_\_\_\_"). Mary's alabaster flask (a year's wages poured out) = pure worship of the *person* of Jesus; Judas = loving the benefits, one tragedy away from apostasy when the cross-cost comes due. "Many better men and women than you and I have walked away from Jesus when suffering struck… it wasn't Jesus they loved, it was the benefits." Worship is loving the person, not merely serving his purposes. Cross-shaped discipleship: following Jesus is following him *into death* first. Deeply personal ("what's our Jesus-plus?" — his own is health/health-insurance for his family). Ties to the eternal vs. horizontal-life theme: "I haven't come to improve the world; I've come to die for sin… and bring you a whole new world."

**New themes to tally:** (22) **Normativity of gospel-shaped church life** (Acts 2 as the natural reflex to grace, not a program). (23) **"Do you love Jesus or the benefits of Jesus?"** — the person vs. the perks; "Jesus-plus" idolatry as the seedbed of apostasy. (24) **Anti-autonomy / God-is-King-not-you** worldview, with political de-catastrophizing. (25) **Ongoing / lifelong repentance** (not a one-time event).

### Batch 3 (Sermons 11–15)
- **S11 Unless the Lord Builds the House (Psalm 127):** Theology of **work** against two idols — Western workaholism (Babel, "make a name for ourselves") and Eastern do-less passivity; both rejected. "Unless the Lord builds it… labor in vain." We are all **kingdom-builders** (not clergy-vs-laity — "a holy priesthood," priesthood of all believers). Diagnostic of Spirit-led vs. flesh-driven work: not "is there bread?" but "how are you *sleeping*?" ("he gives to his beloved sleep" — rest as the fruit of trust). Work *from* finished work, not *for* status. Children/heritage → the deeper "heritage" is the name **Christian** passed through generations; raising kids exposes our insufficiency and drives us to the Lord. Solomon/Ecclesiastes "vanity." Very personal (his clinic-opening flop).
- **S12 Hidden in Christ (Phil 3:1–11):** Ferocious **anti-legalism / anti-Judaizer** — "dogs, evildoers, mutilators." Two perennial errors: adders (Jesus + rites/traditions/rules) and pluralists ("that's great for you, many paths"); both are lies that "lead to death." **Imputed righteousness** as "the central doctrine of this church": God reads either *your* résumé or *Christ's* résumé. Counting all self-righteousness as *rubbish* to gain Christ. "Tasting and seeing" — sin is not loving good things too much but failing to taste Christ's superior goodness (Lewis's "mud pies"). Dying-to-self so life works in others.
- **S13 Union with Christ (Phil 2:1–11):** The gospel in "two sentences." **High Christology / kenosis** — Jesus IS God (morphē = essence), *permanently* incarnate (fully God + fully man forever), bond-servant self-emptying → exalted *higher* than before (he "increased his glory in rescuing the earth"). **Union with Christ → union with one another**: the cup filled endlessly by the Father overflows, so we can count others "more significant" without needing anything back. Disunity = a symptom of unbelief/forgetting the gospel. The mind of Christ is a *gift already given* ("have this thing you have"), not something to muster. Bodily/incarnational — Christians alone can care equally about "your hunger and your salvation."
- **S14 Gathering the Remnant (Zephaniah 1:1–3:20):** Sustained on the **wrath and justice of God** — "the hand of the Lord is either outstretched against you or gathering you in." Sin categories: idolatry (money the American idol), neglect of God, complacency/"patronizing God" ("he will not do good nor ill" — the serpent's lie). **A just God *must* punish sin** — it would be *unjust* to leave it unpunished; our sense of injustice-when-wronged proves the point. Only refuge = being *hidden*/covered in Christ's righteousness. Climax: **Zeph 3:17** — God "will rejoice over you with loud singing"; the Father's joy at the remnant's return is *greater* than ours (prodigal son). Repentance from self-righteousness ("Mercy's Door is not a club for good people").
- **S15 Healing in His Wings (Mark 5:21–34):** **Tenderness and accessibility of Christ** to the desperate (Jairus, high-status) *and* the destitute/ashamed (bleeding woman, 12 years unclean). "How you come to Jesus doesn't matter — he's the same Jesus." He reverses uncleanness (she'd defile him; instead he makes her clean). Malachi 4 "healing in his wings/tzitzit" fulfilled. Pastorally names hidden shame (addiction, abuse, abortion, mental illness) — Jesus calls her *daughter*, sends her in *shalom*/wholeness. On unanswered prayer: "your healing HAS come" (secured eschatologically) — **"want the Healer more than the healing."** Closing refrain: **"don't graduate from the gospel… the gospel is everything; all other theology is teased out of this one central truth."**

**New themes to tally:** (26) **Theology of work & rest** — anti-workaholism + anti-sloth; "sleep" as the evidence of trust; priesthood of all believers as kingdom-builders. (27) **Anti-legalism / two-errors framework** (adding to the gospel vs. relativizing it) — recurs constantly. (28) **Wrath & justice of God as the dark backdrop that makes grace good** — a just God must punish sin. (29) **God's delight/joy over his redeemed** (Zeph 3:17; the Father runs — affective, not merely forensic). (30) **Accessibility & tenderness of Christ to the ashamed**; "want the Healer more than the healing." (31) **"Don't graduate from the gospel"** — the gospel as the permanent center, not elementary material.

### Batch 4 (Sermons 16–20)
- **S16 Be Opened! (Mark 7:31–37):** **Christ the true friend** who "knows exactly what you need" (rebuke vs. tears — situational, not one-dimensional). Jesus "comes back for his rejecters" (Decapolis). Healings are a **foretaste of the kingdom** — sickness/death are "radically abnormal," products of the Fall, *not* "a natural part of life." He *sighs* (Rom 8 groaning) knowing the cross-cost, then heals people he foreknew would disobey — "I'll pick up the bill." **He became mute to restore the mute, deaf to restore the deaf** (bore the Father's silence on the cross). Deeply personal (his father's pancreatic-cancer death; wiping the tears — "the last tears you'll ever cry"). Laying-on-of-hands / ministry of presence; "what's worse than suffering is suffering alone."
- **S17 The Great Commission (Matt 28:11–20):** The world is **not neutral** — it has "swallowed lies" (the guards' cover story), so evangelism needs the Spirit ("nothing we can tell that Judas didn't see"). "The 11, not the 12" — Judas's absence wept over. Some worshiped, "some doubted" — commission given to *both*. **"Go" = across the street as much as across the ocean**; "we are the ends of the earth"; disciple your own children/neighbors/coworkers. Make **disciples, not converts** (lifelong). "No pet doctrines — *all* that I commanded" (justifies verse-by-verse method). Christ's abiding presence is the enablement.
- **S18 Confession & Repentance (Psalm 51):** [re-preach of the Ps 51 material] Asking for **mercy** = "I have no claim/right"; appealing to God's *steadfast covenant love*, not vows to "do better." Hiding sin (David/Bathsheba/Uriah) — the cover-up as heinous as the sin; God will expose (Nathan). Sin primarily **vertical** ("against you only"). Guilt/broken bones can be *from God* ("the bones you have broken" — not always the enemy). The God who commands truth *enables* it ("teaches wisdom in the secret heart"). A.W. Pink again: "not the absence of sin but the grief over it distinguishes a child of God from an empty professor." Anti-penance ("God doesn't want you to whip yourself to satisfy his wrath — it's satisfied in Christ").
- **S19 It's Not Home Without You (Exodus 33):** **The presence of God over every other good** — Moses would rather stay in the desert *with* God than enter the milk-and-honey land without him. Tests the listener: are comfort/security/provision adequate substitutes for God's presence? (rich young ruler, Judas, the guards). Unilateral vs. bilateral covenants — God keeps his one-directional promise. **Christ the greater Mediator** (vs. Moses; the tent) — after the cross we don't wait "on pins and needles"; we "sprint to God," boldly to the throne of grace. **The desert/wilderness is often where we most commune with God** (his father's death again — sanctification through suffering, not around it). "Covering of Christ" vs. the incomplete covering of God's hand (cleft of the rock → the blood of the Son).
- **S20 They Shall Inherit the Earth (Matt 5:5, Beatitudes / John 3:22–30):** Beatitudes are **declarative gospel proclamations, not law or wisdom-to-strive-for** ("blessed ARE" — already true; a gift of the Spirit, not a self-improvement target). **Meekness = humility born of security**, not weakness/low self-esteem — "so sure of my position before God I need nothing from you," so I can lower myself for others (Jesus the model; kenosis again). **John the Baptist** as the case study — "He must increase, I must decrease"; joy in *decreasing*; the bride knows the Bridegroom's voice; conversion is God's gift ("no one receives even one thing unless given from heaven" — monergism again). Eschatological inheritance frees us from clawing for status now.

**New themes to tally:** (32) **Presence of God as the supreme good** — better the desert with God than the promised land without him; Christ the greater Mediator. (33) **Suffering as the crucible of intimacy/sanctification** — the wilderness is where we commune most (recurs with his father's death). (34) **Sickness/death are abnormal (Fall), not "natural"** — creation groans; healings foretaste the kingdom. (35) **Meekness/security-derived humility** — "stop fighting for what's already yours." (36) **Christ the true, personal Friend** who knows exactly what each person needs; ministry of presence.

### Batch 5 (Sermons 21–25)
- **S21 They Shall Be Comforted** — *[DATA NOTE: this transcript is a duplicate of S20 (the meekness / John-the-Baptist sermon), mislabeled. No new content.]*
- **S22 The God Who Keeps His Promises (Psalm 89):** The **four-phase redemptive arc** — Creation / Fall / Redemption / Restoration — and the observation that people (including Christians) neglect one phase; *we neglect **Restoration*** (the not-yet). **God as covenant promise-keeper** (protoevangelium Gen 3:15 → Davidic covenant → Luke 1 → return). **Doubt/lament is normative** (David's Ps 89 "How long, O Lord?" bracketed by blessing) and "sometimes God takes a really long time" (1,000 yrs). Christmas is not escapism/pretending the world isn't sin-soaked; the answer is *eschatological hope*, not denial. Romans 8 "creation waits with eager longing"; the "weight of glory" (2 Cor 4) recurs. "He's coming back and bringing it with him."
- **S23 What Is My Only Confidence? (Psalm 27):** From a "Doctrine & Emotions" series — **emotions are part of the image of God** (God and the sinless Christ feel the full range), though sin distorts them and some (sorrow) exist only because of sin. **Confidence in the Lord vs. self-confidence** — self-confidence is intrinsically limited/situational; God is "steady and sure." God as *light* = a lamp at your feet (you can't see the future, only the One who walks with you) AND the all-illuminating glory. "Denial of sin is not confidence" — name the armies, then outweigh them with God. We come to God "as beggars," not on merit. **"Wait for the Lord"** — confidence can look like *doing nothing* (Christ "went to the cross and stayed"). Introduces **"participation in the Spirit"**: your worst prayer is carried perfectly by the Spirit to the interceding High Priest.
- **S24 A Prayer for Wisdom (Ephesians 1:15–23):** Paul prays not that God would *give* the Ephesians anything but that their eyes be **opened to what they already possess** (the Hearst-painting-in-the-warehouse illustration). **You already have the power** — asking God for "power to evangelize/suffer/obey" can misunderstand the cross: the Spirit indwelling is *the* resurrection power. **Lord AND Savior held together** — worshiping Jesus as Lord-only breeds legalism/self-righteousness; as Savior-only wants gifts without kingship. Faith-in-Christ is the *root*; love-for-the-saints is the *fruit* (dry love ⇒ a faith problem, not a people problem). Sin has a **deadening effect on spiritual perception**. "Look to Jesus" as the answer to hopelessness, uncertainty, weakness. Warm, specific love for named church members.
- **S25 A New Man (Ephesians 4:17–32):** The unbelieving mind operates "in futility" — cut off from seeing anything against the backdrop of Christ; sensuality's "enough is never enough." **The "new self" is really given, not an illusion/bait-and-switch** — the Spirit makes you *actually* able to do righteousness in the moment (not merely counted righteous). **Indicative→imperative** hammered again: put off the old (lying, sinful anger, stealing, corrupting talk) because it no longer fits who you *are*. Sanctification by *believing* something better (Michael's line: "not to be or do something better, but to believe something better — all activity is driven by the engine of belief"). "Don't grieve the Spirit who sealed you." Behaving sinfully = "behaving as orphans again" → run back to the Father.

**New themes to tally:** (37) **Four-phase redemptive-historical frame (Creation/Fall/Redemption/Restoration)** and the neglected *not-yet* / eschatological hope; God the covenant promise-keeper. (38) **Lament & doubt are normative** for the believer (the Psalms give voice; "how long, O Lord"). (39) **Emotions as part of the imago Dei** (Doctrine & Emotions). (40) **"Participation in the Spirit"** — you already possess resurrection power; don't beg for what's given; the Spirit + interceding High Priest carry your imperfect prayers. (41) **The "new self" actually given** — real Spirit-empowered ability, sanctification driven by belief; sin = "behaving as orphans."

### Batch 6 (Sermons 26–30)
- **S26 Obedience as Faith (Ephesians 6:1–9):** **God is the author of the church's story** (Paul "a prisoner of Christ," not of Rome — his chains "written into his story by Christ"); freedom is not autonomy but *re-mastering* — saved from slavery-to-sin *into* bond-service of Christ. Children/parents/servants/masters: **hierarchies flattened, "no dividing walls of hostility"** (addresses the lower party first each time). Careful, historically-honest handling of the slavery passage — Paul's letters dismantled Roman slavery; misused later to justify it. **Gospel-first over social causes**: "when we make the gospel subservient to a secondary issue… it is misguided"; change the world by spreading the gospel, and the good implications follow.
- **S27 Out of the Depths (Psalm 130):** **Dignifying suffering** — name "the depths" instead of pretending you're "on a good holiday on scuba"; suffering "makes sense" in a fallen world (Eugene Peterson: *suffering = pain + awareness* that something is violated). Mercy = "I have no claim." "With you there is forgiveness, **that you may be feared**" — the fear of the Lord produces wonder/security, not cringing. Confident hope "surer than the sunrise" (the watchman). Confession is one-on-one before God, then testified in community.
- **S28 Costly Redemption (Ruth 4:1–12):** **Kinsman-redeemer / Boaz as a type of Christ.** The unnamed nearer redeemer says "I will redeem it" until he *counts the cost* — "every lesser redeemer will fail you in the end" (boss, money, obedience, reputation "never hung on a cross for you"). Real redemption cost Christ everything; the courtroom "decree" = the cross's "paid in full." Elimelech's small unbelief ("maybe God won't provide") cascades into ruin. God takes "jacked-up" stories (Tamar, Rachel/Leah) and makes them beautiful. "The Lord is the hero of this story."
- **S29 The New Earth (Revelation 21:9–22:5):** Concentrated **eschatology / resurrection-anchored hope** — the whole faith stands or falls on the risen Christ, so trust all his other promises. **This world is genuinely cursed and "subjected to futility"** (Gen 3:17; Rom 8) — three wrong responses: make peace with the curse, try to *reform/fix* it in opposition to God, or despair and "be the bigger dog." **Creation groans for OUR glorification** (not vice versa). The New Jerusalem has *no temple* (God himself is the temple), no sun (his glory the light), *no curse* — Eden's tree of life restored and open. Belonging there rests on Christ's record, not ours. **God is long-suffering** — his "slowness" is mercy that let you be saved.
- **S30 Set Apart for Holiness (Leviticus 19:19–37):** **Holiness = being set apart**; only God is holy in himself, and only he can make the unholy holy and set the terms (the "1978 Cougar" illustration — instructions fit the object's distinction). **God's holiness and love are NOT in tension** — "yes God is loving *but* also holy" is rejected; Edwards: holiness is "a most beautiful, lovely thing." Rebukes the reflex "I'm the lovely one" — "you are not the lovely one until Christ makes you lovely." The Law is intensely *specific* — "he's dealing with YOU, not primarily the Canaanites" — and it **condemns** (shows we can't keep it). Climactic line: **"God does not crush the law for love. God crushes *Christ* for love"** — Christ fulfills it, then the Spirit empowers real holiness "that there might be pockets of heaven to invite others into."

**New themes to tally:** (42) **God as author/sovereign over the believer's whole story** (chains included); freedom = re-mastering, not autonomy. (43) **Gospel-first over social/political causes** (change the world by the gospel; the good fruit follows). (44) **Dignifying suffering / lament rather than denial**; the fear of the Lord as wonder. (45) **Christ the true Redeemer vs. every lesser redeemer that fails when it counts the cost.** (46) **Holiness = set-apartness; God's holiness and love are one, not in tension**; the Law condemns so Christ can fulfill — "God crushes Christ for love, not the law."

### Batch 7 (Sermons 31–35)
- **S31 The Other Son (Luke 15:25–32):** The elder brother = **self-righteousness is itself sin** and it exiles you from the Father's feast just as surely as the younger's rebellion. Grace is *offensive* to the elder son because the party celebrates **the Father's character, not the guest's worth** — "the lowlier the guest list, the more the Father's grace is magnified." Sharp application: *who's not on your guest list?* Extending mercy to the undeserving is "the highest indicator" of whether you've actually received mercy. Aimed at the Pharisees (and us); ending left open.
- **S32 A Prisoner of Christ (Ephesians 3:1–13):** Paul's **shrinking self-regard, growing boldness** ("least of the apostles" → "least of the saints" → "chief of sinners") — the more he sees Christ's glory, the smaller and *bolder* he gets. **The gospel work is bigger than any of us** — "not about an election, your rights, your freedoms" (pointed critique of Christians preoccupied with defending liberties they don't use for the gospel). The church, by grafting in every kind, **reveals God's "manifold wisdom" even to angels** (holy and fallen). Union of Jew+Gentile = us subdividing into clans (Dem/Rep, urban/rural, etc.) rebuked. Suffering "for your glory," not mine.
- **S33 Sharing the Gospel (Colossians 1:9–14):** The **two voices / two dominions** (God's word of life vs. the serpent's lie; light vs. darkness). Order of the Great Commission: **make disciples first, THEN teach obedience** — teaching God's commands to non-believers only "manufactures Pharisees" ("the Western American Pharisee" who knows about God but has never met him). John 8 — the Pharisees know the Book but not the Author. "Spread the gospel and *if necessary use words*" is rejected — **use words.** Evangelism must sound like *Christ-exaltation*, not shared politics/ethics. Bold, confrontational: "have you been born again?" Risking a friendship is nothing next to what the apostles/global church risk. **The gospel is the stench of death to some, sweet fragrance to others** — conversion is the Spirit's work, not our persuasion.
- **S34 The Magi & the Maker (Matthew 2:1–12):** **God's love *reigns* over history, nature, nations, rulers, and the future** — not self-governing/random. God authored centuries (Balaam's prophecy, the Babylonian exile spreading his stories east) so pagan Magi would know the star; he *moved a star* (sovereignty over nature). The Gentiles worship while the Jews/Herod are apathetic. Rich **first-Advent / second-Advent** teaching: since the first coming we are **"a new creation awaiting the new creation"** — for the first time *incompatible* with the world, so the post-Christmas "lull" is fitting, not shameful. He came as suffering Messiah; he returns as conquering King. Trust the future because "every page of history was once the future" and God always bent the arc toward the Son.
- **S35 Experiencing Joy (Psalm 16):** **Joy is available *now*, in despair, not only after circumstances change** (preached from his own Father's-Day lament over his estranged, deceased father). Method: David moves from "preserve me" to "I shall not be shaken" purely by *fixing his eyes on God's character* — nothing external changed. **"The boundary lines have fallen for me in pleasant places" — God draws the lines *around himself*** (whatever hems you in draws you nearer to him). **Emotions are not to be suppressed** — rebukes church "stoicism/emotionalism-as-sin"; "there are too many stoics in the church"; bring your shaking knees to God so *he* gets the glory. Joy ≠ mere happiness but is *experiential*, not just a doctrine "over coffee." **Joy is found among "the saints, the excellent ones"** — if you enjoy unbelievers' company more than the people of God's, that signals a wrong view of the Lord. Christ (at the Father's right hand) = where "pleasures forevermore" literally are. Keller: God gives what you'd have asked if you knew what he knows.

**New themes to tally:** (47) **Self-righteousness is sin that exiles from grace**; grace magnifies the *Father's* character, and mercy-to-the-undeserving proves you've received mercy (who's on your guest list?). (48) **Shrinking self / growing Christ = growing boldness**; the gospel enterprise is bigger than us, our rights, or politics; the church displays God's wisdom to angels. (49) **Make disciples before teaching obedience** — teaching commands to unbelievers manufactures Pharisees; evangelism = Christ-exaltation, and conversion is the Spirit's work. (50) **God's love reigns over history/nature/nations/rulers/future** (not random/self-governing); first vs. second Advent; "new creation awaiting the new creation." (51) **Joy available now in suffering**; boundary lines drawn *around God himself*; emotions engaged (not suppressed/stoic) and brought to God; joy is experiential and found among the saints.

### Batch 8 (Sermons 36–40)
- **S36 Jesus Outside the Camp (Hebrews 13:8–16):** **Christ is immutable — "the same yesterday, today, and forever"; we don't define/customize him.** Truth is not subjective ("things don't become true once you understand it"). Fierce attack on **"Jesus-plus" / gospel-plus-culture idolatry** — "God, guns, and country," "a little coffee and a whole lot of Jesus," fusing Christianity with conservatism/socialism/nationalism — the world sees the hypocrisy. **Grace alone / faith alone / Christ alone** (no food-law or self-made righteousness). **The gospel is at home in *every* culture yet at odds with *every* culture** — no culture (Texas, Mascoutah, or Jerusalem) is "primed" for it; Israel crucified the Son. We build "camps" around an invented Jesus; the real Jesus is **outside the camp** (with the least of these, Matt 25) — if you suffer no reproach, you're still inside the camp. "For here we have no lasting city."
- **S37 Pleasure, Prayer & Practice (Philippians 4:4–9):** **Rejoice *in the Lord* always** — not in circumstances (Paul writing joy from prison, facing death). Prayer is "the engine that drives the church"; **every time you pray you do two things: declare "I'm his" (against the lie you're an orphan) and confess "I need you" (weakness/dependence).** "The Lord is at hand" = the general at the gate, victory certain, so "do not be anxious." Gentleness/reasonableness flows from *not needing* others to supply what Christ already gave. Thought life fixed on the *actual characteristics/achievements* of Christ ("you'll talk to *that* Jesus, not the one you made up"). Vulnerable ("I'm terrified of public speaking… nobody can preach a better gospel — there's only one").
- **S38 The Word Became Flesh (John 1:14–18):** **Jesus IS God** ("that's the sentence we need to speak") and **the incarnation is permanent** — he is *fully* human, still bodily at the Father's right hand ("he became punchable… bruised… died"). Corrects the reflex that God "can't relate/understand" — he entered all the way in; the true human who perfectly images God ("*you* don't know what it means to be human — look to me"). **"From his fullness we have all *received* grace upon grace"** — received, not earned; irresistible grace; the well never runs dry (so don't beg as if rationing, or drink from broken cisterns). Trinity + high-priestly-prayer *oneness* ("I in them and you in me"). Christ was **Plan A, not Plan B**; the darkness cannot overcome the light.
- **S39 Ambassadors for Christ (2 Corinthians 5:11–21):** **Deep penal-substitution** — God does *not* pardon sin without punishing it ("he will by no means clear the guilty"); that would make him unjust and leave us knowing we're really guilty. Instead our sin was *heaped on Christ and crushed*; Christ's *perfect life* (never one second not loving God fully) is imputed, so "a just God will not punish sin twice" — "your death already happened." **Sinner-ransomed vs. sinner-condemned** — every person is one or the other. **We are ambassadors** carrying only God's message of reconciliation — "some of us need to shut up" (stop preaching morality/other messages), others "need to speak up." Christ was made *to be sin / a curse* so we become the righteousness of God. Correcting a caricature of an "unapproachable" God — the Father, Son, and Spirit share *one heart*; reconciliation was their joint idea.
- **S40 Sing a New Song to the Nations (Psalm 96):** ⭐ **The most concentrated statement of his burden for global missions.** God's eternal plan (not new in Matt 28) is to ransom "a people from *every* tribe, tongue, and nation." **Salvation is for the nations; judgment is upon the nations; the church is called to carry the message.** Sobering statistics — World C (33% self-identifying Christian), World B (38% with access, not yet believing), **World A (29%, ~2 billion, virtually no access)**; only ~3% of the 1-in-1,800 who go cross-culturally reach World A. **No "innocent native"** — all know God enough to be *condemned* but not saved; **hell = making your own eternal, never-completed payment for sin** ("after ten million years, no closer to the end"). Missions "looks like *singing*" — the natural overflow of seeing God rightly, not heroics ("the Hudson Taylors were just people who saw Jesus rightly"). Deeply personal: he and his wife have a burden for **Japan** (127M, <1% Christian) and study Japanese daily. Where our treasure is, our heart is (Christians give a fraction of a percent to World A missions).

**New themes to tally:** (52) **Christ is immutable / not customizable**; "Jesus-plus" and gospel-fused-with-culture/politics is idolatry the world sees through; the gospel is at home in every culture yet at odds with every culture (find Jesus "outside the camp"). (53) **Prayer as the engine** — declaring sonship + confessing dependence; rejoice *in the Lord*, not circumstances; the Lord "at hand." (54) **Incarnation is permanent and real** — Jesus fully God and fully, bodily human forever, therefore approachable; grace *received* not earned (fullness, irresistible grace). (55) **Penal substitution in full force** — God punishes sin (never merely pardons); Christ crushed + his perfect life imputed; sinner-ransomed vs. sinner-condemned; we are ambassadors of *only* the message of reconciliation. (56) ⭐ **Passionate burden for global/unreached missions** — salvation & judgment for the nations; World A / the ~2 billion with no access; the reality of hell as unending self-payment; missions as the overflow of rightly seeing Christ (Japan burden, "sing a new song").

### Batch 9 (Sermons 41–45) — *John series begins; consistent gospel-centered Christology*
- **S41 The First Disciples (John 1:35–51):** **Jesus is the first missionary — he finds us** (Andrew "found" Simon, but really Jesus found them). Our **identity is a *new name given by Jesus before we've done anything*** (Simon → "Rock" — least fitting yet foreordained), unshakable because Christ defines and secures it (vs. Enneagram/role self-definitions that collapse when we fail). "Come and see" evangelism (let Jesus defend himself). Jesus is God tabernacling — "greater than Bethel." Personal: the story of leaving career/home to plant Mercy's Door, and his readiness to say "when do we leave?" when the Lord moves.
- **S42 Zeal for the Lord (John 2:13–25):** The temple-cleansing is **not a proof-text for our anger** — Jesus's "zeal *consumed* him" means he was willing to be *consumed* (die) for access to God; unless your zeal gets you crucified, don't compare. Jesus makes himself the new **temple** ("destroy this temple… in three days I'll raise it") → then his body → then the church. **Jesus "did not entrust himself" to sign-based believers** — seeing miracles isn't saving faith; "has Jesus entrusted himself to you?" Anti-sign-seeking ("your enemy loves signs").
- **S43 The Savior Heals (John 4:43–54):** ⭐ **Jesus serves the Father's will first, and *wrapped within that* serves us** — not the reverse. The diagnostic: when God says no, do you conclude "he's absent / unable / not good"? (that's treating him as existing to serve *your* will). Don't **domesticate/box Jesus** ("hometown folks" mentality, reducing him to a miracle-worker/ATM). The light goes *into* the dark (Samaria/the official) — you don't clean up first to earn his favor. "Believed… and believed again" — faith is repeated. Vulnerable: he'd *stopped* praying for his sick wife's healing because God's earlier "no" hurt — a pastor confessing the very error he preaches against; "ask again."
- **S44 The Savior's Authority (John 5:18–47):** Jesus **explicitly and repeatedly claims to be God / equal with the Father** (one in ability, will, knowledge, honor — "unless you honor the Son you don't honor the Father"). The **liar-lunatic-Lord trilemma** — "he makes you either kill him or worship him; no middle ground." Answers the three biggest questions: *Is Jesus God?* (yes), *what happens when we die?* (resurrection to life or judgment), *does how we lived matter?* (decidedly no-and-yes). **Judgment reads Christ's record or yours — no grading on a curve**; the standard is *perfection* (what we were made for); "you did not team up with Jesus" — Christ *alone*. "You must receive me."
- **S45 The Savior's Power (John 6:1–21, feeding the 5,000 + walking on water):** After the high deity claims, John shows the **tender, approachable Jesus who is Lord of the *daily meal*, not only eternity.** **Jesus sees you before you see him, takes compassion (sheep without a shepherd), and pre-plans to meet your need** ("much grass" = green pastures) — even denying himself (feeds the crowd before the disciples). Ministers are **distributors, not multipliers, of bread** — "receive from Jesus and give it away; he never calls you to distribute what you haven't tasted." He gives our *fill* but **no to-go bags** — meets today's need so we depend again tomorrow (like manna). ⭐ **"The miracle was that Jesus got in the boat"** — John omits the wind-stilling; the point is Christ *enters our circumstances* even when he doesn't change them. Don't reduce him to a genie/ATM; **"we don't make Jesus king — he *is* king"** (he withdrew when they tried to seize him for an earthly throne).

**New themes to tally:** (57) **Jesus finds us (the first missionary); identity = a new name he gives before we act** — unshakable, not self-defined. (58) **Sign-faith ≠ saving faith** — "has Jesus entrusted himself to you?"; don't domesticate/box/reduce him to a useful genie. (59) ⭐ **Jesus serves the Father's will first, ours within it** — the diagnostic for our idolatry when God says "no"; don't clean up to earn favor (light goes into the dark). (60) **Explicit deity of Christ + liar-lunatic-Lord**; judgment on Christ's record vs. ours, no grading on a curve, standard is perfection, "you must receive me." (61) **The tender/approachable Jesus, Lord of the daily meal** — sees & pre-plans our need, "no to-go bags" (daily dependence), and above all *gets in the boat* (enters our suffering even without changing it).

### Batch 10 (Sermons 46–50) — *John series continues*
- **S46 The Bread of Life (John 6:22–59):** ⭐ Major monergism/election/security sermon. **Seek Jesus as your *treasure*, not for lesser pleasures** ("you seek me because you ate your fill of the loaves" — the "Jesus-plus/coffee-and-Jesus" reflex, Jesus reduced to a useful bread-maker/ATM). Signs are the **Father's *seal* / stamp of approval** pointing to who Jesus is. **Eternal life is a *free gift*** ("what must we do?" is the reflex of a merit-conditioned heart). **Regeneration precedes faith** — "no one comes unless the Father *drags* him" (Greek = drag, like an ox hauling a plow, not "woo"); "all the Father gives me will come… I'll never cast out." **Eternal security rests on not-earning** (adoption illustration — the retro-dated birth certificate; "you can't wriggle free from your adoption"). **Faith is *experiential*, not academic** — "eat my flesh, drink my blood"; Christianity isn't a club or a thing done "up here." Preach hard gospel truths boldly (Jesus thinned his own crowds) because the Father gives people to the Son.
- **S47 Lost Brothers (John 7:1–13):** **Persecution/rejection over your faith — even from family — is normal** ("a person's enemies will be those of his own household," Matt 10). Jesus's *own brothers didn't believe* (evidence for monergism), taunted him, tempted him toward death. Comfort: **"you were his rejecter too"** — "if he can save you, who can't he save?" so *keep praying* for the lost and *don't fear man*. Two errors that both miss Christ as Lord: "good man" and "deceiver." "The world can't hate you if you're of the world" — friendship with the world vs. Christ. His brothers came to faith only *after* the resurrection.
- **S48 Can This Be the Christ? (John 7:14–36):** Manifold **wisdom of God in Christ vs. the folly of the self-wise.** Jesus teaches on the *Father's* authority ("my teaching is not mine but his who sent me") — the test of true teaching: **does it draw you to the Son / magnify Christ?** Strong on the **Holy Spirit** enabling discernment and the **priesthood of all believers** ("I need you in my living room so I can hear from God… your mouth can become the mouthpiece of God"). Warning against **self-made godliness** and worshiping a customized, ever-agreeable God — **"if your God never disagrees with you, question who you're worshiping"** (echoes the anti-idolatry theme). The flesh confuses "do I like it?" with "is it from God?"
- **S49 Rivers of Living Water (John 7:37–52):** **Christ satisfies the thirst he himself placed in you** ("if anyone thirsts, come to me and drink"). Two failures: unbelievers drinking dirty water (sin) or self-righteousness; and believers who **won't *return daily* to the fountain because they feel too unclean** — treating self-cleaning/penance as a prerequisite is *pride* that denies free grace ("the grace you first received is the grace you daily need"). **"I can't forgive myself" = elevating your standard above God's** (a great sin of pride). The Spirit = the river flowing from the believer's heart; every God-glorifying act is "the King glorifying himself through you" (all pressure off). Faith alone in Christ alone is the only prerequisite to drink.
- **S50 The Light of the World (John 8:12–59):** Jesus is **God and Judge** whose "judgment is true" — "unless you believe that I am He, you will die in your sins." ⭐ Contains his **full personal conversion testimony**: raised in earthy/occult-tinged Western Massachusetts (séances, "fairies"), not Christian; found a Bible under a floorboard as a boy but "nothing happened" (heart of stone); suicidal through middle/high school; branded himself Christian to fit in, then traded it for a girl's kiss; his first real prayer was a *shaken-fist* curse blaming God — and *then* the Lord ransomed him from death to life, and Scripture "came alive… every word was food." **Sin kept in darkness pulls toward death; the Light exposes it to heal it** — his stated aim for the church: a culture of *dependence on prayer* + *confession/repentance*, "dragging what's in the darkness into the light." Regeneration is entirely God's doing (heart of stone → heart of flesh).

**New themes to tally:** (62) **Eternal life is a free gift; regeneration precedes faith (the Father "drags"); eternal security rooted in not-earning; faith is experiential (eat/drink Christ), not academic** — Christianity is not a club. (63) **Persecution/rejection (even from family) is normal for the believer** — but "you were his rejecter too," so keep praying, don't fear man. (64) **The test of true teaching = does it magnify Christ and draw you to the Son**; beware self-made godliness and a customized, ever-agreeable God; the Spirit + priesthood of all believers. (65) **Return *daily* to the fountain — refusing grace out of a sense of uncleanness is pride** ("I can't forgive myself" elevates your standard above God's). (66) ⭐ **His own testimony**: occult-tinged non-Christian upbringing, suicidal youth, a shaken-fist first prayer, then sovereign regeneration (heart of stone→flesh); aim for the church = prayer-dependence + confession, "dragging sin into the light."

### Batch 11 (Sermons 51–55) — *John series continues; heavy on suffering, security, confession*
- **S51 Though I Was Blind, Now I See (John 9):** More conversion testimony (a mystical Bible under a floorboard did nothing; on the Gulf of Mexico "the veil was lifted," a submersion-baptism prayer). **You don't need a clinical or theological answer — "I was blind, now I see"** is a sufficient, unassailable testimony (regeneration precedes seeing/believing; Jesus *sought and healed* the man, who only then believed). ⭐ **Sickness is NOT necessarily God's punishment** ("neither this man nor his parents sinned… that the works of God might be displayed") — freeing the chronically ill from the lie that suffering = paying off sin (you owed *death*, not cancer; Christ paid it). The Light *heals* the blind but *blinds* the self-righteous "seeing" (Rom 1). Confessing "Jesus is Lord" is only possible by the Spirit — that confession is your assurance.
- **S52 I Am the Good Shepherd (John 10:1–18):** Christ the **Good Shepherd vs. the thief/robber, hired hand, stranger, gatekeeper.** The shepherd/rancher distinction ("they look identical up until the slaughter") — thieves and hired hands *use* you for their own gain; the Good Shepherd **knows you by name and lays down his life.** Others may die *for* you but can't take death up again or pay for your sin. Jesus **lays it down of his own accord** and takes it up — sovereign, not a sentimental gesture ("there was a real wolf"). "Other sheep not of this fold" (Gentiles) — he *has* them before he calls them (monergism). Pastors/spouses/parents can be "hired hands in disguise" — searching motives; "the thief may be *you*." Priesthood/gospel-community: sheep who know the Shepherd's voice recatch the strays.
- **S53 The Death of a Friend (John 11:1–35):** ⭐ Concentrated **theology of suffering** (opened with his wife's full chronic-illness catalog — lupus, RA, interstitial cystitis, brushite kidney stones). "**He whom you love is ill**" — Lord *and* love *and* illness coexist; sickness is not proof Jesus stopped being Lord, stopped loving, or that you're out of favor / didn't clean up / prayed too late. "**Jesus, when he heard Lazarus was ill, stayed two days longer**" — deliberate delay for God's glory, not divine displeasure or your deficiency. "**Jesus wept**." "I am the resurrection and the life." **The scandal of the gospel vs. all other religions: you don't become something to be right with God — God became something to make you right.** Joni Eareckson Tada again ("the first thing I'll do with new legs is kneel"). The "weight of glory" (2 Cor 4). His own poem on his wife's illness ("my bones, they are the clink").
- **S54 Lazarus, Come Out! (John 11:38–57):** ⭐ **"Jesus makes really dead things really alive."** Four points: the Father/Son/Spirit are always in hearing of each other (model for prayer — the Spirit carries our prayers to the Father); **the dead obey the voice of the Lord** (Abraham Kuyper: "not one square inch… over which Christ does not cry Mine" — not even death); even the reprobate (Caiaphas) *prophesy* about him though trying to silence him (God's total sovereignty); Jesus wins. **The proof no one is "too far gone" is that *you* weren't** — you were dead in your trespasses. Don't keep the stone shut over your sin (Martha's "there will be an odor"). Jesus lets the church "unbind him" — participation in his ministry; stop putting the grave-clothes back on.
- **S55 Anointed for Burial (John 12:1–8):** Lazarus (raised) reclining at table = the "discombobulating" mixture of death's stench and new life — the whole testimony of John. ⭐ **Deeply vulnerable public confession**: his lifelong pornography addiction from age 11, relapsing while his pregnant wife was hospitalized, then confessing to her and to the Lord — "transferring death into the open air that it might kill this too," and instead new life came. **"If this job [pastor] requires me to be in hiding, this is the last job I need."** A living demonstration of the confession/repentance-in-the-light culture he preaches (Mary's extravagant, "wasteful" worship of the person of Jesus frames it — cf. S10).

**New themes to tally:** (67) ⭐ **Suffering/sickness is not necessarily God's punishment or displeasure** — Christ may *deliberately* delay; "he whom you love is ill" (Lord + love + illness coexist); "you don't become something to be right with God — God became something." (68) **Christ the Good Shepherd who knows you by name and lays down (and takes up) his life** — vs. thieves/hired-hands who use you; the thief may be you → run to the Shepherd. (69) **"Jesus makes really dead things really alive"** — the dead obey his voice; his lordship over "not one square inch" excludes nothing; no one is "too far gone" because you weren't. (70) ⭐ **Confession/repentance *in the light* modeled by the pastor himself** — refusing to keep "the stone shut" over sin; a pastor who won't hide (his porn-addiction confession).

### Batch 12 (Sermons 56–60) — *end of John's public ministry; strong Reformed soteriology*
- **S56 Life Through Death (John 12:20–26):** The Greeks seeking Jesus = the nations beginning to be drawn; "the hour has come to be **glorified**" = crucified. "Unless a grain of wheat dies, it remains alone" — Jesus dies to bear **fruit = you**; you're fruit on the merit of *his* death, not your works. Then **cross-shaped discipleship**: "Jesus died that you might *die* that you might live"; **"work out your salvation with fear and trembling"** (if death isn't scaring you, you're probably not dying). The gospel is free but costly — you can't add Jesus to a full life; drop everything to take hold. Rich-young-ruler again.
- **S57 Lifted Up (John 12:27–36):** ⭐ **The real humanity of Jesus** — "now is my soul troubled"; going to the cross genuinely *scared* him (Gethsemane blood-sweat). Temptation-without-sin means his flesh couldn't overpower his deity, yet he was *truly* tempted (so his obedience is genuinely praiseworthy, not robotic). **God's goodness is bounded only by his own nature** — "there's nobody policing God"; he is good *by choice*. **God's glory is our joy** (we were made to reflect him; doing so is our peace). Penal substitution: your very *sin nature/self* was pinned to Christ and judged — "your death already happened" (a historical event); the new self rose. Satan (via Judas) thought the cross was his chance to kill God — instead it was his defeat. Paul Miller's "four J-curves."
- **S58 An Unbelief Foretold (John 12:37–43):** ⭐⭐ **The clearest Reformed/Calvinist statement** — why did Jesus's own people reject him? "So that Isaiah's word would be fulfilled… he has *blinded their eyes and hardened their hearts*." Walks through **Isaiah 53 & 6, Romans 9–11**: God "has mercy on whom he wills and hardens whom he wills"; the potter/clay; **election, reprobation, the remnant ("the holy seed is its stump")**, Gentiles grafted in. Rebukes the arrogance of "I would have believed if I'd seen the signs" — you're no different in yourself. **God is sovereign over hard hearts and turns evil for good** (Israel's rejection → salvation for the nations; Paul the persecutor saved). "When it seems like evil has won, it's just begun." No one is too far gone (the very people who crucified him were saved at Pentecost).
- **S59 Mercy's Door Is Open (John 12:44–50):** Opens with **original sin / total depravity** — "you're not corrupted by the world; you *are* the world… sin is corrupted because of you." Jesus's *last public words* summarize his whole ministry: **(1) "I and the Father are one" — the deity of Christ, the most-repeated message** (unbelief that Jesus is God shows up as adding to his finished work); (2) he came as **light** so we don't *remain* in darkness (darkness is the default; he's the "unquenchable fire" you can't keep lit yourself → eternal security); (3) he came to *save*, not judge, the world — the judgment at Calvary fell on *believers'* sin. ⭐ **The reality of hell preached plainly** (Luke 16, Mark 9, Matt 13/25 — eternal torment, unquenchable fire, weeping/gnashing, Gehenna) as a necessary part of the gospel. **"Mercy's door is still open"** — actual sin, actual judgment, actual hell, actual heaven (Rev 21–22); but *balanced* — corrects the "you'll have blood on your hands in heaven" fire-and-brimstone distortion (no one comes unless the Father gives them). The Great Commission's urgency: "if Jesus isn't back yet, the work's not done."
- **S60 The Way, the Truth, and the Life (John 14:1–11):** "**Let not your hearts be troubled**" — comfort against very rational fear as evil advances (preached the same text at a 24-year-old's heroin-overdose funeral). Believing in Jesus = *earned* belief (tug-of-war), looking to *him* to interpret circumstances rather than circumstances to interpret him. **Jesus prepares your place AND carries you there** — "follow me" is more "grab on" than "keep up" (freight-train, not marathon-runner). The **exclusivity of Christ**: "I am the way… no one comes to the Father except through me." **Jesus is *the truth about God*** — the test for strange teaching: "does this sound/look like Jesus?" Don't treat this world/airplane as the destination (jockeying for legroom) — anti-materialism and a caution against the impulse to "reconstruct the earth" out of anxiety rather than fixing eyes on the Lord.

**New themes to tally:** (71) **Cross-shaped discipleship** — "Jesus died that you might die that you might live"; work out salvation "with fear and trembling"; the gospel free yet costly (drop everything). (72) ⭐ **The real humanity of Jesus** (genuinely afraid at the cross, truly tempted) + **God's goodness is self-bounded, chosen, not policed**; God's glory is our joy. (73) ⭐⭐ **Explicit election / reprobation / God's sovereignty over hardened hearts** (Isaiah 6 & 53, Romans 9–11, the remnant/"holy seed"), and God turning evil for good — no one too far gone. (74) **Total depravity / original sin** ("you don't get corrupted by the world, you *are* the world") + **the deity of Christ as the most-repeated, load-bearing doctrine**. (75) **The reality of hell preached plainly as part of the gospel**, balanced against fire-and-brimstone distortion; "Mercy's door is still open"; the exclusivity of Christ (no other way). (76) **"Let not your hearts be troubled"** — Jesus prepares *and carries*; "grab on, not keep up"; Jesus is "the truth about God" (the test for strange teaching).

### Batch 13 (Sermons 61–65) — *John 14–16, the Farewell Discourse; Holy Spirit, abiding, love, joy*
- **S61 Ask. (John 14:12–14):** "You will do the works that I do, and *greater works*" = **not more bread but more disciples** (foot-washing, dying-to-self, disciple-making — the "boring"/humble works, not the flashy miracles we crave). We want Christ's *glory* without his *suffering/service/death* ("whoever would be great must be servant"). Prayer "in my name" = invoking Christ's name so *the Father is glorified in the Son* (not a magic-word cheat code). Your prayer life follows your activity — if you're building your kingdom, your prayers build your kingdom. The apostles' martyrdoms as the cost of the "works."
- **S62 I Will Not Leave You (John 14:15–31):** "If you love me you will keep my commandments" — obedience *flows from* love, and we fail the law "for lack of love, not lack of understanding" (Eden had one command). **Love of God and love of neighbor are inseparable** — only the one loved-by-God can truly love a neighbor. ## What Adam Vega believes

*Drawn from the sermon notes above. Written November 2026. On women in ministry, families, baptism, and denominations, Adam clarified his views himself that month, and the text follows his own words.*

Adam Vega was not raised a Christian. He grew up in Western Massachusetts, in a world of séances and "fairies." As a boy he found a Bible under a floorboard, and when he read it, he says, nothing happened. He spent middle and high school in despair. For a while he called himself a Christian to fit in, then gave the name up for a girl. The first real prayer he remembers was a curse: he shook his fist at God and blamed him. Then, in his telling, God ransomed him from death to life. On the Gulf of Mexico "the veil was lifted," and the Bible that had meant nothing to him "came alive." Every word of it was food.

He left a career and his home to plant a church, and he preached through whole books of the Bible a verse at a time: John, Hosea, Galatians, Colossians, Habakkuk, Obadiah, the letters of John, the Psalms of Ascent. Jesus sent his followers to teach "all that I commanded," and Adam takes that to rule out a preacher's pet doctrines. He holds that the Bible is the apostles' teaching written down, carrying "the full backing of the authority of Jesus Christ." Its truth is objective: "things don't become true once you understand it." He has told his church, "The only reason to listen to me is if it's really flowing from here," meaning the Bible.

He is afraid of public speaking and has said so from the pulpit. He has spent four hours in prayer on a Sunday morning before preaching. He takes walks at midnight to be still before God, and he writes songs and poems, one of them about his wife's illness.

For readers who know the categories, he is Protestant, and in how he understands salvation he is Reformed, the tradition sometimes called Calvinist. The writers he quotes most often are A. W. Pink, Tim Keller, Charles Spurgeon, and C. S. Lewis, along with John Calvin, Jonathan Edwards, and the Westminster Shorter Catechism. Whatever the text, he works his way back to one subject. In his words, "the gospel is everything; all other theology is teased out of this one central truth."

### Grace first

The gospel, as Adam preaches it, turns on whose record God reads. On the day of judgment God reads either a person's own record or Christ's, and he grades no one on a curve. Jesus lived the life every person owes God, without one second of failing to love him fully, and he died under the punishment human sin deserves. Anyone who trusts him is credited with that perfect life, and their sin counts as already punished at the cross: "a just God will not punish sin twice." Adam has called this credited righteousness "the central doctrine of this church."

He insists on what he calls "getting the order right." What God has done comes first, and what a Christian does grows out of it. Christians obey because God has already made them his children. Obeying in order to become his child is, in Adam's words, "spiritual slavery." The gift is free, and taking it costs a person everything else: "the demand is as total as the offer is free."

He fights two ways of adding to that gift. The first is familiar: adding good behavior, religious habits, morality, family values, or a promise of wealth to what Christ did. The second he names again and again. It is self-punishment, the person who can't forgive himself, who does penance, who feels he has to "come back with something in your hands." Adam calls this the same pride as self-righteousness, since both treat Christ's work as unfinished. "Either his punishment was sufficient or it was not." Saying "I can't forgive myself" sets a person's own standard above God's. He applies the same logic to need and hurt. Past wounds and present poverty earn nothing from God either. In his phrase, there are no deserving poor and no good people in the family of God. Everyone in it came in by mercy.

Christians never move past this. "Don't graduate from the gospel," he tells his church, because "the grace you first received is the grace you daily need." When a Christian sins, Adam's test of whether he believes the gospel is which way he runs. Believing it sends him toward God to confess.

### Jesus

Adam's most repeated claim about Jesus is that he is God. "That's the sentence we need to speak." He presses the old argument that a man who said what Jesus said is a liar, a lunatic, or the Lord: "he makes you either kill him or worship him; no middle ground." Jesus is also fully and permanently human. He still has a body at the Father's right hand. He was afraid of the cross and was tempted in earnest, so his obedience was real obedience. "He became punchable." Adam corrects anyone who says God can't understand what it is to be human.

The Jesus of his sermons is gentle with ashamed people. He calls the bleeding woman "daughter." He weeps with Mary at her brother's grave. He calls people by name. Preaching on the storm at sea, Adam pointed out that John never mentions the wind being stilled. In his reading, "the miracle was that Jesus got in the boat." Christ enters a person's trouble even when he leaves the trouble in place.

He warns that many people worship a Jesus they made up, one who agrees with them about everything. "If your God never disagrees with you, question who you're worshiping." The real Jesus "won't be characterized by his followers," and he claims to be the only way to the Father. When someone brings Adam a strange new teaching, his test is whether it sounds and looks like the Jesus of the Gospels.

### God's choosing

Adam believes salvation is God's work from start to finish. People are dead in sin, and the dead contribute nothing to their rescue. He has described God walking into a morgue and saying, "I'll take that corpse." God gives new life first, and faith follows as its result. Faith itself is a gift God gives a person. Adam knows the teaching offends. When Jesus taught it in John 6, the crowds walked away. Adam calls election "a discomfort until it's a comfort," and he points to his own conversion as a case of it: God changed a heart that had cursed him.

The comfort is that what God starts, he keeps. Because no one earned a place in his family, no one can lose it. A child of God "can't wriggle free" from adoption, and God "doesn't unadopt anybody." Adam preaches this as a man who grew up with an absent father and who adopted his own stepson. He calls the fatherhood of God the heart of the gospel, and he teaches that every human father is a copy of that Father. He tells people to form their picture of God the Father from Christ, including people whose earthly fathers failed them.

He also believes nothing evil takes God by surprise. Jesus chose Judas knowing he would betray him, and Herod and Pilate did what God's hand had planned beforehand. Adam brings this to people on both sides of harm: the sin a person committed and the evil done to him were both accounted for, and God is working both into his plan. He will not let the doctrine make anyone passive. God told Paul, "I have many people in this city," and Adam takes that as a reason to speak boldly to everyone. "In every other relationship you want to be chosen," he said. "Let God love you that way."

### Sin and the cross

Adam teaches that sin starts in the heart and spreads outward into the world. "The heart is an idol factory." Sin is first an offense against God. He traces it to a root: a need, a wound, or an accusation that a person never brings to God, which festers until it turns into sin. Desires themselves are good gifts, meant to be met by the God who gave them. Sin takes a good desire and looks for its fulfillment somewhere else. His counsel is to bring the need to God early, as if God already knows, because he does.

He holds that God's holiness and his love are one thing, and he rejects the habit of saying God is loving but also holy, as though the two pulled against each other. His summary of the cross: "God does not crush the law for love. God crushes Christ for love." God's anger at sin is real. A just God has to punish sin, and leaving it unpunished would make him unjust, as anyone feels the moment someone wrongs them. At the cross Christ drank the cup of God's wrath to the dregs, and there is "not one drop" left for God's children. He has criticized a view, taught by some popular Bible teachers, in which Jesus removed sin's power without paying its penalty. "A wrathless cross is an incomplete gospel."

He preaches hell plainly as part of the gospel. Hell, in his description, is a person making his own payment for sin forever and getting no closer to the end. He also corrects preachers who use hell to frighten Christians, such as telling them they will have blood on their hands for every neighbor they failed to reach. The Father gives people to the Son, and the outcome rests with him.

For a believer the wrath is spent. What remains is a Father's discipline, which Adam distinguishes sharply from punishment: discipline hurts, the child survives it, and its aim is to bring him home. He reads Hosea's hedge of thorns the same way. God makes sin stop working so that his people come back. Hardship in a Christian's life is never God punishing him. Adam tells sick believers that what they owed God was death, which Christ paid, and that their illness is no installment on that debt.

### Suffering and tears

Adam has lived with suffering at close range. His wife lives with chronic illness and chronic pain, and he has called her healing "the longest unanswered prayer of my life." He once confessed to his church that he had stopped asking, because God's earlier no had hurt, and he told them to ask again. He has preached through his father's death and through his own panic attacks.

He teaches that sickness and death are "radically abnormal," results of the Fall. Illness is no sign that God has stopped loving someone. In John 11 the sisters send word, "he whom you love is ill," and Adam stresses that the Lord, his love, and the illness all stand together in that sentence. Jesus waited two days on purpose. A delay is no proof of God's displeasure or of the sufferer's failure.

In his preaching God brings joy through suffering, the way a child comes through labor, and joy is possible in the middle of grief, before anything changes. He wants people to "want the Healer more than the healing." He compares a maturing Christian to a weaned child, who stops wanting only the milk and wants the mother herself. God sometimes withholds comforts, Adam says, and he never withholds himself.

He treats feelings as part of being made in God's image, since God and the sinless Christ feel the full range of them. "There are too many stoics in the church." Lament belongs in a believer's prayers, as it does in the Psalms. He tells people to bring doubt and anger to God with open hands, and he warns against reciting Scripture to drown out feelings that belong in a conversation with God. Doubt is normal for believers. When Jesus gave the Great Commission, some of the eleven worshiped and some doubted, and he gave it to all of them. Despair, Adam says, does not mean a person has turned his back on God.

He comforts people with lines like "nobody ever cried alone," since God keeps every tear, and "God does his best work in the dark," drawn from the sealed tomb before Easter morning.

### Confession

Adam believes honesty is how a Christian lives. "Confession of sin is confession of faith." Sin in a believer's life breaks his fellowship with God while his adoption stays in place, and confession restores the fellowship. God forgives because he is just: the sin was already punished on Christ. The aim he set for his church was a culture of prayer and confession, "dragging what's in the darkness into the light." He wants the church to be the safest place there is to tell the truth and confess. Answering a confession with "gotcha," he says, puts you in Caiaphas's seat.

He practices what he asks. From the pulpit he has confessed that he bullied his younger brother for years as a boy, and told how he confessed it to his brother. He has told his church that he walked into his small group straight from a fight with his wife, that he resented his own congregation one Sunday, and that he had opportunistic thoughts as markets fell during COVID. He confessed a long private struggle with sin, first to his wife and then to the church. After that confession he said, "If this job requires me to be in hiding, this is the last job I need."

He rejects the idea that truth and love must be balanced against each other, calling it a category error: "There is no truth that is unloving, and no love that is untruthful." He holds that forgiveness and lasting hurt can coexist. Someone who harmed another person should not demand that the injured one forget so he can feel better, and a wounded person who still hurts may have forgiven fully, as Jesus forgave while the nails went in.

### Growing up in church

On how God works in families, Adam leans Presbyterian: God ordinarily rescues people across generations through his covenant grace. Going to church makes no one a Christian, and neither does having a Christian mother. His mission field is unbelievers, and some of them sit in church every week.

Adam watches for two temptations. A person raised in the church tends to presume, forgetting that he too had to be born again. A dramatic convert tends to strive, afraid the well will run dry. He thinks both have made the faith about themselves.

He spent years "wearing the jersey" of Christianity without being born again, and he warns that "you can walk and talk like a Christian and nothing changed within you." He said the church he planted was a church "for teenage Adam," the young person who thinks being a good person makes him a Christian. He asks people plainly whether they have been born again.

He is wary of teaching God's commands to people who do not yet know God. In his view it "manufactures Pharisees," people who know about God and have never met him. Jesus told his followers to make disciples and then teach them to obey, and Adam keeps that order. He believes the Holy Spirit opens doctrine to every believer, so he preaches real doctrine to everyone.

He tells Christians that the Great Commission starts with their own children and neighbors: "we are the ends of the earth." Raising children, he says, exposes how insufficient parents are and drives them to the Lord. In his reading of Psalm 127, the heritage that matters most is the name Christian, handed down from one generation to the next.

### The church and other Christians

Adam believes the church is a people Christ has reconciled to God and to one another, a body he directs as its head. He prefers the local church, where people know each other's names, over the celebrity teacher and the downloaded sermon, because those teachers neither know nor love the listener. He warns against loyalty to any teacher over Christ, himself included: taking up Paul's "What is Paul?", he asked, "What is Adam?" He has told his people, "I need you in my living room so I can hear from God."

Love among believers is, for him, the evidence of new life, and he treats a professing Christian who doesn't love other Christians as a mission field. Christians behold their unity more than they build it: it is beautiful because nobody in the room made it. No one sneaks in by the side door, and no one claims the front row, because every member was adopted.

His picture of a church is the one at Philippi: a wealthy businesswoman, a demon-possessed slave girl, and a Roman jailer at the same table. Hospitality, he says, is the first mark of conversion, since Lydia's first act as a believer was to open her house. When the gospel is preached and a hearer believes, he wants baptism to follow right away, which he sees as the normal pattern of Scripture. He leans toward believer's baptism and gladly worships at a church that baptizes infants, and he treats how faith applies to a whole household as a lesser question. He cares for body and soul together. Of his wife he said that all may be well with her soul and "she also needs a back rub." He is equally hard on good works with the gospel left out: "anyone can dig a well; who digs a well in Jesus' name?" The church's first ministry is still to the soul, since only Christ saves anyone from death.

Adam is complementarian. He preached a series on the women disciples so the women in his church would hear what that conviction does not keep them from. In it he taught that Phoebe was a deacon and that the office is open to women, that Paul trusted a woman to carry his letter to the Romans, and that women were the first witnesses and heralds of the resurrection. He told women to bring their ministry to the church without waiting for a man to authorize it, and he honors singleness. He celebrates what men and women share before he speaks to how they differ.

His idea of who counts as a Christian brother is wide, with a firm edge. Anyone who confesses that Jesus Christ came in the flesh is a brother, even when wrong about much else, and "way more people are your brother than you think." He has come to teach that denominations are beautiful, because they show the many forms Christian worship takes, and tragic, because many of them mark conflicts Christians never resolved the way Christ calls his church to resolve them. Those who deny the incarnation, and he names Mormonism, are outside the family and are its mission field. He honors Mary as the mother of the Mediator and the first member of the New Testament church, and he insists she needed a Savior like everyone else.

### Country and politics

Adam calls Christianity fused with a party or a nation idolatry, whether the party is conservative or socialist, and he has named "God, guns, and country" as one version of it. The gospel is at home in every culture and at odds with every culture, so no culture is ready-made for it, his own included. When the Old Testament speaks of Israel, he reads it as the people of God, and he does not read the United States into it. American Christians, in his reading, stand where the Canaanite woman stood: outsiders brought in by mercy.

In a sermon on the Lord's Prayer he said, "there is a King, and it's not you, and it's God… he's not in the White House." Because God rules the nations, Christians need not panic over elections. He has criticized believers who fight hard for liberties they never use for the gospel. Christians reach the world by evangelism and deal with sin inside the church through the gospel and church discipline, and he sets both against efforts to make the surrounding culture "look Christian."

He still names injustice. From the pulpit he has listed his own country's sins: chattel slavery, Jim Crow, the Trail of Tears, the internment of Japanese Americans in the Second World War, and abortion. He handles the New Testament's passages on slavery with care, showing how Paul's letters worked against Roman slavery and how they were later misused to defend slavery. He calls Christians to stand with the small, the fatherless, and the widow. He believes the comfort of the West threatens the church more than persecution does.

### Hope and the nations

Adam calls himself a future-minded believer. The Bible's story runs from creation to fall to redemption to restoration, and he says Christians neglect the last part. He expects Jesus to return as conquering King. He corrects the picture of heaven as disembodied souls or stardust. Believers who die go straight into God's presence, and at the resurrection they receive new bodies to live on a renewed earth. The risen Christ still bears his scars, and Adam believes our wounds will make our worship greater there. He has quoted Joni Eareckson Tada: the first thing she will do with new legs is kneel. He once taught that we would forget this life in heaven, and he has since corrected himself. What a person does here lasts.

He believes God subjected this world to futility on purpose, so that people would stop treating it as their destination. He compares Christians to airline passengers fighting over legroom and forgetting where the plane is going.

Until Jesus returns, the church has work to do: "if Jesus isn't back yet, the work's not done." God's plan from the start has been a people from every tribe, tongue, and nation. Adam preaches about the roughly two billion people with almost no access to the gospel, and about how little of the church's giving reaches them. He and his wife carry a burden for Japan, a nation of 127 million that is less than one percent Christian, and they study Japanese every day. In his view "the Hudson Taylors were just people who saw Jesus rightly," and missions grows out of worship the way a song does. The "go" of the Great Commission covers the street as well as the ocean.

### The question he kept asking

For years Adam asked his church whether they loved Jesus himself or the benefits of Jesus. He named his own temptation: Jesus plus health, and health insurance, for his family. He warned that "many better men and women than you and I have walked away from Jesus when suffering struck," because the benefits were what they loved. His picture of the other answer is Mary of Bethany breaking a flask worth a year's wages over a man about to die, and Jesus saying she had done a beautiful thing to him.
