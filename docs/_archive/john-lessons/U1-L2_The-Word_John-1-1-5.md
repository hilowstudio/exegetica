**Talk budget:** this is deep water, and the temptation is to lecture the doctrine of the deity of Christ. Do not. The question sequence lets students discover the three claims of 1:1 themselves, which lands harder than being told. Your sustained voice stays in the Land.

---

## Move 1 — Quiet time with the Lord · 7 min · ungraded

A student may host now if one is ready; otherwise you host.

- **Settle (1):** read **John 1:1** slowly, once.
- **Journal (5):** *"Sit with one sentence: 'In the beginning was the Word.' What questions does it raise in you? Write them down, even the skeptical ones."*
- **Gather (1):** close the journals.

## Move 2 — Hear the Word · 4 min

Read **John 1:1-5** aloud, slowly, twice, because it is dense poetry. Once by a student, once read slowly by you.

## Move 3 — Work the text · 24 min · questions, not lecture

- **Entry Q (all):** "What does a word actually do? Why do we use words at all?" Turn-and-talk. Land it: a word carries what is inside us out to someone else. John calls Jesus "the Word" because Jesus carries the invisible God out to us and makes him known. That doorway needs no church background, and it is exactly where the Prologue ends up in 1:18.
- **Core Q sequence (John groups, observation in the Reading Journal):**
  - "Where have you heard 'in the beginning' before?" (Genesis 1.) What is John doing by echoing it? (Putting Jesus there, at creation.)
  - Look hard at 1:1: three claims in one verse. The Word already **was**, the Word was **with God**, and the Word **was God**. In your group, put each claim in your own words. (He already existed; he is distinct from the Father; he is fully God.)
  - 1:3, "all things were made through him." What does that make Jesus, next to everything else that exists? (The Maker, not one of the made things.)
  - 1:4-5, "in him was life, and the life was the light of men. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." What two things are in him? (Life, light.) What happens when his light meets the darkness? (The darkness cannot put it out.)
- **Deeper Q (D):** "If Jesus was there at the very beginning speaking creation, then he was never a backup plan. What changes if the cross was Plan A from eternity, and not a rescue after things went wrong?" Let a couple of students wrestle with it.
- **Cold-call checks (named):** Who is "the Word"? Name the three claims of 1:1. What was made through him? What can the darkness not do?

## Move 4 — Land and minister · 6 min · your protected pulpit

- John opens the story not at a manger but in eternity, fixing our eyes on who this Jesus really is before we watch him do anything. The One we will follow verse by verse all year is the eternal God through whom you yourself were made.
- Speak it over them, the way your sermon did: whatever darkness you are walking in, the light of this Christ is not a candle the dark can snuff out. It shines, and the darkness has not overcome it, and it never once has.

## Move 5 — Respond · 4 min · graded participation

- **Reading Journal:** *"'The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.' Where do you most need that to be true right now?"*

## Teacher notes

- Do not rush and do not lecture. The whole theological center of the book sits in these five verses, and students remember what they discovered far longer than what they were told.
