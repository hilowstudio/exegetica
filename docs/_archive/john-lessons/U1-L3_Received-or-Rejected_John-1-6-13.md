**Talk budget:** the "received, not earned" idea is worth your Land, but let the groups discover it from the text first. Sustained voice under ~18 minutes; questions carry the Work block.

---

## Move 1 — Quiet time with the Lord · 7 min · ungraded
- **Settle (1):** read **John 1:9-10** slowly.
- **Journal (5):** *"'He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him.' Sit with that. Write whatever it stirs, even if it is a question."*
- **Gather (1):** close the journals.

## Move 2 — Hear the Word · 4 min
- Students read **John 1:6-13** aloud, slowly. Remind the room where we are: still inside the poem, right after "the light shines in the darkness."

## Move 3 — Work the text · 24 min · questions, not lecture
- **Entry Q (all):** "What does a witness do at a trial? Do they talk about themselves?" Turn-and-talk. Bridge: John the Baptist is a witness. His whole job is to point away from himself to the light (1:8).
- **Core Q sequence (John groups, observation in the Reading Journal):**
  - 1:9-11: the true light came into the world he made, and "his own people did not receive him." What is strange, even tragic, about that? (The Maker came to his own and was turned away.)
  - 1:12: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God." Two words to weigh: **received** and **children of God**. In your group, what is the difference between receiving something and earning it?
  - 1:13: this new birth is "not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God." Who does the birthing? (God, not us.)
- **Deeper Q (D):** "John says being a child of God is a birth, and you cannot birth yourself. How does that change the way we usually talk about 'deciding to follow God'?" Hold it; we will meet it head-on with Nicodemus in chapter 3.
- **Cold-call checks (named):** Was John the Baptist the light? Who did not receive him? What were those who received him given? Whose work is this new birth?

## Move 4 — Land and minister · 6 min · your protected pulpit
- Here is the ache and the door in one breath: the Maker of the world came to his own, and they did not want him. And still, to all who **receive** him, not earn him, not impress him, just open their hands and take him, he gives the right to be called children of God.
- Speak it over the room, the way your sermon did: the fridge is not locked, the storehouse is not being rationed. This is a gift, and the only posture that receives a gift is an open hand.

## Move 5 — Respond · 4 min · graded participation
- **Reading Journal:** *"'To all who received him.' Where do you catch yourself trying to earn something from God instead of receiving it?"*

---

## After the bell
- **Homework:** read **John 1:14-18** for next class, the summit of the poem.
- **Feeds the gradebook:** Participation. The witness, the rejection, and the new birth are content-floor material for quizzes and the final.
- **Sets up:** Lesson 6, the Word made flesh (1:14-18), where we plant the memory verse and land the whole Prologue.

## Teacher notes
- The temptation is to preach "received, not earned" for twenty minutes. Let the groups find it in 1:12 first; it lands harder when they say it than when you do.
- "Born of God" is a seed for Nicodemus. Plant it lightly here and let it grow; do not preach chapter 3 yet.
