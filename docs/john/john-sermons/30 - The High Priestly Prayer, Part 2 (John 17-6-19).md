# The High Priestly Prayer, Part 2 (John 17:6-19)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 17:6-19

---

Our task this morning is to continue in John 17. We've been working straight through the Gospel of John for almost a year and a half now, and we're nearing the end. Last week I said that John 17, the High Priestly Prayer, is my favorite chapter in the Bible.

I said one of the main reasons is that we get to see the inner heart of Jesus. We see what he desires, in an intimate conversation between him and his Father that he allowed to be recorded for us. Which means that here, near the end of John's Gospel, after you've sat under it week by week for almost a year and a half, Jesus is summing up the things that mattered most to him, the things of his inner heart. This is a time to lean in, to ask, okay, what was all of this about? He's praying about the same things he's been talking about through his whole public ministry.

I taught last week that John 17 falls into three sections: the part where Jesus prays for himself, the part where he prays for the disciples who walked with him on earth, and the part where he prays for all who would believe, where he prays for you and me. This week we're in the second part, exploring Jesus' prayer for his disciples. But we don't want to make it three separate prayers. It's one prayer.

Last week we saw that in the first part of his prayer, Jesus asks the Father, glorify me. Glorify me. Why? Because of who I am and because of what I've done. I'm the one to whom you've given authority to give eternal life.

I'm the one to whom you've given authority over all flesh. So glorify me with the glory I had before the world existed, because I have accomplished what you sent me to do. From there he moves on to his disciples, his friends. Let's see what he says. Verse 6: "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world.

"Yours they were, and you gave them to me, and they have kept your word." Over the last six months or so, some of you have said to me, playfully, that it seems I keep hitting the same doctrines week after week. My answer was, and is, that while some doctrines are precious and dear to me, it isn't because I have a pet doctrine I want to read into the text. When you preach straight through a book, you have to preach what it says. If Jesus talks about something a lot, I have to talk about it a lot. And Jesus talks about this a lot.

Here we come to a doctrine that is precious to me and that Jesus talks about a lot, and we see that he even prays about it. In his own conversation with his Father, not so private, since we're reading it, he brings it up. And in this prayer he isn't teaching. He's praying.

He isn't using parables or figurative language. He's simply talking with his Father about what is. So this may be as direct as we'll ever meet this doctrine. We'll find out what is.

Let's see what he says. Verse 6: "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world." I, Jesus, have manifested your name, the Father's name, to the people whom you, the Father, gave me, the Son, out of the world. Stay with me. I, Jesus, have manifested your name, the Father's, to the people whom you, the Father, gave me, the Son, out of the world.

It goes in circles. And this is why we preach it so directly and so often: when Jesus says it, it's important that we understand it. Statisticians say there are more than two billion Christians in the world. I say "supposedly," not to cast doubt on whether they're really Christians; I'm just quoting a statistic.

I haven't met them. That number includes Catholics, the Eastern Orthodox, Protestants and everything that flows from them, all the subdivisions and denominations. And the church splits over lots of different things.

When you see division, and Jesus is going to pray in this passage for unity, you have to ask: why does the church subdivide? What separates us from one another? That would be its own sermon, but my short answer is that there are the things Jesus has said, and there are the things he hasn't said.

When we start building religion, ideologies and beliefs around things he hasn't said, it comes time to fight. Not sinfully. But even as we pursue unity, which we'll talk about in this passage, I want to hold out to you that it is better to divide over the truth that saves than to have unity over a lie that ultimately kills. It's the truth that saves, so we hold it out and let the Lord work it out. And here is what he says.

I, Jesus, manifested the Father's name to the people the Father gave him out of the world. This is the doctrine I teach: regeneration, belonging to God, comes before faith. Regeneration, belonging to God, comes before faith and is not a product of faith. This is an important doctrine, and I want to hold out why, because friends have sometimes accused me of being pedantic, of nitpicking word order.

That's not what's happening here. If we flip the order, we're talking about two entirely different doctrines. There's a reason we're not Catholic, a reason you're here and not at the Catholic church down the road.

There's a reason you're not Muslim. There's a reason you don't believe you have to make the Hajj and pray toward Mecca five times a day, and that this is how you make yourself right with God. There's a reason you don't confess your sins to a priest, or pray the rosary, or say your Hail Marys.

Or seek indulgences to shorten a loved one's time in purgatory. There are all kinds of bad doctrines various groups believe that we reject, and we know why we split over those things. And there are other things we'd call open-handed issues. Some say the Sabbath was ceremonial in nature, that Jesus fulfilled it in his life and is now our Sabbath, so we don't need to observe it.

Others say no, the Sabbath wasn't ceremonial. It comes from the creation account, so it's part of God's moral law, and we're supposed to keep observing it. You're wondering, why are we talking about this? Because we don't break fellowship over that.

It's an open-handed issue. But there are closed-handed issues. You say Jesus isn't God; I say Jesus is God.

We have to part ways until you agree. This doctrine has been treated as an open-handed issue, but depending on how you think about it, it risks becoming a closed-handed one, and I want to hold out why. Many millions, even billions, teach that we are saved by works, and we reject that.

We reject it. Nod your head: we reject that we're saved by works. We are not saved by works. We say we are saved by grace through faith.

That's what we believe the Bible teaches. We're not saved by works. But then a great many people say, of course we're not saved by works; we're saved by faith. And some of you are in this category. You're thinking, wait, aren't we?

You mean well, and your doctrine is correct; you just say it oddly. That's fine. We'll fix that today. But others, when they say we're saved by faith, mean exactly that.

We're saved by faith: my faith is a gift I give God in exchange for salvation. I believe in you, and in return you owe me salvation. My faith is a gift I give you, and that's how we're saved by faith.

When you say that, when you teach that, it's worth breaking fellowship over. That's what I'm holding out to you this morning. Because then all you've actually said is that we're saved by works; you've just told me which work. The work of faith: you must do this, believe, have faith, and then God rewards you with eternity.

That's a works-based idea. But Jesus says something very different here. Listen closely: "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world." He says, "Yours they were, and you gave them to me."

There is a people hidden in the secret heart of God who belong to him. A people who belong to God: "Yours they were." And God the Father gives them to Christ. And when God the Father gives these people to Christ, Christ makes the name of God known to them. Which means you were God's possession before the name of God was made known to you. In the secret heart of God, known to him but not to you, it was already so: you were a free gift to his Son, Jesus Christ. And to those the Father gives to Jesus, Jesus makes the Father's name known.

So here's what he says. Read it again, verses 6 and 7: "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world. Yours they were, and you gave them to me, and they have kept your word. Now they know that everything that you have given me is from you."

We may say we know this, but maybe we don't really know it, so let's know it today. My disciples now know that everything you've given me is from you. But he has just said that one of the things God gave him was you. Do you know that if you belong to Christ, it's because God the Father gave you to him?

Do you know that everything Christ possesses was a gift to him from the Father, that it came from the Father? Your faith didn't produce your salvation. Your faith is a result of your salvation. Regeneration comes before faith; that's what I teach. And I teach it because Jesus says it, and keeps saying it.

He has been saying it all through this Gospel: no one can come to me unless the Father draws him; no one comes to the Father except through me; all that the Father gives me will come to me; and I will lose none of them. These aren't new ideas. Jesus has said these things quite clearly. But now he's praying in these terms too. The people you gave to me, I made you known to them.

Verse 8: "For I have given them the words that you gave me, and they have received them and have come to know in truth that I came from you; and they have believed that you sent me." Here we start to see our part in this. Our part isn't that we traded our faith for salvation.

Our part is that we are receivers. I, Jesus, have given them the words the Father gave the Son, and they have received them and have come to know in truth that I came from you. But we know about Jesus; we've been reading this Gospel. He taught indiscriminately. He went out into the crowds and taught.

He didn't keep these things for the disciples alone. He said them to everyone, and some believed and some didn't. On what basis? Only some would receive the words. Who would receive them?

The ones who belonged to the Father, who were given to the Son. And that's not all. Adam, maybe you're going too far. Well, Jesus says, verse 9, "I am praying for them. I am not praying for the world but for those whom you have given me, for they are yours."

Sometimes I do feel like I'm going too far. But that accusation would have to be made against Jesus. When we think about the role of faith, and we'll get deeper into it as we move through the passage, it's really important to me that you hear me clearly at the start: your faith is not something you produced and then traded for right standing with God. You were saved not by faith; you were saved by grace.

And the Scriptures say, through faith. "By" and "through" are two very different words when it comes to the order of salvation. Let me explain. In Galatians, Paul makes an argument about the order of salvation and righteousness, and he goes all the way back.

He says this isn't even a new idea. What I'm talking about isn't a new idea. He brings up Abraham and the covenant God made with Abraham. Remember that covenant: I will make you a great nation; your children will outnumber the stars; I will give you a promised land; through your offspring blessing will spread far and wide. It was a one-directional, unconditional covenant God made with Abraham before Abraham did anything.

Then Abraham, by grace, believed what God said. And remember the story of Isaac. God calls Abraham to sacrifice his only son, Isaac, the one on whom all those promises hinged.

So Abraham takes his son and sets out for the mountain. And he says to his young men, "Stay here with the donkey; I and the boy will go over there and worship and come again to you." Some have taught that Abraham was lying there because it was awkward to say where he was going. But no.

Abraham was thinking about what God had promised to do through his line, and he understood that somehow, some way, Isaac was coming back with him, because God had made a promise. Hebrews tells us he "considered that God was able even to raise him from the dead." And of course, when he gets there, God provides a substitute, the ram. Scripture counts the faith of Abraham as righteousness. But what did Abraham need faith for? His faith was actively believing what God said.

God had already done the thing. He had already made the promise to Abraham. Faith was Abraham taking hold of the promise and believing it. So we know that we don't secure the promises of God by faith. God makes his promises, and faith flows out of them. We'll go further into that.

"All mine are yours," he says. He says, I'm praying for them. I'm not praying for the world but for those you've given me, for they are yours. He's precise. His will is never at odds with the Father's.

Jesus is praying for the ones the Father has given him. Verses 10 to 13: "All mine are yours, and yours are mine, and I am glorified in them. And I am no longer in the world, but they are in the world, and I am coming to you. Holy Father, keep them in your name, which you have given me, that they may be one, even as we are one.

"While I was with them, I kept them in your name, which you have given me. I have guarded them, and not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled." He means Judas. "But now I am coming to you, and these things I speak in the world, that they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves." So that you don't go home scratching your head, thinking this was just a theological exercise, here's why it matters. First, as I've said, we're talking about the difference between I'm saved by works and I'm saved by grace.

Either I'm saved by something I did, something good in me, or by something good in God. That's the difference if I start saying I'm saved by faith in the sense that I trade God my faith. Your faith is a gift from him, not to him. That's the first reason.

That's important to get right in order to be a Christian at all. But separately, for the Christians in the room, we start to ask: what, then, is the role of faith?

The role of faith is assurance. You're going to be kept in the name of God, and that's what Jesus starts focusing on next. He prays that the Father would keep you in the name of God. Jesus reveals the name of God to you. He makes it known to you.

Then you are kept in it by the power of God. We said last week that what Jesus asks for, he gets. What Jesus wants, he gets. What Jesus prays for, you can take to the bank. By extension, he's praying here for his friends, and you're his friends, that God the Father would keep us in his name, because he's leaving.

He says, it's been my job until now to keep them, and I've done it. I haven't lost one. I said I wouldn't, and he won't. Now I'm going back to the right hand of the Father. Father, keep them.

Keep them. And he will do it by sending the Holy Spirit. While I was with them, I kept them in your name, which you have given me. I have guarded them, and not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled. But now I am coming to you, and these things I speak in the world, that they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves.

Joy. He tells us why he's saying this: I say these things in the world so that my joy may be fulfilled in them. So that my joy may be fulfilled in them.

Jesus says this is for your joy: a salvation that he didn't only begin but holds on to, so that the credit is all his that you are saved at all, that you have any faith to speak of. He says this is for your joy. So if you feel great resistance to what he's saying here, I'd say to you that someone who has received grace doesn't rail against the grace he's received. His joy is fulfilled.

It's the flesh that finds it offensive to admit that God is sovereign over his people. No child rails against God for the way he came to be his child. You were adopted by grace alone. Let me read you Ephesians 1, from verse 3: "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who has blessed us in Christ with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places, even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him.

"In love he predestined us for adoption to himself as sons through Jesus Christ, according to the purpose of his will, to the praise of his glorious grace, with which he has blessed us in the Beloved. In him we have redemption through his blood, the forgiveness of our trespasses, according to the riches of his" what? "grace, which he lavished upon us, in all wisdom and insight making known to us the mystery of his will." That's what I mean when I say you were stored up in the secret heart of God. The mystery of his will is that he would look out on the face of creation and see not one person deserving of grace, and, not because of anything good in us but because of something good in him, pour out grace on any of us and save us by his good will, by the mystery of his will.

"According to his purpose, which he set forth in Christ as a plan for the fullness of time, to unite all things in Christ, things in heaven and things on earth in him." So when we call this an open-handed issue, on the one hand I want to say, sure. On the other hand I want to ask, but does the text say it? Because if it says it, then there's a reason we rail against it, a reason we try to wriggle free of it.

There's a reason we want to redefine it. And those reasons are understandable. I care a lot about the discomfort doctrines can cause. I just don't think that discomfort should be resolved by lying. It can be uncomfortable to see just how sovereign our God is, until it becomes a comfort.

If it's still uncomfortable, keep looking, keep reading, keep praying. Because insofar as it depends on you, church, to muster up enough faith to join the ranks of God, you're doomed. You simply can't do it. Every day you'd look at your life and ask, do I have enough faith today? Do I have enough faith today?

You'd be scrambling to understand whether you can keep your salvation. But if it wasn't you who got you saved, and it isn't you who keeps you saved, then all praise be to Christ that he did this by his own power and will. I want us to believe this because it changes the way we'll engage our community. It changes the way we engage our Christ. Verses 14 to 19: "I have given them your word, and the world has hated them because they are not of the world, just as I am not of the world.

"I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one. They are not of the world, just as I am not of the world. Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth. As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world. And for their sake I consecrate myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth."

He speaks these things in the world so that his joy may be fulfilled in them. And then he moves on to this: they are not of the world. Church, you are not of the world. From eternity past you were stored up in the heart of God for heaven.

That's who you are. That's what you've always been. Through faith, through belief, you came to understand it on a particular day in history. But from God's perspective, this has been the mystery of his will from eternity past. So what is the role of faith?

Jesus says it here: it's how we engage a world we don't belong to. Maybe you've heard this illustration. A kid stands at the edge of a pool, and his father is in the pool calling out to him, jump, son, jump. The kid can't swim. The child has faith in his father and in his ability to catch him.

His father is trustworthy. He's proven himself in all kinds of ways. The child has faith. And now the father has said, jump; I'll catch you.

Now I've been given specific words to believe. And I believe you. I believe you'll catch me. But faith is belief plus action.

Until the child jumps into his father's arms, he has the kind of faith James calls dead: faith without works is dead. Faith is belief in action. And even this is given to you as a gift, for the assurance of your salvation. In other words, you don't strengthen your salvation by faith; you strengthen your assurance of it.

As you put your belief into motion, you see again and again that the living God is alive in you, that you don't belong to this world but to him. And the motion of faith never lets you off the hook from your dependence on Christ. Jesus prays for them: I have given them your word.

The world has hated them, because they are not of the world, just as I am not of the world. I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from the evil one. As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world. And this is the last piece: your faith has a role in unity and mission.

He prays, make them one, just as we are one, and don't take them out of the world. I'm sending them into the world, just as you sent me into the world. The role of faith isn't to save you. The role of faith is for you to carry out God's purposes on the face of the earth before you join him for eternity at the right hand of the Father. So, by the Holy Spirit dwelling in you through faith, you have the opportunity to walk out your sanctification.

What does "sanctify" mean? To sanctify is to set apart for holy service to God. That's why I preach another precious doctrine: the same grace that saved you is the grace that sanctifies you. The idea that you're saved by grace and then have to get to work to make yourself holier is foolish.

There's a disconnect, a contradiction, in saying that my whole nature was changed by an act of God, and now it's up to me and my flesh to add to it, to heap holiness on top. Come on. Jesus prays to the Father, sanctify them; set them apart for holy service to you. And he said in chapters 15 and 16 that this would be done by the power of the Holy Spirit, that the fruit you bear in the world will also be credited to him.

I guess what I'm trying to say, Christian, is that you don't get to take credit for anything. You just get to worship him. Every time you show any proof that he's done something in your life, you get to praise him for it. And every time you show proof of what you are apart from him, you run back to him for grace, because this is all ultimately about him and his glory.

It's about him. It's about him. It's a good thing it's not about you. "As you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world. And for their sake I consecrate myself, that they also may be sanctified in truth." This is the last part, I promise. He says it's for our sake that he consecrates himself, sets himself apart.

Why? So that they also may be set apart in truth. Here Jesus says what he's been saying all along: they're with me. They are united to me.

By setting myself apart, I set them apart, because they're with me. This is no small truth. We're not only talking about our standing with God, although our standing with God is all wrapped up in Christ. We're talking about today, before the day of judgment: you are set apart, not of the world, because Christ has united you with himself and has set himself apart. That's why I've been having so many conversations lately about what faith even means.

We take faith to mean knowledge, and then we take knowledge to mean right thinking, right belief. We reduce faith to a mental exercise, and in that way we're saying we're saved by works after all: saved by what we think, by the work of thinking rightly. The reason one person goes to hell and another to heaven is that one was smarter than the other.

He believed right; you believed wrong. Sorry. That's just wrong. Faith is not belief.

Belief is part of faith, but faith is not belief. Belief plus action is one way to describe faith, but faith is something saved people have. Something saved people do would be a better way to put it. And what you're doing, Jesus says over and over, is actually the fruit of the Holy Spirit in you. So faith is not only a gift; it is the work of the Holy Spirit present in you.

You don't believe anything without his help. You don't do anything without his help. You must have the indwelling Holy Spirit to have even a mustard seed of faith, and a mustard seed of faith is all it takes to change the world. I want us to come to know this forgotten person of the Trinity, the Holy Spirit, who is present in the church. I want us to understand faith.

I want you to understand faith as the product of the indwelling Holy Spirit. If faith is merely the strength of your flesh to think correctly, you're doomed. But if faith is guaranteed because the Holy Spirit has taken up residence in you, then it only reinforces, again and again, the Holy Spirit's witness with your spirit that you are a child of God, set apart before you've done a thing. So go and do. Our affections will be realigned.

Our actions will be realigned. Our mission will be realigned. Our thinking will be realigned. The Holy Spirit will lay claim to every area of our lives because of something God has done in us. I want this to be good news for you.

I want us to believe it. I want us to condemn self-righteousness. I want us to condemn justification by works. I want us to condemn the doctrines that put us into slavery. If you're thinking, I'm not good enough, hear me say: I know. You're not good enough.

I don't want to step away from the pulpit this morning, because I look at you and think about all the conversations of the last six months, and there's a theme in the church: we keep working our way back to one central lie, that Jesus is looking for a contribution from me so that I can stay okay with him, that what really makes me a Christian is what I do. So much guilt and shame and condemnation heaped on ourselves, because we believe something false about how we came to be his in the first place and how we remain his. It was never you.

Your part is to repent and believe, and the Holy Spirit is the one who makes that possible. So let's call on him now to draw us deeply into that.
