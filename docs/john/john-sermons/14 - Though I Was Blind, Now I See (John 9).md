# Though I Was Blind, Now I See (John 9)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 9

---

Last week I spent some time telling you about the moment the words of the Bible came alive for me. As a child I found a Bible and read it; it was interesting, but it didn't do anything. As a teenager I found my way to a church, looking for something, seeking meaning, and I found it delightful to be around people who believed these things, but I didn't believe them myself. The words didn't do anything.

But something changed in a moment when the Holy Spirit brought the words of the Lord alive for me. I want to extend that story a little as you get to know me. I wasn't raised in the church. I couldn't have told the Lord from the guy next door.

I was utterly blind the day I met Jesus. I was maybe nineteen or twenty. I had been walking away from everything to do with the Lord and had been at the end of my sin for some time.

I had tried just about everything you can think of to build significance, meaning, value, security, joy and pleasure, and at the end of each one I was left hungering and thirsting for more. I was in a particular season of sin and despair, a darkness, with regular panic attacks. In the middle of one of them, I was walking along the Gulf of Mexico.

I was on vacation with a woman I shouldn't have been with, and I was walking alone. It was maybe midnight. I could hardly see my hand in front of my face. About a month earlier I had prayed that shaken-fist prayer, the first honest prayer I'd ever prayed to the Lord. In essence I said to him, I acknowledge that something is broken in me, but whatever is broken in me must be your fault, because you're supposed to be a powerful God who made all things.

That's what I'd heard, so whatever fault you find in me must be your fault. And there I was, a month later, walking along the Gulf of Mexico, and I experienced what I can only describe as the veil being lifted. The Holy Spirit bore witness with my spirit, as Scripture says he does. If I could put words to the voice of God in that moment, I heard him tell me that I belonged to him.

Suddenly everything began to change. I prayed my first prayer of humble submission and repentance, acknowledging my lowly estate before him. And not knowing the first thing about baptism, and not claiming this was anything like it, I walked out alone into the Gulf of Mexico, held my breath under the water as long as I could, and said to this God I hardly knew: when I come up out of this water, have your way. What I've been doing hasn't worked. When I come up out of this water, whatever you need to do, do it, because I'm a mess.

I went back to the room and broke it off with that woman. We got off the plane coming home, and I never spoke to her again. I bought a Bible and read it in three months on the night shift at a hospital.

When you read the Bible at that pace, you don't pick up on much. But what I did pick up on was that over and over, people are a mess and keep doing what they shouldn't. And God, for reasons only he knows, keeps forgiving and setting his saving affection on a people. Even though they don't do anything right, and at every chance to turn right or wrong they turn wrong, the Lord in his mercy keeps forgiving. That was really good news for me, because I was the guy who kept doing the wrong thing, and I expected I would go on struggling and doing the wrong thing.

If I had any hope at all, he would have to be a God who saves and forgives by his own power. And reading the word of God as it was lit up for me, I discovered that this is exactly the kind of God we worship. Amen? My response was to act as if I'd discovered him, as if I were the first person ever to hear about this God.

I needed everyone to know. On my day shifts I'd sit at a different cafeteria table every day and ask, have you ever heard of Jesus? I put my job on the line every day, because I needed everyone to know there is a God who forgives people who don't deserve it, who brings them from death to life and can change everything in a moment. That was the beginning of the Lord making a church planter and a pastor out of me.

That's the faithfulness of God in the life of the man speaking to you. And you'll find stories like this all through the people of God. If you spend time with believers, you'll hear stories that sound a lot like our text this morning. What happened? I don't know, but I was blind and now I see. That's the best any of us can do to explain it.

I want you to notice how this unfolds in the life of a person who encounters Jesus Christ. For those of you who know him, I want you to delight in remembering it for yourselves. And for those of you who have stood on the outskirts looking in, like some we'll meet this morning, I pray you'll see what it means to really get your eyes on him.

This is just after the Feast of Booths. Chapter 9, verses 1 to 3: "As he passed by, he saw a man blind from birth. And his disciples asked him, 'Rabbi, who sinned, this man or his parents, that he was born blind?' Jesus answered, 'It was not that this man sinned, or his parents, but that the works of God might be displayed in him.'"

The works of God might be displayed in him. This isn't the main point of my message, but it would be unloving of me not to take a minute on it, in case even one of you is living in this kind of slavery.

It wasn't outside the bounds of accepted thought in ancient Judaism to believe one of two things: that a child in the womb could be held guilty for his mother's sin if she sinned while she was pregnant, or that a child could sin while still in the womb. Some justified this with Jacob and Esau struggling inside their mother.

So for them this was a logical question, a theological question. Whose fault is it? Whose sin caused this man to be born blind? Surely either he or his parents are to blame.

But it's not a matter of who sinned. It's a matter of whose glory will shine in this moment. It was not that this man sinned, but that the works of God might be displayed in him. There's a lot of bad theology in the church today about this, which is why I want to take a minute. If you are marked by illness, by sickness, cancer, disease, an autoimmune condition, whatever it is, if you wake up and it hurts, and it hurts day after day, and you've been told, or told yourself, that this is clearly your fault, that your sin has brought it on you, or that it's someone else's generational curse on your life, I want to free you from that this morning. Jesus says it was neither this man's sin nor his parents' sin, but that the works of God might be displayed in him.

Christian, you're not alone if you believe this. Others here have struggled with it too. You look at your fight against sickness and infirmity and ask, what is God trying to do here? What is he trying to teach me? He's clearly punishing me for something.

If I could just do this or that, obey better, then the Lord's favor would be on me and my illness would be lifted. I want to free you from that, because here's what you're believing. Your sin needed to be paid for, and I affirm that it did, and it was paid for at the cross by Jesus Christ. You didn't owe the Lord cancer for your sin. You didn't owe the Lord bodily suffering for your sin.

You owed your life for your sin. You deserved what I deserved: death. We don't pay off our sin through personal suffering. We don't beat ourselves up or stay low long enough that the Lord eventually turns from his wrath to pity because of what we've endured. That's not what we're called to.

Payment must be made for sin, but you can't pay it, and you aren't helping Jesus with your illness or your suffering. He didn't need your help. He didn't require it. He paid it all. If you are in Christ, I want that to be a message of freedom for you this morning, because you need to look at your sicknesses and the things that hurt in a completely different light and ask a different question.

Not, what did I do wrong? But, how does my Lord want to display his glory here? How does my Lord want to display his glory here? Let's see how he does it for this blind man. Verses 4 to 7: "'We must work the works of him who sent me while it is day; night is coming, when no one can work. As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world.' Having said these things, he spit on the ground and made mud with the saliva. Then he anointed the man's eyes with the mud and said to him, 'Go, wash in the pool of Siloam' (which means Sent). So he went and washed and came back seeing."

The disciples asked, why? Why was he born blind? Why has he spent all these years begging at the gates, blind? And Jesus answers, so that, watch this. He kneads mud packs together, puts them on the man's eyes and tells him to go wash in the pool of Siloam. A couple of things stood out to me in the way Jesus did this miracle.

The first is the easier one. We talked a little about the pool of Siloam a couple of weeks ago. It was several hundred yards from the temple. During the feast that had just ended before this passage, for seven days a priest would take a golden pitcher from the temple, walk down to the pool of Siloam, fill it and carry it back up to the altar, and the people would pray, Lord, save us. Lord, save us. Lord, save us.

And on the last day, the great day of the feast, Jesus stood up and cried, if anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. This is the pool of Siloam, and this is the pool Jesus sends the man to: the pool where the priest filled the pitcher and poured out the water as a symbol of the coming Messiah, a symbol of their salvation.

Jesus says, go wash in that pool. And John, who is careful to explain any term a non-Jewish reader might not understand, makes sure we know that the name of the pool, Siloam, means Sent. And Jesus has said again and again in this Gospel, the Father sent me. I am the Son. I am the Sent One.

So he says, go to the pool called Sent, the pool they fill the pitcher from, and wash in it. But he doesn't only do that. He adds another physical element to the healing.

He makes these mud packs. Now, I'm not sure whether I'm way off theologically on this, so don't take notes; just listen. Here's what I know.

Jesus has claimed to be the light of the world. And John opens his Gospel by declaring the high truths about Jesus: that he is the Word in the creation account, that he is God himself, that all things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made. This Jesus is the Word, by the power of whose word all things were created. And we know something else about the creation story, don't we?

He created all things by the power of his speech alone, until he created whom? Man. Then he doesn't just speak. Our God kneels down in the dirt, and scooping dirt into his hand, he forms man from the dust and breathes into his nostrils the breath of life, joining himself to man and making him in his own image, so that we might reflect him in creation.

In the creation story, our God, creative and all-powerful, looked at the dirt and said, I'll call him Adam, and he made man. And if our God can take dirt and make a whole human, he can take dirt and make eyes. Again, don't write it down. I'm not saying he made little eyeballs.

What I am saying is that the man needed new eyes. So Jesus makes these mud packs from his saliva and the dirt, forms them, and puts them on his eyes. He says, go wash. And the man comes back seeing.

So in some way, figurative or literal, our God, who formed man from the dust, takes these mud packs and gives the man new sight. I'm not the first to read it this way. Irenaeus, a bishop in the second century, said that in the mud Jesus was showing the hand of God, the same hand by which man was formed out of the dust. Verses 8 to 12: "The neighbors and those who had seen him before as a beggar were saying, 'Is this not the man who used to sit and beg?' Some said, 'It is he.' Others said, 'No, but he is like him.' He kept saying, 'I am the man.'"

They wouldn't even listen to him. Be quiet; we'll decide.

I'm the man. I'm the man. Well, he kind of looks like him. No, that's not him.

I'm the man. "So they said to him, 'Then how were your eyes opened?'" And his answer is very simple: "'The man called Jesus made mud and anointed my eyes and said to me, "Go to Siloam and wash." So I went and washed and received my sight.'"

"They said to him, 'Where is he?' He said, 'I do not know.'" At this point in the story, all the man who received his sight can say about Jesus is that he's the man called Jesus. If you're like me, we fill in blanks that aren't there and imagine he's seen Jesus. But he hasn't. He had to go and wash to receive his sight, which means he had never seen the man who told him to do it.

At this point it's just: the man called Jesus, the one I've heard people talking about. He did this. Well, where is he? I don't know. I've never seen him.

If he stood in front of me right now, I couldn't tell you it was him. I've never seen him. But the fact that he can talk about what he has and hasn't seen is the miracle, isn't it? Until now, the answer was, I haven't seen anything.

Now it's, I haven't seen him. Not yet. Let's keep going. Verses 13 to 17: "They brought to the Pharisees the man who had formerly been blind. Now it was a Sabbath day when Jesus made the mud and opened his eyes. So the Pharisees again asked him how he had received his sight. And he said to them, 'He put mud on my eyes, and I washed, and I see.' Some of the Pharisees said, 'This man is not from God, for he does not keep the Sabbath.' But others said, 'How can a man who is a sinner do such signs?' And there was a division among them. So they said again to the blind man, 'What do you say about him, since he has opened your eyes?' He said, 'He is a prophet.'"

That's right and wrong. Here we learn that it was the Sabbath when Jesus did this healing, and Jesus loved to do that. We've been walking through this book, and Jesus constantly does things in ways the people don't want him to, to raise the conflict and start these conversations, so that he can prove himself to be who he claimed to be. So not only does he heal on the Sabbath. This may not interest you as much as it did me, but they're so interested in how he did it not only out of awe at the miracle. They're also trying to catch him out.

They're trying to trip him up. If he did it on the Sabbath, did he do it lawfully? How did he do it? Their Sabbath rules, the traditions later written down in the Mishnah, a giant code book of rulings on how to keep the laws of God, the Sabbath included, tell you what you may and may not do. And according to those rulings, you may not heal on the Sabbath.

Only if someone's life is in danger. Since this was lifelong blindness, this man wouldn't have qualified for healing on the Sabbath. He'd have to wait until tomorrow. I doubt they were thinking of this one, but kneading was also explicitly forbidden on the Sabbath. No kneading on the Sabbath.

And when Jesus made the mud pie, he was kneading. They weren't so interested in the miracle that happened as in how Jesus did it, because they're fixed on the fact that he did it on the Sabbath and looking for a way to trip him up. Then they ask, well, who do you say he is? Suddenly they're interested. This man has been blind all his life, and their culture believed that being blind from birth meant he was cursed by God for his own sin or his parents'.

Suddenly the religious elite are interested in his opinion of who Jesus is. He says, "He is a prophet." Verses 18 to 23: "The Jews did not believe that he had been blind and had received his sight, until they called the parents of the man who had received his sight and asked them, 'Is this your son, who you say was born blind? How then does he now see?'" I love that: "who you say was born blind." They keep their deniability. They won't admit he was born blind, only that you say he was.

"His parents answered, 'We know that this is our son and that he was born blind. But how he now sees we do not know, nor do we know who opened his eyes. Ask him; he is of age. He will speak for himself.' (His parents said these things because they feared the Jews, for the Jews had already agreed that if anyone should confess Jesus to be Christ, he was to be put out of the synagogue. Therefore his parents said, 'He is of age; ask him.')"

"He is of age" likely means he was old enough under their law to speak for himself and give testimony, probably thirteen or older. And the fact that his parents answered this way out of fear of being thrown out of the synagogue tells us they had in fact heard who did it. They had the name in mind, but they were afraid to confess that name, because it would come at a great cost: being thrown out of the synagogue. So they dodged the second questions and answered only the first: this is our son, and he was born blind. They wouldn't go any further. So the Pharisees bring the man back.

Verses 24 and 25: "So for the second time they called the man who had been blind and said to him, 'Give glory to God. We know that this man is a sinner.'" They mean Jesus. A simpler way to put it: glorify God by saying what we already know. Tell us Jesus is a sinner.

"He answered, 'Whether he is a sinner I do not know. One thing I do know, that though I was blind, now I see.'" This is the point in the message where it's good for us to pause and take stock of the progression, and to think about what it means for us to receive the living, regenerating work of Jesus Christ. He doesn't give a clinical answer for what happened to him, because he doesn't have one. He doesn't even give a theological answer, because he doesn't have one.

This man isn't trained in the Torah. He isn't a student of the rabbis' rulings. He's been blind his entire life, begging outside the gates, hoping to get enough today to eat. He's the last man to speak theologically about who Jesus is, whether he's the Christ, whether he should have been able to do this, whether it was lawful on the Sabbath.

None of that. He makes no theological or clinical argument for what happened to him. He simply states what he knows to be true: though I was blind, now I see. And I think that's a template for those of us who have received new life in Jesus Christ. You don't need to have all the answers. You don't need to make a great apologetic.

You don't need to be able to work systematically through the Old Testament and the New to make the claim. Ultimately, if you have new life in the Spirit, you can simply speak from experience about what is true for you: once you were blind, and now you see. I've been ridiculed and mocked all my life by at least some of the people around me because of what I profess to be true. They would love, just as the people around you would love, and even the accuser in your own mind would love, for you to be able to make a clinical or theological argument for what happened to you. But what makes you a Christian isn't what you can describe or define or articulate.

Ultimately the question is, has the Holy Spirit come on you and taken a heart of stone and made it a heart of flesh, or not? My evidence that this is true for me, and it's a terrible apologetic, the evidence you can't take from me, is that I pick this book up and it does anything at all. These words are life to me. There's nothing you could say that could make him not my Lord.

To someone who doesn't have that, I know what that's like. I don't expect you to reason your way to what I have. I didn't reason my way to it. What you need in order to hear these words, receive them and believe them is the Spirit within you, and I can't do that for you.

I can only proclaim that I was blind and now I see, and his name is Jesus. Then you have to go to Jesus and let him do the work. I can't make the mud packs. All I know is that though I was blind, now I see.

Verses 26 and 27: "They said to him, 'What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?'" Again, they're really interested in the mechanics. "He answered them, 'I have told you already, and you would not listen. Why do you want to hear it again?'"

"Do you also want to become his disciples?" I love how sassy this man is. I love it. For some of you, there's hope. If you relate to his sarcasm, Jesus can save you too.

As a side note, Jesus' use of saliva was also a challenge to the power structure of the day. It was generally believed that bodily fluids, blood, urine, saliva, could make a person unclean and could harm you. But both the pagan and the Jewish cultures also understood that such things could have medicinal uses in the right hands. That's a whole subject, and I'm new to it; I was reading an ancient medical text to understand it.

But there was an idea that even saliva had medicinal power in the hands of someone with the authority to use it properly. So Jesus using his own saliva, once the leaders heard the mechanics of it, would have unsettled religious leaders who had opinions about whether it was lawful, or whether he had the right to use something that could normally make a person unclean in order to heal someone. We know this kind of thing from bigger moments in Jesus' ministry too. When he touches a leper, he isn't made unclean; the leper is made clean.

There's a reversal of direction as the power of God moves through Jesus' body in his earthly ministry. So the man says, what, do you want to become his disciples? Is that why you keep asking me for details? Verses 28 and 29: "And they reviled him, saying, 'You are his disciple, but we are disciples of Moses. We know that God has spoken to Moses, but as for this man, we do not know where he comes from.'" I preached to you a few weeks ago that their charge was, he can't be the Christ because we do know where he comes from. First he can't be the Christ because we're not supposed to know where the Messiah comes from, and we know where this man comes from.

Now they don't know where he comes from. This is willful blindness. It's willful ignorance. It's whatever I need to say, whatever I need to believe, whatever contradiction I need to hold in my mind, to arrive at what I've already decided: you cannot be the Christ. Verses 30 to 33: "The man answered, 'Why, this is an amazing thing! You do not know where he comes from, and yet he opened my eyes. We know that God does not listen to sinners, but if anyone is a worshiper of God and does his will, God listens to him. Never since the world began has it been heard that anyone opened the eyes of a man born blind. If this man were not from God, he could do nothing.'"

His theology isn't even great there, but he's closer than they are. Verse 34: "They answered him, 'You were born in utter sin, and would you teach us?' And they cast him out." So they make it personal. We already talked about the question asked at the beginning of the passage.

People assumed he was born in utter sin and that this was why he was blind. Now the Pharisees take a dig at him: your blindness was certainly because you were born in utter sin. They're just trying to tear him down. Whatever illusion they tried to build, that they were the religious leaders asking objective questions to determine what had happened, is out the window.

This is clearly personal for them. They attack the man for being born blind, and then they do what his parents were afraid they'd do: they cast him out, most likely out of the synagogue. Now comes the end of the story.

The wording matters. Verse 35: "Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and having found him he said, 'Do you believe in the Son of Man?'" Jesus sees those who are cast out on account of him.

Some of you, in following Jesus, are experiencing rejection, being cast out even by people you long to be close to, whose approval you want, whose affirmation would make you feel secure. You're cast out because of what you claim about Jesus. Jesus hears. He sees that you've been cast out, and having found you, he says, do you believe? He comes to you. And he brings it all together here, because we need to remember that every sign Jesus does in his earthly ministry serves a higher purpose: to signify and certify who he claimed to be.

He isn't only interested in healing one blind man. He's interested in establishing his supremacy over all the earth and declaring to the whole world through these signs exactly who he is: God himself in the flesh, the very Son, the Messiah, the Sent One, the one who takes away the sin of the world. So he says to the man who is seeing him for the first time, "Do you believe in the Son of Man?"

Verses 36 to 38: "He answered, 'And who is he, sir, that I may believe in him?' Jesus said to him, 'You have seen him, and it is he who is speaking to you.' He said, 'Lord, I believe,' and he worshiped him." This is the simple way people are saved.

We can turn it into a complicated debate, but it looks like this. We have to look at the order. What happened first? Did the man believe and then receive his sight, or did he receive his sight and then, seeing, believe? The order matters.

The Lord is the one who pursued him. The Lord is the one who went to him. He couldn't come to Jesus in his own power. He was utterly blind. Jesus came to him.

Jesus pursued him. Jesus healed his eyes, and once he could see, Jesus presented himself to him, the scales gone, able to look at Jesus. And when Jesus tells him, it's me, the man says, of course it's you. He had no ability to do that before. It was all begun and carried out by Jesus for this man.

And his response is exactly what you'd expect: "Lord, I believe," and he worshiped him. Then Jesus says, verse 39, "For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind." The light of the Son, who claims to be the light of the world, shines so brightly that for those who are blind, dead in the trespasses in which they once walked, unable to climb their way to God, who know it, who know their lowly estate and have no hope of climbing to God, the light of Christ shines for them and turns blindness into sight.

But for those who already see, who claim to know the way, to be good enough, pure enough, holy enough, righteous enough, who claim some other way to the good favor of God based on their own merit, for those who claim they can see, the light of the Son shines so brightly that it blinds them. They can't stand to look at it, because to submit to Christ and come to him humbly as a beggar, not as someone who has earned right standing with God, would mean laying down what they've been clinging to for their righteousness, and they won't do it. Every single person in the world stands in one of these two camps. Either you are blind and the light of Christ makes you see, or you can see and the light of Christ blinds you.

It blinds you further. I take this idea from Romans 1, starting in verse 16. I'll move quickly. Paul writes, "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek. For in it the righteousness of God is revealed from faith for faith, as it is written, 'The righteous shall live by faith.' For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men, who by their unrighteousness suppress the truth. For what can be known about God is plain to them, because God has shown it to them. For his invisible attributes, namely, his eternal power and divine nature, have been clearly perceived, ever since the creation of the world, in the things that have been made. So they are without excuse."

"For although they knew God, they did not honor him as God or give thanks to him, but they became futile in their thinking, and their foolish hearts were darkened. Claiming to be wise, they became fools, and exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images resembling mortal man and birds and animals and creeping things." And a little further down, verse 28: "And since they did not see fit to acknowledge God, God gave them up to a debased mind to do what ought not to be done. They were filled with all manner of unrighteousness, evil, covetousness, malice. They are full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, maliciousness. They are gossips, slanderers, haters of God, insolent, haughty, boastful, inventors of evil, disobedient to parents, foolish, faithless, heartless, ruthless. Though they know God's righteous decree that those who practice such things deserve to die, they not only do them but give approval to those who practice them."

Every person on this earth has been shown everything they need to see to know that their God is the Creator, that they are created beings, and that these things are evil in his sight. No one is without excuse. Scripture teaches that all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. We have been shown what we need to see to acknowledge that our God is God, and yet people won't.

And when they refuse to see him for who he has shown himself to be, the Lord gives them over to a debased mind; sin has literally corrupted the way they think. We can see it in the Pharisees. At this point they are willfully blind. They have to deny the light, because, as John wrote in chapter 3, people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil, and the light would expose them. But Paul writes in 1 Corinthians 12:3 that "no one can say 'Jesus is Lord' except in the Holy Spirit."

That's the difference, and this is how I'll wrap up. Your confidence that you are not among those who deny God, but among the children of God, is this: that you can confess with your lips that Jesus Christ is Lord. The same Paul who wrote about those given over to a debased mind wrote that to the Corinthians.

Your confidence is this: if Jesus is your Lord, you belong to him, because only the Holy Spirit can make you say it. How do I know my standing before my King? Because I know that Jesus is my Lord. And when someone goes down into the waters of baptism, they're obeying the voice of their Lord.

Jesus said in Matthew 28, "Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you." And the first thing a new disciple obeys is the command to be baptized. So if you receive his words as your Lord's and want to go into those waters and obey him in baptism, that's evidence that the Spirit is alive in you, because you're confessing his lordship. But calling him anything other than Lord means you're still an outsider looking in. So I urge you, for yourself, today: believe the gospel.

And rather than trying to reason your way to it, recognize that you need the Spirit to do this for you, and call on him for help. And for those of us who have received that help, let's carry the message to those who need to hear it, not ashamed of the gospel, because we've seen that it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes, and that the blind can be given their sight. Would you pray with me to that end?
