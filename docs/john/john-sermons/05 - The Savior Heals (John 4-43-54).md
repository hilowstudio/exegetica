# The Savior Heals (John 4:43-54)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 4:43-54

---

Let me get us on track with how we got here. In Samaria, Jesus met a woman who had come to draw water at the well, and he told her, I will give you living water. He revealed himself to her as the Christ. She left her water jar, went back into town and told everyone, "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did. Can this be the Christ?"

Then verse 39: "Many Samaritans from that town believed in him because of the woman's testimony, 'He told me all that I ever did.' So when the Samaritans came to him, they asked him to stay with them, and he stayed there two days. And many more believed because of his word. They said to the woman, 'It is no longer because of what you said that we believe, for we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this is indeed the Savior of the world.'" The Savior of the world.

That's a remarkably successful two days of ministry. Jesus has gone among the Samaritans, a people many Jews despised and avoided, and spent time with them. In just two days the people come to him, not to see signs and wonders, not looking for something spectacular, but to declare that this is the Savior of the world. We've heard him ourselves, and we believe. He has been called the Messiah, and he has rightly been called the Savior of the world. He has received the honor he is due from the people he came to save.

So after two days of that, our passage opens in verse 43: "After the two days he departed for Galilee. (For Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honor in his own hometown.)"

Before I preach this to you, let me name the one big truth we'll work through this morning: Jesus Christ doesn't fit inside the boxes we want to put him in. We'll see him do several things here that don't make much sense to our sensibilities. He has just had tremendous success in ministry. He is receiving the honor and glory he is due. People are rightly calling him the Messiah and the Savior. And after two days of that, he says, I'm leaving for Galilee. Why? Because Jesus himself had testified that a prophet has no honor in his own hometown. What?

If you're like me, you read that and think, so Samaria must be his hometown, and he's leaving because he isn't getting the honor he deserves there. But Samaria isn't his hometown. Galilee is. Jesus is from Nazareth, in the south of Galilee. He's saying, I'm leaving the place where I'm receiving the honor I deserve. Why? Because I need to go home, where I will receive no honor. Jesus is willingly leaving the place where he is honored to go to his hometown, where he has said in advance he will not be honored.

That's upside down, and Jesus does a lot of upside-down things. Among people who didn't really know him, who had no familiarity with him or with the laws of God, who were outside the inner circle of God's people, people respond to him rightly. And he says, I'm going back home, to the people who know me, who have seen me before, who know the laws and precepts of my Father, and I won't be honored there.

As we get started, it's important to recognize that Jesus, in his earthly ministry and still today, is primarily serving the will of the Father, and wrapped up within his service to the Father is his service to us. Let me say that again. Jesus is primarily serving the will of the Father, and wrapped up within that is his service to mankind. The heretical reversal we are prone to believe is that Jesus primarily serves the will of mankind, and wrapped within that is his service to the Father: that Jesus exists to serve our will, what we ask of him, want from him and demand of him, and somewhere in there he fulfills a few things for the Father, as if he did some things for his Father for our sake. It's the other way around. Jesus is carrying out the will of the one who sent him, and wrapped up in that is his love and care and service to us.

Why does this matter? Think about your own life. If you're like me, and I know you are, how can we tell when we've got this backwards, when we're believing that Jesus exists first to serve the will of man and only second to serve the Father? Watch what happens when you ask something of Jesus, pray for something, beg him for something, demand something, or expect him to do or say or act the way you've asked. When he doesn't, what conclusion do you move to next? That's the diagnostic. If, when Jesus doesn't give you what you want, you conclude, Jesus, you must not be here; Jesus, you must not be hearing me; Jesus, you must not be able; or Jesus, you're not being a very good Jesus right now, then you believe Jesus exists primarily to serve the will of man. Nowhere in that list is the possibility that you're not getting what you ask for because Jesus doesn't serve the will of man. That the problem is on our side.

Back to Galilee. After two days of success, he leaves for Galilee because a prophet has no honor in his hometown. This is going after dishonor. He is seeking the place where he will be rejected, despised and dishonored. That's why he goes, and that's what the text says.

"So when he came to Galilee, the Galileans welcomed him, having seen all that he had done in Jerusalem at the feast. For they too had gone to the feast." What? He just said he was going home to Galilee, where a prophet receives no honor. And when he gets to Galilee, they welcome him, because they had seen all he did in Jerusalem at the feast.

We have to go back to that feast. This is why we move through the whole book in order. After the wedding at Cana, Jesus goes into the temple at Passover, drives out the money-changers and merchants, overturns the tables, and then does many signs. And at the end of that passage it says that many believed in his name when they saw the signs he was doing. "But Jesus on his part did not entrust himself to them, because he knew all people and needed no one to bear witness about man, for he himself knew what was in man." The people at the feast who saw his signs and began to believe, thinking, this man is the real deal, are exactly the people Jesus did not entrust himself to. Those who put their faith in him because of signs and wonders are not the kind of people Jesus entrusts himself to.

Now he comes back to Galilee, and these same people welcome him. Hey, it's our boy, he's back! The guy who does the signs and the miracles, our buddy from our town. We were there the day he did the thing. Jesus, what tricks do you have up your sleeve today? They welcome and celebrate him as a miracle worker, but they don't yield to him as the Son of God and the Savior of the world. They don't declare, as the Samaritans did, this is the Messiah, the Savior of the world. To them he's just our boy Jesus.

Hear me correctly: this welcome is dishonor. Crowding around Jesus to get near him so that by association I can befriend power, attach myself to this hometown hero and tell stories about how I was there when he did the cool stuff, is dishonor. Why? Because we've already seen in chapter one who this Jesus is. The book opens, "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. By the power of his word, Jesus created everything. So when you look around and wonder what your sign is that Jesus has power, here's your sign: all of this is his handiwork. And chapter one also says, "He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him. He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him." We're watching that come to pass. Instead of looking at everything around them and seeing his handiwork, they say, show me a trick so I can believe.

So what does he do? "So he came again to Cana in Galilee, where he had made the water wine." He goes deeper in, to ground zero of his public miracles, where all the excitement started, to the very same people. Jesus is turning things on their head. He refuses to be celebrated as a celebrity or as a mere miracle worker who can meet your needs, a man you buddy up to so he'll do something for you or serve your purposes. He goes deeper and deeper into that crowd, and in a minute they'll receive a rebuke from him.

But first, let's talk about the danger of the hometown. None of us is from Galilee, but we can fall into the trap of thinking of ourselves as Jesus' hometown folks. Not the Samaritan, the outsider Jesus had to go to the ends of the earth to pursue, but his hometown crowd: Jesus is our boy, Jesus is our guy. The text shows us the danger of thinking that way: domesticating Jesus, pretending you're one of him and he's one of you in a way he never meant when he said we are one, making him fully man but denying that he is fully God.

I've known people who attended a megachurch whose famous pastor was later removed from ministry. The way they talked about it: hey, this guy is my pastor, I go to this church. As if the association were a badge of honor, proof that I'm in the inner circle, the crowd that really gets it, the people who really know Jesus. It's dangerous, and we do a version of that with Jesus when we get too comfortable.

In evangelical circles like ours, we often want to box Jesus into this book. We write systematic theologies, and I love them, but we write them five times as long as the Bible, mining everything God has revealed about himself and writing it all down: okay, it's settled. God, this is who you are, this is what you're allowed to do, and this is how I understand you. And we can't let Jesus break out of that box, because that's deeply uncomfortable for us. We want to domesticate him. Others, like the people in his hometown, just want to reduce him to a miracle worker, chasing signs and symbols while denying that everything around them is his handiwork and that he is God himself. It's dangerous to be hometown folks in either way.

And let me warn you: if you run after signs and believe because of them, you will believe anyone who can show you a sign. Someone claims to predict the future and nails it: that's my guy. Someone does something you can't explain. You know who loves signs and wonders? Your enemy. He loves them because they can deceive.

"And at Capernaum there was an official whose son was ill." The word for official is basilikos, a royal official. The only royalty in the region is Herod Antipas, who will behead John the Baptist. So most likely this is an official of Herod's court. It's possible he was a Roman official, but more likely he served Herod. Either way, he belongs to the powers that will one day reject Jesus. And his son is ill.

"When this man heard that Jesus had come from Judea to Galilee, he went to him and asked him to come down and heal his son, for he was at the point of death. So Jesus said to him, 'Unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe.'"

A heartbroken father seeks out Jesus and asks him to heal his dying son, and what does Jesus say? Unless you see signs and wonders, you will not believe. Lest you tame him, put him in a box, domesticate him or figure him out as just a nice guy. In Greek you can see that the "you" is plural. In English it's harder to see. He's saying, unless y'all see signs and wonders, y'all will not believe. He's speaking to the man and to the crowd huddled around him, clamoring for a sign. Not a very nice Jesus.

"The official said to him, 'Sir, come down before my child dies.'" He doesn't argue. He just asks again. You are my only hope. Please, just come. Maybe I don't want to get caught up in all of that. Just come.

Did Jesus go with him as he asked? No. He tells him something else: "Go; your son will live." And "the man believed the word that Jesus spoke to him and went on his way." This is the same structure we saw in Samaria. The people believed the word of the woman, then spent time with Jesus and said, we no longer believe because of what you said; we've heard him ourselves. This man believed the word of Jesus himself, Jesus the Word, not Jesus the miracle worker, not Jesus' reputation for doing cool stuff. Jesus suddenly has to be someone who can heal his son with a word from about fifteen miles away. That's very different from a miracle worker.

When Jesus says, go, I'm not coming, it means there will be no razzmatazz, no spitting in the mud, no theatrics. Jesus just says, go, he'll be fine. And now this father of a dying child has to walk the miles back home on foot, trusting that the man he sought out and heard about can heal his son just by saying go.

"As he was going down, his servants met him and told him that his son was recovering. So he asked them the hour when he began to get better, and they said to him, 'Yesterday at the seventh hour the fever left him.' The father knew that was the hour when Jesus had said to him, 'Your son will live.' And he himself believed, and all his household."

We read in verse 50 that the man believed the word Jesus spoke to him. Then in verse 53 we read that he himself believed, and all his household. What does that mean? If we can't grasp it, we'll get our own faith wrong. It means he believed again. He believed, and then he believed again. That is the nature of the Christian life. As Jesus keeps revealing himself to be who he is, we believe and then believe again. And as we keep believing, our households are caught up in it.

So the hometown of Jesus will be shaped not by great public signs for all to see, but by this: a man from the royal court of Herod, or of Rome, comes and falls at his feet, Jesus doesn't even go with him, and the man goes home privately and finds his son made well. Jesus is the God who heals by the power of his word from fifteen miles away. And hear it: fifteen miles is no different, for Jesus, from the distance between heaven and earth today, as he sits at the right hand of the Father. Jesus needs only to will a thing, and he can do it here in this world for you and for me.

So here's the question. When this man goes to Jesus, does he go cleaned up? Is he trying to create a space where Jesus will come in and do what he wants? Is he making himself lord? Does he believe Jesus exists primarily to serve the will of man or the will of the Father? He has no claim to this miracle. When he asks, Jesus says, unless you see signs you won't believe; you people just want signs. Jesus is frustrated with them. He wants them to see him for who he is. This man has no right to this miracle. And what does he say? Please. Please. And Jesus does it. He heals the man's son from fifteen miles away. He speaks a word and it is done, for an undeserving man. He speaks a word and the Samaritans are saved, an undeserving people.

Talk about turning things on their head. How often do we try to rebuild things for Jesus so that his favor will come on us? Be honest. If we clean ourselves up, if we can just get back to being a Christian nation, a Christian household, if we can do things the right way, honor God with our actions and obey the law, if we can make this place bright enough that the light will come and walk among us, what are we doing? The light leaves the places that think they have believed in him and goes into the darkness so that people there will believe in him. We clean ourselves up for him as if God waited until we got it right to pour out his favor. But the light shines in the darkness. Tell me which Samaritan asked Jesus to come and bring revival to Samaria. Tell me what they were doing right to deserve it. Tell me what this official was doing right to deserve healing in his home, to have his household, from the court of Herod or of Rome, adopted into the family of Christ. No. Jesus walks into dark places, and we ought to be going into dark places with this message.

Let me stay here with you for a minute. You have things that are at the point of death. This man had no claim on Jesus, no claim on a miracle, no claim to have Jesus bend to his will. He heard that Jesus had come, went to him, asked, believed, and then believed again. So what is the dead or dying thing in your life right now, the thing at the point of death? Hearing this, go to him. You know where he is seated. You know where to find him. Go to the Son. Ask. Believe. And believe again.

Recently a fellow pastor asked me about my wife. She suffers from chronic illness. He asked, "Do you pray for her healing?" It was just brotherly conversation. I said, no, I think I stopped praying for that a long time ago. I pray for her comfort. I pray for her healing one day. But I don't really pray for her permanent healing anymore. And he said, can we pray for that right now? I had asked God for it, and he didn't say yes when and how I wanted. And because I wanted him to serve me rather than me to serve him, I had concluded: you must not hear me, or you must not be able, or you must not be good. And I don't like thinking about those things, so I'm not going to talk to you about that anymore, God. I'm a pastor, telling you that.

So I have to believe these things are happening in your heart too. It takes courage to let Jesus be who he actually is, to believe he serves the will of the Father first. When you ask him for something and he doesn't give it, it means what you asked for was not the will of the Father. If you have a problem with his answer, your problem is with the will of the Father, because Jesus always serves the Father's will first, and in serving it, he is always serving you. Can I believe that? Can you? Can I believe it again? Can I ask again? Jesus didn't say yes to this official at first. He rebuked him: unless you see signs and wonders, you will not believe. And what did the man do? He asked again, and Jesus said yes.

So I want to encourage you: the thing you stopped asking him for, ask him again. Ask him again and again. If you receive a rebuke, ask again.

We live at a different point in history from this man. From a signs-and-wonders perspective, many of us would love to see a pillar of cloud and fire moving through the wilderness. But I've heard it said, and I believe it, that the Hebrews in the wilderness would gladly have traded that pillar of cloud and fire for a crucified Jesus who paid the ransom for the sins of the world, his Holy Spirit living in them and his very word in their hands. All the signs and wonders we could ever ask for have been poured out richly on us, and the one who does those signs lives in you. At this point in history it makes no sense for that official to have had more courage than you to come before the Son. If you rightly believe that the Spirit lives in you and that you have open access to the throne of grace, you will call on this Jesus, who needs only to will a thing and he does it. Talk to that Jesus. Talk to him today. What broken thing would he have you bring to him again today, believing he can make it right? Our Jesus is that good.

So I invite you into that this morning. Some of you know what the people around you have been asking the Lord for through long suffering. If you do, don't only pray for yourself. Go find a brother or sister who has been praying through many tears for a long time, and pray for that man or woman today. Believe together. Talk to the Jesus who is like this. Break free of the club, of the hometown mentality, of thinking too small of Jesus. Let him be who he actually is, because he is better than you think. He's better than I think. Let's pray.
