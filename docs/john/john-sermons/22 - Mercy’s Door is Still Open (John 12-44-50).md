# Mercy’s Door is Still Open (John 12:44-50)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 12:44-50

---

If you're anything like me, raised in a non-Christian home or in a Christian home with questionable doctrine, you were probably taught growing up, outright or more quietly, that we come into this world clean. At worst you come into the world as a blank canvas, able to develop toward good or evil; at best you come into the world pure, undefiled and innocent. Then over time the influence of the world gets a grip on you and steals your innocence. We see it in some of our common expressions.

We want to preserve the innocence of our children. We want to preserve their purity, as if that's their natural state. So it's something out there, something around us, the corrupt world, that gets into us some time after we're born, and we join the rest of the world in its corruption. In that, I'd say, we're rejecting, or misunderstanding, or have an underdeveloped theology of what's called original sin: the sinful state we inherit from our first parents, Adam and Eve, in the garden. Human beings were created to glorify God perfectly and to reflect him, and when we rejected him in the garden, the Lord placed curses on the human race, on our fallen nature.

In rejecting him in the garden, we were cast into a state of sin. We have a sinful nature, and that nature governs everything we do, so that when you sin, you're doing what comes naturally to you. It's not that you're corrupted by the world. It's that you are the world.

You are the world. The world is corrupted because of you. You're not corrupted because of it. Do you understand?

Well, jeez, Adam. Great intro. Why are you leading with something so heavy?

Why come in so hot? I'm coming in hot with this heavy beginning because if we don't get our eyes on our starting position, a really solid foundation, which we're supposed to have after walking through twelve chapters of John, then what we read this morning won't land as solidly as I want it to. I want the words of Jesus to land solidly on you this morning. And for them to be as wonderful as they are,

and to hit as hard as they're meant to, we have to understand that we needed salvation. We've been going straight through this book since we started, because I want us to really know it. But when I read, "And Jesus cried out," my first instinct was to ask, to whom? If you weren't reading this book one Sunday at a time, but reading it straight through, you'd remember what we read two weeks ago, and I recapped it a little last week. Verses 36 and 37 say, "When Jesus had said these things, he departed and hid himself from them. Though he had done so many signs before them, they still did not believe in him."

Over the last several weeks we've seen a progression. Jesus makes a last appeal to the people in Jerusalem: I am the light; come to me while I'm still among you. He calls on his doubters and those who reject him to repent and come to him while he's still here. Then it says he departed from them. Then last week I preached John's aside, where he explains the unbelief of the people. Jesus wasn't there for any of that. Jesus had departed. And while he's gone, John pauses to say, let me explain why he was rejected like this.

Then this week opens with, "And Jesus cried out." The simple answer, the more boring but accurate one, is that since John knows he cried out, John was probably there. The disciples were probably there. But John gives us no context: who was there, whom he said it to, why he said it, when he said it. Since next week we move into the upper room,

where Jesus shares a meal with his closest friends, it's possible he said this to them on the way to that meal, or in the upper room itself. But it's guesswork. The reason John puts this declaration here without context is that the passage isn't context-specific or context-dependent. These words aren't for particular people on a particular day for a particular reason. They're here for you.

These are, in fact, the very last words of Jesus Christ's public ministry. The very last. After this he withdraws, and then comes the cross.

After that he'll rise. After that he'll ascend. But his earthly life and ministry as the God-man walking around doing signs, teaching, rebuking the Pharisees and the Sadducees, all that we've seen, is done.

And this crying out is the last of it: the last recorded words of Jesus Christ's public ministry and teaching in John. So it comes as no surprise to me.

It shouldn't surprise you that what we meet in this declaration at the end of his public ministry is a summary of the teaching of Jesus. For anyone who struggles to summarize the message of Jesus Christ and his life on earth, who struggles to share the truth about Jesus with a friend, who struggles to believe the whole of Jesus' teaching in your own life: he found it sufficient to sum it up in four or five statements here, one paragraph. This would be an incredible place to turn to get a big-picture grasp of the whole teaching of Jesus' public life and ministry. If that doesn't get you excited for what he has to say, I don't know what will. Verses 44 and 45: "And Jesus cried out and said, 'Whoever believes in me, believes not in me but in him who sent me. And whoever sees me sees him who sent me.'" It's the loudest, most repeated teaching of Jesus Christ's public life and ministry.

Say it with me: I and the Father are one. He has said it so many times in his ministry that it doesn't surprise me at all that it's the first thing he says in the last public paragraph of his life. I and the Father are one. I am God.

He is one with the Father; he is God. We read it in John 8:58, when Jesus said, "Before Abraham was, I am." He says it again and again. It's in the very introduction to this Gospel; I've quoted it for you several times: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made."

In John 14 he says, "Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father. How can you say, 'Show us the Father'?" And Paul writes in Colossians 1:15, "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation. For by him all things were created, in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or rulers or authorities—all things were created through him and for him."

"And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together. And he is the head of the body, the church. He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, that in everything he might be preeminent. For in him all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell, and through him to reconcile to himself all things, whether on earth or in heaven, making peace by the blood of his cross." The same letter, chapter 2, verse 9:

"For in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily, and you have been filled in him, who is the head of all rule and authority." You can practically close your eyes, flip to a page and point, and you'll read in some form a declaration of the majesty of Jesus Christ, of his unity with the Father, of his Godhood. And some of you are thinking, man, Adam, it feels like every week you're up here telling me Jesus is God. But I know you don't believe it with your whole being, because I know I don't believe it with my whole being, and here's the proof. There's plenty of proof.

Here's the big one I see when we talk honestly together. We start believing we need to add to the work of Christ to be made right with God. We start believing we need to add to the work of Christ, who is God, to make ourselves right with God. And the root unbelief there is that Jesus isn't God. Otherwise we're saying God isn't satisfied with the work God has done to make people right with God.

God has found something lacking in what he has done to save you. That's, I was going to say stupid. I've been slinging insults up here for weeks. I'll try not to. Try not to.

It's silly. When we pray, when we close our eyes and picture the Christ, the Son, we bring different attributes to mind than when we close our eyes and picture the Father. Don't we? Think about it. Are they one?

Really? Because it was he who said it, not me: if you've seen me, you've seen the Father. Is your understanding of God shaped by your looking at the Son? This is no small doctrine, and it can't be repeated too often.

It's at the heart of everything else we believe: that Jesus is himself God. Whoever believes in me believes not in me but in him who sent me, and whoever sees me sees him who sent me. Verse 46: "I have come into the world as light, so that whoever believes in me may not remain in darkness." Here we go with the light again.

We've been talking about Jesus as the light almost every week for twelve chapters. Jesus really likes to talk about this. Why? There's something important here, which goes back to my introduction.

"May not remain in darkness." The key word in this passage for me is "remain." Focus on it: remain in darkness. Jesus doesn't show up shining light while a devil with pointy horns over here urges you into darkness, and you stand on neutral ground in between, choosing between light and darkness. That's a false story.

It isn't the truth. Until the light shines on human hearts, you remain in darkness. It's your starting position, your default position. You are in darkness.

You were dead in the trespasses in which you once walked, but you've been made alive, joined with Christ in his resurrection. And it's his light that changed that for you, that pierced the darkness so that you would not remain in it. All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. Your starting position was darkness, and you were powerless to bring yourself out of it. There was nothing you could do.

The light had to shine into the darkness. And Jesus says he came into the world as light, so that whoever believes would not remain in darkness. But here's the beauty of the light of the Son shining into human hearts to gather a remnant, to form a church, to ransom a people for himself. What happens when you turn off a light?

It goes dark again, doesn't it? Darkness is the default, and you have to do something to light a room. If you stop, if you flip the switch, it goes back to darkness. Jesus did not shine his light into the hearts of the church to light things up for you temporarily, so you could get a sense of how things are and then become your own light, imitating his light from then on and trying to keep the light on yourself. Remove the Christ, and you remove the light, and you go back to darkness.

And human hands have done, and keep doing, everything they can to put out this light. We can drive nails into his wrists and ankles. We can take the breath out of his lungs. We can bloody him and nail him to a cross and mock him, and it only makes the light burn brighter for the world. The grave could not keep him. Nothing could put out this light.

He came into the world as light, so that whoever believes would not remain in darkness. Your security, your ability to stay in the light, doesn't depend on your ability to keep the light on. He is the light that cannot be put out, burning for you. Verse 47: "If anyone hears my words and does not keep them, I do not judge him; for I did not come to judge the world but to save the world."

That's interesting, because Jesus has had a lot to say about judgment in John, hasn't he? And here he says, if anyone hears my words and does not keep them, I do not judge him. We'd expect him to say, if anyone hears my words and does keep them, I don't judge him. Wouldn't we?

Or is that just me? If anyone hears my words and does not keep them, I do not judge him, for I did not come to judge the world but to save the world. Good news.

We've moved from one, Jesus is God; to two, he has come as light to bring people out of darkness into light; to three, he did not come into the world to judge it but to save it. Well, if he isn't going to judge those who hear his words and don't keep them, who exactly is being judged? Don't forget that when he speaks these words, he's days away from being nailed to a cross with sin on his shoulders. The full judgment and wrath of God is about to be poured out on the Son.

So who exactly is judged here, if Jesus didn't come to judge those who hear his words and don't keep them? You. You were the one who was judged. At Calvary the wrath of God was not poured out on unbelievers. At Calvary the wrath of God was poured out on those who would believe, whose sins, whose sinful nature, whose very selves were nailed to Christ and taken into the grave with him, so that they might take up new life.

What does that mean? There are much better passages for me to preach this idea from, and I'll try to show some restraint. I know this is big.

If this rattles you, come and talk to me, but we have to think it through. I'm not speaking figuratively, and neither was Paul when he wrote about being joined with Christ in his death. A couple of weeks ago we talked about the seed that falls into the earth and dies and bears much fruit, and about being united with him in a death like his. I'm not speaking figuratively. Let's try to picture it.

Here's a word picture. He did not look at you, remove your sin from you, leave you behind as a blank canvas, and take just your sin to the cross so you'd have a fresh chance. He took you. He took you to the cross with him.

This is a hard doctrine, mainly because of our underdeveloped theology of bodies and spirits, which is why I have to hold my tongue; that's a whole separate sermon. But here's the idea. One day you're going to die. What will happen to your body? It will be cremated.

Or you'll be buried in the ground and it will decay into dust. Your body will be gone. And yet, just as Jesus told the thief on the cross, "today you will be with me in paradise," somehow you, whatever "you" means once your body is gone, will be with Jesus.

Without your body. Well, I'll get my new body. Not right away; that's another sermon. You'll receive your glorified body at the resurrection of the church, at the second coming of Christ, when he brings heaven to earth, establishes the new heaven and the new earth and restores all things. Until we receive our glorified bodies, we have not fully received the promises that have been made.

We'll be with Jesus in perfect harmony and perfect delight. And yet part of us will still be waiting for him to come into his kingdom, so that we can take up our glorified bodies. Because as a human being you were made both body and spirit. You need both to be complete.

It's what you were made for. Here's a picture: think of the garden. First God formed Adam from the dust, and then what did he do? He breathed the breath of life into his nostrils, and the man became a living creature.

Your humanity is your body, formed and fashioned, joined with the breath of life from God. You are both body and spirit, and both must die, and both must rise again. They don't both happen at the same time. Your spiritual death happened at Calvary. Your old self died there.

Judgment was poured out on the corrupt sinful nature that was yours, and you were made a new creation. A right spirit was renewed within you. The Holy Spirit bears witness with your spirit that you are now a child of God, a heart of stone made a heart of flesh. We're talking about the actual death of the sinful, corrupt human spirit, replaced with a new creation, a resurrected spirit. So when you die your bodily death, your body is just catching up with what has already happened to your soul.

Your body joins your soul in its actual death and waits for its resurrection. Otherwise, what's so great about heaven? Where was I going with that? "If anyone hears my words and does not keep them, I do not judge him; for I did not come to judge the world but to save the world." Here's where I was going: the death that happened at Calvary, the judgment poured out on sin, was for you.

It was for you. So who is it not for? Verse 48. We're talking about two comings. This is one of them.

Verse 48: "The one who rejects me and does not receive my words has a judge; the word that I have spoken will judge him on the last day." This is the nature of the Messiah's comings. Christ Jesus came the first time as the suffering Messiah to open the way of salvation, to give up his life as the atoning sacrificial Lamb and take it up again, so that the church might be ransomed from the consequences of sin, from the penalty of sin and death.

That was the first coming. He came as the Lamb. When he comes back, he comes as the Lion. "The one who rejects me and does not receive my words has a judge; the word that I have spoken will judge him on the last day."

One reason we celebrate that our judgment has already happened is that the judgment coming next is far more frightening. When the wrath and judgment of God the Father were poured out on you, you were nailed to the Savior, and the Savior took up his life again and brought you with him. You received resurrection. On the last day, those who receive the judgment of God will not be nailed to the Savior. Their sin will be on their own shoulders.

They will answer for their sin without the Mediator. And Jesus had a great deal to say about that. We don't like preaching it, but in Luke 16 Jesus calls hell "this place of torment." In Mark 9:43 he says it's a place of unquenchable fire. In 9:48 he says it's where the worm does not die.

In Matthew 13:42 he says it's a place of weeping and gnashing of teeth, of anguish and regret. In Luke 16 he says it's a place from which there is no return, not even to warn your loved ones. In Matthew 25 he calls it the outer darkness, where your surroundings match your insides. In Matthew 10 he calls it Gehenna, the name of the Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem, where Israel's kings once burned their own children in sacrifice, and which later Jewish writers describe as a place where the city's refuse burned and maggots bred. Jesus had a great deal to say about what awaits those who face judgment without his mediation.

And it's a necessary part of the gospel story. Jesus is the light that pierces the darkness. Jesus is patient and enduring, steadfast in his love, waiting until the last of those he is ransoming is added to the church, until the last one he is bringing out of darkness into light comes in. People are still being saved. If Jesus isn't back yet, then the work isn't done yet.

If Jesus isn't back yet, then people are still being brought out of the domain of darkness into the kingdom of light. And about eternal life, remember what Jesus said in John 10:27: "My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me. I give them eternal life, and they will never perish." That's what he means when he says here that he has not spoken on his own authority.

Verses 49 and 50: "For I have not spoken on my own authority, but the Father who sent me has himself given me a commandment—what to say and what to speak. And I know that his commandment is eternal life. What I say, therefore, I say as the Father has told me." The words of Jesus recorded from his earthly life and ministry are eternal life.

He spoke what the Father commanded him, he and the Father being one. His message is life. The gospel is life. And he said of his sheep, my sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me, and I give them eternal life, and they will never perish.

And a couple of chapters later, in John 14:1, Jesus says, "Let not your hearts be troubled. Believe in God; believe also in me. In my Father's house are many rooms. If it were not so, would I have told you that I go to prepare a place for you? And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself, that where I am you may be also."

Eternal life is real. Jesus actually died for your actual sin. He actually rose for your actual life, so that you would dwell with him in an actual heaven for an actual eternity. And actual people are actually dying with their actual sin on their actual selves, and the actual judgment of the actual Jesus will actually fall on them.

And they will pay for their actual sin for an actual eternity in an actual hell. Do we believe it? What joy, on the one hand, that you and I were spared, by the grace of God alone, from answering for our sin. And what a horrifying prospect for those who spit him out, who love the glory that comes from man more than the glory that comes from God. In Revelation 21 we read what awaits those who are found in him, compared with what awaits those who are not. John writes, "Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more."

"And I saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, 'Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them as their God.'" Amen? "'He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more,'"

"'neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away.'" Do you believe it? "And he who was seated on the throne said, 'Behold, I am making all things new.'" Do you believe it?

"Also he said, 'Write this down, for these words are trustworthy and true.' And he said to me, 'It is done! I am the Alpha and the Omega, the beginning and the end. To the thirsty I will give from the spring of the water of life without payment. The one who conquers will have this heritage,'"

"'and I will be his God and he will be my son.'" Do you believe it? Verse 22 of the same chapter: "And I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb. And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it, for the glory of God gives it light, and its lamp is the Lamb. By its light will the nations walk, and the kings of the earth will bring their glory into it, and its gates will never be shut by day—and there will be no night there."

"They will bring into it the glory and the honor of the nations." This light that pierces the darkness, the light Jesus came into the world to be, will be the light we walk by in eternity with him. We'll need no sun or moon, because his glory will be our light.

The glory and honor of the nations will be brought into it. He is still saving people from every tribe, tongue and nation. He is gathering his church from the corners of the earth. He is saving a dead and dying people. And the way your perfect, holy, all-knowing God has seen fit to do it is through the church.

The church is to spread the gospel to the corners of the earth, starting with your home, your spouse, your kids, your neighbor, your coworker. Paul writes in 2 Corinthians 2:14, "But thanks be to God, who in Christ always leads us in triumphal procession, and through us spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of him everywhere. For we are the aroma of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing, to one a fragrance from death to death, to the other a fragrance from life to life."

And Romans 1:16: "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes." We're not playing house here. This is no trifling thing. This is no small thing. We're talking about eternal matters. It's fitting that these are the parting words of Jesus' public ministry. So as you go and respond to him in obedience to the Great Commission, to spread this gospel, this light, this message of salvation, I encourage you to turn to these verses.

John 12:44 to the end. What do you need to know to share the gospel? Jesus is God. His judgment has not yet been poured out on unbelievers. Mercy's door is still open. It's not too late.

It's not too late to repent and turn to him. See the grace of Jesus continuing his appeal. But there is a real day of judgment coming, and it will be his word that separates the sheep from the goats. And all that will matter on that day, when the Father looks at you in the hour of his judgment, is whether you'll have the record of Christ's perfect life, death and resurrection read over you:

"Well done, good and faithful servant." You've been covered in the blood of the Lamb. Or will you have the record of your own life and deeds read over you, and be found wanting before a holy God? These are hard things to say, and yet they were life to you. They were life to me, and they are life to a great multitude whom our Father knows.

We ought to be so encouraged. When I was about ten, I went to a church with a fire-and-brimstone Baptist preacher. I didn't hear a lot of hell preached outside that church, to tell you the truth, but in that church it was as if hell were the only thing in the Bible. He'd go on and on, and he would say, and this is bad theology, that in heaven we will all have blood on our hands for the people we were supposed to give the gospel to but didn't.

That's not true. And that's why it's so important that we read straight through the whole Gospel, and why we're taking it slow. Jesus said no one can come to the Father except through him.

No one can know the Father except through him. And no one can come to him unless the Father gives that person to him, and if the Father gives you to him, you will come to him. That isn't only a description of how salvation works.

It's an assurance that Jesus will ransom his church. It's as sure as the sunrise. So take part in what he's going to do. He will save. He will ransom.

Will you obey? Do you love God more than you love your comfort? Will you be the church? Hear me. I never want to be heavy-handed up here, but hear me anyway.

You're only here instead of there, here instead of with him, because the work isn't done, because he's still saving, because he hasn't closed the book of life or brought his judging sword on the nations, because he is still patiently enduring and ransoming his church, and he said he would do it through his body. So if we're not doing that, what are we doing? I just want to urge you to boldness. I am not ashamed of the gospel.

It is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes. Let's pray for that boldness together.
