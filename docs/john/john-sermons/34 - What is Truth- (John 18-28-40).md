Well, something, or we wouldn't have brought him to you. We're trying to get back to the Passover. There are a couple of things I want to point out. The first is that they were concerned about defilement. They wouldn't enter the governor's headquarters because it was a Gentile residence, and they believed that entering a Gentile's residence would make them ceremonially unclean, and then they couldn't take part in the Passover.

So they're concerned with their ability to go and take part, once they're done with this, in the feast that remembers the Lord passing over them in Egypt. Let's hurry up and kill this innocent man so we can go and enjoy our feast. How could they be so preoccupied with staying ceremonially clean before God while making a mockery of what God's word says about justice? Is it so surprising, church? We're not above being preoccupied with our ceremonial cleanliness, our outward religious observances, while inwardly we're wicked, blind, even deaf to the purity we need inside, the cleansing we need on the inside, because we're so sure that on the outside we're doing everything right. We're good boys. We're good girls. Surely Dad is proud of us, because we keep the rules. For some of you, your righteousness is little more than your church attendance.

I also want to point out that Pilate comes out to them, and we need a little history on Pilate.

Pilate was the Roman prefect over the region of Judea. Sometimes Christians use the term "Bible stories." It's a fine term, but for me it can carry a hint of fiction. We're talking about historical men and historical events, real people in real time standing on real ground in real history. And a lot is known about Pontius Pilate outside the Bible.

He shows up in a number of historical works about this era in the Near East. As the Roman governor of Judea, he had military, judicial and financial responsibilities and commanded the Roman troops in the province. And something he did that those before him hadn't done: he brought the Roman standards into the holy city. He had military standards bearing the images of Caesar and the emblems of the Roman Empire brought into Jerusalem. The prefects before him hadn't done that, in order to keep the peace with the Jews. But he insisted.

The Jewish historian Josephus tells the story in his history of the Jewish War. The Jews sent a delegation to Caesarea, where Pontius Pilate was based, and appealed to him for five days to remove these Roman standards from their holy city. On the sixth day, Pilate had his soldiers surround the crowd with swords drawn: accept the Roman standards, or die.

And the Jews fell to their knees, bared their necks and said, we would rather die than have these images in our holy city. They called his bluff, and he removed the standards. Not long after, history records that Pilate took money from the sacred treasury to build an aqueduct. Luke records that Pilate mingled the blood of some Galileans with their sacrifices. Many think this happened at an earlier Passover, when worshipers slaughtered their own lambs.

If so, at a previous Passover Pilate had Galileans slain as they were making their sacrifices, so that their blood was mixed with the blood of their sacrifices. But the incident I find most interesting as we look at this exchange between the Jewish leaders and Pilate is when he set up shields bearing the name of the emperor Tiberius in Herod's former palace in Jerusalem. Herod's sons objected, and so did the Jews, and they wrote to Tiberius begging him to have the shields taken down. Tiberius was furious with Pilate and wrote back ordering him to remove the shields to the temple of Augustus in Caesarea. So, by many accounts, Pilate was already falling out of favor with Rome, and he had always been out of favor with the Jews.

Depending on whether you've heard these texts preached before, Pilate is sometimes presented as a man morally torn over what to do. I don't get that picture at all. I see a man obsessed with his security, his position in Rome and his ambition, who hates the Jews, has always hated the Jews, and who here is walking on thin ice. And the Jewish leaders know it, because they've had some victories. They've called him out twice and won.

Once, when he brought the Roman standards into Jerusalem and threatened to kill them, they stared him down. And again, when they wrote to Tiberius and Tiberius overruled him. So they bring Jesus before him, and they won't come inside. And Pilate, who has no respect for the Jews, respects their custom and comes out to them. We see real weakness in him there.

He's scared, because his position is in jeopardy. So he says, what accusation do you bring against this man? "This was to fulfill the word that Jesus had spoken to show by what kind of death he was going to die." Jesus had to die on a Roman cross. They weren't going to stone him. Because their accusation was heresy and blasphemy, under Jewish law they could have stoned him. But if he's some Jewish king, a religious king in some other sense, Pilate doesn't really care. Jesus doesn't give him that out. Before I comment on the nature of the kingdom of God, which I'll save for a bit, I want to finish understanding what Pilate is going through. Pilate has to keep the peace.
