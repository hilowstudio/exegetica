# The High Priestly Prayer, Part 1 (John 17:1-5)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 17:1-5

---

I've said that this is my favorite chapter in the whole Bible. There are so many reasons, but I want to give you three main ones. Number one: this prayer gives us insight into what Jesus is doing right now. We know from Scripture that Jesus Christ is our Mediator, that he has ascended and is seated at the right hand of the Father, that he is interceding for you and me, praying for us all day long. We know we have a great High Priest who stands in for us.

There is one Mediator between God and man, the Lord Jesus Christ. And this Lord isn't only interceding for us on the day we stand at the judgment seat; he's appealing to the Father for you and me every single day. He prays for you. And this prayer gives us some insight into what those prayers are like. I think that's awesome.

Number two: it gives us insight into the inner life of Jesus Christ, into what he desires and wants. All the Gospel accounts tell us a lot about what he said, they show us how the disciples were confused by what he said, and they record his actions, all of it incredible. But this prayer shows us some of what's going on in the mind of Jesus, in his heart, his deep desires. I think that's awesome.

And third, it's the only prayer like it we have. I love that this is the one they chose to record for us. The Father and the Son said, let's leave this one for them. Jesus was always withdrawing to talk to his Father.

He was always praying, and this is the one they give us. Maybe you'd count "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do," and others like it. But this is a full prayer, a big prayer, the longest of his prayers we have, and I love it. That's why we're taking three weeks to work through it in three segments. So let's jump in, chapter 17, verse 1.

It begins like this: "When Jesus had spoken these words, he lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, 'Father, the hour has come.'" When Jesus had spoken these words: what words? The words we looked at last week. Hopefully you were here; if not, here's a quick recap.

Last week Jesus was talking about childbirth. What? Well, he was talking about turning sorrow into joy.

At the center was verse 20 of chapter 16, where Jesus says, "Truly, truly, I say to you, you will weep and lament, but the world will rejoice. You will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will turn into joy. When a woman is giving birth, she has sorrow because," listen, "her hour has come, but when she has delivered the baby, she no longer remembers the anguish, for joy that a human being has been born into the world." And after he has spoken these words, he lifts his eyes to heaven and says, "Father, the hour has come."

What hour? She has sorrow because her hour has come, but when she has delivered the baby, she no longer remembers the anguish, because new life has been brought forth. Jesus has just promised his disciples that he will turn their sorrow into joy when the hour comes, when joy will burst out through sorrow as life springs out through anguish.

And you'll remember your anguish no more. That hour has come. What hour? This hour. By the next morning, they will crucify Jesus.

That's the hour we're talking about. We've been hearing about this hour all through this Gospel, and in all the Gospels. All the way back at the wedding at Cana, he says, "Woman, what does this have to do with me? My hour has not yet come." The authorities try to seize him.

And John says no one laid a hand on him, because his hour had not yet come. When the demons meet Jesus in Matthew 8, they cry out, "Have you come here to torment us before the time?" The hour has been approaching, but it hasn't yet arrived.

And here Jesus says, the hour has come. "Father, the hour has come." For what? "Glorify your Son that the Son may glorify you."

When Jesus prays for himself, he prays for his own glory, and he's the only one who's allowed to do that. I can't stand up here this morning as Pastor Adam and pray, Father God, glorify me before your people. Only one, without sin and without flaw, can pray "glorify me," and it's the Son. This morning we'll explore a little of why the Son can be singularly focused on his glory in his prayer before his death.

He gives us the first reason: glorify me so that I may glorify you. He isn't saying, let's put on a show. Glorify me so that I can glorify you, Father. Verses 2 to 5: "since you have given him authority over all flesh, to give eternal life to all whom you have given him. And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent. I glorified you on earth, having accomplished the work that you gave me to do. And now, Father, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed."

I love short passages like this, because I can read it, take portions and put them together, and you won't lose your way. Several times he asks God the Father to glorify him, and he gives really two grounds: glorify me because of who I am, and glorify me because of what I've done.

And the chief purpose is that I could glorify you, and that you could glorify me in your presence as it was before the world existed. So I want to take these one at a time. Glorify me because of who I am. Glorify me so that I may glorify you, verse 2, "since you have given him authority over all flesh." Authority over all flesh.

As you walk in the faith, you'll meet people who question or wonder: did Jesus ever claim to be God? Start typing that into a search bar and it will fill itself in for you; it's a very common question. Or a better way to put it: was it Jesus' own understanding of himself that he was one with God? First I want to hold out to you that a day should come in every Christian's life when you never have to ask that question again.

It just jumps off the page over and over, and this is one of the passages where you see it. Authority over all flesh has been given to him. Who has authority over all flesh? God.

So Jesus is either blaspheming in a major way, or he's teaching us something. He claims to have all authority over flesh. Then he doubles down and claims the power to give eternal life. Authority over all flesh and the power to give eternal life: yes, I'd say Jesus understood himself to be God.

And if that's not enough, he says, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed. Before the world existed. He understands something about himself that we have to understand if we're to believe in him. When we talk about Jesus being glorified in God's presence with the glory he had with him before the world existed, we're talking about the aseity of God. It's a concept you'll hear in theological circles. The doctrine of the aseity of God is that nothing gives him his life, and nothing can take it.

He simply is. I know we struggle with this. The doctrine of the Trinity is hard. The doctrine of the eternity of God is hard, because we aren't three persons, and we aren't eternal in that way. We'll live forever, but we had a beginning, so we don't understand eternity.

But as a concept it isn't actually hard to understand. We all rightly understand that every created thing has a beginning, and that all created things are always becoming. Always becoming. Eventually becoming dead, and then decomposing, becoming dirt.

Becoming, becoming, becoming. Beginnings and ends. So we work backward, and the great puzzle of all humanity is this: what is the thing that never became, so that all other things could become from it? What is the uncreated thing?

There must be an origin with no origin. And Jesus says, hey, that's me. So for the Son to be glorified is to glorify the Father, and to glorify the Father is to glorify the Son. That's the chief prayer Christ prays for himself here in his final hour.

The hour has come. The baby is about to be born. The whole creation has been groaning together in the pains of childbirth until now, but now it's time for life to spring forth. Glorify me, so that I may glorify you, Jesus says, because I have authority over all flesh.

That's who I am. Because I have the power to give eternal life. That's who I am. Because I was before the world existed. Glorify me. I have every right to ask it.

And there's only one to whom God the Father will say, let's do it: the one of whom John wrote, "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made. In him was life." John is making this claim about Jesus, and Jesus makes it of himself.

You don't have to trust me, but you can trust Jesus on this. In him is life, the power to give eternal life. So here Jesus claims not only to be the Creator of all physical life, but to be the giver of eternal life. It's in him.

That's why he's worthy of all glory. And then he says, glorify me not only because I have authority over all flesh and give eternal life, but also because I have accomplished the work you gave me to do. Jesus says, I've done it. The very thing you sent me into the world for, when I left the eternal throne I had before the world began and took the form of a baby, the form of a servant, and lowered myself even further, becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross. "Therefore God has highly exalted him and bestowed on him the name that is above every name, so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father."

This Jesus says, I've done it. He prays, I have accomplished the work you gave me to do, and by the next afternoon, on the cross, he will say it again. What will he say? "It is finished."

Finished. What work? Saving work, for you and me. Eternal-life-giving work, for you and me. If Jesus Christ came not to be served but to lay down his life as a ransom for many, I think it's right for us to ask: how is this love?

Let me ask you: how do you know God loves you? You might answer with Scripture: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son."

How is that love? Well, he took the penalty of sin, and the wages of sin is death, so he conquered death for me. And hell. Why is that love? Because hell is terrible. I don't want to go to hell.

He spared me from hell, from wrath, from God's judgment. How is that love? What good does it do you? Well, instead of hell, I get to go to heaven. Okay.

How is that love? We can be doctrinally right, layer upon layer, answering from Scripture all the way down, and end up at heaven, and then heaven is a vague answer. Why is that love? Well, heaven's awesome. My friends will be there.

I've heard the streets are paved with gold. What is it that makes this love? Glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed. Listen to this. Chapter 17, verse 3:

"And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." So if I asked you, what is eternal life, would you turn to John 17 to answer me? What is eternal life? Jesus says it is to know us.

To know us. And I don't mean know about; I mean know. To know us. How did Jesus love you? By bringing you into his presence and bringing his presence into you, by ushering you into the presence of the glory of God.

What was formerly barred to you is now sealed to you. This is eternal life, that they know you, that they know you. So the next time someone asks, how do you know God loves you? Because he sent his Son. How is that love?

Because his Son made him known to me. I'll borrow from next week's passage, where he says, "I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world. Yours they were, and you gave them to me, and they have kept your word. Now they know that everything that you have given me is from you. For I have given them the words that you gave me, and they have received them."

His whole focus, when he turns to pray for his friends, is: I did it. Did what? Made you known to them. I'm talking about known as displayed in the cross.

I'm talking about known as the Holy Spirit takes up residence in you and keeps bearing witness to the things of Christ. All the things he's been teaching. And I'm talking about Jesus risen, seated at the right hand of the Father today, so that one day you won't be asking, how do I know? His presence will be right there.

That's why he can say, glorify me. Saying "glorify me" is the very same thing as saying "love them," because to display his glory before you is to love you. It's to give you eternal life, to bring you into the knowledge of his glory. And we make this a head exercise, because we tend to think about knowledge the way the Greek philosophers did.

And if faith, if knowing God, is mainly something that happens up here in the head, then we've just said a whole lot of people can never know him. I'm not talking about mental assent to some doctrinal truths about God. I'm talking about knowing. I'm talking about him taking over the controls of your life, because the Holy Spirit has sealed you for eternity, and one day you'll behold him with your eyes and walk with him forever. I'm talking about knowing him the way Jesus Christ the Son knows him.

That isn't like knowing a book. It's like a relationship. It's more than a relationship. It's unity. It's one. It's union. It's oneness.

It's knowing him because you have been brought into the life of God. That's stealing from the part where he prays for me, but I'll do it for a second. Verses 24 to 26 of the same chapter. Jesus says, "Father, I desire that they also, whom you have given me, may be with me where I am, to see my glory that you have given me because you loved me before the foundation of the world.

"O righteous Father, even though the world does not know you, I know you, and these know that you have sent me. I made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known, that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them." We're not talking about thinking about something. We're talking about something being in you.

The love of God in you, and Christ himself in you. That is to know. And Jesus is saying that the world does not know you, but these know, because I have made you known. How? He's in you, church. You know God. So, as Paul tells Timothy, avoid the irreverent babble of what is falsely called knowledge.

When someone says to you, prove it, explain it, give me all the arguments so I can reason my way to it with you, answer back: I didn't reason my way to it. You don't get there by thinking.

How do I know? Because he's in me. If you don't know, he isn't in you. Ask for the Holy Spirit. Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved. Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved.

Jesus spoke these words about turning sorrow and anguish into joy. Then he lifted his eyes to heaven and said, Father, the hour is here. It's time. It's go time.

Let's go. Through pain, let's bring forth joy. How? Glorify the Son so that the Son may glorify you, since you have given him authority over all flesh, to give eternal life to all whom you have given him. This is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent. I glorified you on earth, having accomplished the work that you gave me to do.

And now, glorify me in your own presence with the glory that I had with you before the world existed. The flow for us, number one, is to look at the cross as the pains of childbirth. And when he speaks of authority over all flesh: Pilate will soon say to him, "Do you not know that I have authority to release you and authority to crucify you?" And Jesus will answer, "You would have no authority over me at all unless it had been given you from above."

What we're talking about is a Jesus who willingly went to the cross to carry out the work God gave him. It means that even when it looks as though sin is winning, it never is, because all flesh yields to his authority. We can do nothing to thwart his plan.

His plan, Plan A, if I may be so bold, was always to march to that cross for the redemption of the world. He didn't rework it because of the flesh. The flesh was always under his authority. That's really good news. Because just as sin couldn't keep him dead the first time, you can't crucify him again.

The world has been trying to keep this man dead. I'm not kidding. However we slice it, the world will do anything to kill Jesus, to silence Jesus, and yet he lives. He lives, and he lives, and he lives, and his kingdom keeps spreading. Why?

Because he has authority over all flesh and authority to give eternal life, and he just keeps doing it. There's nothing you can do about it. That's good news, because you would if you could. So for us to have eternal life, we need to know him, but we can't know him, because our minds were darkened. So what does he do?

He gives us a new mind and makes us a new creation, baptizing us in the Holy Spirit. That's the accomplished work Jesus is talking about. The cross was the crescendo, showing us the character of God, the love of God. But the result was a birth. The product was you.

The product was you. That's good news too. And because he says he has accomplished the work, you don't have to help him. That's the other application. The work Jesus was given to do, he says, he has done. So what is your role? Not to help him accomplish the work God gave him.
