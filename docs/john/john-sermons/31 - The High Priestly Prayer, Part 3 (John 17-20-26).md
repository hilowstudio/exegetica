So here we are in the third section. Even though I'm preaching it in three sections, as we've said, it's very important to remember that this isn't three prayers. It's one prayer, and it builds on itself.

Jesus opens with a prayer for himself: Father, glorify the Son, so that the Son may glorify you. Glorify me with the glory I had with you before the foundation of the world. Glorify me so that I may glorify you. Why?

Because of who I am and because of what I've done. Because you have given me authority over all flesh and the right to give eternal life, and I have come and done what you sent me to do. I emptied myself of my glory to bring redemption among men, and now restore me, Father, to the glory I had with you before the foundation of the world. That's how his prayer opens. Then he turns and looks at his disciples.

He begins to pray for them, the men who walked with him during his earthly ministry. He starts speaking back to the Father what the Father has always known, eternal truths about how these disciples came to be God's possession. He speaks in really encouraging language about how they became his and came to know the Father. I have kept them. I've kept them all the days I've walked the earth, except the son of destruction, that the Scripture might be fulfilled. I haven't lost one, but I'm no longer in the world.

They are in the world, and I'm coming back to you. I'm not of the world, and they aren't of the world, but they are in the world, and I won't be anymore. So his prayer for his disciples is that the Father would take up what he himself had been doing: hold on to them, sustain them. Last week we preached how central and important this is.

If we don't rightly understand how we came to be God's possession, we'll fail to understand how we remain his. Here Jesus prays, Father, it was I who kept them, and now I'm asking that you, Father, would keep them in your name. I guarded them; now you guard them. He was praying for their security. And we said that what Jesus wants, Jesus gets.

So the church, the disciples, are held by God. Then he begins to move toward mission and unity. I held a lot of that teaching for today, because he continues it as he turns his attention to you and me. He prays, I'm leaving the world, but I'm sending them into the world. Father, I don't ask that you take them out of the world. I'm sending them into the world just as you sent me into the world.

Then he declares that he has consecrated himself, set himself apart, so that we would be set apart in the world. And he prays that we'd be kept from the evil one. Then he looks past the disciples in the room. That brings us to this morning. He fast-forwards two thousand years and sees you, and you, and says to his Father, verses 20 and 21: "I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word, that they may all be one,

"just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me." He begins to see all who will believe. And we see him doubling down on the teaching of this prayer. There's such certainty in what he says. I do not ask for these only, but also for those who will believe in me through what? Their word, the word of these eleven.

Listen. You are here today, church, because people believed the word Christ gave to his church. That's no small thing. It's a hard word, but I want you to receive it as a true word. You are in Christ because you received his word. And he delivered his word to you, as of first importance, by the power of the Holy Spirit, through the church.

A while back I told you the history of the original disciples: only one of them lived to old age, and the rest died brutal deaths as they took the gospel to the nations. In the same vein as the apostles, church history is full of stories of brutal deaths, of martyrdom, as people carried the gospel message to the nations. Not only the original disciples. I'm talking about men burned at the stake for translating the Bible into English.

I'm talking about men burned at the stake for opposing the church of Rome, or the Church of England, for resisting those who tried to co-opt Christianity as a lever for power on the face of the earth. The Lord brought the gospel to your ears through much suffering. And Jesus prayed for them, and he's praying for you: for those who will believe through their word. Church, you will never save anyone.

You're not going to save anybody. You know this, but I need you to really know it. You're not going to save anybody. But you'd better believe you're going to see people saved, as the Holy Spirit in you brings the gospel through your lips and lands it effectually in the hearts of the elect and brings them into new life. That's a privilege only the church gets to see.

The Holy Spirit works through the mouths of believers to land the gospel message in the hearts of those who will believe. This is your story. If you are a Christian, you became one by hearing and believing the gospel. It was a profound act of the Holy Spirit in you, through the gospel delivered by the church, through the Holy Spirit, through the death of Christ. And so he's praying for them.

For all who will believe through their word. They did it. He did it through them. Look at the history. You see the Holy Spirit moving in great ways through massive persecution throughout church history, from the beginning until today.

There are certainly periods of waxing and waning before, ultimately, a boom. Think about it: Christianity came into existence through persecution. By a death on a cross, man was saved.

And as the evil powers of the earth bore down on those who carried the gospel, the gospel flourished even as their blood was spilled. In parts of the world like China today, the underground church is spreading the gospel very effectively. We're seeing Christianity boom in parts of the world where it's most dangerous to be a Christian. And it's in parts of the world like the West, where it's least dangerous to be a Christian, that we see the faith waning. So what I hold out to you this morning is that there's a far greater danger to the church than persecution.

It's fat, and sugar, and sex, and entertainment, and money, and power, and status, and ability, and air conditioning. A day can come when you cozy up to the world, and the world is so good to you, that it becomes very hard to say, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ. An earlier generation was willing to spill their blood to bring the gospel to us, and we're unwilling to suffer a little embarrassment to bring the gospel to someone else. Am I willing to suffer a little discomfort to bring the gospel to another?

Jesus prays for you. With his eyes fixed on those who will believe through their word, he asks this: that they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they also may be in us, so that the world may believe that you have sent me. So that the world may believe that you have sent me. The central message today is unity.

Christ prayed for unity in the church, that we would be one. When we talk about oneness, we'll talk about it on two levels. We'll talk about unity with one another, unity within the church in all that she is and does. But far more important, we'll talk about unity with Christ, oneness with him in spirit. In other words, the engine behind all that the church is and does.

Think of how Jesus described his unity with the Father: not my words, but his words. Not my will, but his will. I do only what I see the Father doing. And when he says, just as we are one, I'm praying that they would be one, it's the same: unity in action, unity in spirit, unity in word. That we would be one. As Ephesians 4:4 to 6 puts it: "There is one body and one Spirit—just as you were called to the one hope that belongs to your call—one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all, who is over all and through all and in all."

You're still here because he prayed that the Lord God would not take you out of the world, because he wanted to send you into it, just as he went into it. The reason the trumpet hasn't sounded, and Jesus hasn't said, enough, and all of that, is that the number stored up in the secret heart of God has not yet been saved. There is a multitude. Think about what's on Jesus' heart here.

Why does he pray for our unity? So that the world may believe that you have sent me. He settles for nothing less than the nations. He wants the world. And you can almost see it in Jesus as he prays this.

I don't want to invent a doctrine, so guard your ears as I say this. Something is happening to Jesus as he prays. You can see it, because in his mind he leaves the moment. Back in the previous section he says, "I am no longer in the world," while he's standing with the men he says are in the world. In his mind, during this prayer, he's already somewhere else.

And in this part of the prayer he'll pray that they would be with him where he is, while they're standing right there with him. Jesus has gone somewhere else in his mind, and I believe he's been carried there by the Spirit. The Holy Spirit, who carried him all through his earthly ministry, is preparing him for that final hour. He's seeing with greater clarity what is to come. He's seeing his glory. He's seeing his throne room.

He's seeing the place he came from. He's preparing to be restored to the glory he left to come for us. And he says, so that the world may believe that you have sent me, let's bring them in. I'm bringing them with me, and through them I'm going to go and get the nations. Come on.

That's something you get to see because of the Holy Spirit in you. A. W. Tozer wrote, "It is doubtful whether God can bless a man greatly until He has hurt him deeply." That quote went deep with me about a year before I moved to help plant a church, and it's still true: God has to break a man before he will love his neighbor. How long will you bear the name of Christ, with the Spirit of Christ within you, and look across the street at the lost and push it out of your mind?

He came for you. Your missional God came for you. Christ Jesus came for you. And he says, as you sent me into the world, so I have sent them into the world. You can't produce what I'm talking about here.

Only by pleading with God, so that he would break a man's heart enough, will that man love his neighbor. You must understand the lengths your God went to ransom you before you'll care at all about the depths you'll need to reach to bring the gospel to your neighbor. We are to have unity in mission. The church is to look like one in the way it functions in mission, and the way it looks is meant to be the way Jesus looked in mission.

It's the way the apostles looked in mission, the way the church of the first and second centuries looked in mission. It looks like counting everything as loss to bring the gospel to those who will believe by hearing. It's a unique privilege to join the ranks of the saints marching to the nations with this message of good news, of salvation by faith alone in Christ, not by anything good in us, but by something good in God. Verses 22 and 23: "The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one even as we are one,

"I in them and you in me, that they may become perfectly one, so that the world may know that you sent me and loved them even as you loved me." So again: so that the world would know that you sent me, so that the world would know the love with which you have loved me, so that the world would know the love with which you love them. Perfectly one. So we're not only talking about unity in mission. This isn't merely saying, get in the game; start behaving the way the church has behaved in the past.

Is that what I'm saying? No. I'm saying, be compelled by the same love the Father poured out on the Son. Jesus says that's the love he has for you, and that love compels you to go. You must fix your eyes on the message, the one message, the gospel of Jesus Christ. Only it can produce gospel action.

More specifically, only he can produce gospel action. His message is never separated from his person. So when we talk about this unity, on the one hand I want to caution you: I'm not talking about striving for unity.

I'm not talking about a football team: one team, one mission. Lots of people can unite around any number of things just by wanting it badly enough. Corporations are built on unity around a mission statement. I'm not talking about self-willed mission or self-willed unity.

I'm not talking about, why can't we all just get along? I'm talking about what Jesus is talking about: perfectly one, just as we are one. You can't do that, church. You can't do what he's talking about. Only he can.

That's why he says, "The glory that you have given me I have given to them, that they may be one even as we are one." What glory? Isaiah 42:8: "I am the LORD; that is my name; my glory I give to no other."

How can Jesus say, the glory you have given me I give to them? Is God contradicting himself when he gives his glory to Jesus? No. When the Father gives his glory to the Son, he isn't giving his glory to another. He's giving it to himself.

But now the Son says, the glory you have given me I give to them. Can he do that? Yes, he can. How? "I in them."

By the Holy Spirit, my friends. He doesn't hand his glory over to another, but he can dwell in you. And by union with you, by making you one with him, you share in his glory, a partaker of his glory, because you've been made alive in him. That's where we get that teaching: "It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me." Let me make the case that our union with Christ by the Holy Spirit is what he means when he says he shares his glory with us.

1 Corinthians 6:17: "But he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him." One spirit with us, because he has given us his Spirit. Colossians 1:27: "To them God chose to make known how great among the Gentiles are the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory." "Anyone who does not have the Spirit of Christ does not belong to him." 1 Corinthians 12:13: "For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body—Jews or Greeks, slaves or free—and all were made to drink of one Spirit." God chose to make known the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory. You are made one with God because he literally dwells in you.

In several conversations, and several sermons in a row now, I keep bringing this up because it's so important. What makes you a Christian? When you ask someone, what makes you a Christian, if they start talking about something they did or something they believe, they're getting it wrong. At least they're describing it wrong.

If it doesn't start with "he did this," we're at the very least describing it wrong, or else we're truly not Christians. Many people will say on that day, didn't I do this, and this, and this? That's a dangerous game to play. If I ask, what makes you a Christian, and you start telling me what you do or what you did,

that's dangerous. What makes you a Christian is something that happened to you, something that happened in you: something begun by God, carried out by God and sustained by God. It's the person of God, the Holy Spirit, dwelling in you, baptizing you in the Holy Spirit, bringing you from death to life, putting the flesh to death and raising you as a new creation. This is supposed to be Christianity 101.

What makes me a Christian is the Holy Spirit in me. But then I ask, how do you know the Holy Spirit is in you? We don't believe the Holy Spirit is in us, that the Holy Spirit does things.

And when he does things in us, we don't credit the Holy Spirit. We take the credit ourselves. Jesus says we will be perfectly one because he is in us. "I in them and you in me" makes us perfectly one. So what about when we don't act like we're one?

Guess what? We are. You're one with your spouse; you know the teaching that two become one flesh in marriage.

We're talking about a unity that is true of the church because we are united in Christ by his Holy Spirit, all attached to the same vine. So we can't produce it, but we do enjoy it. You are one. I believe they all fit into two categories. The first: if someone carries a different gospel, have nothing to do with him. If someone doesn't hold the same gospel we do, they're not one with us. They're the mission field.

They're the ones we're here to bring the true gospel to. The second is the unrepentant sinner.
