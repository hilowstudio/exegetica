# The Savior’s Authority (John 5:18-47)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 5:18-47

---

Let me remind you how we got here, because if we don't, this will fall flat and we'll miss things. We left off with Jesus healing the paralyzed man at the pool of Bethesda, a man who had been an invalid for thirty-eight years. Jesus asks him, "Do you want to be healed?" and says, "Get up, take up your bed, and walk." The man gets up, picks up his bed and starts walking. At this point the Jews say to him, "It is the Sabbath, and it is not lawful for you to take up your bed." And the man, miraculously healed and carrying his bed, answers, "The man who healed me, that man said to me, 'Take up your bed, and walk.'" They ask who the man was, and he doesn't know.

Later Jesus finds him in the temple and says, "See, you are well! Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen to you." He is telling him: you've received a temporary healing for a far lesser problem. What you need is healing for your soul, healing from sin. The man, now knowing who healed him, goes and tells the Jews it was Jesus. And John says that this was why the Jews were persecuting Jesus, because he was doing these things on the Sabbath. "But Jesus answered them, 'My Father is working until now, and I am working.'"

That's where we ended last time, and a few things will set the stage. First, when Jesus healed the man, he told him to take up his bed and walk. Without the context we'll miss it. Jewish teaching, later written down in the Mishnah, listed thirty-nine kinds of work forbidden on the Sabbath, and one of them was carrying things from one domain to another: from inside your home to outside it, for instance. In Jerusalem they literally drew lines to mark how far you could go before you had carried something from one domain into another. Perfect Sabbath-keeping meant carrying nothing, even trivial things. So when Jesus tells the paralyzed man, on the Sabbath, take up your bed and walk, he is telling him to break one of the rules the Jews had built around the Sabbath. He invites controversy, and he gets the controversy he was looking for. The Jews confront the man: it is not lawful for you to do this. He says, Jesus told me to. So they go after Jesus, and they persecute him all the more for breaking the Sabbath.

These Jews cared deeply about getting the Sabbath right. They wrote out the thirty-nine categories. They thought hard about what work means so they could avoid it. And they reasonably asked: does God have to keep the Sabbath? It sounds funny, but it's a good question. Does the God who instituted the Sabbath require it of himself? This God who rested on the seventh day, does he work on the Sabbath? And they rightly concluded, yes. The God who upholds all things by the word of his power, who feeds the birds every morning and sustains all things, doesn't take a day off. God can work on the Sabbath. But no one else can.

Jesus knows this. He creates the controversy and sets up the moment when they come and accuse him, persecuting him for doing these things on the Sabbath, and he says, in effect, I know only God can work on the Sabbath. That's why I'm working. This is the earliest direct statement in which Jesus likens himself to God.

Every word in today's passage comes straight from Jesus' mouth. It's long, and I'll try to get through it in a reasonable time, but we're reading it as one piece because they heard it as one piece. It's a single interaction with Jesus, so we want to hear his words together. They accuse him: only God can work on the Sabbath. And Jesus says, that's right. My Father is working until now, and I am working. Verse 18 begins our passage: "This was why the Jews were seeking all the more to kill him, because not only was he breaking the Sabbath, but he was even calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God."

By looking straight at Jesus' words this morning, I hope we'll let him answer three questions for himself. The first is: is Jesus God?

Christianity lives and dies on the claim that Jesus is himself God. That's where we part ways with most other world religions. Muslims, Jews and Latter-day Saints, for example, do not believe that Jesus is God, one with the Father and equal to him. What Christians uniquely claim is that Jesus is God, equal with the Father. But that can't be true unless Jesus claimed it himself. The world has charged that people started saying these things about Jesus later, that he never meant us to see him that way. So we need to let Jesus speak for himself. Here he deliberately creates the controversy and sets up this interaction. People challenge him: only God can work on the Sabbath. Jesus answers, yes, that's why I'm working. And they seek all the more to kill him. Why? Because they understood him to be making himself equal with God. Was he? Let's see.

Verse 19: "So Jesus said to them." Pay attention to the "so." He speaks in response to the fact that they want to kill him all the more for making himself equal with God. If they misunderstood him, if he wasn't claiming equality with God, if they were putting words in his mouth, here is his chance to clear it up. Let's see what he says. "Truly, truly, I say to you, the Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise. For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself is doing. And greater works than these will he show him, so that you may marvel."

They've just said, you're making yourself equal with God. And he answers, I do only what God does. Everything I say is as if God were saying it. Everything I do is as if the Father were doing it. I only ever do what God is doing. In other words, if you want to know what God is like, look at me. If you want to know what God says, listen to me. If you want to know God's will, look at me.

And why? "For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself is doing." Now he claims a unique intimacy with the Father. By the love shared between them, the Father shows the Son everything he is doing, and by that love the Son does everything the Father shows him. They are uniquely one, he claims, always in perfect unity. "And shows him all that he himself is doing." That's a big claim. It means Jesus has never said what we often say: God works in mysterious ways. God, what are you doing right now? I'm leaning on the little I do understand to keep believing you're good, because I really don't understand what you're doing. Jesus claims that the Father loves the Son in such a unique and united way that he shows him everything he is doing. At all times Jesus knows what the Father is doing, and not only knows it but carries it out himself, bodily. So did Jesus claim to be God and equal with God? I have to say yes.

"And greater works than these will he show him, so that you may marvel." Well, Jesus, you've just said you are the God of the Sabbath, that only God can work on the Sabbath and that's why you're working. You're claiming that you and the Father are one, that you are perfectly in tune with the Father at all times and carry out only his will, in unique unity and love with him. What greater works could you possibly show?

Verse 21: "For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will." Jesus is telling the people who want to kill him over the Sabbath, I can bring dead people to life. And he has just said that the Son can do nothing of his own accord, only what he sees the Father doing, always carrying out the Father's perfect will. So when Jesus says, I give life to whom I will, whom do you will? Whomever the Father wills. And whom does the Father will? Whomever I will. He is speaking from every angle he can to make sure this is understood. We are one. I am God.

Verses 22 and 23: "For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him." He's speaking to people who understood God the Father to be the creator and judge of all things. And he says, the Father judges no one. He has handed all judgment to the Son. Why? So that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him. Now he's saying, I'm not only equal with God in ability, in what I do or in knowing his will. I am equal with God in honor. I am worthy of every drop of honor the Father is worthy of, so much so that unless you honor me, you cannot honor the Father.

Layer upon layer, he makes sure we can't miss that he claims to be God, on equal footing with God. Jesus claims that the Son and the Father are one, equal in glory and honor and majesty and love. Imagine if I said this to you. Imagine if anyone did: if you want to honor God, bow to me. If you want to know what God wants for you, ask me. This claim is so high they're going to kill him for it. We know the end of the story. It doesn't end with his death, but they will kill him for it.

And it requires the same of each of us. By making these claims, he leaves us no middle ground. We don't get to make him just a good teacher or a misunderstood prophet. He doesn't let any of us off the hook. He makes you either kill him or worship him. Faced with these claims, you have to say he is a liar, or a lunatic, or God. He allows no middle ground.

Verse 24: "Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life."

So we're letting Jesus answer three questions. First, is Jesus God? Does he even claim to be? Second, what happens when we die? Third, does how we lived have anything to do with it? These are the big questions of life, the ones everyone has to answer, and we need to let Jesus answer them himself. He has just claimed not only to be God, but in verse 24 he says that whoever hears his word and believes him who sent him has eternal life and does not come into judgment. God the Father has given the Son the right to judge, and the one who believes the words I speak will not face judgment. Here is how it is decided whether or not you will face it. Charles Spurgeon is said to have called this verse worthy to be written in gold and spread over all the earth: that we would not come into judgment but pass from death to life, by believing in Jesus for eternal life.

We'll come back to that, but let's stay with what he's doing here. "Whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me." Once again: when I speak, and you believe, you are believing him. Whoever hears my word and believes us, in effect, has eternal life. He claims the right to speak for God the Father. That person "does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life." Hear those words this morning, judgment, death and life, and soberly consider what Jesus is saying. The Father has given me alone the right to judge. We will face Jesus as judge. And yet we will not come into judgment or condemnation but will pass from death to life. On what basis? That we hear his word and believe him.

Verses 25 to 30: "Truly, truly, I say to you, an hour is coming, and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself. And he has given him authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of Man. Do not marvel at this, for an hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out, those who have done good to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment. I can do nothing on my own. As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just, because I seek not my own will but the will of him who sent me."

Put it together. I alone have been given the right to judge, and I will not judge those who hear my word and believe him who sent me. They will pass from death to life. This is the already and the not yet of Christianity. An hour is coming, and he'll talk about that hour in a moment, but it is also now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live. Through his preaching and teaching, people were going to be brought from death to life. He is speaking, at least in part, about spiritual death and spiritual life for those who would believe the gospel he preached: his disciples, or the Roman centurion who would confess him as he watched him die. Right now, in his earthly ministry, Jesus is saving people by the power of his word. He is also talking about literally raising the dead, like Lazarus or Jairus' daughter. By the power of his word he will literally bring people from death to life. And all of it points to one central truth: he alone, by the power of his word, can bring dead people to life. To those who say, you make yourself equal with God, Jesus answers, I can raise the dead. The power of my word brings the dead to life.

We were dead in the trespasses in which we once walked, until the word of God took root in our hearts, took a heart of stone, turned it into a heart of flesh and brought us into new life. If you are in Christ, that is your story. If that isn't your story this morning, hear this: an hour is coming, and is now here, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live.

Verses 28 and 29: "Do not marvel at this, for an hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out, those who have done good to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment." We'll get to that last sentence, but first hear this: everyone is getting up. We read in John 1 that by the power of his word all things were created, and by it all things are sustained. The reason the stars stay where they are, the sun rises and sets, the planets keep spinning, the plants keep growing and you keep eating is that the power of the word of God is sustaining all things at all times. Jesus is saying to everything, keep doing what I made you to do. Keep doing it. Everything happens because Jesus wills it by the power of his word. So it's no great thing for Jesus to say to all who are in the tombs, come back. Up with you. They stay in the tomb because his word keeps them there, and by his word they'll get up. He is claiming total authority over all things.

And on that day, he says, those who have done good will come out to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment. If we read that sentence without the whole counsel of Jesus' ministry and the rest of Scripture, we'll conclude that Jesus will put us on a scale and measure whether we've done enough good to receive life.

But Jesus has just said otherwise. When the rich young ruler comes to him in Mark and asks, "Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?" Jesus answers, "Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone." He's saying, don't call me good teacher; call me good God. Only God is good. And if the Epistles are true, "None is righteous, no, not one," and "all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God." So on that day, when Jesus says to those in the tombs, get up, and pours out his judgment, inviting those who have done good to the resurrection of life and sending those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment, who could be found among the good? Who?

You have to reckon with these words of Jesus. You will not be graded on a curve on the day of judgment. Jesus is not going to weigh your good deeds against your sin to decide whether you get into eternal life. That's simply not how it works. And if you were graded on a curve or put on a scale, in every case your sin would far outweigh your good, and you would have no hope. Maybe you don't believe me and want to argue. I'll allow it. But even if the scales tipped toward the good, the standard is perfection. God created you in his image to reflect his goodness and glory and majesty perfectly in creation. That's what you were made for: to live on this earth perfectly reflecting the face of God, as Jesus did. As Jesus just claimed: I only ever do the will of the Father; I am in perfect unity with him. That is what you were made for. Your very existence, doing anything but that, is rebellion and treason against the throne of God, and for that we will be judged, whether or not you gave soup to a hungry man.

It mocks the cross to be a rebel attacking the throne of God with a dagger, claiming that I can be the god of my own life, the master of my own life, that I can do this without you, God, and then say, but look at the good things I'm doing. That is an affront and an offense to a holy God who requires perfection of us. It's what we were made for. So who stands a chance on the day he raises the dead, some to the resurrection of life and some to the resurrection of judgment?

He already said it, back in verse 24. Who are the ones who have done good? "Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life." Who receives the resurrection of life? The one who has heard his word and believed him who sent him. We are saved by grace alone, through faith alone, in Christ alone.

It's very likely that someone in this room believes it is some combination: Christ's mercy, his perfect life lived on your behalf, his death offered as payment for your sin and his resurrection, plus your good works, that wins you favor with God. That God is pleased with you and will say on the day of judgment, well done, good and faithful servant, the teamwork between you and Jesus was enough for me to welcome you into eternal life. I'm telling you that is a damnable lie. You did not team up with Jesus to do enough good works to satisfy the wrath of God against a sinful, rebellious humanity. You are made right with God on the merits of Christ alone. Christ alone. You didn't help him. You didn't meet him halfway. God is not taking pity on the deserving poor. It is Christ alone who gains you merit with the Father.

The truth is that we were made for perfection. So when Jesus lived bodily on this earth, he lived the life you were designed to live, on your behalf. Then he died the death we deserved, to pay for the sins we committed and he did not. And then he alone, because he is God, rose victorious over the punishment for our sin, so that he could invite us into the reward he was given. This is the Christian life. If Jesus is not God, we are of all people most to be pitied. If Jesus is not God, he could not have risen from the dead. Only God can do that. If Jesus is not God, he could not take the penalty of death for me, and he could not rise again from that penalty for me. That's simply not possible. If he's just a man, just a prophet, there's no reason to listen to any of this. His claim to be God, equal with the Father, is everything.

Verses 30 and 31: "I can do nothing on my own. As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just, because I seek not my own will but the will of him who sent me. If I alone bear witness about myself, my testimony is not true." Again he's referring to Jewish law. People can't simply assert things and have them accepted; their justice system required witnesses. So he says, on what basis can you believe me? If I alone bear witness about myself, my testimony is not true. So let me prove it.

Verses 32 to 34: "There is another who bears witness about me, and I know that the testimony that he bears about me is true. You sent to John, and he has borne witness to the truth. Not that the testimony that I receive is from man, but I say these things so that you may be saved." I love this. My wife laughed when I read it to her the night before I preached it. The testimony that Jesus is God is not human testimony, but for their sake, because he knows they'll insist on a human witness and won't accept "God testifies" by itself, he says, you sent to John. Witness number one: John. He has borne witness to the truth.

Verses 35 and 36: "He was a burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to rejoice for a while in his light. But the testimony that I have is greater than that of John. For the works that the Father has given me to accomplish, the very works that I am doing, bear witness about me that the Father has sent me." Witness number two: my works. I have John to back me up, and I have my works. When I speak, and fifteen miles away a dying child is healed, my works testify that I am God. When I tell the paralyzed man, take up your bed and walk, and he does, it testifies that I am God.

Verses 37 and 38: "And the Father who sent me has himself borne witness about me. His voice you have never heard, his form you have never seen, and you do not have his word abiding in you, for you do not believe the one whom he has sent." The Father himself testifies about me, but that witness does you no good, because you've never heard his voice or seen his form.

Verses 39 and 40: "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life." And finally, the Scriptures testify about me. I have an abundance of witnesses, he says. But his word does not abide in you. If it did, then when I appeared, you would have said, it's you! These are the Scriptures they knew, the Law and the Prophets, and above all the books of Moses. Jesus is saying, when you search the books of Moses and the law of God, if his word abided in you, then when I appeared you would have recognized me: you're the one the words were telling me about and promising me. It's you. Instead I came, and you didn't know me. His word does not abide in you, or you would have recognized me.

Think about this claim. Imagine Jesus saying to you: when you read your Bible, if you don't look up afterward and say to me, it's you, then you don't understand your Bible. It's a lofty claim. Scriptures written over many centuries were about me, this thirty-something man named Jesus.

Verses 41 to 43: "I do not receive glory from people. But I know that you do not have the love of God within you. I have come in my Father's name, and you do not receive me." His word does not abide in you. His love does not abide in you. For the love of God to abide in you, you must receive Jesus. This is the crux of the Christian life. You must receive Jesus, once and for all. On the day the dead rise by the power of Jesus' word and you stand before the judgment seat, one of two things will be read over you, over all of us: either the record of your own deeds or the record of Christ's deeds on your behalf. The only difference between one person and the next will be whether they received Jesus on their behalf, or whether they tried to give an account to God on their own merit. If you do that, you won't be able to withstand the judgment of the Father. You won't.

Speaking to the most religious people on the planet, Jesus says, you don't know me. His word does not abide in you, and his love does not abide in you. You who put your hope in the very Scriptures are dead and headed for eternal judgment. You must receive me. How sobering is that? They knew their Bibles better than you. They knew them better than me. These were the religious elite, trained as scholars to study the Scriptures so they would be ready to recognize the Messiah. And God is standing face to face with them, and they want to kill him.

Verses 43 to 47: "If another comes in his own name, you will receive him. How can you believe, when you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God? Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father. There is one who accuses you: Moses, on whom you have set your hope. For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me. But if you do not believe his writings, how will you believe my words?"

This is the last piece I want you to hear. Is Jesus God? Yes, at least he says so. What happens after we die? We wait a while, and then he says, come on back, and we all rise, either to eternal life or to judgment. And does how we lived have anything to do with that? The answer is decidedly no, and yes.

Here he says, "Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father," and it's deeply sad. It doesn't even need to come to that, he says, because you didn't put your hope in me. In Matthew he says that on the day of judgment some will claim to have put their hope in him, and he will say, I never knew you. So he isn't saying he will accuse no one. Jesus will answer those who say, I put my hope in Jesus, because either they did or they didn't. That is either true or a lie. But to these men he says, there's no need for me to accuse you. You didn't put your hope in me. You put your hope in Moses, so Moses will accuse you. If your hope is in Moses, you'll need to do whatever Moses said. But Moses said your hope was me. He wrote about me. So the accusation of Moses is on your head. And if you won't believe his writings, which were about me, how can you believe my words? You are rejecting me.

Let me sit in that for a minute. Maybe ten years ago I heard a pastor walk through this exercise. Suppose God let you set your own standard of righteousness. He hands you the power to make the list of what it means to be a good person. Go ahead and try it in your head, but make sure you account for everything. This is how I want my daughters treated. This is how I want to be treated. This is acceptable. This is good enough. You came up with it. Given enough time, maybe ten minutes, every one of us would break the standard we wrote ourselves. Even as fallible standard-setters who are extremely lenient with ourselves, we would find that we violate our own standard of righteousness.

And that's what the world is doing every day. It's what we are doing every day. We reject the standard of righteousness God has set and say we can do better, that we can come up with our own. And you're walking around with guilt. I know, because I live life with people, and they live life with me. We carry guilt and condemnation, not even because we failed God, but because we failed to meet our own expectations of ourselves, lamenting that I'm not as good as I wanted to be. If that's what we're banking on, setting our own standard of righteousness and then keeping it, then on that day Jesus won't even have to accuse us. The standard we wrote will accuse us.

But the standard Jesus sets is this: on that day, every one of us, by free invitation, may stand covered in Christ. The whole law of God, the law of Moses these men were looking to, has found its fulfillment in God himself, Jesus Christ in the flesh. He fulfilled the law on our behalf, so that on that day we can take hold of another's righteousness and not our own. It is the only way, the only truth and the only life, and Jesus alone claims it.

So we are all put in that position, today and every day, to answer the question. Let me invite you to consider it. We live in a very postmodern, secular age that wants to believe there is no knowable, objective truth and that no one knows what happens after we die, so we'll find out when we get there. That perspective would make a lot of sense if the God who created the universe hadn't come in human flesh and told us what happens after we die. But he did. It's written for us, we've received it, and his word enables us to believe it. And he says that those who hear it and believe it are invited into eternal life.

So this is the moment. Maybe you've never done that. Today is the day to respond, maybe for the first time, to this truth: by the word of Jesus alone, dead men can live. The moment is here. He says the hour is coming and is now here when the dead will hear his voice and enter new life. So I invite you this morning to respond to the gospel. And if it isn't your first time, respond again. Because hear the implications of the gospel: no judgment, no condemnation. How would it wreck and remake your life if you really believed that God looks at you, sees Jesus, and says, spotless, perfect, holy, righteous, good and faithful servant? That is what he has purchased for you if you are found in him. And we're invited to live in it.

So think about the people around you, the guilt and shame and condemnation they have shared with you, things the Lord has let you, and perhaps only you, know through your friendship with them. We who have tasted and seen this forgiveness and mercy have a unique ability to turn to our brothers and sisters and remind them of the gospel they have received once and for all: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus." I invite you to do that now.
