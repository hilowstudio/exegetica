# Rivers of Living Water (John 7:37-52)

**Series:** Gospel of John — Adam Vega Sermon Library
**Passage:** John 7:37-52

---

We've been at the Feast of Booths for the last couple of weeks. It started with Jesus' brothers trying to push him to go to the feast on their timing. He had been avoiding Judea for some time, because the last time he was there he healed a man on the Sabbath and got into a verbal sparring match over it. He made himself equal with God, and they started trying to kill him.

They wanted him dead and were searching him out. So he had been ministering away from Jerusalem, and now he has come back into Judea during the Feast of Booths. They're still seeking to kill him. They're still looking for him.

And what did Jesus do last week? In the middle of the feast he went into the temple and taught, and everyone marveled. The tension rose: some believed in him, while others wanted to arrest him and kill him. The chief priests and Pharisees sent officers to arrest him. And Jesus told them, I will be with you a little longer, and then I am going to him who sent me.

He was talking about his death and his ascension. We saw how Jesus helped us see the contrast between the manifold wisdom of God that he embodies and the folly of those who are wise in their own sight. We gloried in the power of the Holy Spirit to make us wise in the things of Christ, and we were cautioned against the power of the flesh to do the opposite: to make us mistake our own passions for the word of God. And we saw the difference Jesus draws between a self-made godliness and a righteousness that comes from the Holy Spirit.

All of that informs the way Jesus speaks today. This is the same sequence of events at the same feast, so let's not separate what we heard last week from what we hear from Jesus today. Verses 37 to 39: "On the last day of the feast, the great day, Jesus stood up and cried out, 'If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, "Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water."' Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive, for as yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified."

John takes special care at the beginning of this section to note that it was the last day, the great day of the feast. The Feast of Booths was the seventh and last of the appointed feasts God gave his people in Leviticus 23. He had led them out of slavery in Egypt toward the promised land, but for forty years his leading went through a desert wilderness, where he had to provide supernaturally for a huge host of hungry and thirsty people. Their God went before them in a pillar of cloud and fire to provide for them. When he moved, they followed him. And when he stopped, they set up camp in little booths.

These were temporary dwelling places. To this day, during the Feast of Booths, Jewish families still build little booths with leafy roofs beside their homes and live in them for the week. They still keep this feast of remembrance of the way the Lord provided for them in the wilderness. In Jesus' day, during this feast, there was a daily ritual at the temple. A priest went out with a golden pitcher and made the long walk down to the Pool of Siloam. He dipped the golden pitcher into the water and carried it the several hundred meters back up to the temple, with a great procession behind him, through what was called the Water Gate, while the people sang Psalms 113 through 118, the great psalms of praise.

When he got back to the temple with his pitcher of water, he poured it out at the altar. It was a sign that called the people to remember how the Lord brought water from the rock, how he made the bitter water sweet in those days in the wilderness, how the Lord provided for them. Water has always been a symbol for the Jews of what comes down from heaven.

So in pouring out this golden pitcher of water at the altar, they remembered that their provision came down from heaven. Down from heaven their thirst was met in the wilderness. And for seven days the priests did this. But on the eighth day, the last day, the great day, which many take to be the day John means and the day we're in this morning, there was no such ceremony. On the eighth day the priest did not go down to the Pool of Siloam.

He did not fill the golden pitcher, and he did not pour it out at the altar where sacrifices had been offered all week. And as the water was poured each day, the people prayed the prayer of Psalm 118: Lord, save us. Lord, save us. Lord, save us. For those seven days it was a prayer of remembrance, of the forty years when they called on the Lord to save and deliver them.

But that prayer was never only remembrance. It looked forward. Send again, Lord, not water from heaven to meet my physical needs and quench my physical thirst, but send your promised Messiah. Lord, rescue us, as you promised, with an eternal rescue.

So picture it. For seven days the priest has poured out this water in the very temple where Jesus, in the middle of the feast, set himself up to teach. Now it's the eighth day. No pitcher. No water. All week the people have cried, Lord, save us. Lord, save us. Lord, save us.

And standing right there is the Messiah, the one they've been praying for all these years. He stands up and cries out, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink." If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink.

"Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water.'" You are thirsty. You've been thirsty your entire life, and some of you have been drinking everything you can get your hands on to quench that thirst. You've gone down every avenue of sin, drinking dirty water and hoping it will satisfy you.

Or you've drunk a different kind of sin, self-righteousness. You've done every good work you could set your mind to and busied yourself trying to become something, trying to be enough, so that you could satisfy this deep thirst within you. And Jesus is calling out, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink." Let him come to me and drink. The one who put the thirst within you is the only one who can quench it.

This thirst within you cries out to be filled: to be truly satisfied, truly known, to experience true belonging, to have true righteousness, true acceptance, true holiness, a true identity. These deep desires of your heart have you calling out, I'm thirsty. And Christ himself has entered the picture to satisfy that thirst. I see two things happening, inside the church and outside it. Outside the church, it looks like what I just described.

We're clearly thirsty, because we spend our lives searching, devouring and consuming whatever we think will fill us up. And it doesn't stop the day you receive the Holy Spirit. In each moment you walk by the Spirit, certainly, the thirst is quenched. But day by day, in the battle with your flesh, you turn from the fountain of living water and go back to drinking what fails to satisfy. And before you've really tasted the fountain of living water, you go back again and again, like an addict: just a little bit more.

I know the last time I drank this I was thirsty again. I know the more I drink, the thirstier I get. But just a little more, maybe a little bigger, a little greater, a little darker, a little lighter. If I work harder, if I go deeper into this sin, my thirst will finally be quenched. If you've never tasted the sweet water, the river of living water, if your whole life has been a story of thirst, and you've called out to God again and again, Lord, if only you would do this, Lord, if only you would do that, and you feel as though you're praying to a stone wall, to a deaf God, I want to tell you that you probably are praying to a stone wall and a deaf God. Because what I know about my God, what I know about Jesus Christ, is that he stood up in the crowd and said, if anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink.

That's what he does. So if you're calling out for water and no water comes, what I know is that you're not praying to Jesus. That's not who you're talking to. And what I've seen in the church is that we often fashion for ourselves a God who looks a little like Jesus. We've talked about this over the last few weeks.

We fashion a God of our own making. He looks a lot like us. He thinks a lot like us. He very conveniently wants for us everything we want.

And his primary job is to deliver what we think we need and want. So I've got this Adam-shaped God, and when I ask him to do what I made him to do and he doesn't do it, I shake my fist at the Christian God as if he isn't answering my prayers. But I'm not even talking to him. I'm talking to a god of my own making.

So naturally, all I hear is my own words bouncing back at me. So I caution you: if you're calling out for something to drink and you're not receiving a cup of cold water, the first thing worth asking is, whom am I asking? Whom am I asking? The second is, why am I asking? The reason you come to Jesus is that you thirst.

He said in the Sermon on the Mount, "Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they shall be satisfied." You will be satisfied. So whom are you asking, and why are you asking? Is it because you're thirsty? Because this morning we're going to meet a people who aren't thirsty, and a people who are.

There's a difference between them. And I know our instinct is to say, we're the thirsty ones. But there's a person in this room who is the most common Christian I run into when it comes to doubt and unbelief.

And don't think I'm above this, because I'm not, and neither are you. We start to believe that Jesus is there for our initial thirst, for that moment of salvation, the prayer you prayed that day at church or whatever it was. But after that, it becomes a partnership: he carries the water halfway to you, and you meet him halfway to take the pail. Your ongoing thirst-quenching becomes something you do in partnership with him. It's something you earn.

So many of us, through guilt and shame, feel like this: Jesus saved me, and I haven't lived the life he would have me live. I haven't reflected the life of a child that I think he would want from me. I haven't looked like a son of God in all the ways I know I ought to. And so I keep myself from coming again to the fountain of living water. This one is very common. If this is you, let yourself hear it.

Somehow it feels like an act of righteousness, an act of holiness, to beat yourself up. It's a kind of penance. You won't come and drink again from the fountain of living water because you haven't cleaned yourself up enough to feel righteous enough to drink from that pool again. You don't want to overuse the grace of your God, as if you could. And as you withdraw from him to clean yourself up, hear this, because I need to hear it from you too.

The day you start believing you need to clean yourself up before you can return to the fountain of living water, you've started to believe that a person can clean himself up and become worthy of the fountain of living water. And now you've rejected the very foundation of the gospel: that we don't earn our righteousness; it is given to us as a free gift of grace, by faith alone in Christ alone. It certainly doesn't feel like pride to sit in guilt and shame and beat ourselves up, trying to work off our sin debt so that we can approach the living water. But what we're telling God is that it can be done. So let me remind you this morning: the grace you first received is the grace you need every day. You never reach a point where you're dealing with God as a partner, where you have to do your part before you can drink freely, to your heart's delight, of the grace available to you in Jesus.

No one can bring a charge against you. No one can bring evidence against you that would make God change his mind, because his eyes were wide open the day he set his saving affection on you. So drink, and keep drinking until you're satisfied. Come back daily to the fountain of living water. Jesus stood up and cried out, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink."

"Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water.' Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive." Whom you have received. Whom you have received. Some of you are holding off on baptism because you don't feel you've reached a certain level of Christianity, that you've leveled up enough to be worthy of baptism. Or you don't feel you've leveled up enough to expect God to answer your prayers, your cries for living water.

You don't feel you've leveled up enough to be worthy of serving the bride of Christ, or of doing whatever it is. As if the story of your walk with Jesus was ever about what you do and what you bring to the table. Here Jesus is speaking of a heart with rivers of living water flowing from it, and he means the Holy Spirit. God pours out his Spirit on you, turns your heart of stone into a heart of flesh, and testifies to you day by day that you are his child, a child of God. That's the Holy Spirit in you, a gift of God to you.

That's the river of living water that flows from your heart: the Holy Spirit. So every moment that you do anything that glorifies the King, it's the King glorifying himself by his Spirit through you. He takes all the credit. He takes all the glory, which means all the weight and all the pressure is off you. It's not about you.

It's about him. It's always been about him. But some of us won't drink because we feel we haven't helped him enough, as if we had to help pay off our sin by doing penance and punishing ourselves for our guilt and shame, as if the punishment of Christ were not enough. I say to you, repent, because that sin is far greater than whatever it is you won't let go: telling Christ he needs your help to pay off your sin. Every time you say, I know God forgives me, but I can't forgive myself, you're raising yourself above your Creator. You're saying, he can forgive me by his lower standard, but by my higher standard I can't forgive myself.

Stop. It's a great sin of pride to tell your God, who is perfect and holy and has forgiven you, that somehow you can't forgive you. Forgiving yourself is not a requirement for drinking from the living water. His forgiveness is, and he offers it to you freely. So come and drink.

That's the Spirit in you, calling you to drink, pouring out that river for you again and again. So drink. Now let's see what happens. They hear this. Jesus is saying, you don't need the pitcher at the altar. I am your sacrifice. I am your living water.

And as they hear these words, verses 40 to 44: "When they heard these words, some of the people said, 'This really is the Prophet.' Others said, 'This is the Christ.' But some said, 'Is the Christ to come from Galilee? Has not the Scripture said that the Christ comes from the offspring of David, and comes from Bethlehem, the village where David was?' So there was a division among the people over him. Some of them wanted to arrest him, but no one laid hands on him."

Then, verses 45 and 46, the officers who were sent to arrest him last week come back: "The officers then came to the chief priests and Pharisees, who said to them, 'Why did you not bring him?' The officers answered, 'No one ever spoke like this man!'" I couldn't do it. Have you heard him? I'm not grabbing that man.

Something is happening here, and I wonder whether we'll let ourselves hear him speaking the way he speaks and respond the way the officers did. There were times when Jesus spoke in a way that made people say, let's grab him. That was a few weeks ago. Here Jesus speaks in a way that makes the men sent to grab him say, I can't touch him. And I wonder if this morning Jesus would have you look at him the same way. Whatever you thought, that you could handle him, mold him, make him into something he's not, he'd say, take your hands off. Look, really look, and listen to what I'm saying.

Take and drink. This soul-satisfying drink is the sacrificial life, death and resurrection of Jesus applied to you, and the Holy Spirit in you. This renewal, washed in the blood of Christ and made new by the Holy Spirit, is what you were made to experience. The thirst you feel, the thirst that creeps back in as you turn again to your flesh: you were made to turn to him.

And the Holy Spirit, who only ever does the will of God, constantly prompts you again and again to go back to the source of your living water. I want to invite you to do that in your heart this morning as we look at the opposite reaction, because here it is. Verses 47 to 49: "The Pharisees answered them, 'Have you also been deceived? Have any of the authorities or the Pharisees believed in him? But this crowd that does not know the law is accursed.'"

Their first response is, you can't believe that. Don't let yourself believe it. That's hogwash, that's foolish, you've been deceived.

And I know that's the first thing we start to believe when we drift away from the fountain of living water to drink again the stagnant, muddy water that has only ever made us sick. We start to hear these words: you've been deceived. He's not that good. He won't do what he said he'd do. He's not who he said he is. He's not all that.

You need to provide for yourself. You need to take care of yourself. That's what we start believing. That's the first thing. The second is what they say next: have any of us believed in him?

Sure, the crowds have. But look at us. We're the religious elite, the authorities, the Pharisees and the chief priests. We haven't believed, so let that be your sign.

Some of us are turned off from the fountain of living water because we look at religious people, see how they relate, or don't relate, to the fountain of living water, and take our cue from them. That's what the Pharisees want the officers to do here. Take your cue from us. We're the religious people, and we don't believe. Follow us.

If you're surrounded by religious people who love to talk about the laws and commands of God, who can quote Scripture left and right, but who won't drink from the fountain of living water, who have never experienced the grace of Jesus but can quote you a systematic theology, I'm telling you, they're leading you astray. They're leading you away from the Lamb. They say the crowd that doesn't know the law is accursed. But for these men, the law has been a curse.

They've memorized it, they search it. Do you see how ridiculous it would be if Jesus Christ, Lord of the universe, stood in front of you this morning in all his glory, with the Spirit testifying to him, descending like a dove at the Jordan, and you said, what town do you come from? Because that's the test. You can't allow your Savior to come in any form other than the one you understood and liked. What they're doing here is a joke.

They are willfully ignorant. Verses 50 to 52: "Nicodemus, who had gone to him before, and who was one of them, said to them, 'Does our law judge a man without first giving him a hearing and learning what he does?' They replied, 'Are you from Galilee too? Search and see that no prophet arises from Galilee.'" You'll remember Nicodemus from chapter 3.

They couldn't let Jesus be Jesus, because he didn't fit in the box they'd drawn for themselves. Jesus wasn't born in Galilee, but that wouldn't have mattered to these men, would it? They were going to make him not fit no matter what. Otherwise Jesus could simply have said, no, you're confused: I was born in Bethlehem and raised in Galilee. Cleared that up for them, as if they'd then say, oh, we worship you, King. No.

That's why we don't preach the gospel as an academic exercise, or lean on apologetics to reason people into faith. The fountain of living water has to be poured out on them. How did Jesus say we drink from it?

Read it again, verse 38: "Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water.'" The only requirement for drinking from this well is faith alone, in Christ alone. I can't urge you enough: lay down religion. Lay down your good works, your penance and your self-punishment, your guilt and shame, and come again to the fountain of living water by faith alone, and receive Jesus Christ, his life, death and resurrection on your behalf. This is who we're called to be.

And when you go out to people who are desperately thirsty, as you once were and as you often are, the message on your lips is this message alone: all who thirst, come and drink; believe in the Son. That is my simple charge this morning, because it was Jesus' simple charge to this crowd: come and drink. Church, come again and drink.

That's it. A one-point sermon. Jesus did it. And if you're looking for the takeaway, the practical part, because you want to drink but don't know how, let me say this: there will be a day when you stand before your King.

I want to make this as black and white as possible. You will stand before your King, and you will answer to him either with the record of your own deeds or with the record of the life, death and resurrection of Jesus Christ. That's the account you'll give before your Father in heaven. If your hope before the judgment seat of God is that someone stood in for you, then that's what you drink day in and day out. You forsake your own lesser righteousness and say, thank you, Jesus. Thank you, Jesus.

Let your sin drive you to him. Let your acts of righteousness drive you to him. Let all of it point to him. That's what it means to drink from him and share in him. Anything other than that calls for repentance, for confessing it to the Lord and saying, I've turned again to my own righteousness. I've turned again to my sin and my filth. I've turned again to my good deeds, and I lay it all down.

And I come again to the throne of grace that I might drink. So let's pray to that end. Confess the dirty water you've been drinking, spit it out, and drink again from the fountain of living water, because that invitation is eternal. And then speak the gospel to a brother or sister who is struggling in their faith, so that they might drink it up as it flows from your heart for them.
