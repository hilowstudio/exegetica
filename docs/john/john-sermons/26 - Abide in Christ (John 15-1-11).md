In first-century Palestine, when you started a vineyard, you didn't let the vines bear fruit for the first three years.

You'd prune them, and prune them, and prune them, so that over those three years they'd grow in size and strength until they could bear the weight of fruit. Then, once a vine was bearing fruit, from about the fourth year on, it went through an annual pruning: for three months of the year the vinedresser would go and clean off the shoots. There were three ways to tend a vine once it was bearing fruit. First, you'd top the branches.

That means shortening them when they get too long. Think about how a vine works; I don't want to assume we all know, since most of us aren't farmers. The vine supplies sap to the branches. You top them off, shortening the branch so it isn't spending sap on growing more branch, but puts its energy into more fruit. But if we won't be shaped by the word alone, and none of us is that simple, the Lord is kind enough to treat us as children and bring every other kind of pruning and cleansing into our lives, so that we bear the fruit he will produce in us. So the takeaway this morning is, first, breathe out, if you are a true branch. The fruit in your life is evidence of your salvation. And if you can't see it, turn to a brother or sister who knows you and ask them to tell you about the fruit in your life. It may just be that you have a nasty inner voice. That's common enough.

Some years ago our church did a marriage study using Tim Keller's book The Meaning of Marriage. My favorite part was an exercise that asked us to recognize that the Holy Spirit has given you a unique position, as a spouse, to see the work he's doing in this other brother or sister in the faith that maybe no one else can see. The challenge was simply to turn to your spouse and tell them two or three or four or five things you see the Lord doing in them right now, over the last year, the last ten years, whatever. It was meaningful. I don't think we ever got to the next activity.

The room just became full of spouses building one another up, telling each other, this is where I see Jesus working in you right now. And your spouse isn't the only one who can do that. That's the nature of life together as believers. Turn to friends who know you and ask, I don't see fruit; do you?

And receive encouragement from a brother or sister in the faith. And if everyone says, no, I don't see it, then come and see me. My concern this morning is that we'll go into another year treating abiding like a job, like a law, that some of us will feel okay because of our church attendance, as if abiding in church were the same thing as abiding in the vine, as if our Bible reading or our prayer were the same thing as abiding in the vine. Your activities flow out of your abiding.

Your activities are not your abiding. Getting these categories right will matter a great deal for your peace and your growth this year. Be first. Then, from your state of being, do, so that your works testify to your state of being. Does that make sense? Let's pray to that end together.
