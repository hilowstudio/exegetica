# John 5 quiz: the healing at the pool and the Son's authority

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John
> **Covers:** John 5:1-47 (Unit 8, "Sign 3 and the Son's authority," Lessons 1-5, with sermon 06, "The Savior's Authority," and the "Jesus on Trial," "Only God Can," and "Already / Not Yet" handouts)
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 21 multiple-choice questions at 2 points each, 2 short written answers at 4 points each.
> **Total:** 50 points, about 40 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz, so keep your ESV open. Many of the answer choices are things people often say about this chapter. Your job is to find the answer the text itself gives. The choices use plain words, with no quotation marks or verse references, so find the verse named in each question and read it before you choose. The multiple-choice questions are worth 2 points each. The two written answers at the end are worth 4 points each and are graded by hand.

## Part 1: the man at the pool (John 5:1-9)

**1. "One man was there who had been an invalid for thirty-eight years" (5:5). When Jesus asks if he wants to be healed, what does the man say is stopping him (5:7)?**
- A. He doesn't believe God would heal someone who has been sick as long as he has.
- B. Nobody will help him into the water when it's stirred, and someone always beats him in. ✓
- C. He thinks his illness is a punishment he has to finish serving before God will help him.
- D. He has made peace with being sick and has stopped trying to reach the water at all.

*Feedback: John 5:2-7. "Sir, I have no one to put me into the pool when the water is stirred up, and while I am going another steps down before me." Thirty-eight years is longer than most of your parents have been alive. He lies among a crowd of blind, lame, and paralyzed people, and he keeps trying ("while I am going"), so option D doesn't fit. He has no helper, no way in, and nobody reaching for him. That's the man Jesus walks up to.*

**2. Jesus "saw him lying there and knew that he had already been there a long time," and he asked him, "Do you want to be healed?" (5:6). Why ask a man who has been sick for thirty-eight years a question with such an obvious answer?**
- A. To scold him for lying by the pool for years instead of trying harder to get in.
- B. To find out whether the man had enough faith for the healing to work on him.
- C. To learn how long the man had been sick, since nobody had told him yet.
- D. The question brings his helplessness into the open, and that's where Jesus meets him. ✓

*Feedback: John 5:6-8. John says Jesus already "knew that he had already been there a long time," so the question wasn't for Jesus' information. The man never even says yes. He says, "I have no one" (5:7). The question brings his helplessness into the open and stirs a hope he had given up on. Then Jesus answers it himself: "Get up, take up your bed, and walk" (5:8). There's no scolding and no condition, and the man shows no faith before he's healed, so option B can't be right. Grace walks up to him and asks if he wants it.*

**3. Your ESV jumps from verse 3 to verse 5. The footnote prints verse 4, about an angel who stirred the water so that the first one in was healed. Why is verse 4 in a footnote?**
- A. The oldest copies of John don't have it, so someone likely added it later to explain verse 7. ✓
- B. The translators found the angel story embarrassing, so they moved it down where fewer people would read it.
- C. The verse was damaged in the oldest scroll, and scholars aren't sure what it said.
- D. Jesus' enemies added the angel later so the healing would look like the pool's magic.

*Feedback: John 5:3-7 and the ESV footnote at 5:3. The footnote says "some manuscripts insert" these words, which means the oldest copies don't have them. Scholars compare thousands of early copies, and when a line shows up only in later ones, a careful Bible tells you so and still prints the words for you. That's a reason to trust your Bible: nothing is hidden. Option C gets it backward, since we know exactly what verse 4 says. The man's own words in verse 7 still mention the stirred water, so people at the pool really did hope in it, and John reports what the man hoped. Nothing in the story hangs on the water. Jesus never touches it. He heals with a word.*

**4. Jesus says, "Get up, take up your bed, and walk," and "at once the man was healed" (5:8-9). How does the healing happen?**
- A. Jesus helps him down into the pool the next time the water is stirred, and he's healed.
- B. The man finally believes strongly enough, and his faith makes his legs well.
- C. Jesus speaks, and the man is healed at once, without the water and without asking. ✓
- D. He gets stronger over the next few days as he practices walking on his own.

*Feedback: John 5:8-9, with 4:50 and 1:3. One sentence does what thirty-eight years and a pool never could: "at once the man was healed, and he took up his bed and walked." It's the same word that healed the official's son from miles away ("Go; your son will live," 4:50), the Word through whom "all things were made" (1:3). Option B can't be right: the man never asks Jesus to heal him and doesn't even know who Jesus is (5:13). The power is in Jesus' word.*

## Part 2: Jesus on trial (John 5:10-18)

**5. "Now that day was the Sabbath" (5:9). The leaders tell the healed man, "It is the Sabbath, and it is not lawful for you to take up your bed" (5:10). What was the problem with carrying his mat?**
- A. Their tradition counted carrying a load from one place to another as Sabbath work. ✓
- B. A healed man had to show himself to the priest before he could carry anything home.
- C. The mat belonged to the pool's owners, so carrying it away counted as stealing it.
- D. The Ten Commandments name carrying a bed, word for word, as forbidden on the Sabbath.

*Feedback: John 5:9-10, with Jeremiah 17:21-22. God's law said to keep the Sabbath holy and do no work, and through Jeremiah God had said, "do not carry a burden out of your houses on the Sabbath" (Jeremiah 17:22). To guard that command, the teachers' tradition, later written down in the Mishnah, listed thirty-nine kinds of forbidden work, and carrying something from one domain to another was one of them. They were trying to honor God. And Jesus told the man to pick up his bed on that very day (5:8). He started this argument on purpose.*

**6. On your trial sheet you took the witness statements. Read what the healed man says and does in 5:11, 13, and 15. What does John tell us about him?**
- A. He had begged Jesus for help for years, and afterward he followed Jesus and told the whole city.
- B. He didn't know who healed him until Jesus found him, and then he told the leaders it was Jesus. ✓
- C. He knew Jesus was the Messiah at once and boldly confessed it in front of the leaders.
- D. He refused to tell the leaders who healed him, even after he learned Jesus' name.

*Feedback: John 5:11-16. "The man who had been healed did not know who it was, for Jesus had withdrawn" (5:13). He never asked for healing, didn't know Jesus' name, and never says he believes. Then "Afterward Jesus found him in the temple" (5:14). Jesus went looking for him. The man's last statement hands the leaders Jesus' name, and the persecution follows (5:15-16). John doesn't tell us why he went and told them, so we don't guess. What John does show is grace landing on a man with nothing to offer: Jesus healed him before he believed, before he knew him, and before he said thank you. Remember this man when you reach chapter 9, where another healed man gets bolder about Jesus the harder he's pressed.*

**7. In class we asked what could be worse than being sick for thirty-eight years. Jesus tells the man, "See, you are well! Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen to you" (5:14). What is Jesus saying? (Compare 9:1-3.)**
- A. Every sickness is God paying people back for their own sins, so the man had earned his illness.
- B. If the man sinned again, Jesus would take back the healing and he'd be lame once more.
- C. Sin isn't a big deal to Jesus, so this is friendly advice for living a happier life.
- D. Facing God's judgment with sin unforgiven is worse than any sickness of the body. ✓

*Feedback: John 5:14, with 9:1-3, 5:29, and 5:24. Jesus takes sin seriously, and he cares about the whole man. A healed body is a small thing next to a forgiven soul, and the worse thing he warns about is the judgment he speaks of a few verses later (5:29). John doesn't say what caused this man's illness. In chapter 9 the disciples ask about a blind man, "who sinned, this man or his parents?" and Jesus answers, "It was not that this man sinned, or his parents, but that the works of God might be displayed in him" (9:2-3). So sickness isn't a scoreboard of a person's own sins, and option A is the cruel idea Jesus shuts down. The rescue from it is in this same chapter: whoever hears Jesus and believes "does not come into judgment" (5:24).*

**8. Verse 18 gives the two charges against Jesus: "not only was he breaking the Sabbath, but he was even calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God." His defense is 5:17: "My Father is working until now, and I am working." Does Jesus deny the charges?**
- A. He denies them. He only meant that God is everyone's Father, so he claimed nothing special.
- B. He denies them. He was claiming to be a prophet, and the leaders overreacted to his words.
- C. He doesn't deny them. He calls God his own Father and claims his Father's work as his own. ✓
- D. He doesn't deny them at first, but later in the chapter he takes the claim back to calm them.

*Feedback: John 5:17-23, with Philippians 2:6 and 1:1. Jesus denies neither charge. He answers the Sabbath charge by calling the work his Father's and his own, and that answer is exactly what makes the second charge stick. If the leaders had misunderstood him, this was his chance to clear it up. Instead he says, "Truly, truly," and goes further: the Son does whatever the Father does, gives life, judges everyone, and is owed the Father's own honor (5:19-23). The word John uses for "equal" is the same word Paul uses for Christ, who "did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped" (Philippians 2:6). Some people say Jesus never claimed to be God and that his followers said it about him later. Here the leaders heard the claim plainly and wanted him dead for it. It's what John told you on his first page: "the Word was God" (1:1).*

**9. The teachers knew God rested on the seventh day (Genesis 2:2-3). They also knew God keeps the sun rising, the rain falling, and every heart beating on the Sabbath. So God works on the Sabbath, and no one else may. With that in mind, what is Jesus claiming in his defense, "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (5:17)?**
- A. Healing doesn't count as work, so he hadn't actually done anything wrong on the Sabbath.
- B. The Sabbath was a bad rule all along, and God never really cared whether people kept it.
- C. He does the Father's own Sabbath work right alongside him, which only God may do. ✓
- D. God stopped working when creation was finished, so now Jesus does the work God no longer does.

*Feedback: John 5:16-18, with Genesis 2:2-3 and Hebrews 1:3. The teachers asked a good question: does the God who gave the Sabbath keep it himself? And they answered it rightly. God "rested on the seventh day from all his work that he had done" (Genesis 2:2), and yet he "upholds the universe by the word of his power" (Hebrews 1:3) every day, the Sabbath included. Only God may work on the Sabbath, and Jesus as good as says, that's why I'm working. He takes the Sabbath as his own working day, the place only God holds. Option A misses his own words, "I am working." Option B misses that God himself "blessed the seventh day and made it holy" (Genesis 2:3). Option D misses "My Father is working until now." Jesus didn't throw out the Sabbath. He showed them its Lord.*

## Part 3: only God can (John 5:19-23)

**10. On your case file you answered how much of the Father's work Jesus says he does. Read 5:19: "The Son can do nothing of his own accord, but only what he sees the Father doing. For whatever the Father does, that the Son does likewise." What is Jesus claiming?**
- A. Some of it. The Father hands down smaller jobs to the Son, the way a master trains a helper.
- B. All of it. The Son never acts apart from the Father and does whatever the Father does. ✓
- C. None on his own. He was only a man, so every miracle had to be done by the Father instead.
- D. Only the Sabbath work. The Son takes over the Father's work on the day the Father rests.

*Feedback: John 5:19-20, with 5:17 and 14:9. "Whatever the Father does" leaves nothing out, so the answer is all of it. "Nothing of his own accord" sits in the same sentence. A rival god would go off and act on his own. The Son never does, because he and the Father are one. Verse 20 tells you why: "the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself is doing." Jesus never has to say, "God works in mysterious ways." He always knows what the Father is doing, and he does it. So if you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus: "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9). Option D runs into 5:17: the Father "is working until now."*

**11. On your case file you found two things the Old Testament says only God can do: "I kill and I make alive" (Deuteronomy 32:39), and "Shall not the Judge of all the earth do what is just?" (Genesis 18:25). Read 5:21-22. What does Jesus claim?**
- A. He gives life to whomever he wants, and the Father has handed all judgment over to him. ✓
- B. He prays for God to give life and asks God to judge fairly, the way the prophets did.
- C. He teaches people about life and warns them about judgment, the way any good rabbi would.
- D. He will stand before the Father at the judgment and be judged along with everyone else.

*Feedback: John 5:21-22, with Deuteronomy 32:39, 1 Samuel 2:6, Genesis 18:25, and Psalm 96:13. The Old Testament is plain about both. "The LORD kills and brings to life; he brings down to Sheol and raises up" (1 Samuel 2:6). And the LORD "comes to judge the earth. He will judge the world in righteousness" (Psalm 96:13). Now Jesus: "the Son gives life to whom he will" (5:21), and "the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son" (5:22). Prophets prayed and taught and warned. Jesus claims the two jobs the Old Testament gives to God alone. Option D has it backward: the one standing in front of the leaders is the Judge.*

**12. On your trial sheet the jury had two possible verdicts. GUILTY: Jesus claims to be equal with God, and he is not. NOT GUILTY: Jesus claims to be equal with God, and he is. Then Jesus says all should "honor the Son, just as they honor the Father" (5:23). Is "just a good teacher" a fair third verdict?**
- A. Yes. Every good teacher deserves honor, and Jesus deserves more of it than any teacher who ever lived.
- B. No. A teacher who claims God's honor is either wrong about himself or far more than a teacher. ✓
- C. Yes. Honoring the Father is what really matters, whatever a person decides about Jesus.
- D. No. Jesus never claimed anything special about himself, so there's no case to decide.

*Feedback: John 5:18-23, with 5:41 and 20:28. Imagine anyone else saying it: if you want to honor God, honor me the same way. Jesus claims God's work, God's power over life and death, and God's own honor. A teacher who says all that about himself is lying, deluded, or telling the truth. He doesn't leave "good teacher" on the table. You either want him gone or you worship him. Option C is the exact thing 5:23 shuts down: whoever doesn't honor the Son "does not honor the Father who sent him." Option D runs into 5:18, where the leaders want him dead for what he claimed. Near the end of John, Thomas falls at his feet: "My Lord and my God!" (20:28).*

**13. The leaders put Jesus on trial. Then he tells them, "the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son" (5:22), and near the end he adds, "There is one who accuses you: Moses" (5:45). Who is really on trial in John 5?**
- A. The healed man, since he broke the Sabbath by carrying his bed and then reported Jesus.
- B. Jesus alone, since the leaders held the authority to judge any teacher who broke the law.
- C. The crowd at the pool, since they watched the sick man for years and never helped him.
- D. The leaders and everyone who hears him, since the one they're judging is the Judge. ✓

*Feedback: John 5:22-24 and 5:45-47. The courtroom flips. The leaders sit as judges over Jesus, and he tells them the Father has handed all judgment to him (5:22). By the end they're the ones accused, and the accuser is their own Moses (5:45). Everyone who reads John 5 ends up in that courtroom, because everyone has to decide what to do with him. In the middle of it Jesus holds out the verdict anyone can have: whoever hears his word and believes "does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life" (5:24).*

## Part 4: already and not yet (John 5:24-30)

**14. In class you rewrote 5:24 as a judge's ruling in the case of God v. a sinner who believes Jesus: "Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life." Is the case finished or still waiting?**
- A. Still waiting. The verdict comes at the last day, after God has weighed the person's whole life.
- B. Still waiting. The believer has a real chance at life, as long as they keep doing enough good.
- C. Finished. The believer earned this verdict by working up enough faith to deserve it.
- D. Finished. The believer already has eternal life and has already passed from death to life. ✓

*Feedback: John 5:24, with 20:31, 3:36, and Romans 8:1. The charge is sin, and the verdict is no judgment. Look at the verbs: the believer "has eternal life," present tense, and "has passed from death to life," already done. The case is closed the day a person trusts him. "Truly, truly" is Jesus staking his own word on it. Option C turns faith into a payment. Faith is the empty-handed look to Jesus, and the verdict rests on him. This is the promise of 20:31, "that by believing you may have life in his name," at full strength. Paul says it too: "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1).*

**15. On your verb hunt you sorted 5:24-29 into ALREADY and NOT YET. In verse 25 the hour "is coming, and is now here." In verse 28 the hour "is coming." What two resurrections is Jesus describing?**
- A. Both verses describe the same day at the end of time, said in two different ways.
- B. Verse 25 means believers rise, and verse 28 means unbelievers stay in their graves forever.
- C. Verse 25 is dead hearts made alive now, and verse 28 is every body raised at the last day. ✓
- D. Both are pictures of a fresh start in this life, and neither means the dead will really rise.

*Feedback: John 5:25-29, with Ephesians 2:1-5, Daniel 12:2, and 11:43-44. "Is now here" means verse 25 was already happening as Jesus spoke. Paul describes that hour: "you were dead in the trespasses and sins," and God "made us alive together with Christ" (Ephesians 2:1, 5). Verse 28 drops "now here" and widens to "all who are in the tombs," the bodily resurrection at the last day. Jesus borrows Daniel's words for it: "some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt" (Daniel 12:2). In John 11 Jesus acts out both when he calls, "Lazarus, come out," and a dead man walks out of his tomb (11:43-44). All rise, so option B fails, and tombs are real, so option D fails. A believer is raised twice: now, from spiritual death, and at the end, from the grave.*

**16. "For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself. And he has given him authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of Man" (5:26-27). Why can the Son give life and judge? (Compare Daniel 7:13-14.)**
- A. His life is his own, as the Father's is, and the Father made him Judge as the Son of Man. ✓
- B. He borrowed life from the prophets before him, and the people chose him as their judge.
- C. Everyone has life within, and Jesus, as a son of man like us, simply discovered it first.
- D. Life and judgment stay with the Father alone, and the Son of Man only announces them.

*Feedback: John 5:26-27, with 1:4, 5:22, and Daniel 7:13-14. We have life only because it's given to us, moment by moment. The Son has life "in himself," the way the Father does. He gives life because he has it: "In him was life" (1:4). "Son of Man" points to Daniel's vision of "one like a son of man" who came before God, and "to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom" (Daniel 7:13-14). The one who will judge the world is a man, one of us, and he is the eternal Son. Option D is ruled out a few verses earlier: "the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son" (5:22).*

**17. Jesus says that at the last day "those who have done good" will come out "to the resurrection of life" (5:29). In class we read John 6:28-29 beside it. Which comes first, life or good deeds?**
- A. Good deeds come first. People whose good outweighs their bad receive life at the end.
- B. Neither one comes first, since in the end God gives life to everyone no matter how they lived.
- C. They arrive together. Life is God's reward for anyone who tries their best, whatever they believe.
- D. Life comes first. Whoever believes already has life, and good deeds grow out of it. ✓

*Feedback: John 5:24-29 and 6:28-29, with Philippians 3:9. The crowd asks Jesus, "What must we do, to be doing the works of God?" and he answers, "This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent" (6:28-29). They asked for a list, and he pointed them to himself. Jesus has already told you who escapes judgment: whoever hears his word and believes "has eternal life. He does not come into judgment" (5:24). Nobody is graded on a curve, and nobody's good outweighs their sin before a holy God. On the last day one of two records is read over each person: the record of your own deeds, or the record of Christ's deeds for you. Paul wanted to "be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own" (Philippians 3:9). Good deeds come as the fruit of the life Jesus gives. They never pay for it. Option B runs into "the resurrection of judgment" in the same verse.*

## Part 5: the witnesses, and the one Moses wrote about (John 5:31-47)

**18. Jesus says, "If I alone bear witness about myself, my testimony is not true" (5:31). Their law required two or three witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). Read 5:33-39. Which four witnesses does Jesus name?**
- A. His disciples, the crowds at the pool, the healed man, and the angel who stirred the water.
- B. John the Baptist, the works the Father gave him, the Father himself, and the Scriptures. ✓
- C. Abraham, Elijah, the high priest in the temple, and the man who was healed at the pool.
- D. His own word about himself, his mother Mary, his disciples, and the crowds who followed.

*Feedback: John 5:31-39, with Deuteronomy 19:15. "A single witness shall not suffice... Only on the evidence of two witnesses or of three witnesses shall a charge be established." Jesus meets their own standard and goes past it. John the Baptist "has borne witness to the truth" (5:33). "The very works that I am doing, bear witness about me" (5:36). "The Father who sent me has himself borne witness about me" (5:37). And the Scriptures: "it is they that bear witness about me" (5:39). Four witnesses, all pointing at one person. Option D starts with his own word alone, the one witness 5:31 rules out.*

**19. Jesus calls John the Baptist "a burning and shining lamp" (5:35). Turn back to 1:6-9. Why a lamp?**
- A. John was the true light, and Jesus came after him to carry on the work John started.
- B. John's preaching was so fiery and harsh that it burned the people who heard it.
- C. A lamp helps people see something else, and John's light pointed them to Jesus. ✓
- D. A lamp burns out, so John's light warned that Jesus' ministry would soon be over too.

*Feedback: John 5:33-36 and 1:6-9, with 1:15 and 3:30. "He was not the light, but came to bear witness about the light. The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world" (1:8-9). A lamp is lit from somewhere else, and it shines so you can see something else. People were glad of John "for a while" (5:35), and John knew where his light pointed: "He must increase, but I must decrease" (3:30). Jesus says, "the testimony that I have is greater than that of John" (5:36). Option A has it backward. John came first, but he said, "He who comes after me ranks before me, because he was before me" (1:15).*

**20. "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:39-40). What is Jesus faulting these men for?**
- A. For studying the Bible too much. Real faith doesn't need careful reading.
- B. For not studying hard enough. More careful study would have shown them the truth.
- C. For reading the wrong books. The Scriptures they had weren't really about him.
- D. For refusing to come to him. They read the Scriptures for life and missed him. ✓

*Feedback: John 5:39-44, with Luke 24:27. These men knew their Bibles better than anyone in this room, and Jesus never faults the study. He faults what they did with it. They read for life and walked right past the Life the words were pointing to: "you refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:40). Jesus names the heart under it: "you receive glory from one another and do not seek the glory that comes from the only God" (5:44). That's a warning for every careful Bible reader. Keep studying, and look for him as you do. After he rose, Jesus did exactly this with two disciples: "beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself" (Luke 24:27).*

**21. "There is one who accuses you: Moses, on whom you have set your hope. For if you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me" (5:45-46). What is Jesus saying?**
- A. Moses wrote about Jesus, so the man they trusted stands against them for missing it. ✓
- B. Moses was wrong about many things, so his books could be set aside now that Jesus had come.
- C. Moses wrote the Messiah's name and birth year plainly, so they had no excuse to miss him.
- D. Moses would stand at the judgment and accuse Jesus of breaking the Sabbath law he gave.

*Feedback: John 5:45-47, with 1:45, 3:14-15, and Deuteronomy 18:15. They hoped in Moses, and Moses had been writing about Jesus all along. Philip saw it in chapter 1: "We have found him of whom Moses in the Law and also the prophets wrote" (1:45). You've seen some of it this year. Moses lifted up the bronze serpent, and Jesus said, "so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (3:14). Moses promised, "The LORD your God will raise up for you a prophet like me" (Deuteronomy 18:15). So the man they counted on to defend them accuses them, because they took his books and missed his subject. Jesus never sets Moses aside, so option B fails. Believing Moses leads straight to him.*

## Part 6: written answers (4 points each, graded by hand)

**22. On your case file you set what only God can do beside what Jesus claims. Write (1) one thing the Old Testament says only God can do, with an Old Testament reference, (2) where Jesus claims that same thing in John 5:19-23, with the verse, (3) what God says in Isaiah 42:8 and what Jesus says in John 5:23, and (4) what must be true about Jesus if both verses are true.**

*Feedback: A full answer names one thing the Old Testament says only God can do with a reference, shows where Jesus claims it in 5:19-23, sets Isaiah 42:8 beside 5:23, and states what must be true about Jesus if both verses are true.*

Rubric: 1 point for a God-only act with a reference (gives life, Deuteronomy 32:39 or 1 Samuel 2:6; judges the earth, Genesis 18:25 or Psalm 96:13; gives his glory to no other, Isaiah 42:8). 1 point for Jesus' matching claim with the verse (5:19, 21, 22, or 23). 1 point for both verses (God gives his glory to no other; honor the Son just as the Father). 1 point for the conclusion (Jesus shares the glory God gives no one else, so he is God, equal with the Father). Part 4 asks what follows from the verses, so it needs no profession.

Model answer: The Old Testament says only God gives life: "I kill and I make alive" (Deuteronomy 32:39). Jesus claims the same: "the Son gives life to whom he will" (5:21). In Isaiah 42:8 God says, "my glory I give to no other." In John 5:23 Jesus says all should "honor the Son, just as they honor the Father." God gives his glory to no one else, so if both verses are true, Jesus must be God, equal with the Father.

**23. Jesus says the Scriptures "bear witness about me" and Moses "wrote of me" (5:39, 46). Write (1) what these Bible experts did with the Scriptures and why, with a verse, (2) what they refused to do, with a verse, (3) one place in Moses' books (Genesis to Deuteronomy) that points to Jesus, from our study of John, with a reference from each book, and (4) how it points to him.**

*Feedback: A full answer says the experts searched the Scriptures thinking they would find eternal life there, that they refused to come to Jesus for life, names one passage in Moses' books matched to a passage in John, and explains how it points to Jesus.*

Rubric: 1 point for what they did and why (searched the Scriptures, thinking they had eternal life in them, 5:39). 1 point for the refusal (refused to come to Jesus for life, 5:40). 1 point for a passage in Moses matched to John, both referenced: the bronze serpent (Numbers 21:8-9; John 3:14-15), the prophet like Moses (Deuteronomy 18:15; John 1:45), the tabernacle (Exodus 40:34; John 1:14), Jacob's ladder (Genesis 28:12; John 1:51), or any accurate link. 1 point for how it points to Jesus.

Model answer: They searched the Scriptures "because you think that in them you have eternal life" (5:39), and yet they "refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:40). Moses wrote about Jesus when God told him to set a bronze serpent on a pole, and whoever was bitten "would look at the bronze serpent and live" (Numbers 21:9). Jesus says, "so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life" (John 3:14-15). Jesus on the cross is God's one cure for sin, and whoever looks to him in faith lives.

## Teacher notes

**Sources.** Along with the five lesson guides and sermon 06, the quiz draws on three class handouts, saved in `docs/john/john-materials/`: "Jesus on Trial" (L2) in Q6 to Q8, Q12, and Q13; "Only God Can: A John 5 Case File" (L3) in Q10, Q11, and Q22; and "Already / Not Yet" (L4) in Q14, Q15, and Q17. Sermon 06 supplies the Sabbath reasoning in Q9, the trilemma in Q12, and the two records in Q17. Stems that name a handout step also quote the verses, so a student who missed that day can still answer from the text. Sermon 06 says carrying was "number one" of the thirty-nine forbidden kinds of work. In the Mishnah's list (Shabbat 7:2) it comes last, so Q5's feedback says only that it was "one of them."

**Answer choices and references.** No answer choice carries a verse reference or quotation marks, and the instructions tell students so. Correct answers put John's words in plain language, so students have to read the verse to see which choice says what John says. Quotations live in the stems and the feedback. Choices in each question run the same length and shape, so length and an extra explaining sentence give nothing away: the correct choice is the longest in 2 of 21 questions and the shortest in 6. Where wrong answers come in pairs (Q8, Q12, Q14), the correct answer shares an opening with one of them, so there's no odd one out to spot. The key is balanced: A five times, B five, C five, D six. Forms shuffles the options, so the balance matters for the printed copy.

**How the wrong answers work.** Every wrong answer is something people believe or easily assume, and none of them is a defensible reading of the verse in the stem. Several are ruled out by a nearby verse, and the feedback names it: Q1 D by "while I am going" (5:7), Q2 C by "knew that he had already been there a long time" (5:6), Q9 D and Q10 D by "My Father is working until now" (5:17), Q11 D and Q16 D by 5:22, Q12 D by 5:18, Q17 B by "the resurrection of judgment" (5:29), Q18 D by 5:31. Q7 A says every sickness is payback for the sick person's own sin, which 9:3 refutes. The quiz never claims to know what caused this man's illness, since John doesn't say. Q17 keeps to the scale, life as a reward for trying, and "everyone gets life," which the sermon and the lessons take on directly. The sermon's "teamwork with Jesus" line isn't an option, because some students' churches teach a form of it and the lessons didn't take it on in class. Where the beliefs come from:

**Memory-verse alignment.** Unit 8 plants no new verse, so the quiz asks no one to recite anything. Q14's feedback sets 5:24 next to 20:31 and Romans 8:1, so going over it reviews 20:31. John 5:24 is the optional off-line stretch for Unit 8. Q13, Q14, and Q17 lean on it, and none asks anyone to recite it.

**Grading.** Grade the written answers for meaning and accurate references, the way you grade memory verses for sense. Content is graded and belief stays invitational: Q22 asks what follows from two verses, as Part 3 of the case file did, and Q23 asks what the text says, so any student can earn full marks without professing anything. The trial sheet's big question ("what will you do with him?") stays off the quiz for the same reason. On Q22, accept any of the case-file rows or any other accurate God-only act, and for part 4 accept "he is God" or "he is equal with the Father" when the student ties it to the two verses. On Q23, accept any accurate link from Moses' books to John, including ones from class not listed in the rubric.

**Timing.** Twenty-one open-Bible questions plus two written answers make 40 minutes tight. Q7 sends students to 9:1-3, Q17 to 6:28-29, Q19 back to 1:6-9, and Q22 and Q23 to the Old Testament. Several stems are long (Q8, Q12, Q14). Watch the time the first time you give it.

**Handle with care when you go over it.** On Q7, watch the student with sickness in the family. The quiz says what the text says: John doesn't tell us what caused this man's illness, and 9:3 shuts down the scoreboard. Lead with 5:24, the rescue from the "nothing worse" warning. On Q6, John doesn't give the man's motive for reporting Jesus, so keep it out of discussion in both directions. On Q8 and Q13, John's "the Jews" (5:10, 15, 16, 18) means the hostile leaders in this scene. Say "the leaders": Jesus, the healed man, and every early witness are Jewish, and Q13's answer puts every reader in the courtroom with them. On Q14, Q15, and Q17, "the resurrection of judgment" is sober, and the passage answers the fear in 5:24. Lead with the rescue. On Q14 option C, faith receives the verdict and never pays for it, the year's guard against "try harder to believe." On Q20, keep the rebuke off the diligent student: Jesus faults refusing to come to him, never the study. On Q3, a student may ask what else is footnoted. John 7:53-8:11 is the next one, in Unit 11, and the answer is the same: nothing is hidden, and the footnote prints it.

**For going over the quiz.** On Q9, the sermon's line is worth saying aloud: they charged him with doing what only God may do on the Sabbath, and Jesus answered, that's right, that's why I'm working. Mark 2:28 gives the Synoptic version: "the Son of Man is lord even of the Sabbath." On Q8, the disciple tier can hear the "equal" of 5:18 (isos) in Philippians 2:6. On Q11, Isaiah 42:8 is the case file's third row, and it sets up Q22. On Q10 to Q17, point out "Truly, truly" three times in the discourse (5:19, 24, 25), the case file's hint for why the 5:24 verdict is final. On Q12, the Westminster Confession cites 5:23 for worship given to the Father, the Son, and the Spirit, and to God alone (21.2). On Q15, it cites 5:25 for God's call that brings the spiritually dead to life (10.2). On Q16, it cites 5:22 and 5:27 for Christ as the appointed Judge (33.1), and the Heidelberg Catechism cites 5:22 at Q50. On Q18, the healing at the pool is one of the works that witness (5:36), and Jesus points back to it in 7:23. On Q21, Luke 24:27 and 24:44 are the course's method in Jesus' own words: "everything written about me in the Law of Moses and the Prophets and the Psalms must be fulfilled."

**Coverage.** Every question maps to a content objective from the five lessons or to a class handout. Not quizzed as items of their own: reciting 5:24 (optional stretch), the works as a witness (in Q18's feedback), 5:30 (in Q10 through 5:19), and 5:41-44 (in Q20's feedback, the part L5 lets compress).
