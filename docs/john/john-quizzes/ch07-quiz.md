# John 7 quiz: rivers of living water

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John
> **Covers:** John 7:1-52 (Unit 10, "Rivers of Living Water," Lessons 1-4, with sermons 10-12). The note on the text, 7:53-8:11, is not quizzed.
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 21 multiple-choice questions at 2 points each, 2 short written answers at 4 points each.
> **Total:** 50 points, about 40 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz, so keep your ESV open. Many of the answer choices are things people often say about this chapter. Your job is to find the answer the text itself gives. The choices use plain words, with no quotation marks or verse references, so find the verse named in each question and read it before you choose. The multiple-choice questions are worth 2 points each. The two written answers at the end are worth 4 points each and are graded by hand.

## Part 1: the feast and his hour (John 7:1-13)

**1. At the Feast of Booths, families lived for a week in shelters of branches to remember God keeping Israel in the wilderness (Leviticus 23:42-43). John 1:14 says the Word "dwelt among us," which means he pitched his tent among us. What does it mean that Jesus comes to the feast of tents (7:2, 10)?**
- A. The God who kept Israel in the desert has come to live among his people. ✓
- B. Jesus came only because his brothers had finally talked him into going.
- C. The feast was about the harvest alone, so it has nothing to do with Jesus.
- D. Jesus came to do away with Israel's feasts and start new holidays instead.

*Feedback: John 7:2-10, with Leviticus 23:42-43, Exodus 13:21, and 1:14. The booths reminded Israel that God "made the people of Israel dwell in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 23:43), and that he went before them "by night in a pillar of fire to give them light" (Exodus 13:21). Now "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (1:14). During the Feast of Tabernacles, the Word was tabernacling among his people. The God of heaven came down into a tent to live with his creation, and the people camping out to remember God's nearness had God himself in their streets. Option B runs into 7:10: he went up after his brothers had gone, in private.*

**2. His brothers dare him, "show yourself to the world" (7:4), "for not even his brothers believed in him" (7:5). Jesus answers, "My time has not yet come" and "I am not going up to this feast" (7:6, 8), and later goes up "not publicly but in private" (7:10). What does this show about Jesus?**
- A. He lied to his brothers about the feast and went up behind their backs.
- B. His Father sets his time, the hour of the cross, and no dare can change it. ✓
- C. He was too afraid of dying to go up to Jerusalem at all that year.
- D. He wanted the crowd's praise as much as his brothers did, just on his own schedule.

*Feedback: John 7:3-10, with 2:4, 12:23, and 10:18. "My time" points to the hour of his cross, the hour he spoke of at Cana (2:4) and finally announces: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (12:23). Jesus didn't lie. He was telling his brothers no: you don't set my timeline; my Father sets my timeline. Then he went up at the appointed time. Options C and D miss him entirely. He kept the timeline his Father had set and the Scriptures had foretold, and he waited for that hour patiently, for our sake: "I lay it down of my own accord... This charge I have received from my Father" (10:18).*

**3. Jesus tells his brothers, "The world cannot hate you, but it hates me because I testify about it that its works are evil" (7:7). Why does the world hate Jesus?**
- A. He was harsh and unkind to anyone who disagreed with him.
- B. He wanted power for himself and threatened the rulers of his day.
- C. He broke the law of Moses again and again, and on purpose.
- D. He tells the world the truth that its works are evil. ✓

*Feedback: John 7:7, with 3:17-19. The brothers fit right in with the world, so the world had no quarrel with them. Jesus tells the world the truth about itself, and a true diagnosis can make a patient angry at the doctor. John explained it in chapter 3: "the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil" (3:19). He also told you why the light came: "God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him" (3:17). Jesus names our sin because he came to save sinners.*

**4. "For not even his brothers believed in him" (7:5). Read Acts 1:14 and 1 Corinthians 15:7. What did the risen Jesus do for his brothers?**
- A. Nothing, since they never believed and worked against his followers for years.
- B. He left them behind, and the Bible never mentions them again after this.
- C. He appeared to James, and his brothers joined his followers in prayer. ✓
- D. He sent them away to preach in far-off lands without seeing them again.

*Feedback: John 7:5, with Acts 1:14 and 1 Corinthians 15:7. After the resurrection the disciples prayed "together with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brothers" (Acts 1:14), and the risen Lord "appeared to James" (1 Corinthians 15:7). James became a leader of the church in Jerusalem. Jesus died for those who rejected him, his own brothers among them, and you were one of them too. If he can save you, whom can't he save? Keep praying for the people you love who don't believe.*

**5. At the feast "there was much muttering about him among the people. While some said, 'He is a good man,' others said, 'No, he is leading the people astray'" (7:12). Which group was right?**
- A. The first group, since calling him a good man is the most anyone should say about him.
- B. The second group, since his teaching stirred up trouble everywhere he went.
- C. Both fell short, since he is far more than a good man and leads no one astray. ✓
- D. Both were right, since a good man can still lead people astray by accident.

*Feedback: John 7:12-13, with 5:18 and 5:23. You saw this in John 5. Someone who claims to be equal with God (5:18) and says all should "honor the Son, just as they honor the Father" (5:23) isn't merely a good man. If his claim is false, he's leading people astray. If it's true, he's far more than good. If he isn't God, he isn't even a good man. The crowd tried to stand in the middle, and they whispered while they did it: "for fear of the Jews no one spoke openly of him" (7:13). Fear keeps people quiet about Jesus. Among his people, you can ask your questions out loud.*

## Part 2: who taught you? (John 7:14-24)

**6. Jesus teaches in the temple, and the leaders marvel, "How is it that this man has learning, when he has never studied?" (7:15). Jesus answers, "My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me" (7:16). What is he claiming?**
- A. Every word he teaches comes straight from the Father who sent him. ✓
- B. He is a prophet who sometimes passes on a message from God.
- C. He studied in secret for years under the greatest rabbis in Jerusalem.
- D. He made up his teaching himself, which is why it sounded so new.

*Feedback: John 7:15-18, with 1:1 and 1:18. The leaders thought the right to teach God's word came from schooling. A scribe always quoted the teachers before him. A prophet said, "Thus says the LORD," and then went back to his own voice. Jesus says every word he speaks comes from the mouth of the Father. That's more than a prophet. John's first page tells you why: Jesus is the Word who "was with God" and "was God" (1:1), the one who has made the Father known (1:18). Option B makes him one prophet among many.*

**7. "The one who speaks on his own authority seeks his own glory; but the one who seeks the glory of him who sent him is true, and in him there is no falsehood" (7:18). What does Jesus say about himself?**
- A. He seeks his own glory, the way every great leader has to.
- B. He seeks his Father's glory, and there is no lie in him at all. ✓
- C. He is honest most of the time, like the very best rabbis.
- D. He cares more about the crowd's praise than the leaders' approval.

*Feedback: John 7:16-18. Every other teacher you will ever hear is a sinner who sometimes chases his own glory. Jesus never did. The Father sent him, and he sought his Father's glory so fully that he was willing to be shamed and killed for it, so "in him there is no falsehood." That's why you can trust every word he says, including the invitation he cries out at the end of the feast: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink" (7:37).*

**8. "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God or whether I am speaking on my own authority" (7:17). Which comes first in this verse?**
- A. Knowing comes first. Prove the teaching true by argument, then decide whether to obey it.
- B. The will comes first. Whoever wants to do God's will is the one who will know. ✓
- C. Feelings come first. A teaching is from God if it gives you peace when you hear it.
- D. Study comes first. Anyone who studies long enough will know for certain on their own.

*Feedback: John 7:17, with 6:45. Read the order: "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know." By nature it's no one's will to do God's will, so where does the will come from? From God: "Everyone who has heard and learned from the Father comes to me" (6:45). The Spirit gives the will ("It is the Spirit who gives life," 6:63), and the teaching that comes from the Father always magnifies the Son. So test what you hear by whether it draws you to Jesus. Option C makes your feelings the judge: if I like it, God must be saying it. Here's a question worth asking: if your God never disagrees with you, whom are you worshiping?*

**9. Jesus had healed a man on the Sabbath (5:8-9), and the leaders wanted him dead (7:1). He answers, "If on the Sabbath a man receives circumcision, so that the law of Moses may not be broken, are you angry with me because on the Sabbath I made a man's whole body well?" (7:23). What does his answer show about him and the Sabbath?**
- A. He is Lord of the Sabbath, and making a man whole is God's work on it. ✓
- B. He was a lawbreaker who ignored the Sabbath whenever he felt like it.
- C. He thought the Sabbath was out of date and no longer needed keeping.
- D. He was a gifted healer who happened to be working on a Sabbath.

*Feedback: John 7:21-24, with 5:8-9, 5:17-18, and Leviticus 12:3. The law said to circumcise a baby boy "on the eighth day" (Leviticus 12:3), and when that day fell on a Sabbath they did it anyway, so the law wouldn't be broken. If the sign of God's promise could be given on the Sabbath, how much more could God make a man whole on it? That's what happened at the pool: one word, "Get up, take up your bed, and walk" (5:8), and a man lame for thirty-eight years walked. In John 5 Jesus answered the same charge, "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (5:17), and the leaders understood: he was "making himself equal with God" (5:18). The Sabbath is his day, and he spends it making broken people whole. Option C misses that he never set the Sabbath aside.*

## Part 3: where he comes from and where he is going (John 7:25-36)

**10. Some people in Jerusalem say, "We know where this man comes from, and when the Christ appears, no one will know where he comes from" (7:27). How does Jesus answer them (7:28-29)?**
- A. He agrees with them and says plainly that he is not the Christ.
- B. He tells them he grew up in Jerusalem, so they are wrong about Galilee.
- C. They know his hometown, but they don't know the Father who sent him. ✓
- D. He refuses to answer and walks out of the temple without a word.

*Feedback: John 7:25-29, with 1:1 and 1:18. "You know me, and you know where I come from. But I have not come of my own accord. He who sent me is true, and him you do not know. I know him, for I come from him, and he sent me." They knew Nazareth. They didn't know that he came from the Father. A reader of John knows more than the crowd: "In the beginning was the Word" (1:1). Be slow to think you have Jesus figured out.*

**11. "They were seeking to arrest him, but no one laid a hand on him, because his hour had not yet come" (7:30). Why couldn't they arrest him?**
- A. The Father had set his hour, as Scripture foretold, and it hadn't come. ✓
- B. The temple guards were too slow to catch him in the crowded courts.
- C. He hid himself among the crowds at the feast, and no one could find him.
- D. The whole crowd believed in him and wouldn't let anyone come near him.

*Feedback: John 7:30, with 2:4, 10:18, 12:23, and 19:36. They didn't fail by accident. Jesus lived on his Father's timeline, the one the Scriptures had foretold, and no one could hurry it. When the hour came, he announced it: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (12:23). He went to the cross in obedience to his Father: "I lay it down of my own accord... This charge I have received from my Father" (10:18). And John keeps showing it all happening "that the Scripture might be fulfilled" (19:36). Add 7:30 to your hour line beside 2:4. Option D runs into 7:32: the crowd was divided, and the leaders sent officers.*

**12. "I will be with you a little longer, and then I am going to him who sent me... Where I am you cannot come" (7:33-34). The leaders guess he might go off to teach the Greeks (7:35). Where does he mean?**
- A. To the Greeks living outside Israel, just as the leaders guessed.
- B. Into hiding in Galilee until the leaders stopped hunting for him.
- C. Back to Nazareth, where his family could keep him safe from them.
- D. Back to the Father who sent him, by way of the cross and rising again. ✓

*Feedback: John 7:33-36, with 13:1 and 20:17. "I am going to him who sent me." He came from the Father, and he goes back to the Father, through the cross, the empty tomb, and his going up to heaven. John says it again before the Last Supper: Jesus knew "that his hour had come to depart out of this world to the Father" (13:1). There's an irony in option A. The leaders were wrong about where he was going, but his message did reach the Greeks, through his followers, all over the world.*

**13. "Many of the people believed in him. They said, 'When the Christ appears, will he do more signs than this man has done?'" (7:31). Read 20:30-31. Why did John write down the signs?**
- A. To show that Jesus was the greatest wonder-worker Israel ever had.
- B. To show that God heals anyone who has enough faith in him.
- C. So you may believe Jesus is the Christ and have life in his name. ✓
- D. To show that miracles matter more than anything Jesus taught.

*Feedback: John 7:31 and 20:30-31. "These are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:31). In 7:31 many said no one could do more signs than this man. A sign points past itself to Jesus, and John wrote them down so that you would believe in him and live. Option A stops at the wonder and misses the one it points to. Option B makes the sign about the faith of the person healed, and the man at the pool never even asked to be healed (5:7).*

## Part 4: if anyone thirsts (John 7:37-44)

**14. All week at the feast a priest carried water in a golden pitcher from the pool of Siloam and poured it out at the altar, while the people sang Psalm 118: "Save us, we pray, O LORD!" (Psalm 118:25). Then "on the last day of the feast, the great day, Jesus stood up and cried out, 'If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink'" (7:37). What is Jesus saying?**
- A. The priests should let him be the one to pour the water this year.
- B. Everyone at the feast should drink from the pool of Siloam to be healed.
- C. The one they prayed for is standing there, and he is the living water. ✓
- D. The feast was wasting water that thirsty people in the city needed.

*Feedback: John 7:37-38, with Psalm 118:25 and Exodus 17:6. For a week the people remembered water from the rock in the desert and cried, "Save us, we pray, O LORD!" Many take "the great day" to be the eighth day, when there was no pitcher and no water at the altar. And standing right there is the Messiah, the one they had been praying for. In effect he says, you don't need the pitcher at the altar; I am your sacrifice; I am your living water. The one who put the thirst within you is the only one who can quench it.*

**15. "Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive, for as yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified" (7:39). What is the living water?**
- A. The Holy Spirit, given to everyone who believes once Jesus was glorified. ✓
- B. The water from the pool of Siloam, which could heal anyone who drank it.
- C. Good feelings that flow out of a person who prays and worships enough.
- D. The teachings of Moses, which flowed out to Israel from Mount Sinai.

*Feedback: John 7:37-39, with 16:7 and 20:22. John tells you himself: "this he said about the Spirit." The rivers wait on the cross: the Spirit comes once Jesus is "glorified," John's word for his death, his rising, and his return to the Father. Jesus says it again the night before he dies: "if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you" (16:7). And after he rose, "he breathed on them and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit'" (20:22). Option C turns the gift into a reward for trying hard. The Spirit is given to "those who believed in him."*

**16. "As yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified" (7:39). The night before he dies, Jesus says, "if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you" (16:7). Why did the Spirit have to wait?**
- A. The disciples weren't good enough yet to be given the Holy Spirit.
- B. God hadn't yet decided whether he would send the Spirit at all.
- C. The Spirit comes only to people who have learned the whole law.
- D. Jesus had to die, rise, and go to the Father, and then send him. ✓

*Feedback: John 7:39, with 16:7 and 20:22. The God of all creation took on human flesh, laid down his life, took it up again, and went back to the Father, and from there he sends the Spirit. After he rose, Jesus "breathed on them and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit'" (20:22). Option A makes the Spirit a reward for being good enough. The Spirit is the gift Jesus died and rose to give. God gets all the glory, so all the pressure is off you.*

**17. "Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water'" (7:38). How does a person drink this water?**
- A. By coming to the temple at every feast, the way the priests did.
- B. By believing in Jesus and coming to him thirsty, just as they are. ✓
- C. By cleaning up their life first, so they're worthy to come to him.
- D. By drinking water blessed by a priest from the pool of Siloam.

*Feedback: John 7:37-38, with 4:14 and 6:35. "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. Whoever believes in me." Coming to him and believing in him is drinking, the same way 6:35 told you how to eat the bread of life. All you need is thirst. He cried it out while the officers sent to arrest him were listening (7:32, 45-46), so option C gets it backward. People who feel too dirty to come back to Jesus have misread the invitation. You don't clean yourself up before you drink. The grace you first received is the grace you need every day: come and drink, and keep coming.*

**18. At the well Jesus promised that "whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty again" (4:14). In Capernaum he said, "whoever believes in me shall never thirst" (6:35). At the feast he cries, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink" (7:37). What is he offering every time?**
- A. Water from a holy well or pool that heals the sick who drink from it.
- B. A set of rules that will keep you from ever wanting anything else again.
- C. Good feelings that come from going to church and worship every week.
- D. Himself and his Holy Spirit, the living water, for all who believe. ✓

*Feedback: John 4:10-14, 6:35, and 7:37-39. Three places, one promise, one giver: a woman at a well, a crowd in a synagogue, a city at a feast, and each time Jesus offers himself. John tells you what the living water is: "Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive" (7:39). Whoever comes to Jesus and believes receives the Holy Spirit, "a spring of water welling up to eternal life" (4:14). Jesus is what your soul needs. Sin promises to satisfy if you go a little deeper, and it only leaves you drinking dirty water. When the thirst comes back, come back to him. The grace you first received is the grace you need every day.*

**19. Some object, "Is the Christ to come from Galilee? Has not the Scripture said that the Christ comes from the offspring of David, and comes from Bethlehem, the village where David was?" (7:41-42). What do John's readers know about Jesus?**
- A. He was born in Galilee, so the Scripture was wrong about the Christ's birthplace.
- B. Bethlehem was a town in Galilee, so both sides in the crowd were right about him.
- C. He is David's son, born in Bethlehem, the ruler God promised through Micah. ✓
- D. The Scripture never says where the Christ will be born, so the objection is made up.

*Feedback: John 7:40-43, with Micah 5:2. "But you, O Bethlehem Ephrathah... from you shall come forth for me one who is to be ruler in Israel, whose coming forth is from of old, from ancient days" (Micah 5:2). The objectors knew the promise and never asked where Jesus was born. Jesus wasn't born in Galilee. He was born in Bethlehem (Matthew 2:1), of David's line (Luke 2:4). He is David's son from David's town, and Micah says this ruler's "coming forth is from of old." The objection meant to rule him out points straight at him.*

## Part 5: no one ever spoke like this man (John 7:45-52)

**20. The officers sent to arrest Jesus come back empty-handed and say, "No one ever spoke like this man!" (7:46). What does that show about Jesus?**
- A. He was a skilled speaker who knew how to win over a crowd of guards.
- B. He frightened the officers with threats, so they were afraid to touch him.
- C. He talked the officers into becoming his disciples right there in the temple.
- D. His words carry a power no one else's do, for he is the Word made flesh. ✓

*Feedback: John 7:45-46, with 1:1 and 1:14. These were officers sent to do a simple job, and they came back with nothing but a sentence: "No one ever spoke like this man!" In effect: I'm not grabbing that man; I can't touch him. They spoke better than they knew. What kind of man says, "Come to me and drink"? Only God can make that offer. "In the beginning was the Word" (1:1), "and the Word became flesh" (1:14). If you thought you could handle Jesus or mold him into what you like, take your hands off and listen to him.*

**21. The Pharisees say, "Have any of the authorities or the Pharisees believed in him? But this crowd that does not know the law is accursed" (7:48-49). What did these experts in the law miss?**
- A. Nothing, since knowing the law well is what makes a person right with God.
- B. The law couldn't save them, and its Savior stood right in front of them. ✓
- C. The crowd actually knew the law far better than the Pharisees ever did.
- D. The officers should have arrested the crowd along with Jesus that day.

*Feedback: John 7:47-52, with 5:39-40. They looked down on the crowd for not knowing the law, and they couldn't see who was standing in front of them. Jesus had told them, "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:39-40). For these men the law had become a curse. On the last day you stand with the record of your own deeds or with the record of Jesus' life, death, and resurrection. The only requirement for drinking from this well is faith alone, in Christ alone.*

## Part 6: written answers (4 points each, graded by hand)

**22. Write (1) what was poured out at the altar each day of the feast and where it came from, (2) Jesus' invitation in 7:37-38 and how a person drinks, with the verse, (3) what John says the living water is in 7:39, and (4) one other place in John where Jesus makes a promise like this, with the reference.**

*Feedback: A full answer says water from the pool of Siloam was poured at the altar, gives Jesus' invitation and says a person drinks by believing in him, names the Spirit as the living water, and gives another promise of water or bread in John with its reference.*

Rubric: 1 point for the water rite (water carried from the pool of Siloam and poured out at the altar each day). 1 point for the invitation and how to drink (anyone who thirsts may come to him and drink; drinking is believing in him, 7:37-38). 1 point for 7:39 (the Spirit, given to those who believe once Jesus is glorified). 1 point for another promise with its reference: living water at the well (4:10-14), the bread of life (6:35), or the Helper sent (16:7) and the Spirit breathed (20:22).

Model answer: Each day of the feast, water from the pool of Siloam was poured out at the altar. On the last day Jesus cried, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink" (7:37). You drink by believing in him (7:38). John says the living water is the Spirit, "whom those who believed in him were to receive" (7:39). Jesus promised the same thing to the woman at the well: "whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty again" (4:14).

**23. Jesus answers three questions about himself in 7:14-36. Write (1) whose teaching he says he gives, (2) what the crowd says about where he comes from and what Jesus says they don't know, (3) where he says he is going, each with a verse from John 7, and (4) what a reader of John knows about where Jesus came from, with a verse from John 1.**

*Feedback: A full answer says his teaching is the Father's, that the crowd knows his hometown but doesn't know the Father who sent him, that he is going back to the one who sent him, and gives a verse from John 1 on where he came from.*

Rubric: 1 point for whose teaching (the Father's, the one who sent him, 7:16). 1 point for where he comes from (they know his hometown, and they don't know the Father who sent him, 7:27-29). 1 point for where he is going (to him who sent him, 7:33). 1 point for a verse from John 1 (with God and was God, 1:1; became flesh and dwelt among us, 1:14; at the Father's side, 1:18). Every part asks what the text says, so it needs no profession.

Model answer: Jesus says, "My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me" (7:16). The crowd says they know where he comes from (7:27), but Jesus says, "He who sent me is true, and him you do not know" (7:28). He says, "I am going to him who sent me" (7:33). A reader of John knows he came from God: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God" (1:1).

## Teacher notes

**What every question asks.** What the passage shows about Jesus. Each key says something true about who he is or what he does for sinners: God pitching his tent among us, keeping his Father's hour to the cross, telling the world the truth so it can be saved, winning his own unbelieving brothers, speaking every word from the Father with no falsehood in him, making a man whole on the Sabbath as its Lord, keeping the hour his Father set and the Scriptures foretold, the living water the feast prayed for, sending the Spirit after the cross, David's eternal son, the Word no officer could seize, the Savior the law pointed to. Each wrong answer is a smaller Christ people believe in, or a belief you named in your sermons. Story details appear only as the way in, and the feedback lands on the gospel.

The note on the text (7:53-8:11) is quizzed on neither this quiz nor the John 8 quiz, by your ruling.

**Answer choices and references.** No answer choice carries a verse reference or quotation marks. Correct answers put John's words in plain language, and quotations live in the stems and the feedback. Forms shuffles the options, so the letter balance matters only for the printed copy.

**How the wrong answers work.** Every wrong answer is something people believe or easily assume, and none is a defensible reading of the verse in the stem. Many are beliefs you named: Jesus lied about the feast, he was afraid to die, he was waiting to prove himself (Q2); some people are too far gone (Q4); a good man (Q5); credentials make a teacher, or he was a prophet who sometimes spoke God's words (Q6); if I like it, God must be saying it (Q8); a lawbreaker (Q9); the arrest failed by chance (Q11); a reward for being good enough (Q16); sin satisfies if you go deeper (Q18); Jesus was born in Galilee (Q19); knowing the law makes you right with God (Q21). Baptism is not an option anywhere.

**Memory-verse alignment.** Unit 10 plants no verse. Q18 sets 6:35 beside 7:37, which reviews last week's verse.

**Grading.** Grade the written answers for meaning and accurate references. Both ask what the text says, so any student can earn full marks without professing anything.

**Handle with care when you go over it.** On Q4, watch the student whose family doesn't believe: the brothers who mocked him prayed with the church after he rose. On Q18, the student who feels too guilty to come back is the one the invitation names: "If anyone thirsts." Forgiving yourself is not a requirement for drinking; his forgiveness is. On Q2, Q11, and Q21, John's "the Jews" means the authorities hunting Jesus (7:1, 13), and the rejection turns on every reader: we were the scoffers.
