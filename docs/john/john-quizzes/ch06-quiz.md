# John 6 quiz: the bread of life

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John
> **Covers:** John 6:1-71 (Unit 9, "The Bread of Life," Lessons 1-7, with sermons 07-09 and the "Enough," "One Night, Three Witnesses," and "Chasing the Baker" handouts)
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 21 multiple-choice questions at 2 points each, 2 short written answers at 4 points each.
> **Total:** 50 points, about 40 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz, so keep your ESV open. Many of the answer choices are things people often say about this chapter. Your job is to find the answer the text itself gives. The choices use plain words, with no quotation marks or verse references, so find the verse named in each question and read it before you choose. The multiple-choice questions are worth 2 points each. The two written answers at the end are worth 4 points each and are graded by hand.

## Part 1: the Lord who is enough (John 6:1-15)

**1. Jesus asked Philip, "Where are we to buy bread, so that these people may eat?" Then John adds, "He said this to test him, for he himself knew what he would do" (6:5-6). What does this show about Jesus?**
- A. He was unsure what to do and hoped one of the twelve had a plan.
- B. He cared about their souls, but their empty stomachs didn't concern him.
- C. He let the crowd work out its own problem before he stepped in.
- D. He saw their need before anyone asked, and he knew what he would do. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:5-9. "He himself knew what he would do." In John, Jesus raises the need himself. He sees you before you see him, he knows your need before you ask, and he already knows what he will do. Philip counted money and Andrew counted loaves (6:7-9), and neither of them counted on Jesus. Option B misses that the Lord of heaven stoops to be the Lord of our daily meal. He cares about your today as well as your forever.*

**2. Jesus "took the loaves, and when he had given thanks, he distributed them to those who were seated," as much as they wanted, and twelve baskets were left over (6:11-13). In the wilderness God "rained down on them manna to eat" (Psalm 78:24). What does this sign show about Jesus?**
- A. He feeds his people himself, as the LORD fed Israel in the wilderness. ✓
- B. He gave them a stockpile of bread, so they wouldn't need him tomorrow.
- C. He got the crowd to share their lunches, and there turned out to be plenty.
- D. He was only a kind teacher, and the story shows nothing more than his kindness.

*Feedback: John 6:4-14, with Exodus 16:4 and Psalm 78:24. It was Passover, the feast of the Exodus (6:4), and a crowd had followed Jesus up the mountain with nothing to eat (6:3, 5). God once said, "Behold, I am about to rain bread from heaven for you" (Exodus 16:4). Now Jesus feeds thousands from five barley loaves, and everyone eats "as much as they wanted." He calls up the manna story to show the people who he is: the God who fed Israel in the wilderness is standing on the hillside. Option C turns the sign into a lesson on sharing, and John says Jesus did the distributing. Option B misses that he meets today's need so that we will need him again tomorrow.*

**3. Philip says two hundred denarii "would not be enough" (6:7). Later Philip says, "Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us" (14:8). God tells Paul, "My grace is sufficient for you" (2 Corinthians 12:9). It's one Greek word in all three. On your handout you asked whether "enough" is a quantity or a person. What does John show you?**
- A. Enough means having more than you need, so you never have to worry about it again.
- B. Enough means learning to want less, until you don't need anything at all.
- C. Enough is the Lord himself, standing right in front of Philip both times. ✓
- D. Enough means saving up carefully, so there is always some left for tomorrow.

*Feedback: John 6:7, 14:8-9, and 2 Corinthians 12:9. In John 6 Philip counts bread. In John 14 he asks to see the Father, and Jesus answers, "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9). Both times the enough Philip wants is standing in front of him. Paul's thorn was never taken away, and God still called his grace sufficient. Sometimes God gives twelve baskets, and sometimes the hard thing stays, but either way he is the enough. Option D is the manna lesson turned upside down: Israel's saved-up manna "bred worms and stank" (Exodus 16:20).*

**4. "Perceiving then that they were about to come and take him by force to make him king, Jesus withdrew again to the mountain by himself" (6:15). Why didn't Jesus let the crowd make him king?**
- A. He is already the King, and no crowd can make him king on its own terms. ✓
- B. He never wanted to rule over anyone, so he refused ever to be a king at all.
- C. He was afraid of what the Romans would do to him and to his followers.
- D. He was worn out from feeding the crowd and needed to rest by himself.

*Feedback: John 6:14-15, with 1:49. The crowd had just eaten their fill, and they wanted a king who would keep the bread coming. But we don't make Jesus king. Jesus is the King, and he doesn't need our help to be who he is. Nathanael already said it in chapter 1: "You are the King of Israel!" (1:49). Jesus went up the mountain alone, with his Father, and his road to the throne would run through the cross. Option B misses 1:49: he is the King, on his Father's terms.*

## Part 2: the Lord of the sea (John 6:16-24)

**5. "It is I; do not be afraid" (6:20). In Greek, "It is I" is egō eimi, which can mean "I am." You read Exodus 3:14 ("I AM WHO I AM"), Job 9:8 (God "trampled the waves of the sea"), and Psalm 77:19 ("Your way was through the sea"). What do those verses suggest about the one walking on the water?**
- A. He treads the sea the way only God does, so even his plain words point to God. ✓
- B. He is a spirit or a ghost, and that is exactly why the disciples were frightened.
- C. He is a great prophet, given power over water the way Moses and Elijah were.
- D. He is showing that anyone with enough faith can walk on water like he does.

*Feedback: John 6:19-20, with Exodus 3:14, Job 9:8, and Psalm 77:19. God is the one "who alone stretched out the heavens and trampled the waves of the sea" (Job 9:8). Now Jesus walks across the sea and says, "It is I." On anyone else's lips egō eimi is the ordinary way to say "It's me." The healed blind man says it about himself in 9:9. The weight comes from who is saying it and where he is standing: on the sea only God treads. Later Jesus says it with no doubt left: "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58). Option C misses that Moses and Elijah never walked on water. God drove the sea back for Moses (Exodus 14:21).*

**6. John never says the storm stopped. He says they took Jesus into the boat (6:21). In your debrief, what did that show about Jesus?**
- A. His main job is to fix our circumstances, so the calm sea is the real miracle.
- B. He waits until his friends have rowed hard enough before he helps them.
- C. He comes to his friends in the middle of their trouble, before it's fixed. ✓
- D. He only helps people who are brave enough not to be afraid of him.

*Feedback: John 6:16-21. "It was now dark, and Jesus had not yet come to them. The sea became rough because a strong wind was blowing" (6:17-18). Then he came to them across the water. He did whatever he had to do to get into the boat with them, and John ends the story there, because the greater thing is that he gets into the trouble with you. Option A makes him a fixer of circumstances. What Jesus offers is himself. Option D runs into verse 19: they "were frightened," and he came anyway and said, "do not be afraid." He doesn't wait for brave people.*

## Part 3: the bread that endures (John 6:22-29)

**7. The crowd crossed the sea to find Jesus. He told them, "you are seeking me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves" (6:26). What did they want, and what did they miss?**
- A. They wanted his teaching, and missed the free bread he could give them.
- B. They wanted his bread, and missed that he himself is God's gift. ✓
- C. They wanted a king, and missed that he was only a prophet after all.
- D. They wanted proof, and missed that the whole miracle was only a story.

*Feedback: John 6:25-27 and 6:35. Jesus won't be hyped up. He skips their "when" and tells them why they came: the loaves. A sign points past itself, the way a road sign points you to a city, and they stopped at the bottom of the arrow, full stomachs. They were chasing a baker, and he is the bread: "I am the bread of life" (6:35). There's a big difference between seeking Jesus as your treasure and seeking him to get some lesser pleasure. Option C gets him backward. They wanted to crown him (6:15), and he is far more than a prophet.*

**8. "Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give to you" (6:27). On your T-chart, which one belongs under FOOD THAT ENDURES?**
- A. The manna Israel gathered every morning in the wilderness.
- B. The barley loaves the crowd ate on the hillside the day before.
- C. Jesus himself, the true bread the Father gives from heaven. ✓
- D. The leftover pieces the disciples gathered into twelve baskets.

*Feedback: John 6:26-35 and 6:49. The loaves were gone by the next day. The manna spoiled overnight (Exodus 16:20), and "Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died" (6:49). The food that endures is Jesus himself: "I am the bread of life" (6:35). And look who gives it: "which the Son of Man will give to you." The Father has set his seal on him, his stamp of approval: he is the one God sent to give eternal life (6:27). Jesus isn't telling anyone to stop eating or quit their job. He is warning them about living for things that run out, and about chasing him to get them.*

**9. The crowd asks, "What must we do, to be doing the works of God?" Jesus answers, "This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent" (6:28-29). They asked about works, plural, and he named one. Why is believing a strange kind of "work"?**
- A. It's the hardest work of all, so only the most devoted people earn eternal life.
- B. It only counts once you've added enough good deeds to go along with it.
- C. It's a feeling you build up by trying harder, until you're sure you've done it.
- D. To believe is to trust someone else to do what you can't, so it earns nothing. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:27-29. They were just told that eternal life is a gift the Son of Man "will give," and their first question is what they must do. Jesus answers with one work, and it's the empty-handed look of faith: trusting him to do what you can't do yourself. Believing rests on what someone else does, so you can't take credit for it. He moves the work off you and onto God, who sent his Son to do the work at the cross, and faith itself is his gift (Ephesians 2:8). Options A and C turn believing into one more thing to be good at. Option B adds deeds to it. Jesus asked for neither.*

## Part 4: I am the bread of life (John 6:30-40)

**10. The crowd says, "Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness; as it is written, 'He gave them bread from heaven to eat'" (6:31). Read 6:32-33. What does Jesus correct?**
- A. That the manna ever happened, since the story was only told to teach a lesson.
- B. Who gave the bread and when: his Father gave it, and gives the true bread now. ✓
- C. That the manna came from the ground, since bread can't really fall from heaven.
- D. That their fathers ate manna at all, since only Moses and the priests were fed.

*Feedback: John 6:31-33, with Exodus 16:4 and 16:15. "It was not Moses who gave you the bread from heaven, but my Father gives you the true bread from heaven." Look at the verbs: the manna was given back then, and the Father "gives" the true bread right now. Then Jesus says what that bread is: "the bread of God is he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world" (6:33). The true bread is a person, and he's standing in front of them. Exodus said it plainly too: "It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat" (Exodus 16:15).*

**11. "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst" (6:35). Read it phrase by phrase. How is this bread received?**
- A. By coming to Jesus and believing in him, which the verse says two ways. ✓
- B. By never feeling hungry or thirsty again for the rest of your life.
- C. By doing the works Moses commanded, the way Israel gathered manna.
- D. By seeing enough signs that you can finally be sure he is from God.

*Feedback: John 6:35-36. The two halves of the verse say one thing twice. Coming to him is believing in him, and never hungering is never thirsting: one promise of being full. The manna ran out and spoiled. This bread never runs out, so keep coming to him every day. Option B reads it like a stomach promise, the same mistake the crowd made. Option D runs into the next verse: "you have seen me and yet do not believe" (6:36). Seeing wasn't enough. Coming to him and trusting him is how this bread is eaten.*

**12. On your handout you collected the promises in 6:35-40. A friend tells you, "Jesus would never take someone like me." What does 6:37 promise?**
- A. He takes people only after they've cleaned up their lives on their own.
- B. He will never send away anyone who comes to him, whoever they are. ✓
- C. He takes people who were raised in church and know their Bibles well.
- D. He takes anyone who has done more good in life than bad.

*Feedback: John 6:37-40. "Whoever comes to me I will never cast out." There's no list to clean up first, no family background to show, and no scale to balance. And he keeps the ones who come: "this is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day" (6:39). If you had to earn your place, you'd have to keep earning it. Nobody who comes to Jesus is turned away.*

**13. Jesus says, "I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will but the will of him who sent me" (6:38). Read 6:39-40. What did the Son come down from heaven to do?**
- A. Teach people how to earn their way into heaven by living good lives.
- B. Give every person a second chance to choose God after they die.
- C. Make his followers healthy, rich, and comfortable in this life.
- D. Lose none the Father gave him, give them life, and raise them up. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:37-40. "This is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day." Everyone who comes to Jesus was the Father's gift to him, and the Son came down from heaven to keep every one. He will not lose a single one, not even your body (6:39), so you're not going to mess this up. Your standing rests on his obedience to the Father, the way an adopted child's new birth certificate carries his new name from the day he was born. Option A turns the rescue into a self-help plan. Jesus gives life to "everyone who looks on the Son and believes in him" (6:40).*

## Part 5: the living bread (John 6:41-51)

**14. The crowd grumbles, "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How does he now say, 'I have come down from heaven'?" (6:42). Read 1:14. What is true about Jesus?**
- A. He only seemed to be a man, and his body was never truly human flesh.
- B. He was a good man whom God adopted as his Son later in life.
- C. He is a real man with a mother, and the Son who came from heaven. ✓
- D. He was an angel who took a human body to bring God's message.

*Feedback: John 6:38-42 and 1:14. Both halves are true. Jesus had a mother the crowd knew (2:1), and "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (1:14). He also "came down from heaven" (6:38, 41). The crowd thought they knew exactly where he came from, and once they realized the bread he meant was himself, they grumbled. Options A and D deny that he is truly human, and option B denies that he was the Son before he came. Only a real man who is also the eternal Son could give his flesh "for the life of the world" (6:51).*

**15. "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him. And I will raise him up on the last day" (6:44). The Greek word for "draws" is the one John uses for hauling a full net onto the shore (21:6, 11). What does Jesus say about coming to him?**
- A. Anyone can come to him whenever they decide, with no help from God at all.
- B. No one comes unless the Father brings him, and Jesus raises up the one he brings. ✓
- C. Only people who grew up knowing his family are able to come to him.
- D. People come once they have seen enough miracles to be sure of him.

*Feedback: John 6:44, with 21:6, 11 and 6:37. The word means to draw or drag, the way the disciples hauled in a net so full they could barely move it. An ox doesn't woo the plough. The Father reaches in, takes a heart that is dead in sin, and draws it into new life, and every one he draws comes. Then Jesus promises the rest: "I will raise him up on the last day." Coming to Jesus is God's work from start to finish. If you wonder whether you were drawn, the answer is in 6:37: whoever comes, he will never cast out.*

**16. "The bread that I will give for the life of the world is my flesh" (6:51). What is Jesus pointing to?**
- A. The bread he multiplied on the hillside, which he will hand out again soon.
- B. His body being cut up and given as food to the crowd in Capernaum.
- C. His example of hard work, which people should copy to earn eternal life.
- D. His death on the cross, where his own body is given so the world may live. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:51, with 1:14, 1:29, and 10:11. "For" is the word of sacrifice: "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (10:11). "Flesh" sends you back to the first page: "the Word became flesh" (1:14). And "for the life of the world" is the Lamb of God, "who takes away the sin of the world" (1:29). Option B is how the crowd heard it: "How can this man give us his flesh to eat?" (6:52). Jesus is talking about the cross, where he gave his flesh in our place, for our sins.*

## Part 6: true food and true drink (John 6:52-59)

**17. Set two verses side by side. 6:40 says, "everyone who looks on the Son and believes in him should have eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day." 6:54 says, "Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood has eternal life, and I will raise him up on the last day." What does the match show?**
- A. They describe two kinds of people, who get two different rewards at the end.
- B. Believing gives life only now, while feeding gives life only at the last day.
- C. Feeding on him means receiving him by faith, with the same life and raising. ✓
- D. Jesus wanted the crowd to eat his actual body right there in the synagogue.

*Feedback: John 6:35, 6:40, and 6:54. Mark what's the same: whoever it is, they have eternal life, and Jesus raises them on the last day. The only difference is "looks on the Son and believes in him" where 6:54 says "feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood." So to feed on his flesh and drink his blood is to receive him, his body broken and his blood shed, by faith, and 6:35 already told you this bread is received by coming and believing. In the Bible, blood marks a life given up in death: "the life of the flesh is in the blood" (Leviticus 17:11). Jesus means, I die in your place so that you may live. Trust him and his death for you, and you live on him the way you live on food.*

**18. "Whoever feeds on my flesh and drinks my blood abides in me, and I in him. As the living Father sent me, and I live because of the Father, so whoever feeds on me, he also will live because of me" (6:56-57). What does Jesus promise the one who feeds on him?**
- A. He lives in Jesus and Jesus in him, and his life comes from Jesus. ✓
- B. He will never feel hungry for ordinary food again as long as he lives.
- C. He will become like Jesus by copying his example on his own.
- D. He will stay close to Jesus only as long as he works hard enough.

*Feedback: John 6:56-57, with 15:4-5. Food becomes part of you, and you live on it. Jesus says it's like that with him: whoever feeds on him "abides in me, and I in him," and "will live because of me." He is in us and we are in him, and the life is his. Options C and D put the believer's life back on the believer's effort. In John 15 Jesus says it again with a vine: "Abide in me, and I in you," "for apart from me you can do nothing" (15:4-5). Christianity is life in Christ, received from him every day.*

## Part 7: to whom shall we go? (John 6:60-71)

**19. Jesus asks the twelve, "Do you want to go away as well?" (6:67). Why does Peter stay (6:68-69)?**
- A. He understood everything Jesus had said about the flesh and the blood.
- B. He knew there were many paths to God, and Jesus was the best one.
- C. There's no one else to go to, since Jesus has the words of eternal life. ✓
- D. He hoped Jesus would still become king and give the twelve high places.

*Feedback: John 6:63 and 6:67-69. "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life, and we have believed, and have come to know, that you are the Holy One of God." Peter echoes what Jesus had just said: "The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life" (6:63). He didn't understand everything (option A). He stayed because there was nowhere else to find life. Option B misses his point: there are not many paths to eternal life. There is one. Option D is the crowd's dream from 6:15, the king they tried to make.*

**20. Jesus told them, "unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you" (6:53), and "no one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father" (6:65). "After this many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him" (6:66). What made them leave?**
- A. Jesus gave them a long list of new rules that no one could ever hope to keep.
- B. Jesus sent them home because they had asked him far too many questions.
- C. The bread had run out, so they had no reason left to follow him any longer.
- D. He said they had no life apart from him, and that coming is the Father's gift. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:53-66. Jesus told them the truth about themselves. Apart from him they were dead, with "no life in you" (6:53), and they couldn't even come to him unless the Father granted it (6:65). There's nothing you can do; the Lord must do it. People want a say in their salvation, and many of his disciples walked away at grace. Option C misses what John says: they left after his words, "This is a hard saying" (6:60), and some did not believe (6:64). Option B can't be right either: Jesus sent no one away, and he even asked the twelve, "Do you want to go away as well?" (6:67). The key to the whole chapter is 6:63: "It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh is no help at all."*

**21. "Jesus knew from the beginning who those were who did not believe, and who it was who would betray him" (6:64). Still he chose Judas as one of the twelve (6:70-71). What does this show about Jesus?**
- A. He was fooled by Judas, the same way the other disciples were.
- B. Nothing evil catches him off guard, and he goes to the cross knowing. ✓
- C. He didn't care who followed him, as long as the crowds stayed large.
- D. He chose the twelve in a hurry and later wished he had chosen better.

*Feedback: John 6:64 and 6:70-71, with Psalm 41:9, John 13:18, and Acts 4:27-28. Jesus chose the twelve, not the eleven, knowing who Judas was. David had written, "Even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted his heel against me" (Psalm 41:9), and Jesus quotes it at the Last Supper (13:18). Nothing evil in this life catches him off guard. Judas did not take his life; Jesus laid it down (10:18). The worst betrayal in history served God's plan to save: the rulers did "whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place" (Acts 4:28). If a Roman cross can be an instrument of life, so can every evil thing God allows in yours.*

## Part 8: written answers (4 points each, graded by hand)

**22. Write (1) what the crowd asks for in 6:30-31 and the Old Testament story they bring up, (2) who Jesus says gave the bread from heaven and who gives the true bread now, with the verse, (3) what 6:35 says the bread of life is and how a person receives it, and (4) one way Jesus is greater than the manna, with a verse.**

*Feedback: A full answer says the crowd asked for a sign like the manna, names the Father as the giver of the true bread now, says Jesus is the bread of life received by coming to him and believing, and gives one way he is greater than the manna with a verse.*

Rubric: 1 point for the request (a sign, like the manna their fathers ate in the wilderness, 6:30-31; Exodus 16). 1 point for the giver (the Father, where the crowd credited Moses, and he gives the true bread now, 6:32). 1 point for 6:35 (Jesus is the bread of life, received by coming to him and believing in him). 1 point for one way he is greater, with a verse: the manna's eaters died, and whoever eats this bread lives forever (6:49-51, 58); the manna spoiled overnight (Exodus 16:20), and this food endures (6:27); the manna fed hungry bodies for a day, and whoever comes to him shall not hunger (6:35); the manna fed Israel, and this bread gives life to the world (6:33, 51).

Model answer: The crowd wanted a sign like the manna: "Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness" (6:31). Jesus said it wasn't Moses who gave that bread, "but my Father gives you the true bread from heaven" (6:32). In 6:35 Jesus says, "I am the bread of life." You receive him by coming to him and believing in him. He is greater than the manna because "Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died," but whoever eats this bread "will live forever" (6:49, 51).

**23. On your Chasing the Baker handout you collected Jesus' promises in 6:35-40. Write (1) and (2) two promises Jesus makes to the person who comes to him and believes, each with its verse, (3) which promise you would show a friend who says, "Jesus would never take someone like me," and (4) why that promise answers the friend.**

*Feedback: A full answer names two promises from 6:35-40 with their verses, picks one for the friend, and explains how it answers the fear that Jesus would turn them away.*

Rubric: 1 point for each of two promises with its verse: shall not hunger and shall never thirst (6:35); will never cast out (6:37); will lose nothing and will raise it up on the last day (6:39); will have eternal life and be raised on the last day (6:40). 1 point for the promise chosen for the friend (most will choose 6:37; accept any promise the student ties to the friend's fear). 1 point for why it answers the friend (whoever comes is welcomed, with no list to clean up first and no one turned away). Part 4 asks what the promise says, so it needs no profession.

Model answer: Jesus promises, "whoever comes to me shall not hunger" (6:35). He also promises, "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (6:37). I would show my friend 6:37, because it says "whoever." Jesus doesn't list the kind of person he takes. Anyone who comes to him, he will never send away.

## Teacher notes

**What every question asks.** What the passage shows about Jesus. Each key says something true about who he is or what he does for sinners: he sees the need first, he is the God who fed Israel, he is enough, he is the King, he walks the sea and gets into the boat with you, he is the bread from heaven, he keeps everyone the Father gives him, he gives his flesh for the life of the world. Each wrong answer is a smaller Christ people believe in: a kind teacher, a prophet and no more, a fixer of circumstances, a giver of stockpiles, a rewarder of effort. Story details appear only as the way in, and the feedback lands on the gospel.

**Sources.** The keys and feedback follow your three sermons on John 6 on the class shelf ("The Savior's Power" on 6:1-21, "The Bread of Life" on 6:22-59, and "Did I Not Choose You?" on 6:60-71), your handouts ("Enough," "One Night, Three Witnesses," and "Chasing the Baker" with its key), and the student videos (Spoken Gospel's John 6 and Sproul's "The Bread of Life"). The keys were then checked against the nine Piper sermons on John 6 and Sproul's "The Bread of Life" lecture that the course holds for you; they confirm the keys, and their lines sharpen several feedback notes (Moses and God, the seal, the cross in our place, "not even your body"). Where any source differs, the quiz follows you and the text. Sproul's lecture calls egō "another form of the verb to be"; egō is "I" and eimi is "am," as Q5 says. The twelve baskets carry no meaning in any key (you said one per disciple; the video says the tribes). Q6 says only that John doesn't mention the wind (you said it did cease). Q15 keys what 6:44 says, the Father brings and Jesus raises up, and leaves out "against his will." Q17 keys eating his flesh as receiving his body and blood by faith, and no option names or rules out the Lord's Supper. Q20 keys what Jesus told them before they left: no life apart from him (6:53), and coming is the Father's gift (6:65).
