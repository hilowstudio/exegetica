# John 6 and 7 quiz: the bread of life and rivers of living water

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John
> **Covers:** John 6:1-71 and 7:1-52 (Units 9 and 10), given together on Tuesday, Nov 17, 2026, the year John 7 was taught in one day. The note on the text, 7:53-8:11, is not quizzed. Next year each chapter has its own quiz (ch06-quiz.md and ch07-quiz.md).
> **Format:** open Bible (ESV). 25 multiple choice, 4 matching, 4 fill in the blank. No written answers.
> **Points:** multiple choice 3 each, matching 3 each, fill in the blank 3 each (the last is 4)
> **Total:** 100 points, about 40 minutes

Review copy with the answer key inline. The correct answer is marked ✓, and the line under each question is the feedback students see once grades are released.

## Instructions (students see this)

This is an open-Bible quiz on John 6 and John 7, so keep your ESV open. Many of the answer choices are things people often say about Jesus. Your job is to find the answer the text itself gives. The choices use plain words, with no quotation marks or verse references, so find the verse named in each question and read it before you choose. Each multiple-choice question is worth 3 points. In the matching, choose what each saying of Jesus shows about him, 3 points each. In each fill-in-the-blank, type the one word from your ESV that fills the blank, 3 points each, and the last is worth 4.

## Part 1: the bread of life (John 6)

**1. Jesus asked Philip, "Where are we to buy bread, so that these people may eat?" Then John adds, "He said this to test him, for he himself knew what he would do" (6:5-6). What does this show about Jesus?**
- A. He was unsure what to do and hoped one of the twelve had a plan.
- B. He cared about their souls, but their empty stomachs didn't concern him.
- C. He let the crowd work out its own problem before he stepped in.
- D. He saw their need before anyone asked, and he knew what he would do. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:5-9. "He himself knew what he would do." In John, Jesus raises the need himself. He sees you before you see him, he knows your need before you ask, and he already knows what he will do. Philip counted money and Andrew counted loaves (6:7-9), and neither of them counted on Jesus. Option B misses that the Lord of heaven stoops to be the Lord of our daily meal. He cares about your today as well as your forever.*

**2. Jesus "took the loaves, and when he had given thanks, he distributed them to those who were seated," as much as they wanted, and twelve baskets were left over (6:11-13). In the wilderness God "rained down on them manna to eat" (Psalm 78:24). What does this sign show about Jesus?**
- A. He feeds his people himself, as the LORD fed Israel in the wilderness. ✓
- B. He gave them a stockpile of bread, so they wouldn't need him tomorrow.
- C. He got the crowd to share their lunches, and there turned out to be plenty.
- D. He was only a kind teacher, and the story shows nothing more than his kindness.

*Feedback: John 6:4-14, with Exodus 16:4 and Psalm 78:24. It was Passover, the feast of the Exodus (6:4), and a crowd had followed Jesus up the mountain with nothing to eat (6:3, 5). God once said, "Behold, I am about to rain bread from heaven for you" (Exodus 16:4). Now Jesus feeds thousands from five barley loaves, and everyone eats "as much as they wanted." He calls up the manna story to show the people who he is: the God who fed Israel in the wilderness is standing on the hillside. Option C turns the sign into a lesson on sharing, and John says Jesus did the distributing. Option B misses that he meets today's need so that we will need him again tomorrow.*

**3. Philip says two hundred denarii "would not be enough" (6:7). Later Philip says, "Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us" (14:8). God tells Paul, "My grace is sufficient for you" (2 Corinthians 12:9). It's one Greek word in all three. On your handout you asked whether "enough" is a quantity or a person. What does John show you?**
- A. Enough means having more than you need, so you never have to worry about it again.
- B. Enough means learning to want less, until you don't need anything at all.
- C. Enough is the Lord himself, standing right in front of Philip both times. ✓
- D. Enough means saving up carefully, so there is always some left for tomorrow.

*Feedback: John 6:7, 14:8-9, and 2 Corinthians 12:9. In John 6 Philip counts bread. In John 14 he asks to see the Father, and Jesus answers, "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9). Both times the enough Philip wants is standing in front of him. Paul's thorn was never taken away, and God still called his grace sufficient. Sometimes God gives twelve baskets, and sometimes the hard thing stays, but either way he is the enough. Option D is the manna lesson turned upside down: Israel's saved-up manna "bred worms and stank" (Exodus 16:20).*

**4. "Perceiving then that they were about to come and take him by force to make him king, Jesus withdrew again to the mountain by himself" (6:15). Why didn't Jesus let the crowd make him king?**
- A. He is already the King, and no crowd can make him king on its own terms. ✓
- B. He never wanted to rule over anyone, so he refused ever to be a king at all.
- C. He was afraid of what the Romans would do to him and to his followers.
- D. He was worn out from feeding the crowd and needed to rest by himself.

*Feedback: John 6:14-15, with 1:49. The crowd had just eaten their fill, and they wanted a king who would keep the bread coming. But we don't make Jesus king. Jesus is the King, and he doesn't need our help to be who he is. Nathanael already said it in chapter 1: "You are the King of Israel!" (1:49). Jesus went up the mountain alone, with his Father, and his road to the throne would run through the cross. Option B misses 1:49: he is the King, on his Father's terms.*

**5. "It is I; do not be afraid" (6:20). In Greek, "It is I" is egō eimi, which can mean "I am." You read Exodus 3:14 ("I AM WHO I AM"), Job 9:8 (God "trampled the waves of the sea"), and Psalm 77:19 ("Your way was through the sea"). What do those verses suggest about the one walking on the water?**
- A. He treads the sea the way only God does, so even his plain words point to God. ✓
- B. He is a spirit or a ghost, and that is exactly why the disciples were frightened.
- C. He is a great prophet, given power over water the way Moses and Elijah were.
- D. He is showing that anyone with enough faith can walk on water like he does.

*Feedback: John 6:19-20, with Exodus 3:14, Job 9:8, and Psalm 77:19. God is the one "who alone stretched out the heavens and trampled the waves of the sea" (Job 9:8). Now Jesus walks across the sea and says, "It is I." On anyone else's lips egō eimi is the ordinary way to say "It's me." The healed blind man says it about himself in 9:9. The weight comes from who is saying it and where he is standing: on the sea only God treads. Later Jesus says it with no doubt left: "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58). Option C misses that Moses and Elijah never walked on water. God drove the sea back for Moses (Exodus 14:21).*

**6. John never says the storm stopped. He says they took Jesus into the boat (6:21). In your debrief, what did that show about Jesus?**
- A. His main job is to fix our circumstances, so the calm sea is the real miracle.
- B. He waits until his friends have rowed hard enough before he helps them.
- C. He comes to his friends in the middle of their trouble, before it's fixed. ✓
- D. He only helps people who are brave enough not to be afraid of him.

*Feedback: John 6:16-21. "It was now dark, and Jesus had not yet come to them. The sea became rough because a strong wind was blowing" (6:17-18). Then he came to them across the water. He did whatever he had to do to get into the boat with them, and John ends the story there, because the greater thing is that he gets into the trouble with you. Option A makes him a fixer of circumstances. What Jesus offers is himself. Option D runs into verse 19: they "were frightened," and he came anyway and said, "do not be afraid." He doesn't wait for brave people.*

**7. The crowd crossed the sea to find Jesus. He told them, "you are seeking me, not because you saw signs, but because you ate your fill of the loaves" (6:26). What did they want, and what did they miss?**
- A. They wanted his teaching, and missed the free bread he could give them.
- B. They wanted his bread, and missed that he himself is God's gift. ✓
- C. They wanted a king, and missed that he was only a prophet after all.
- D. They wanted proof, and missed that the whole miracle was only a story.

*Feedback: John 6:25-27 and 6:35. Jesus won't be hyped up. He skips their "when" and tells them why they came: the loaves. A sign points past itself, the way a road sign points you to a city, and they stopped at the bottom of the arrow, full stomachs. They were chasing a baker, and he is the bread: "I am the bread of life" (6:35). There's a big difference between seeking Jesus as your treasure and seeking him to get some lesser pleasure. Option C gets him backward. They wanted to crown him (6:15), and he is far more than a prophet.*

**8. "Do not work for the food that perishes, but for the food that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give to you" (6:27). On your T-chart, which one belongs under FOOD THAT ENDURES?**
- A. The manna Israel gathered every morning in the wilderness.
- B. The barley loaves the crowd ate on the hillside the day before.
- C. Jesus himself, the true bread the Father gives from heaven. ✓
- D. The leftover pieces the disciples gathered into twelve baskets.

*Feedback: John 6:26-35 and 6:49. The loaves were gone by the next day. The manna spoiled overnight (Exodus 16:20), and "Your fathers ate the manna in the wilderness, and they died" (6:49). The food that endures is Jesus himself: "I am the bread of life" (6:35). And look who gives it: "which the Son of Man will give to you." The Father has set his seal on him, his stamp of approval: he is the one God sent to give eternal life (6:27). Jesus isn't telling anyone to stop eating or quit their job. He is warning them about living for things that run out, and about chasing him to get them.*

**9. The crowd says, "Our fathers ate the manna in the wilderness; as it is written, 'He gave them bread from heaven to eat'" (6:31). Read 6:32-33. What does Jesus correct?**
- A. That the manna ever happened, since the story was only told to teach a lesson.
- B. Who gave the bread and when: his Father gave it, and gives the true bread now. ✓
- C. That the manna came from the ground, since bread can't really fall from heaven.
- D. That their fathers ate manna at all, since only Moses and the priests were fed.

*Feedback: John 6:31-33, with Exodus 16:4 and 16:15. "It was not Moses who gave you the bread from heaven, but my Father gives you the true bread from heaven." Look at the verbs: the manna was given back then, and the Father "gives" the true bread right now. Then Jesus says what that bread is: "the bread of God is he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world" (6:33). The true bread is a person, and he's standing in front of them. Exodus said it plainly too: "It is the bread that the LORD has given you to eat" (Exodus 16:15).*

**10. Jesus says, "I have come down from heaven, not to do my own will but the will of him who sent me" (6:38). Read 6:39-40. What did the Son come down from heaven to do?**
- A. Teach people how to earn their way into heaven by living good lives.
- B. Give every person a second chance to choose God after they die.
- C. Make his followers healthy, rich, and comfortable in this life.
- D. Lose none the Father gave him, give them life, and raise them up. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:37-40. "This is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day." Everyone who comes to Jesus was the Father's gift to him, and the Son came down from heaven to keep every one. He will not lose a single one, not even your body (6:39), so you're not going to mess this up. Your standing rests on his obedience to the Father, the way an adopted child's new birth certificate carries his new name from the day he was born. Option A turns the rescue into a self-help plan. Jesus gives life to "everyone who looks on the Son and believes in him" (6:40).*

**11. The crowd grumbles, "Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How does he now say, 'I have come down from heaven'?" (6:42). Read 1:14. What is true about Jesus?**
- A. He only seemed to be a man, and his body was never truly human flesh.
- B. He was a good man whom God adopted as his Son later in life.
- C. He is a real man with a mother, and the Son who came from heaven. ✓
- D. He was an angel who took a human body to bring God's message.

*Feedback: John 6:38-42 and 1:14. Both halves are true. Jesus had a mother the crowd knew (2:1), and "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (1:14). He also "came down from heaven" (6:38, 41). The crowd thought they knew exactly where he came from, and once they realized the bread he meant was himself, they grumbled. Options A and D deny that he is truly human, and option B denies that he was the Son before he came. Only a real man who is also the eternal Son could give his flesh "for the life of the world" (6:51).*

**12. Jesus told them, "unless you eat the flesh of the Son of Man and drink his blood, you have no life in you" (6:53), and "no one can come to me unless it is granted him by the Father" (6:65). "After this many of his disciples turned back and no longer walked with him" (6:66). What made them leave?**
- A. Jesus gave them a long list of new rules that no one could ever hope to keep.
- B. Jesus sent them home because they had asked him far too many questions.
- C. The bread had run out, so they had no reason left to follow him any longer.
- D. He said they had no life apart from him, and that coming is the Father's gift. ✓

*Feedback: John 6:53-66. Jesus told them the truth about themselves. Apart from him they were dead, with "no life in you" (6:53), and they couldn't even come to him unless the Father granted it (6:65). There's nothing you can do; the Lord must do it. People want a say in their salvation, and many of his disciples walked away at grace. Option C misses what John says: they left after his words, "This is a hard saying" (6:60), and some did not believe (6:64). Option B can't be right either: Jesus sent no one away, and he even asked the twelve, "Do you want to go away as well?" (6:67). The key to the whole chapter is 6:63: "It is the Spirit who gives life; the flesh is no help at all."*

**13. "Jesus knew from the beginning who those were who did not believe, and who it was who would betray him" (6:64). Still he chose Judas as one of the twelve (6:70-71). What does this show about Jesus?**
- A. He was fooled by Judas, the same way the other disciples were.
- B. Nothing evil catches him off guard, and he goes to the cross knowing. ✓
- C. He didn't care who followed him, as long as the crowds stayed large.
- D. He chose the twelve in a hurry and later wished he had chosen better.

*Feedback: John 6:64 and 6:70-71, with Psalm 41:9, John 13:18, and Acts 4:27-28. Jesus chose the twelve, not the eleven, knowing who Judas was. David had written, "Even my close friend in whom I trusted, who ate my bread, has lifted his heel against me" (Psalm 41:9), and Jesus quotes it at the Last Supper (13:18). Nothing evil in this life catches him off guard. Judas did not take his life; Jesus laid it down (10:18). The worst betrayal in history served God's plan to save: the rulers did "whatever your hand and your plan had predestined to take place" (Acts 4:28). If a Roman cross can be an instrument of life, so can every evil thing God allows in yours.*

## Part 2: rivers of living water (John 7)

**14. At the Feast of Booths, families lived for a week in shelters of branches to remember God keeping Israel in the wilderness (Leviticus 23:42-43). John 1:14 says the Word "dwelt among us," which means he pitched his tent among us. What does it mean that Jesus comes to the feast of tents (7:2, 10)?**
- A. The God who kept Israel in the desert has come to live among his people. ✓
- B. Jesus came only because his brothers had finally talked him into going.
- C. The feast was about the harvest alone, so it has nothing to do with Jesus.
- D. Jesus came to do away with Israel's feasts and start new holidays instead.

*Feedback: John 7:2-10, with Leviticus 23:42-43, Exodus 13:21, and 1:14. The booths reminded Israel that God "made the people of Israel dwell in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt" (Leviticus 23:43), and that he went before them "by night in a pillar of fire to give them light" (Exodus 13:21). Now "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (1:14). During the Feast of Tabernacles, the Word was tabernacling among his people. The God of heaven came down into a tent to live with his creation, and the people camping out to remember God's nearness had God himself in their streets. Option B runs into 7:10: he went up after his brothers had gone, in private.*

**15. His brothers dare him, "show yourself to the world" (7:4), "for not even his brothers believed in him" (7:5). Jesus answers, "My time has not yet come" and "I am not going up to this feast" (7:6, 8), and later goes up "not publicly but in private" (7:10). What does this show about Jesus?**
- A. He lied to his brothers about the feast and went up behind their backs.
- B. His Father sets his time, the hour of the cross, and no dare can change it. ✓
- C. He was too afraid of dying to go up to Jerusalem at all that year.
- D. He wanted the crowd's praise as much as his brothers did, just on his own schedule.

*Feedback: John 7:3-10, with 2:4, 12:23, and 10:18. "My time" points to the hour of his cross, the hour he spoke of at Cana (2:4) and finally announces: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (12:23). Jesus didn't lie. He was telling his brothers no: you don't set my timeline; my Father sets my timeline. Then he went up at the appointed time. Options C and D miss him entirely. He kept the timeline his Father had set and the Scriptures had foretold, and he waited for that hour patiently, for our sake: "I lay it down of my own accord... This charge I have received from my Father" (10:18).*

**16. "For not even his brothers believed in him" (7:5). Read Acts 1:14 and 1 Corinthians 15:7. What did the risen Jesus do for his brothers?**
- A. Nothing, since they never believed and worked against his followers for years.
- B. He left them behind, and the Bible never mentions them again after this.
- C. He appeared to James, and his brothers joined his followers in prayer. ✓
- D. He sent them away to preach in far-off lands without seeing them again.

*Feedback: John 7:5, with Acts 1:14 and 1 Corinthians 15:7. After the resurrection the disciples prayed "together with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brothers" (Acts 1:14), and the risen Lord "appeared to James" (1 Corinthians 15:7). James became a leader of the church in Jerusalem. Jesus died for those who rejected him, his own brothers among them, and you were one of them too. If he can save you, whom can't he save? Keep praying for the people you love who don't believe.*

**17. At the feast "there was much muttering about him among the people. While some said, 'He is a good man,' others said, 'No, he is leading the people astray'" (7:12). Which group was right?**
- A. The first group, since calling him a good man is the most anyone should say about him.
- B. The second group, since his teaching stirred up trouble everywhere he went.
- C. Both fell short, since he is far more than a good man and leads no one astray. ✓
- D. Both were right, since a good man can still lead people astray by accident.

*Feedback: John 7:12-13, with 5:18 and 5:23. You saw this in John 5. Someone who claims to be equal with God (5:18) and says all should "honor the Son, just as they honor the Father" (5:23) isn't merely a good man. If his claim is false, he's leading people astray. If it's true, he's far more than good. If he isn't God, he isn't even a good man. The crowd tried to stand in the middle, and they whispered while they did it: "for fear of the Jews no one spoke openly of him" (7:13). Fear keeps people quiet about Jesus. Among his people, you can ask your questions out loud.*

**18. Jesus teaches in the temple, and the leaders marvel, "How is it that this man has learning, when he has never studied?" (7:15). Jesus answers, "My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me" (7:16). What is he claiming?**
- A. Every word he teaches comes straight from the Father who sent him. ✓
- B. He is a prophet who sometimes passes on a message from God.
- C. He studied in secret for years under the greatest rabbis in Jerusalem.
- D. He made up his teaching himself, which is why it sounded so new.

*Feedback: John 7:15-18, with 1:1 and 1:18. The leaders thought the right to teach God's word came from schooling. A scribe always quoted the teachers before him. A prophet said, "Thus says the LORD," and then went back to his own voice. Jesus says every word he speaks comes from the mouth of the Father. That's more than a prophet. John's first page tells you why: Jesus is the Word who "was with God" and "was God" (1:1), the one who has made the Father known (1:18). Option B makes him one prophet among many.*

**19. Jesus had healed a man on the Sabbath (5:8-9), and the leaders wanted him dead (7:1). He answers, "If on the Sabbath a man receives circumcision, so that the law of Moses may not be broken, are you angry with me because on the Sabbath I made a man's whole body well?" (7:23). What does his answer show about him and the Sabbath?**
- A. He is Lord of the Sabbath, and making a man whole is God's work on it. ✓
- B. He was a lawbreaker who ignored the Sabbath whenever he felt like it.
- C. He thought the Sabbath was out of date and no longer needed keeping.
- D. He was a gifted healer who happened to be working on a Sabbath.

*Feedback: John 7:21-24, with 5:8-9, 5:17-18, and Leviticus 12:3. The law said to circumcise a baby boy "on the eighth day" (Leviticus 12:3), and when that day fell on a Sabbath they did it anyway, so the law wouldn't be broken. If the sign of God's promise could be given on the Sabbath, how much more could God make a man whole on it? That's what happened at the pool: one word, "Get up, take up your bed, and walk" (5:8), and a man lame for thirty-eight years walked. In John 5 Jesus answered the same charge, "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (5:17), and the leaders understood: he was "making himself equal with God" (5:18). The Sabbath is his day, and he spends it making broken people whole. Option C misses that he never set the Sabbath aside.*

**20. "They were seeking to arrest him, but no one laid a hand on him, because his hour had not yet come" (7:30). Why couldn't they arrest him?**
- A. The Father had set his hour, as Scripture foretold, and it hadn't come. ✓
- B. The temple guards were too slow to catch him in the crowded courts.
- C. He hid himself among the crowds at the feast, and no one could find him.
- D. The whole crowd believed in him and wouldn't let anyone come near him.

*Feedback: John 7:30, with 2:4, 10:18, 12:23, and 19:36. They didn't fail by accident. Jesus lived on his Father's timeline, the one the Scriptures had foretold, and no one could hurry it. When the hour came, he announced it: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (12:23). He went to the cross in obedience to his Father: "I lay it down of my own accord... This charge I have received from my Father" (10:18). And John keeps showing it all happening "that the Scripture might be fulfilled" (19:36). Add 7:30 to your hour line beside 2:4. Option D runs into 7:32: the crowd was divided, and the leaders sent officers.*

**21. All week at the feast a priest carried water in a golden pitcher from the pool of Siloam and poured it out at the altar, while the people sang Psalm 118: "Save us, we pray, O LORD!" (Psalm 118:25). Then "on the last day of the feast, the great day, Jesus stood up and cried out, 'If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink'" (7:37). What is Jesus saying?**
- A. The priests should let him be the one to pour the water this year.
- B. Everyone at the feast should drink from the pool of Siloam to be healed.
- C. The one they prayed for is standing there, and he is the living water. ✓
- D. The feast was wasting water that thirsty people in the city needed.

*Feedback: John 7:37-38, with Psalm 118:25 and Exodus 17:6. For a week the people remembered water from the rock in the desert and cried, "Save us, we pray, O LORD!" Many take "the great day" to be the eighth day, when there was no pitcher and no water at the altar. And standing right there is the Messiah, the one they had been praying for. In effect he says, you don't need the pitcher at the altar; I am your sacrifice; I am your living water. The one who put the thirst within you is the only one who can quench it.*

**22. "As yet the Spirit had not been given, because Jesus was not yet glorified" (7:39). The night before he dies, Jesus says, "if I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you. But if I go, I will send him to you" (16:7). Why did the Spirit have to wait?**
- A. The disciples weren't good enough yet to be given the Holy Spirit.
- B. God hadn't yet decided whether he would send the Spirit at all.
- C. The Spirit comes only to people who have learned the whole law.
- D. Jesus had to die, rise, and go to the Father, and then send him. ✓

*Feedback: John 7:39, with 16:7 and 20:22. The God of all creation took on human flesh, laid down his life, took it up again, and went back to the Father, and from there he sends the Spirit. After he rose, Jesus "breathed on them and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit'" (20:22). Option A makes the Spirit a reward for being good enough. The Spirit is the gift Jesus died and rose to give. God gets all the glory, so all the pressure is off you.*

**23. At the well Jesus promised that "whoever drinks of the water that I will give him will never be thirsty again" (4:14). In Capernaum he said, "whoever believes in me shall never thirst" (6:35). At the feast he cries, "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink" (7:37). What is he offering every time?**
- A. Water from a holy well or pool that heals the sick who drink from it.
- B. A set of rules that will keep you from ever wanting anything else again.
- C. Good feelings that come from going to church and worship every week.
- D. Himself and his Holy Spirit, the living water, for all who believe. ✓

*Feedback: John 4:10-14, 6:35, and 7:37-39. Three places, one promise, one giver: a woman at a well, a crowd in a synagogue, a city at a feast, and each time Jesus offers himself. John tells you what the living water is: "Now this he said about the Spirit, whom those who believed in him were to receive" (7:39). Whoever comes to Jesus and believes receives the Holy Spirit, "a spring of water welling up to eternal life" (4:14). Jesus is what your soul needs. Sin promises to satisfy if you go a little deeper, and it only leaves you drinking dirty water. When the thirst comes back, come back to him. The grace you first received is the grace you need every day.*

**24. The officers sent to arrest Jesus come back empty-handed and say, "No one ever spoke like this man!" (7:46). What does that show about Jesus?**
- A. He was a skilled speaker who knew how to win over a crowd of guards.
- B. He frightened the officers with threats, so they were afraid to touch him.
- C. He talked the officers into becoming his disciples right there in the temple.
- D. His words carry a power no one else's do, for he is the Word made flesh. ✓

*Feedback: John 7:45-46, with 1:1 and 1:14. These were officers sent to do a simple job, and they came back with nothing but a sentence: "No one ever spoke like this man!" In effect: I'm not grabbing that man; I can't touch him. They spoke better than they knew. What kind of man says, "Come to me and drink"? Only God can make that offer. "In the beginning was the Word" (1:1), "and the Word became flesh" (1:14). If you thought you could handle Jesus or mold him into what you like, take your hands off and listen to him.*

**25. The Pharisees say, "Have any of the authorities or the Pharisees believed in him? But this crowd that does not know the law is accursed" (7:48-49). What did these experts in the law miss?**
- A. Nothing, since knowing the law well is what makes a person right with God.
- B. The law couldn't save them, and its Savior stood right in front of them. ✓
- C. The crowd actually knew the law far better than the Pharisees ever did.
- D. The officers should have arrested the crowd along with Jesus that day.

*Feedback: John 7:47-52, with 5:39-40. They looked down on the crowd for not knowing the law, and they couldn't see who was standing in front of them. Jesus had told them, "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me, yet you refuse to come to me that you may have life" (5:39-40). For these men the law had become a curse. On the last day you stand with the record of your own deeds or with the record of Jesus' life, death, and resurrection. The only requirement for drinking from this well is faith alone, in Christ alone.*

## Part 3: what Jesus says about himself (matching, 3 points each)

**26. Each line is something Jesus says about himself. Find it in your ESV and choose what it shows about him. Three of the choices are never used.**
Choices: He alone satisfies the soul's hunger for good; He welcomes every sinner who comes to him; He gives eternal life now to everyone who believes; He comes from the Father who sent him; He is a good teacher, like many other rabbis; He rewards people who try hard enough; He is a prophet like Moses and nothing more
- a. "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger" (6:35) → He alone satisfies the soul's hunger for good
- b. "Whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (6:37) → He welcomes every sinner who comes to him
- c. "Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever believes has eternal life" (6:47) → He gives eternal life now to everyone who believes
- d. "I know him, for I come from him, and he sent me" (7:29) → He comes from the Father who sent him

*Feedback: John 6:35, 6:37, 6:47, and 7:29. Each of these is Jesus talking about himself, and each says more than any teacher or prophet could say. He is the bread that satisfies, the Savior who never turns away anyone who comes, the giver of eternal life to everyone who believes, and the Son who came from the Father. The three choices left over are the smaller Christs people settle for.*

## Part 4: fill in the blank (3 points each, the last is 4)

**27. Fill in the blank with one word from your ESV: "This is the work of God, that you ______ in him whom he has sent" (6:29).**
Answer: believe

*Feedback: John 6:28-29. They asked what works they must do, and Jesus named one: believe in him. Believing rests on what Jesus does, so it earns nothing, and faith itself is God's gift.*

**28. Fill in the blank with one word from your ESV: "The bread that I will give for the life of the world is my ______" (6:51).**
Answer: flesh

*Feedback: John 6:51. Jesus is pointing to the cross. He gives his own body so that sinners may live: "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (10:11).*

**29. Fill in the blank with one word from your ESV: "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal ______" (6:68).**
Answer: life

*Feedback: John 6:67-69. When many walked away, Peter stayed. There are not many paths to eternal life. There is one, and his name is Jesus.*

**30. Fill in the blank with one word from your ESV: "Now this he said about the ______, whom those who believed in him were to receive" (7:39). (4 points)**
Answer: Spirit | Holy Spirit

*Feedback: John 7:37-39. The living water Jesus promised at the feast is the Spirit, given to everyone who believes in him once Jesus had died, risen, and gone back to the Father.*

## Teacher notes

**What every question asks.** What the passage shows about Jesus. The multiple-choice questions come from the two chapter quizzes, which were rebuilt on your sermons on John 6 and 7 (on the class shelf), your handouts, and the student videos, and checked against the Piper and Sproul sermons the course holds for you. Their teacher notes give each question's sources and the beliefs behind its wrong answers. The matching asks what four of Jesus' sayings show about him, and its three unused choices are the smaller Christs people settle for. The blanks are gospel words: believe, flesh, life, Spirit.

**Why this quiz.** This year the class taught John 7 in one day (Monday, Nov 16) to catch up, so John 6 and John 7 are quizzed together on Tuesday for 100 points. The full chapter quizzes stay for next year: `ch06-quiz.md` and `ch07-quiz.md`, each 21 multiple choice and 2 written answers for 50 points.

**Format.** Twenty-five multiple-choice questions at 3 points (75), four matching lines at 3 (12), and four blanks at 3, 3, 3, and 4 (13), for 100. No equal points per kind can make exactly 100 with these counts, so the last blank carries the extra point. Each matching line is its own drop-down in Forms, scored on its own. The blanks are graded automatically against the answers listed, in common capitalizations. Forms marks a misspelling wrong, so look over the blanks before you release grades ("Release grade: later, after manual review," set by hand as usual).

**Recitation.** John 6:35 is due at this quiz with every earlier verse (20:31, 1:12, 1:14, 3:16, 4:24). Anyone who hasn't recited yet recites as they turn the quiz in. John 7 plants no verse. Matching line a is 6:35.

**Timing.** Twenty-five open-Bible questions plus eight quick items make about 40 minutes.

**Left out to fit the period.** From John 6: questions 9, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19. From John 7: questions 3, 7, 8, 10, 12, 13, 15, 17, 19. The matching covers 6:35, 6:37, 6:47, and 7:29, and the blanks cover 6:29, 6:51, 6:68, and 7:39.
