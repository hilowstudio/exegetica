# Project guide, Q3: Teaching the Farewell Discourse

> **Quarter:** 3 (spring) · **Mode:** group (interdependent roles + confidential peer evaluation)
> **Introduced:** Tuesday, January 20 (re-entering the book) · **Due:** Thursday, March 18 (at the Farewell Discourse checkpoint)
> Read everything in the ESV.

## The assignment

> Your group is the teacher. Take your section of Jesus' last night with his friends and teach it so the class understands it and meets Christ in it.

This quarter you're not the student. You're the teacher. Your group takes one stretch of John 13 to 17 and teaches it to the class, so a person who's never opened a Bible gets it, and a person who grew up in church still learns something.

## The one idea to build everything on

Chapters 13 to 17 are the Farewell Discourse: one long conversation, one night, one dying man talking to his friends. And the whole thing is Jesus giving his frightened friends himself. Watch what he hands them, chapter by chapter:

- **John 13, the towel and the basin.** Himself as the servant-King, and his own love as the pattern for theirs (13:34-35).
- **John 14, the way to the Father.** Himself as the way home, and the promise of the Helper (14:6).
- **John 15, the vine.** Himself as the vine they live from, "apart from me you can do nothing" (15:5).
- **John 16, sorrow into joy.** The Spirit, and his finished victory, "I have overcome the world" (16:33).
- **John 17, the prayer.** Himself as the praying High Priest carrying them to the Father (17:3).

You teach this well when the class leaves having *met* one of those, the servant-King, the way, the vine, the Spirit's sender, the praying High Priest. Not just having received an outline. A group that teaches its chapter so the class meets Jesus in it is doing the very thing John did, and the very thing Jesus did that night.

## The one thing that will make or break your lesson

These chapters are full of commands: love one another, abide in me, let not your hearts be troubled. Here's the trap. If you teach "love one another" as a rule to try harder at, you've taught the branch to make fruit without the vine. Jesus commands the love in the very same breath he supplies it: "love one another, *as I have loved you*" (13:34). The command rests on the gift. "Apart from me you can do nothing" (15:5). So when you teach a command from your chapter, always show where the power for it comes from: Christ himself, given first. Never leave the class with "so try to be better." Leave them with "so stay joined to him."

## Group roles (everyone has a real, graded job)

No one carries the group and no one coasts. Assign these at the pitch, and put each person's name to a role:

- **Lead teacher / anchor**: owns the through-line, makes sure the lesson lands on Christ and names what he asks.
- **Text keeper**: owns the passages and quotes, makes sure the lesson stays true to what John actually says.
- **Maker / producer**: owns the format and the build (video, slides, art, script).
- **Audience captain**: owns reaching the whole room: the picture for the newcomer, the weight for the churched student, the questions for the class.

A group of three doubles up a role; a group of five splits the maker role. Every member teaches part of the live lesson.

## Voice and choice: pick your format

The skills are fixed by the rubric. The shape is yours:

- a taught lesson with slides and a handout
- a video or short documentary
- a set of first-person monologues from the upper room
- a piece of art or an installation with live commentary
- a written teaching piece the class reads and discusses

Whatever the format, every member has a speaking part in the live presentation.

## The milestones (the process is graded, milestone by milestone)

Your teacher will give you the exact dates. The order is fixed:

1. **Pitch.** Your section, your audience, your format, your roles (names to jobs), and why.
2. **Plan.** An evidence map: every passage and quote, and how the lesson is structured to land on Christ.
3. **Draft or rehearsal,** with peer feedback from another group.
4. **Present** the lesson live, and field questions from the class.

A fifteen-minute in-class check-in each project week is there for roadblocks. Listening groups owe each presenting group one written affirmation and one honest question.

## The Maker's Note and the peer evaluation (both required)

- **Maker's Note** (each person, individually): a few honest sentences on why your group chose this format and angle, what your role was, what was hard, what you'd do differently.
- **Confidential peer evaluation** (each person, individually): an honest, private account of who did what. This protects the people who carried real weight and keeps the grade fair.

## How to make it actually land

- **Land on Jesus, and say what he asks.** Your chapter ends on who Jesus is. Name it, and name what he's asking of the room.
- **Rest every command on the gift.** Show where the power comes from. The love is supplied before it's commanded.
- **Use the actual text.** Read the verses out loud. Point at what Jesus really says.
- **Teach it as a friend's last words.** This is a dying man comforting scared friends the night before he's killed. Teach it gently, in that key. That's where people lean in.

## Rubric

Each row scored 1 (beginning) to 4 (exemplary), multiplied by its weight, summed, and divided by 4 for the percentage. This is the group's shared score on the lesson.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| **Grasp of the passage** | 30% | The section is read accurately and in context, no misreadings; the group clearly understands it. |
| **Christ at the center** | 20% | The lesson lands the class on who Jesus is, and rests every command on what he gives. The room meets him. |
| **Independent insight** | 20% | The group's own honest thinking and a real teaching angle, not a recycled outline. |
| **Communication** | 15% | Clear, organized, prepared; reaches the whole three-tier room; every member teaches. |
| **Live Q&A** | 15% | The group answers follow-ups with real understanding. |

**Your project grade combines** this group score on the rubric + the graded milestones (process) + your individual Maker's Note + your individual contribution and the confidential peer evaluation. A strong group product with a weak individual contribution does not earn a strong individual grade.

**A word on the grade and your heart.** This grades whether your group accurately understood and taught the passage, which is content. It does not grade anyone's personal faith. Teach it so the class meets Jesus, and show me you met him in it yourselves.
