# Reading guide, Unit 21: The Spirit and the coming joy

> Passage: John 16:1-33
> Big question: it's the night before the cross, and Jesus tells his friends hard truth and deep comfort in the same breath. How do both fit together?
> New memory verse: John 16:33. You'll learn it in lesson 7.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

We're near the end of the Farewell Discourse (John 17 is Jesus' prayer, then the cross). This whole chapter does two things at once. Jesus refuses to lie about what's coming: persecution, grief, scattering, trouble. And he refuses to leave them without comfort: the Helper is coming, their sorrow will turn to joy, the Father loves them, and he has already beaten the world. Watch a dying man be completely honest and completely comforting at the same time.

## Lesson 1: warned so you won't fall (John 16:1-7)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verses 1 through 7.

Jesus tells them straight: you'll be thrown out of the synagogues, and people will even kill you thinking they're serving God. He says why he's telling them: so that when it happens, they'll remember he warned them and won't fall away. Then he says something surprising: it's actually better for them that he leaves, because his going is what sends the Helper.

- **Observe.** In verse 1, why does Jesus tell them these hard things ahead of time? In verse 7, why is his leaving good for them?
- **Interpret.** A coward hides bad news. Jesus tells his friends the truth about what's coming so the shock won't break their faith. How is being warned honestly a kind of love?
- **Respond.** Jesus says his leaving is "to your advantage" because it brings the Helper. Where do you tend to think you'd be better off if Jesus were physically here instead of present by his Spirit? Write honestly.

## Lesson 2: the Spirit puts the world on trial (John 16:8-11)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verses 8 through 11. This is a short-day, single-focus lesson.

Jesus says when the Helper comes, he'll "convict the world" about three things: sin, righteousness, and judgment. The word "convict" is a courtroom word: to prove someone guilty and wrong. The world put Jesus on trial. Now the Spirit puts the world on trial.

- **Observe.** In verse 8, what three things will the Spirit convict the world about? In verse 9, what is the "sin" really about?
- **Interpret.** Take the three one at a time. Sin: the world's deepest guilt is not believing in Jesus (verse 9). Righteousness: Jesus is proven right by going to the Father (verse 10). Judgment: the ruler of this world (Satan) is already condemned (verse 11). How does each one point straight back to Jesus?
- **Go deeper (optional).** The Spirit does this "convicting" through the church telling people about Jesus. How does that make sharing your faith part of the Spirit's courtroom work?

## Lesson 3: he will glorify me (John 16:12-15)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verses 12 through 15.

Jesus has more to say than they can handle right now, so he promises the Spirit of truth will "guide you into all the truth." And he says exactly what the Spirit's aim is: "he will glorify me." The Spirit takes what belongs to Jesus and makes it known.

- **Observe.** In verse 13, what will the Spirit of truth do? In verse 14, whom will the Spirit glorify?
- **Interpret.** Some people claim the Spirit gave them brand-new revelations that go beyond or around the Bible. But Jesus says the Spirit "will not speak on his own authority" and "will glorify me." How does that guard against someone saying "the Spirit told me" to justify anything they want?
- **Go deeper (optional).** The Spirit takes what is Jesus', and Jesus says everything the Father has is his too (verse 15). What does that tell you about how the Father, Son, and Spirit work together?

## Lesson 4: your sorrow will turn into joy (John 16:16-22)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verses 16 through 22.

Jesus says "a little while" and they won't see him, then "a little while" and they will. The disciples are confused. So he explains with a picture: they'll grieve like a woman in labor, but the pain will give way to a joy no one can take, the way labor gives way to holding the baby.

- **Observe.** In verse 20, what will happen to their sorrow? In verse 22, what does Jesus say about the joy that's coming?
- **Interpret.** Notice the childbirth picture carefully. The joy doesn't just come after the pain. It comes through the pain, the way a baby comes through labor. How is the cross the biggest example of that, and how does it change the way you think about your own hard seasons?
- **Respond.** Jesus doesn't tell them to fake being happy. He tells grieving people their grief is real and it will turn to joy. Where do you need to hear that a hard season isn't the end of the story?

## Lesson 5: the Father himself loves you (John 16:23-28)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verses 23 through 28.

Jesus opens up prayer for them: ask the Father in his name, and you'll receive, so your joy will be full. Then he corrects a huge misunderstanding. He's not a middleman who has to talk an angry Father into loving them. He says it plainly: "the Father himself loves you."

- **Observe.** In verse 24, what does Jesus tell them to do so their joy will be full? In verse 27, why does the Father love them?
- **Interpret.** A lot of people secretly think Jesus is nice but God the Father is angry, and Jesus has to hold the Father back. Verse 27 blows that up: "the Father himself loves you." How does that change how you picture God when you pray?
- **Respond.** "Ask, and you will receive." That's not a wish machine. Praying in Jesus' name means asking for what he'd want. What's one thing you could pray for this week that lines up with what Jesus wants?

## Lesson 6: scattered, but not alone (John 16:29-32)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verses 29 through 32.

The disciples say, "now we get it, now we believe!" Jesus gently pushes back: "do you now believe?" Then he tells them the hard truth: within hours they'll scatter and leave him completely alone. And he holds his own comfort: "yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me."

- **Observe.** In verse 31, how does Jesus respond to their confident "we believe"? In verse 32, what will they do, and what does Jesus say about himself?
- **Interpret.** The disciples were sure they had it together, and Jesus knew they'd fold that very night. Yet he doesn't shame them. What does it tell you about Jesus that he grounds their future on his faithfulness instead of their confidence?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Jesus says he'll be left alone by his friends, "yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me." Hold that next to the cross. Both things are true. What comfort is there in a Jesus who kept trusting the Father even while everyone ran?

## Lesson 7: I have overcome the world (John 16:33)

**Read it.** John chapter 16, verse 33. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Jesus lands the whole discourse in one sentence. He promises two things at once: "in the world you will have tribulation," and "take heart; I have overcome the world." Trouble is guaranteed. So is his victory. The peace is found in him.

- **Observe.** In verse 33, where does Jesus say you'll find peace? What two things does he promise?
- **Interpret.** "I have overcome the world" is written as something already finished, done, standing done. He said it the night before the cross, as if the win were already in the books. How does knowing the victory is already won help you face trouble that hasn't come yet?
- **Respond.** This verse doesn't promise an easy life. It promises trouble and it promises that Jesus already beat the thing that brings it. Which half do you need more today, and why?
- **Memory verse.** John 16:33: "I have said these things to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world."

## Key words to know

- **The Helper (the Paraclete).** The Holy Spirit, "called alongside" to help. His job here: convict the world, guide the church into all truth, and glorify Christ.
- **Convict.** A courtroom word. To prove someone guilty and wrong. The Spirit proves the world wrong about sin, righteousness, and judgment.
- **A little while.** The short gap of the cross and the disciples' grief before the joy of the resurrection.
- **Overcome.** To conquer, to win. Jesus "has overcome the world," a finished victory that still stands.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus spends his last night telling scared friends the truth and comforting them at the same time. He warns them so they won't fall, sends his Spirit to convict the world and point to him, promises that their sorrow will turn to joy the way labor turns to a baby, tells them the Father himself loves them, and stays steady even as they run. Then he ends where the whole discourse was heading: trouble is coming, but he has already overcome the world. The peace was never in the circumstances. It's in him.
