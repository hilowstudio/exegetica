# Reading guide, Unit 15: Sign 7 (Lazarus)

> Passage: John 11:1-57
> Big question: when someone you love dies, does God care, and is death the end?
> New memory verse: John 11:25. You'll learn it in lesson 2.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This is the biggest sign in the whole book, and the most tender. Jesus' close friend Lazarus dies, and Jesus shows up four days late, on purpose. He cries at the grave. Then he calls a dead man out of the tomb. And this sign, the one where he gives life back, is the thing that finally gets the leaders to decide to kill him.

## Lesson 1: the report, the delay, and Thomas (John 11:1-16)

**Read it.** John chapter 11, verses 1 through 16.

Lazarus is sick, and his sisters send word to Jesus. Jesus loves them, and he waits two more days before going, so that Lazarus is dead and buried by the time he arrives. He calls death "sleep" and says he's going to wake Lazarus up.

- **Observe.** In verses 5 and 6, what does the text say about Jesus' love for this family, and what does he do right after hearing Lazarus is sick?
- **Interpret.** Jesus waited on purpose, and he loved them the whole time. How can a delay that hurts be an act of love, if he's after something bigger than a quick fix?
- **Respond.** Have you ever felt like God showed up late, or not at all? Write about it honestly. This chapter is going to speak to exactly that.

## Lesson 2: I am the resurrection and the life (John 11:17-27)

**Read it.** John chapter 11, verses 17 through 27. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Lazarus has been dead four days. Martha comes out to meet Jesus and says, "if you had been here, my brother would not have died." Jesus tells her he'll rise again. She thinks he means someday, at the end. Jesus says something bigger: the resurrection isn't just an event someday. It's him, standing right there.

- **Observe.** In verse 24, when does Martha think her brother will rise? In verse 25, what does Jesus say he himself is?
- **Interpret.** Martha believed in a resurrection "on the last day," a doctrine and a date. Jesus says, "I am the resurrection." How is it different to have your hope be a Person standing in front of you instead of an event far away?
- **Respond.** Jesus asks Martha, "Do you believe this?" It's the question of the whole chapter. How would you answer it right now, honestly?
- **Memory verse.** John 11:25: "I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live."

## Lesson 3: Jesus wept (John 11:28-37)

**Read it.** John chapter 11, verses 28 through 37.

Mary comes and falls at Jesus' feet, saying the same thing her sister did. Seeing everyone weeping, Jesus is "deeply moved" and "greatly troubled," and then the shortest verse in the Bible: "Jesus wept." He's about to raise Lazarus, and he still cries.

- **Observe.** In verse 35, what does Jesus do? In verse 33, what two words describe how he felt inside?
- **Interpret.** Jesus knows he's about to fix everything in five minutes, and he cries anyway. What does it tell you about Jesus that he doesn't rush past your grief?
- **Go deeper (optional).** "Deeply moved" is a strong word that can mean anger. Jesus cries at the grave and burns at death at the same time. Who or what do you think he's angry at?

## Lesson 4: Lazarus, come out (John 11:38-44)

**Read it.** John chapter 11, verses 38 through 44.

Jesus comes to the tomb, a cave with a stone across it. He has them roll the stone away, even though Martha warns there will be a smell after four days. He prays out loud, then shouts, "Lazarus, come out!" And the dead man walks out, still wrapped in his grave clothes.

- **Observe.** In verse 43, what does Jesus say to the dead man? In verse 44, what happens?
- **Interpret.** Back in John 5, Jesus said a day is coming when "the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God." Here he calls one dead man by name and the man obeys. What does that show you about Jesus' voice?
- **Respond.** Lazarus came out still wrapped in grave clothes, and he would die again someday. Jesus later walked out of his own tomb and left the grave clothes behind, alive forever. Why does that difference matter for you?

## Lesson 5: the sign that got Jesus killed (John 11:45-57)

**Read it.** John chapter 11, verses 45 through 57.

Many people believe when they see this. But the leaders panic. They hold a council, and the high priest Caiaphas says it's better for one man to die for the people. He means it coldly, as politics. John tells us God meant it as something much deeper. From that day, they plan to kill Jesus.

- **Observe.** In verse 50, what does Caiaphas say is "better"? In verse 53, what do the leaders decide to do?
- **Interpret.** Caiaphas meant "kill Jesus to protect ourselves." But John says he was actually prophesying that Jesus would die "for the nation" and to gather God's scattered children into one. How is it strange and amazing that God used a wicked man's words to announce the gospel?
- **Respond.** The greatest sign of life, raising the dead, is what finally gets Jesus killed. He gave Lazarus his life back knowing it would cost him his own. What does that tell you about how far Jesus was willing to go?

## Key words to know

- **Sign.** John's word for Jesus' miracles. This is the seventh and biggest one, a window showing that Jesus is the resurrection and the life.
- **Resurrection.** Being raised from death to life. Jesus says the resurrection isn't only a future event. It's a Person, him.
- **Resuscitation vs resurrection.** Lazarus was brought back to life and would die again. Jesus rose to never die again. Lazarus's raising points to the greater thing Jesus does.
- **Caiaphas's prophecy.** The high priest said Jesus should die "for the people." He meant it as cold politics. God meant it as the gospel: Jesus dying in the place of his people.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus is the resurrection and the life, not someday, but standing right in front of you, in the present tense. He shows up to death and he cries real tears and he hates it, and then he shouts it down with one word. His grave clothes are folded in an empty tomb. And he raised his friend knowing it would send him to his own cross. When someone you love dies, this is the God who weeps with you and who has already beaten the thing you fear most.
