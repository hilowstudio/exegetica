# Reading guide, Unit 27: Do you love me

> Passage: John 21:1-25
> Big question: the last chapter. The risen Jesus goes and finds the friend who denied him, and asks one question. What is it?
> New memory verse: John 21:17, the last one you'll learn. In lesson 4.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This is the final chapter of John, and it's one of the tenderest scenes in the whole Bible. Peter denied Jesus three times, warming himself at a fire, on the night Jesus was arrested. Now the risen Jesus makes breakfast on a beach, over a fire, and gently gives Peter three chances to say "I love you," one for each denial. The whole book ends here, with a failure restored and a question of love. Watch how Jesus treats people who let him down.

## Lesson 1: back to the boat (John 21:1-3)

**Read it.** John chapter 21, verses 1 through 3.

Some of the disciples are back in Galilee, and Peter says, "I'm going fishing." The others go with him. They fish all night, and catch nothing.

- **Observe.** In verse 3, what does Peter decide to do, and how does the night go?
- **Interpret.** Fishing was Peter's old job, the life Jesus first called him out of. After failing so badly (denying Jesus three times), Peter drifts back to the familiar, and the net comes up empty all night. How is that empty night a picture of the old life without Christ (remember 15:5, "apart from me you can do nothing")?
- **Respond.** When you've messed up or feel lost, where do you tend to drift back to? Write honestly.

## Lesson 2: it is the Lord (John 21:4-8)

**Read it.** John chapter 21, verses 4 through 8.

At dawn, a man on the shore tells them to throw the net on the right side of the boat. Suddenly the net is so full they can't haul it in. John says, "It is the Lord!" And Peter, the moment he hears it's Jesus, throws himself into the sea to get to shore first.

- **Observe.** In verse 6, what happens when they cast the net where the stranger says? In verse 7, what does Peter do when he realizes it's Jesus?
- **Interpret.** They fished all night with all their skill and caught nothing. One word from Jesus fills the net. What does that show about where real fruit comes from? And what does it tell you about Peter's heart that, even after denying Jesus, he can't get to him fast enough?
- **Go deeper (optional).** This catch is a lot like the very first time Jesus called Peter (Luke 5), when he said "you'll be catching men." Why might Jesus stage the same miracle now, at the end?

## Lesson 3: breakfast on the beach (John 21:9-14)

**Read it.** John chapter 21, verses 9 through 14.

On the shore, there's a charcoal fire with fish and bread already cooking. Jesus says, "come and have breakfast." He serves them himself. This is the third time the risen Jesus appears to his disciples.

- **Observe.** In verse 9, what's waiting for them on the beach? In verse 12, what does Jesus invite them to do?
- **Interpret.** Look carefully at "charcoal fire" in verse 9. There's only one other charcoal fire in the whole New Testament, and it's the fire Peter stood at when he denied Jesus three times (John 18:18). Why do you think Jesus builds a fire, on purpose, that would remind Peter of his worst night? What's he getting ready to do?
- **Respond.** Jesus doesn't scold Peter or ignore him. He cooks him breakfast first. What does it tell you about Jesus that he cares for someone's hunger before he deals with their failure?

## Lesson 4: do you love me (John 21:15-17)

**Read it.** John chapter 21, verses 15 through 17. This is the memory-verse lesson, your last one.

After breakfast, by the fire, Jesus asks Peter three times, "do you love me?" Peter, who denied him three times, gets to say "I love you" three times. And each time, Jesus gives him a job: "feed my lambs," "tend my sheep," "feed my sheep."

- **Observe.** How many times does Jesus ask Peter if he loves him (verses 15-17)? What does Jesus tell Peter to do after each answer?
- **Interpret.** Peter denied Jesus three times at a fire. Now, at another fire, Jesus gives him three chances to say "I love you." Why three? How is Jesus healing the exact wound, denial by denial? And notice: Jesus doesn't ask Peter "how much do you know" or "how sorry are you." He asks, "do you love me." Why is that the question that matters most?
- **Respond.** Jesus restores failures and then gives them something to do. "If you love me, feed my sheep." Where could your love for Jesus turn into caring for someone this week?
- **Memory verse.** John 21:17: "He said to him the third time, 'Simon, son of John, do you love me?' Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, 'Do you love me?' and he said to him, 'Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.' Jesus said to him, 'Feed my sheep.'"

## Lesson 5: follow me (John 21:18-19)

**Read it.** John chapter 21, verses 18 and 19. This is a short-day, single-focus lesson.

Jesus tells Peter that one day he'll stretch out his hands and be led where he doesn't want to go. John explains: Jesus was telling Peter the kind of death he'd die to glorify God. Then Jesus says two words: "follow me."

- **Observe.** In verse 18, what does Jesus predict about the end of Peter's life? In verse 19, what does John say it meant, and what does Jesus tell Peter to do?
- **Interpret.** Peter once swore he'd die for Jesus, and then he folded at a fire. Now Jesus tells him he really will die faithful, glorifying God. How is that prediction actually grace, not doom? And what does "follow me" cost, if it might cost your life?
- **Respond.** The coward at the fire became a man who died for Jesus, because the Spirit changed him. Where do you need courage to follow Jesus, even when it costs you something?

## Lesson 6: what is that to you (John 21:20-25)

**Read it.** John chapter 21, verses 20 through 25.

Peter sees the disciple John and asks, "Lord, what about him?" Jesus answers, "if I want him to stay until I come, what is that to you? You follow me." Then John, the writer, signs off: he's the eyewitness, his testimony is true, and Jesus did so many things that the world couldn't hold all the books.

- **Observe.** In verse 21, what does Peter ask about John? In verse 22, how does Jesus answer?
- **Interpret.** Peter just got his job and his future, and immediately he looks over at John and asks, "what about him?" Jesus tells him, gently, to keep his eyes on his own call: "you follow me." Why is comparing your path to someone else's a distraction from following Jesus? Where do you do that?
- **Go deeper (optional).** John ends the whole Gospel by saying Jesus did more than any book could hold (verse 25). After everything you've studied this year, why is that a fitting way to end a book about Jesus?

## Key words to know

- **Charcoal fire.** The specific kind of fire in verse 9, the same word used only one other place: the fire of Peter's denial (18:18). The place of failure becomes the place of grace.
- **"Feed my sheep."** Jesus' charge to Peter (21:17). Love for Christ turning into caring for his people. Peter later tells other leaders to do the same (1 Peter 5:2).
- **"Follow me."** The last thing Jesus says in the Gospel (21:19, 22), spoken to a restored failure.
- **The eyewitness.** John, the disciple Jesus loved, who saw these things and wrote them down, and swears his testimony is true (21:24).

## New memory verse this unit

Learn this one. It's your last. You'll be taught it in lesson 4:

> John 21:17: "He said to him the third time, 'Simon, son of John, do you love me?' Peter was grieved because he said to him the third time, 'Do you love me?' and he said to him, 'Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you.' Jesus said to him, 'Feed my sheep.'"

That's sixteen memory verses, the whole story of Jesus in John. Keep all of them sharp: John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16, 4:24, 6:35, 8:12, 10:11, 11:25, 13:34-35, 14:6, 15:5, 16:33, 17:3, 19:30, and now 21:17. Two of them come alive right here: 10:11 (the good shepherd) hands Peter a share in shepherding, and 13:34-35 (love one another) becomes "feed my sheep."

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The Gospel ends on a beach at dawn. The risen Lord of everything cooks breakfast for the men who ran, and goes looking for the friend who denied him. He builds a fire like the one Peter failed at, and heals the wound denial by denial, giving Peter three chances to say "I love you" and a flock to care for. He tells Peter he'll die faithful, and says the last words of the book: "follow me." That's how John ends the story of Jesus: with a Savior who restores failures, and one question that holds everything, "do you love me?"
