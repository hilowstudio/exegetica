# Key words of the Prologue (John 1:1-18)

> A keep-and-read card, not homework. These are the load-bearing words of chapter 1, and John plants them here on purpose. You will meet every one of them again, all year. Learn what they mean now and the rest of the Gospel reads clearer.

**The Word** (Greek *Logos*). John's title for Jesus before he ever uses the name Jesus. God spoke, and the speech turned out to be a person: the one who was with God and was God (1:1), and who became a man (1:14). When you see "the Word," read "Jesus, God's own self expressed."

**Flesh** (Greek *sarx*). "The Word became flesh" (1:14) means he became truly and fully human, a real body you could touch, while staying fully God. Not a costume, not a ghost, not God pretending. Fully God and fully man, one person.

**Dwelt / tabernacled** (Greek *skēnoō*). The verb means "to pitch a tent." Back in Exodus, God's glory came down and filled the tent of meeting so he could live right in the middle of his people (Exodus 40). John uses that exact word: in Jesus, God pitched his tent among us and moved into the neighborhood in a body. The same word describes the end of the story, when God tabernacles with his people forever (Revelation 21:3).

**Glory** (Greek *doxa*). The visible weight of God's presence, the brightness that filled the tent. "We have seen his glory" (1:14) is an eyewitness claim: real people watched it, in a real face.

**Grace and truth** (Greek *charis kai alētheia*). When the LORD passed by Moses at the tent, he proclaimed himself the God "abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness" (Exodus 34:6), in Hebrew *hesed we'emet*. John says the Word came "full of grace and truth" (1:14). That is the same pair. What God announced about himself to Moses, you now see in the face of Jesus.

**Only Son** (Greek *monogenēs*). The only one of his kind, unique, the one-and-only Son from the Father (1:14, 1:18). The word was used for an only child (as Isaac was to Abraham). There is no other like him.

**Made him known** (Greek *exēgeomai*). To lead out, unfold, explain. This is the root behind our word "exegete." "No one has ever seen God; God the only Son... has made him known" (1:18). You cannot see God directly. You can look hard at Jesus and see him. Jesus is God's own explanation of himself, and that is the reason this whole course exists.
