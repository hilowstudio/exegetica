# Reading guide, Unit 14: The Good Shepherd

> Passage: John 10:1-42
> Big question: whose voice do you actually follow, and who is actually holding onto you?
> New memory verse: John 10:11. You'll learn it in lesson 2.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

There's no real break between chapter 9 and chapter 10. Jesus is still talking to the leaders who threw the blind man out of the synagogue. He calls himself the Good Shepherd, and he's telling those leaders they're the bad shepherds the Old Testament warned about. Watch for two "I am" sayings in this chapter: "I am the door" and "I am the good shepherd."

## Lesson 1: the fold, the voice, and the door (John 10:1-10)

**Read it.** John chapter 10, verses 1 through 10.

Jesus paints a picture: sheep in a fold, a gate, a shepherd whose voice the sheep know. The sheep follow their shepherd's voice and run from a stranger's. Then Jesus says, "I am the door." He's the one way in, and he came to give real life.

- **Observe.** In verses 3 through 5, how do the sheep know their shepherd? What do they do with a stranger's voice?
- **Interpret.** In verse 9 Jesus says, "I am the door." In verse 10 he says he came so people can "have life and have it abundantly." Given that a thief is in the picture, what kind of "abundant life" is he promising?
- **Respond.** Lots of voices want to lead you. Whose voice are you actually following right now, and does it want you or want something from you?

## Lesson 2: the good shepherd (John 10:11-18)

**Read it.** John chapter 10, verses 11 through 18. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Jesus says, "I am the good shepherd," and in the same breath tells you what makes him good: he lays down his life for the sheep. A hired hand runs when the wolf comes, because the sheep aren't his. The good shepherd dies for his. And Jesus says no one takes his life from him. He lays it down on purpose, and he takes it back up.

- **Observe.** In verse 11, what does the good shepherd do for the sheep? In verses 12 and 13, why does the hired hand run?
- **Interpret.** Jesus says he knows his sheep and they know him, "just as the Father knows me and I know the Father" (verse 15). How deep is that knowing?
- **Respond.** The good shepherd knows his sheep by name. What would it change in a normal day to actually believe Jesus knows you by name and would die for you?
- **Memory verse.** John 10:11: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."

## Lesson 3: the two hands (John 10:19-30)

**Read it.** John chapter 10, verses 19 through 30.

It's now the Feast of Dedication (Hanukkah), and the leaders demand Jesus tell them plainly if he's the Christ. Jesus says his sheep hear his voice, and then he makes an enormous promise: his sheep will never perish, and no one can snatch them out of his hand or the Father's hand. Then he says, "I and the Father are one."

- **Observe.** In verses 28 and 29, whose two hands hold the sheep? What does Jesus promise will never happen to them?
- **Interpret.** In verse 30 Jesus says, "I and the Father are one." Why does he bring up his oneness with the Father right after talking about two hands holding the sheep?
- **Respond.** Jesus says your safety rests on his grip and the Father's grip, not on how tightly you hold on. If that's true, what fear could you set down?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 27 says his sheep "hear my voice... and follow me." How does following show that a person really is one of his sheep, without making the following the thing that saves them?

## Lesson 4: the stoning and the Psalm 82 argument (John 10:31-38)

**Read it.** John chapter 10, verses 31 through 38.

The crowd grabs stones because Jesus, a man, is making himself God. They understood him exactly. Jesus answers with a clever argument from their own Scriptures (Psalm 82), and then points them to his works as proof that he and the Father are one.

- **Observe.** In verse 33, why does the crowd want to stone Jesus? What do they say he's doing?
- **Interpret.** Jesus quotes Psalm 82, where God called human judges "gods" because they held God's office. His point: if Scripture could call those men "gods," how is it blasphemy for the one God actually sent to call himself God's Son? How does that answer the charge?
- **Respond.** In verse 38 Jesus offers a lower rung to people who can't believe him yet: "believe the works." If you're not sure about Jesus, what would it look like to start with what he actually did?

## Lesson 5: vindicated at the Jordan (John 10:39-42)

**Read it.** John chapter 10, verses 39 through 42.

They try to arrest Jesus and he slips away. He goes back across the Jordan, to the very spot where John the Baptist first pointed people to him. And there, where it all started, many people believe.

- **Observe.** In verses 41 and 42, what do the people say about John the Baptist, and what do they do about Jesus?
- **Interpret.** Way back in chapter 1, John the Baptist called Jesus "the Lamb of God." Now people say, "everything John said about this man was true." Why does John record that the story comes back around to where the Baptist first testified?
- **Respond.** The chapter opens with people grabbing stones and closes with people believing. Where are you in that chapter right now, and which direction are you moving?

## Key words to know

- **Good shepherd.** What Jesus calls himself. "Good" means the true, beautiful, real shepherd, and his goodness shows up in dying for his sheep.
- **The door.** Another thing Jesus calls himself. He's the one way into safety and life with God.
- **The two hands.** In verses 28 and 29, the sheep are held in Jesus' hand and the Father's hand at once. No one can pull them out.
- **Feast of Dedication.** Hanukkah, a winter feast celebrating the temple being rededicated. The priests used to examine themselves at this feast with Ezekiel 34, the chapter about God's shepherds. Jesus shows up and says he's the true shepherd.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus is the Good Shepherd the whole Old Testament pointed to, the LORD himself come to gather his sheep. He knows you by name, he lays down his life for you on purpose, and he holds you in a grip you can't fall out of. He's one with the Father. The leaders reach for stones, and the people at the Jordan reach for faith. He's the shepherd worth following, because he's the shepherd who died for the sheep.
