# Reading guide, Unit 16: The hour has come

> Passage: John 12:1-50
> Big question: what kind of King did Jesus come to be, and what does it cost to follow him?
> Memory work: no new verse this unit. Keep your nine review verses sharp.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

John 12 is the last chapter of Jesus' public ministry. After this, the crowds are gone and Jesus is alone with his twelve disciples. Everything the whole book has been building toward finally arrives here: "the hour has come." Watch a King ride into his city on a donkey to die.

## Before winter break: recap and memory check

Our first class of this unit is a half-day, right before break. There's no new reading for it. We'll look back over the whole first half of John, the seven signs and the "I am" sayings, and you'll take a memory quiz on all nine verses you've learned so far. Come ready to say them.

## Lesson: the King anointed for burial (John 12:1-19)

**Read it.** John chapter 12, verses 1 through 19.

Six days before Passover, Mary pours a whole jar of expensive perfume on Jesus' feet. Judas complains it was wasteful. Jesus says she's preparing him for his burial. Then a huge crowd welcomes Jesus into Jerusalem, waving palm branches and calling him King, and he rides in on a donkey.

- **Observe.** In verse 7, what does Jesus say Mary's gift was for? In verse 6, why did Judas really object?
- **Interpret.** The crowd wanted a King who'd fight Rome and win. Jesus rides in on a donkey, an animal of peace, headed for a cross. What kind of King did they want, and what kind did they get?
- **Respond.** Mary gave a year's wages of perfume because she saw who Jesus really was. What would it look like to treat Jesus like he's worth everything?

## Lesson: the hour, the seed, and the cross (John 12:20-36)

**Read it.** John chapter 12, verses 20 through 36. This lesson has an optional memory verse.

Some Greeks (non-Jews) come looking for Jesus, and Jesus says the hour has finally come. He talks about a grain of wheat that has to fall and die to bear fruit. His soul is troubled at what's coming. A voice speaks from heaven. And Jesus says that when he's lifted up (on the cross), he'll draw all people to himself.

- **Observe.** In verse 24, what has to happen to a grain of wheat before it bears fruit? In verse 23, what does Jesus say has finally come?
- **Interpret.** Jesus is the grain of wheat. He dies first, and his death is what grows the harvest. How does his death lead to life for a whole world?
- **Respond.** In verse 27 Jesus admits his soul is troubled about the cross. He was really afraid, and he went anyway. What does it mean to you that Jesus faced something terrifying out of love for you?
- **Optional stretch verse.** John 12:24: "unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone, but if it dies, it bears much fruit."

## Lesson: the unbelief foretold, and the glory Isaiah saw (John 12:37-43)

**Read it.** John chapter 12, verses 37 through 43.

After all the signs, most people still didn't believe. John says this fulfilled what Isaiah wrote, both that few would believe and that God hardened stubborn hearts. Then John says something stunning: Isaiah saw Jesus' glory, seven hundred years earlier.

- **Observe.** In verse 37, what did the people do even after seeing so many signs? In verse 41, whose glory did Isaiah see?
- **Interpret.** John holds two things together here: the people "would not" believe (their fault) and "could not" believe (God's hardening). How can both be true at once? (This is a hard one. It's okay to sit with it.)
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 42 says many leaders believed but wouldn't say so, because they feared the Pharisees and "loved the glory that comes from man." Where do you stay quiet about Jesus because of what people would think?

## Lesson: mercy's door is open (John 12:44-50)

**Read it.** John chapter 12, verses 44 through 50.

These are Jesus' last public words in the whole book. He says believing in him is believing in the Father, that he came as light, and that he came to save the world, not to judge it. But his word will judge people on the last day.

- **Observe.** In verse 47, what does Jesus say he came to do? In verse 48, what will judge a person on the last day?
- **Interpret.** Jesus says he came to save, not to condemn, and also that his word will judge people at the end. How do both of those fit together? (Think about a first coming and a second coming.)
- **Respond.** These are the last things Jesus says to the crowds before he goes silent. If you knew someone was about to stop talking to you, and these were their final words, how closely would you listen?

## Key words to know

- **The hour.** Jesus' word for the appointed time of his death. The whole book has said it "had not yet come." Here it finally arrives.
- **Lifted up.** Jesus' way of talking about the cross. It means both raised up to die and raised up in glory, at the same time.
- **Grain of wheat.** Jesus' picture of himself. A seed has to be buried and die before it grows a harvest. His death is how life comes to the world.
- **The Book of Signs.** The first half of John (chapters 1 to 12), full of Jesus' signs. It ends here, and the second half turns to the cross.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The hour has come. Jesus is the King who rides in to die, the grain of wheat who falls into the ground so a whole world can live, the one lifted up on a cross to draw people from every nation to himself. Isaiah saw his glory centuries ago. He came to save, not to condemn. This is the last thing he says to the crowds, and the door is still open: come to the light.
