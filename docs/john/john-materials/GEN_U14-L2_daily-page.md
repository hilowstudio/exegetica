# Daily page, Unit 14 Lesson 2: The good shepherd (John 10:11-18)

> Keep this page next to you while we work. Write on it. It is yours to keep, and it is today's Reading-Journal page.

## 1. Quiet time (the first 7 minutes)

> "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep." (John 10:11, ESV)

Who has ever put themselves on the line for me? Write about that person, or about wishing you had one.

## 2. Hear the Word

**Read: John 10:11-18** (out loud if you can).

Where we are: Jesus says he is the good shepherd, and then tells you exactly what makes him good.

## 3. Work the text (find it yourself, in class)

In verse 11, what does the good shepherd do for the sheep? In verses 12 and 13, why does the hired hand run when the wolf comes?

______________________________________________________________

Read verses 14 and 15. Jesus says he knows his sheep and they know him, and he compares it to one other relationship. Which one?

______________________________________________________________

Read verses 17 and 18. Who does Jesus say takes his life from him? Write his own words about laying it down and taking it up.

______________________________________________________________

*Go deeper (optional):* the Old Testament kept promising a shepherd. Read Isaiah 40:11, where the LORD himself "will tend his flock like a shepherd" and carry the lambs. Hold the question of who Jesus is claiming to be when he stands up and says, "I am the good shepherd."

## 4. The one thing from today

Say verse 11 back in your own words. What makes Jesus the good shepherd?

______________________________________________________________

## 5. Respond

The good shepherd knows his sheep by name and dies for them. What would change in a normal day if you really believed Jesus knows you that personally and did that for you? Write honestly. No one grades what you believe.

**Memory:** new verse. Learn John 10:11: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep." Learn it in two chunks: (1) "I am the good shepherd," (2) "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."
**At home:** ask me what "good" means here, and why it means dying.
