# Semester 1 Final, TEACHER COPY with answer key: The Book of Signs (John 1-12)

> **Course:** Sophomore Bible, Gospel of John · **Window:** January 11-14 · **Weight:** 15% of the semester grade
> **Covers:** John 1-12 (Units 0-16), the whole Book of Signs. Graded on correctness.
> **Memory:** the nine anchors planted this semester (20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16, 4:24, 6:35, 8:12, 10:11, 11:25).
> All Scripture ESV. **This is the teacher copy. Answer keys are marked `>> KEY`. The student copy omits them.**

**Teacher notes before you print.**
- The through-line of the whole exam is John 20:31. Nearly every content question can be answered in the form "this shows Jesus is ___." Grade the christological payoff, not the incidental detail. A student who lists Cana's six stone jars but misses "he manifested his glory" has missed the sign.
- Three-tier friendly: Parts A-C are the floor (every prepared student can pass on memory, the signs, and the "I am" sayings). Part E (the essay) is the ceiling.
- Content is gradable; belief is invitational. Every question here is answerable as comprehension of what John shows. Nothing on this final asks a student to profess faith. Keep it that way when you grade the essay: grade whether they *see* the Christ John shows, never whether they agree.
- Suggested weighting: A 25, B 20, C 15, D 20, E 20 (100). Adjust to your period length.

## Part B. The seven signs. Match each sign to its reference, and in one phrase say what it reveals about who Jesus is.

Signs (in order): water into wine · the official's son · the invalid at Bethesda · the feeding of the five thousand · walking on the water · the man born blind · the raising of Lazarus.

References to match: 2:1-11 · 4:46-54 · 5:1-9 · 6:1-14 · 6:16-21 · 9:1-7 · 11:1-44.

>> KEY (Part B):
>> - **Water into wine, 2:1-11**: reveals his glory; the Bridegroom who brings the joy and abundance the prophets promised. John calls it "the first of his signs" where he "manifested his glory."
>> - **The official's son, 4:46-54**: his bare word gives life across distance; the word carries the authority of his presence.
>> - **The invalid at Bethesda, 5:1-9**: the Son gives life to the helpless and works as the Father works; equal with the Father.
>> - **The feeding of the five thousand, 6:1-14**: the true bread from heaven; he is himself the bread of life.
>> - **Walking on the water, 6:16-21**: the divine "I am" ("It is I; do not be afraid") who treads the sea; deity, and nearness in the storm.
>> - **The man born blind, 9:1-7**: the light of the world who gives sight, and who exposes the seeing as blind.
>> - **The raising of Lazarus, 11:1-44**: the resurrection and the life; the last and greatest sign, life spoken into a tomb.
>> Full credit needs the reference AND a christological phrase (what it reveals), not just the event.

## Part C. The "I am" sayings. 

C1. List the "I am" sayings from the Book of Signs and give the reference for each (there are five predicated sayings and one absolute "I am" in chapters 1-12).

>> KEY (C1): Predicated: **bread of life (6:35)**, **light of the world (8:12)**, **the door (10:7)**, **the good shepherd (10:11)**, **the resurrection and the life (11:25)**. Absolute: **"before Abraham was, I am" (8:58)**. (Accept the five predicated sayings; award the ceiling point for naming 8:58.) Note for grading: the way (14:6) and the vine (15:5) are in the Book of Glory and are NOT expected here.

C2. What does "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58) claim that the other "I am" sayings do not, and how did the crowd react (8:59)?

>> KEY (C2): It echoes the divine name God gave Moses at the burning bush, "I AM" (Exodus 3:14), so it is a claim to deity, to existing eternally before Abraham. The crowd picked up stones to throw at him (8:59), because they understood it as a claim to be God. The predicated sayings name what he is *for* people (bread, light, shepherd); the absolute "I am" names who he *is*.

## Part D. Short answer (the christotelic heart, one or two sentences each). Grade for the sense.

D1. In the Prologue, who is the Word, and what happens in 1:14?

>> KEY: The Word is God (1:1), eternal, the one through whom all things were made. In 1:14 the Word "became flesh and dwelt among us," the eternal, divine Son taking on a human life, and his glory was seen, "full of grace and truth." (The incarnation.)

D2. What does a "sign" do in John's Gospel, and why does John say he recorded them (20:30-31)?

>> KEY: A sign is a miracle that points past itself to reveal who Jesus is and to call for belief. John recorded them "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." A sign is a window on the person, not a wonder for its own sake.

D3. What must a person have to see the kingdom of God (chapter 3), and what image does Jesus use in 3:14-15 for how he will save?

>> KEY: A person must be "born again" / born from above, a birth worked by the Spirit (3:3-8), which no one can perform for themselves. In 3:14-15 Jesus points to the bronze serpent Moses lifted in the wilderness (Numbers 21): as it was lifted up, so the Son of Man must be lifted up, and whoever looks to him in faith lives. (The cross.)

D4. At the well in chapter 4, what does Jesus offer the Samaritan woman, and what does he say true worship is (4:24)?

>> KEY: He offers "living water," the Spirit welling up to eternal life (4:10-14), and tells her the hour has come to worship the Father "in spirit and truth," because "God is spirit" (4:24), so worship is no longer bound to a mountain or temple.

D5. In chapter 5, what does Jesus claim about his relationship to the Father, and why did it make the leaders want to kill him (5:18)?

>> KEY: He claims the Son does whatever the Father does, gives life and judges, and is to be honored as the Father is honored (5:19-23). They sought to kill him because he was "calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God" (5:18).

D6. What is the sixth sign (chapter 9), and what does John show through the man's growing sight and the leaders' response?

>> KEY: The healing of the man born blind. As the man sees more and more (from "the man called Jesus" to "a prophet" to worshiping him), the seeing leaders grow blind and are hardened. The Light gives sight and also judges: "For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind" (9:39).

D7. In the Good Shepherd chapter, what does the shepherd do for the sheep (10:11), and what does Jesus say about his life in 10:18?

>> KEY: The good shepherd "lays down his life for the sheep" (10:11). He lays it down of his own accord; no one takes it from him (10:18). He dies by his own authority, willingly, not as a helpless victim.

D8. Chapter 12 closes the public ministry. What does Jesus mean by "the hour has come" (12:23), and what does the grain of wheat picture (12:24)?

>> KEY: "The hour" is the appointed time of his death and glorification, held back through the whole first half (2:4). The grain of wheat that falls into the earth and dies to bear much fruit (12:24) pictures his death as the way to fruitfulness. He is glorified *by* being lifted up on the cross (12:32-33).

## Part E. Essay (choose ONE). Write a clear, organized response that shows you understand what John is showing about who Jesus is. You are graded on comprehension, not on whether you personally believe it.

**E1.** John says he wrote the signs so his readers would believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God (20:30-31). Trace at least three of the seven signs and explain what each reveals about who Jesus is, and how the set builds toward that purpose.

**E2.** The "I am" sayings run through the Book of Signs. Choose three and explain what each shows about who Jesus claims to be. Then explain what "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58) adds that the others do not.

**E3.** The Book of Signs ends on "the hour has come" (12:23) and on unbelief (12:37). Explain what John means by "the hour," and how chapter 12 turns the Gospel from the public signs toward the cross.

>> KEY (Part E), load-bearing points a full-credit answer hits:
>> **E1:** a sign is a work that points past itself to the person doing it (2:11, the sign reveals his glory and the disciples believe); three signs named accurately with what each shows (see Part B key); the signs escalate toward the claim that he holds life and death (climaxing at Lazarus, 11:25); John selected them for the stated aim of 20:31.
>> **E2:** the predicated sayings each name what he is FOR people, a need he meets (bread 6:35, light 8:12, shepherd 10:11); the absolute "I am" of 8:58 echoes the divine name of Exodus 3:14, a claim to deity, which is why they took up stones (8:59); together they portray a Christ who is the God of the burning bush and the one who gives himself to be bread, light, and shepherd.
>> **E3:** the hour is the appointed time of his death and glorification, held back since Cana (2:4) and now arrived; the grain of wheat must fall and die to bear fruit (12:24); he is glorified BY being lifted up (12:32-33), a glory defined by the cross; the public ministry has shown enough and the world's unbelief (12:37-40) turns the Gospel toward his own in chapter 13.
>> Grade the essay for comprehension of John's argument. A searching student who explains what John wants his reader to SEE earns full marks. Do not require or reward personal profession.
