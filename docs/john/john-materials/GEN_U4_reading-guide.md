# Reading guide, Unit 4: The new birth

> Passage range: John 3:1-21 (four lessons)  
> Memory verse: John 3:16 (ESV)  
> The one big question this unit asks: What does it take to get into God's kingdom, and can I do it myself?  
> You will need: your Bible (ESV), your Reading Journal, a pen  
> Course: Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John: Yorkville Christian School  
> Learning objectives: Students will read John 3:1-21 with understanding and personal response. Students will know and review the unit memory verse, John 3:16.  
> Scripture quoted in the ESV.

---

## How to use this guide

- This isn't homework to fill in. It's a map for four lessons. You'll do the real writing in your **Reading Journal** and the real talking in class.
- For each lesson, **read** the passage first, then work the short prompts. Bring your answers to the discussion.
- Keep your Bible open. Answer from what the text actually says before you reach for what you already think.

> **What a good observation looks like:** a good observation points at the words on the page. "Jesus says the same phrase three times" is an observation. "Jesus wants us to be nice" is not. Notice first. Interpret second.

### Meet Nicodemus (read this once, at the start)

Nicodemus is a big deal in his world. He's a Pharisee, a teacher, a member of the ruling council. He's the last guy you'd expect to be told he's missing something. Watch him across these four lessons. He shows up **at night** in chapter 3, and John wants you to notice that. Hold onto him. We'll see where he ends up.

## Lesson 1: The night visit and the new birth

**Read John 3:1-8.**

The passage: *"Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews. This man came to Jesus by night... Jesus answered him, 'Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God.'... 'The wind blows where it wishes, and you hear its sound, but you do not know where it comes from or where it goes. So it is with everyone who is born of the Spirit.'"* (ESV)

**Observe** (what the text says)

1. Nicodemus opens with what he already knows about Jesus (v. 2). What does he call Jesus, and how sure does he sound?
2. Jesus keeps saying "born again" / "born of the Spirit." Underline every time birth comes up in these eight verses. How many times?

**Interpret** (what it means, and who Jesus is here)

3. "Born again" also means "born from above": a birth that comes down from God, not one you produce. Nicodemus hears "again" and pictures crawling back into the womb (v. 4). Why would Jesus telling a successful religious man that he needs a brand-new birth be such a shock?

**Respond** (what it asks of you)

4. Jesus says entering the kingdom is something God does to a person, not something a person achieves. If that's true, where does it leave your own effort, and how does that land on you: as bad news, good news, or both?

5. **Go deeper (optional):** Read John 1:13. It describes people "born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God." How does that verse back up what Jesus tells Nicodemus?

> **Navigation help:** John is the fourth book in the New Testament, right after Matthew, Mark, and Luke. Find John, then chapter 3. The little **3** is the chapter, the little numbers inside are verses. Ask if you're lost. Nobody's grading you for finding the page.

## Lesson 2: "How can this be?" and the snake on the pole

**Read John 3:9-15.**

The passage: *"Nicodemus said to him, 'How can these things be?'... 'No one has ascended into heaven except he who descended from heaven, the Son of Man. And as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life.'"* (ESV)

**Observe**

6. Nicodemus is completely stuck (v. 9). What's his exact question, and what does that tell you about how well the smartest religious teacher is following along?
7. Jesus says He can talk about heaven because He "descended from heaven" (v. 13). What claim is Jesus making about where He came from?

**Interpret**

8. In verse 14 Jesus points back to a strange story. When Israel was dying of snakebite, God told Moses to put a bronze snake on a pole, and anyone who **looked** at it lived (Numbers 21:8-9). Jesus says the cross is that pole. What is Jesus saying you have to do to live: and notice, it's a look, not a work.

**Respond**

9. The people bitten by snakes couldn't cure themselves. They just looked and lived. Where in your own life do you try to fix yourself before you'll look to Jesus?

10. **Go deeper (optional):** "Lifted up" comes back in John 12:32, *"And I, when I am lifted up from the earth, will draw all people to myself."* The phrase means both "nailed up on the cross" and "raised up in glory." How does one event carry both meanings?

## Lesson 3: God so loved the world

**Read John 3:16-17.** This is the unit's memory verse. Read it slowly. You've probably heard it a hundred times, and that's exactly the problem. Read it like it's news.

The passage: *"For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life. For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him."* (ESV)

**Observe**

11. Circle the verb that says what God actually did. It's one word, and it costs the Giver everything.
12. Look at verse 17. What does it say God sent the Son to do, and what does it say He did **not** send the Son to do?

**Interpret**

13. Three words in verse 16 are worth slowing down on:
    - **"so loved"** means "loved like *this*" (the *way* He loved), not just "loved so much." Point back to verse 14. The way God loved is the cross.
    - **"the world"** isn't the people who deserved it. It's the same dark world that verse 19 says loved darkness. God loved *that* world.
    - **"gave"** means a Father handed over His Son.
    
    Say verse 16 back in your own words, one plain sentence, using all three: the way God loved, the world He loved, and what it cost Him.

**Respond**

14. If God loved the hostile, dark world (and that includes you on your worst day), what does that do to the idea that you have to clean yourself up before God will want you?

15. **Go deeper (optional):** Jesus told Nicodemus his people should have already known about the new birth. Read Ezekiel 36:25-27, written centuries earlier: God promises to *"sprinkle clean water on you"* and *"put my Spirit within you."* How is John 3 the arrival of that old promise?

## Lesson 4: Light, darkness, and the verdict

**Read John 3:18-21.**

The passage: *"Whoever believes in him is not condemned, but whoever does not believe is condemned already... And this is the judgment: the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness rather than the light because their works were evil... But whoever does what is true comes to the light, so that it may be clearly seen that his works have been carried out in God."* (ESV)

**Observe**

16. Verse 19 says what the "judgment" actually is. In the text's own words, what is it? (Hint: it's about light showing up and how people responded.)
17. According to verses 20-21, what's the difference between the person who stays away from the light and the person who comes to it?

**Interpret**

18. Notice the order. Verse 17 says God sent Jesus to **save**, not to condemn. Then verse 18 says people who refuse Him are condemned "already." The verdict isn't God hunting people down. Light came, and people chose the dark. How does that keep God kind *and* keep sin serious at the same time?

**Respond**

19. Coming to the light means letting yourself be seen: the real you, not the version you post. What makes that scary, and what in these verses makes it safe?

20. **Go deeper (optional):** Remember Nicodemus came "by night" (3:2)? Read John 7:50-51 and John 19:39. He speaks up for Jesus, then helps bury Him with about seventy-five pounds of spices, out in the open. He's edging out of the dark, slowly, over years. If the new birth can look like that instead of one dramatic moment, what does that mean for someone who feels only half-sure?

## Key words to know

- **Pharisee:** a member of a strict Jewish religious group, serious about keeping God's law. Nicodemus was one, and a leader on top of that.
- **Kingdom of God:** God's rule and reign, and the life you get to share in when you belong to Him. Jesus says you can't even *see* it without the new birth.
- **Born again / born from above:** a fresh start that God works in a person by His Spirit. It comes down from Him. You don't manufacture it.
- **The Spirit:** the Holy Spirit, God Himself at work in a person. In verse 8 the same word means both "wind" and "Spirit," which is Jesus' whole point: you can't control the wind, and you can't control this.
- **Son of Man:** Jesus' name for Himself, the One who came down from heaven (v. 13).
- **Condemned:** declared guilty. Verse 17 says Jesus didn't come for that. Verse 18 says refusing Him leaves a person there already.

## Memory verse: John 3:16 (ESV)

> "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."

**Review note:** You'll be asked to write or say this from memory by the end of the unit. Learn it in three chunks:
1. *"For God so loved the world,"*
2. *"that he gave his only Son,"*
3. *"that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."*

Say it out loud once at the start of each lesson this unit. By Lesson 4 it should be yours. Aim to have it down cold within these two weeks.

## Where this whole unit lands: Jesus

Nicodemus shows up with a résumé and gets told none of it can get him in. The new birth is something God has to do, worked by His Spirit, given to people who look in faith to Jesus lifted up on the cross: the snake on the pole, the Son the Father gave. The unit moves from a problem you can't solve ("you must be born again") to the Person who solves it. Your job isn't to work up the right feeling or perform the right religion. Your job is to look to Christ and live.
