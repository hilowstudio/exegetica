# Quiet-time journal: the first 7 minutes of class

> Course: Sophomore Bible: The Gospel of John  
> Yorkville Christian School  
> Rhythm: Daily (the first 7 minutes of every class)  
> This page is yours: **ungraded**, **personal**, and **never read aloud**  
> Centering verse: John 20:31 (ESV)  
> Learning objective: Students will build a simple daily habit of prayer and journaling.

## Why we start here

John tells us why he wrote his Gospel:

> "but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." (John 20:31, ESV)

That's the whole point of this class in one sentence. John wrote so you'd meet Jesus and find life in him. These 7 minutes are your space to do that on your own, before we dig into the text together.

## How this works

- The clock starts when class starts. Phones are away and face-down or in your bag.
- This journal is **not graded** and **not collected to be read**. I check that you're writing, not what you wrote.
- What you put here stays yours. I won't ask you to share it, and no one reads it aloud.
- Quiet means quiet. Give the people around you room to think.

> **If you're not sure what you believe about God, you're welcome here.** You don't have to fake it. Write your honest questions to him and see what happens. "God, are you real?" is a real prayer, and it's a good place to start.

## The 5 steps

Do these in order. The whole thing takes 7 minutes.

1. **Settle.** Put the phone away. Take three slow breaths. Let the noise of the hallway drop off.

2. **Read the centering verse.** Read John 20:31 slowly, twice. The second time, read it like it's for you.

3. **Journal.** Write whatever's real. What stood out, what you're carrying today, a question, a thank-you, a worry. There's no right answer and no format to follow. Just write.

4. **Pray.** Talk to God about what you just wrote. Out loud in your head, in a sentence or a paragraph. Honest and short is fine.

5. **Sit.** Stop writing. Be still for the last minute. You don't have to produce anything. Just be there.
