# Reading guide, Unit 25: The cross

> Passage: John 19:1-42
> Big question: this is the center of the whole book. Watch the King go to his throne, a cross, and finish the work of saving you.
> New memory verse: John 19:30. You'll learn it in lesson 9.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This is the holiest ground in the Gospel, and we walk it slowly, with reverence. Everything the whole book has said about Jesus comes together here. We're not here to gawk at the horror. We're here to worship the King who reigns from a cross and finishes the work of saving his people. One thing carried over from last unit: when John says "the Jews" in these chapters, he means the specific leaders driving the execution. Jesus, his mother at the cross, John, and the men who bury him are all Jews. This is about human sin and God's rescue, and it never gives anyone a reason to look down on Jewish people.

## Lesson 1: crowned with thorns (John 19:1-3)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 1 through 3.

Pilate has Jesus flogged. The soldiers twist together a crown of thorns, jam it on his head, throw a purple robe (the color of kings) on him, and mock him: "Hail, King of the Jews!" while they hit him.

- **Observe.** In verses 2 and 3, what do the soldiers put on Jesus, and what do they say to him?
- **Interpret.** The soldiers mean the crown and robe as cruel jokes. But they're accidentally telling the truth: he really is the King. And the thorns are the curse from Genesis 3, pressed onto the head of the one carrying the curse for us. How is this mockery actually a true coronation?
- **Respond.** This is a real body, real blood, real pain, and Jesus took it for you. Sit with that for a second before you write. What does it stir in you to see the King treated like this?

## Lesson 2: behold the man (John 19:4-7)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 4 through 7.

Pilate brings the beaten Jesus out and says, "Behold the man!", trying to get the crowd to pity him. He says twice more that he finds no guilt in Jesus. The crowd screams "Crucify him!" and says Jesus must die because he claimed to be the Son of God.

- **Observe.** In verse 5, what does Pilate say as he presents Jesus? In verses 4 and 6, what does Pilate keep saying about Jesus' guilt?
- **Interpret.** Pilate means "behold the man" as "look at this pitiful figure." But he's unknowingly pointing at the true Man, the second Adam, the Son of God. And notice verse 7: they want him dead for claiming to be God's Son, which is exactly what he is. How is Jesus condemned for telling the truth about himself?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Pilate declares Jesus innocent over and over, and hands him to die anyway. What does it say about the cross that the judge himself found no crime?

## Lesson 3: authority from above (John 19:8-11)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 8 through 11.

Pilate, unnerved, asks Jesus where he's from. Jesus stays silent. Pilate brags that he has the power to free or crucify him. Jesus answers calmly: you'd have no authority over me at all unless it were given you from above.

- **Observe.** In verse 10, what does Pilate claim he has power to do? In verse 11, what does Jesus say about where Pilate's authority comes from?
- **Interpret.** Pilate thinks he's in charge. Jesus tells him his power is borrowed, given from above, and that the one who handed Jesus over carries the greater sin. How is Jesus, the prisoner, actually the calmest and most in-control person in the room?
- **Respond.** Jesus says there's a "greater sin," so guilt is real and it comes in degrees. Nobody here is off the hook, and some are more responsible than others. Where do you tend to excuse yourself because someone else did worse?

## Lesson 4: no king but Caesar (John 19:12-16)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 12 through 16.

The leaders pressure Pilate: freeing Jesus would make him disloyal to Caesar. Pilate brings Jesus out and says, "Behold your King!" They scream for his death. And when Pilate asks, "Shall I crucify your King?", the chief priests say the saddest line in the book: "We have no king but Caesar."

- **Observe.** In verse 15, what does Pilate ask, and what do the chief priests answer?
- **Interpret.** It's Passover, the feast that celebrates God as Israel's true King. And the leaders, at that feast, say they have no king but Caesar, a pagan emperor. They disown their own promised Messiah-King. How is that line a verdict they pronounce on themselves?
- **Respond.** "We have no king but Caesar" is what it sounds like to claim God while your real loyalty is somewhere else. Be honest: who or what is the actual king of your life right now, if you watch where your time and love go?

## Lesson 5: there they crucified him (John 19:17-18)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 17 and 18. This is a short-day, single-focus lesson.

Jesus carries his own cross out to a place called the Skull, Golgotha. And John says it in one short sentence: "there they crucified him," with two others, one on each side, and Jesus in the middle.

- **Observe.** In verse 17, where do they take Jesus, and what does he carry? In verse 18, what happens there?
- **Interpret.** The most important event in history, and John gives it one sentence. No dwelling on the gore, no long description. Why do you think he says so little? What does it ask of you to just stop and behold it?
- **Respond.** Today we simply sit at the foot of the cross. Write what it means to you that the Son of God was crucified. Take your time.

## Lesson 6: the King proclaimed (John 19:19-22)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 19 through 22.

Pilate writes a sign for the cross: "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews," in three languages, so everyone can read it. The chief priests want it changed. Pilate refuses: "What I have written I have written."

- **Observe.** In verses 19 and 20, what does the sign say, and in how many languages? In verse 22, how does Pilate respond to the priests' complaint?
- **Interpret.** Pilate posts the truth about Jesus, in the languages of religion, empire, and culture, over the cross for the whole world to read, and God uses even Pilate's stubbornness to proclaim it. How is the cross actually Jesus' throne, and the sign his royal title?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Pilate meant the sign as a jab at the Jewish leaders. God meant it as an announcement to the nations. How do you see God's control working through people who aren't even trying to serve him?

## Lesson 7: dividing his clothes (John 19:23-24)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 23 and 24.

The soldiers split up Jesus' clothes, and gamble for his seamless tunic rather than tear it. John points out this fulfills Psalm 22, written a thousand years before: "they divided my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots."

- **Observe.** In verse 24, what do the soldiers do with the tunic, and what does John say it fulfilled?
- **Interpret.** Soldiers gambling for a dying man's clothes are just being callous. They have no idea they're acting out a psalm written a thousand years earlier, to the detail. What does it tell you about God's control over the cross that even the soldiers' dice landed on prophecy?
- **Respond.** Nothing at the cross was an accident, right down to a gambling game over clothes. Where do you need to trust that God is in control of details that feel random or out of your hands?

## Lesson 8: behold your mother (John 19:25-27)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 25 through 27.

His mother Mary stands near the cross, watching her son die. Jesus, in agony, sees her and John, and provides for her: "Woman, behold your son," and to John, "behold your mother." From then on, John takes Mary into his own home.

- **Observe.** In verses 26 and 27, what does Jesus say to his mother and to John? What does John do after that?
- **Interpret.** Jesus is dying the worst death there is, and he stops to make sure his mother will be cared for. He's obeying the command to honor your father and mother right to the end. What does it show you about Jesus that he's thinking of his mom in that much pain?
- **Respond.** Even on the cross, Jesus made sure the people he loved were taken care of. Who in your life needs you to think of them, even when you're stressed or hurting? What's one way to care for them this week?

## Lesson 9: it is finished (John 19:28-30)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 28 through 30. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Jesus says, "I thirst," fulfilling Scripture, and they lift sour wine to his mouth on a branch. Then he says one word that changes everything: "It is finished." And he bows his head and gives up his spirit.

- **Observe.** In verse 30, what does Jesus say right before he dies? What does he do after saying it?
- **Interpret.** "It is finished" is one word in Greek, tetelestai. It's a shout of victory, not a groan of defeat. Merchants stamped this word on a bill when it was fully paid: paid in full. Jesus means the work of saving you is complete, the debt is paid, and there's nothing left to add. How does that change the way you hear his last word?
- **Respond.** "He bowed his head and gave up his spirit." He chose the moment. Nobody took his life; he laid it down (10:18). And his last word was "done, paid in full." If the most important work is already finished, what does that free you from trying to earn?
- **Memory verse.** John 19:30: "When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, 'It is finished,' and he bowed his head and gave up his spirit."

## Lesson 10: blood and water (John 19:31-37)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 31 through 37.

To hasten death, soldiers break the legs of the two men beside Jesus. But Jesus is already dead, so they don't break his (fulfilling the Passover-lamb law, no bone broken). Instead a soldier stabs his side, and blood and water pour out. John, who saw it, swears it's true, so you'll believe.

- **Observe.** In verse 33, why don't they break Jesus' legs? In verse 34, what happens when the soldier pierces his side? In verse 35, why does John tell us he saw it?
- **Interpret.** The spear proves Jesus was really, completely dead, not just passed out. John, an eyewitness, wants you sure of that. Why does it matter so much that Jesus actually died? And how does "no bone broken" make Jesus the true Passover Lamb (look at Exodus 12:46)?
- **Go deeper (optional).** John says he watched this happen and is telling the truth "so that you also may believe." How is an eyewitness who saw the spear go in an answer to someone who doubts the whole story?

## Lesson 11: the burial (John 19:38-42)

**Read it.** John chapter 19, verses 38 through 42.

Two secret disciples come out of hiding. Joseph of Arimathea asks Pilate for the body. Nicodemus, who first came to Jesus at night, brings a huge amount of burial spices, fit for a king. They wrap Jesus and lay him in a brand-new tomb in a garden.

- **Observe.** In verses 38 and 39, who comes to bury Jesus, and what does Nicodemus bring? In verse 41, where do they lay him?
- **Interpret.** Joseph and Nicodemus were secret disciples, afraid to be known. Now, when it looks hopeless, they step out publicly and honor the dead Jesus. What changed them? And notice: everyone who came to that tomb came to bury a dead man. Why does that matter for what happens next?
- **Respond.** Two men who hid their faith finally came out into the open at the hardest moment. Where are you hiding that you follow Jesus? What would stepping into the open look like for you?

## Key words to know

- **Golgotha.** "The place of a skull," where Jesus was crucified.
- **"It is finished" (tetelestai).** Jesus' last word (19:30). One Greek word, a victory shout, the term for a debt paid in full. The work of salvation, complete.
- **The Passover Lamb.** Jesus, whose bones weren't broken (19:36), dying as the true Passover Lamb (Exodus 12) who takes away the sin of the world (1:29).
- **Eyewitness.** John himself, who saw the crucifixion and the spear, and wrote it down so you'd believe (19:35).

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The King reigns from a cross. He's crowned with thorns and enthroned on a charge nailed over his head, proclaimed to the whole world in three languages. Every detail of his death, the divided clothes, the thirst, the unbroken bones, the pierced side, was written centuries before and fulfilled to the letter, because God was in control of all of it. He cared for his mother while he died. And with his last breath he shouted the greatest news there is: "It is finished." The debt is paid in full. He laid his life down himself, and finished the work of saving you. Then they laid him in a tomb, and Easter was three days away.
