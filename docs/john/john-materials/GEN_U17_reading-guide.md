# Reading guide, Unit 17: Re-entering the book

> Where we are: crossing from the Book of Signs (John 1-12) into the Book of Glory (John 13-21)
> This unit: one class to get oriented, and to launch the spring group project
> Read it in the ESV.

## Welcome back

Last semester we worked through the first half of John, the Book of Signs. Twelve chapters, seven signs, the big "I am" sayings, all answering one question: who is this Jesus? It ended with "the hour has come."

Now we cross into the second half, the Book of Glory (chapters 13 to 21). The whole feel changes. The crowds are gone. Jesus is alone with his twelve closest friends on the night before he dies. What comes next is the most personal, tender part of the whole Bible: his last words to the people he loves, then his death, then the empty tomb.

Here's the door we walk through, John 13:1:

> "Now before the Feast of the Passover, when Jesus knew that his hour had come to depart out of this world to the Father, having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end."

He loved them to the end. That line is the key to everything ahead.

## The map: Jesus' last night (John 13-17)

The next five chapters are called the Farewell Discourse. It's one long conversation, one night, one dying man talking to his friends. Here's what's in it:

- **John 13, the towel and the basin.** Jesus wraps a towel around himself and washes his disciples' feet, including the feet of the one who will betray him. He gives a new command: "love one another, as I have loved you."
- **John 14, the way home.** "Let not your hearts be troubled." Jesus comforts scared men. "I am the way, and the truth, and the life." He promises the Helper, the Holy Spirit.
- **John 15, the vine.** "I am the true vine." Stay connected to me, because apart from me you can do nothing. Love one another. The world may hate you.
- **John 16, sorrow into joy.** The Spirit's work is explained. "Your sorrow will turn into joy." "I have overcome the world."
- **John 17, the prayer.** Jesus prays out loud, for himself, for his disciples, and for everyone who will ever believe, including you. "That they may be one."

Two verses to feel the heart of it:

> John 14:6: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me."
>
> John 15:5: "I am the vine, you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."

## Your spring project: teaching the Farewell Discourse

This quarter, you're the teacher. Your group takes one section of Jesus' last night with his friends and teaches it to the class, so everyone understands it and meets Jesus in it.

**What you'll do.** Work in a group, each person with a real role. Pick your format (a video, an infographic, a first-person monologue, a documentary, art with commentary, or a written piece). Teach your chapter so a person who's never opened a Bible gets it, and a person who has grown up in church still learns something.

**The milestones (the process is graded too, milestone by milestone).**
1. **Pitch:** your topic, audience, format, and why.
2. **Plan:** an evidence map, every passage and quote you'll use, and how it's structured.
3. **Draft or rehearsal,** with feedback from your peers.
4. **Present** it live, with questions from the class.

We'll have a fifteen-minute in-class check-in each project week for roadblocks. At the end, you'll write a short **Maker's Note**: why you chose your format and angle, what was hard, what you'd do differently. Your grade combines the group's work, your own contribution, and a confidential peer evaluation.

**How to teach it so people actually meet Jesus.** Four things to keep in front of you:

1. **Land on Jesus, and say what he asks.** Your chapter ends somewhere on who Jesus is: the servant-Lord, the way to the Father, the vine, the Spirit-giver, the praying High Priest. Name that, and name what he's asking of the room. A lesson that ends on "so be a nice person" missed the point.
2. **Use the actual text.** Read the verses out loud. Point at what Jesus really says. Feelings fade by lunch; the words stay.
3. **Reach the whole room.** Give the person new to the Bible a picture they can hold (the towel, the vine, the empty chair the Helper fills), and give the person who knows it something with real weight.
4. **Read it as a friend's last words.** This is a dying man comforting scared friends the night before he's killed. Teach it in that key, gently. That's where people lean in.

## Memory work this unit

There's no new memory verse this unit. Keep your nine sharp:

> John 20:31, John 1:14, John 1:12, John 3:16, John 4:24, John 6:35, John 8:12, John 10:11, and John 11:25.

The next new verse comes in the very next unit, John 13:34-35, the command Jesus gives on this very night:

> "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another."

## Where this all lands on Jesus

The signs showed you who Jesus is. Now watch what that same Jesus does with his last night: he kneels with a towel and washes feet. The Lord the whole first half revealed is the servant on the floor. His glory and his humility are one person. The best of John is in front of you, and this quarter, you get to teach it.
