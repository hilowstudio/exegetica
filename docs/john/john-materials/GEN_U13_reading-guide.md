# Reading guide, Unit 13: Sign 6

> Passage: John 9:1-41
> Big question: the blind man ends up seeing, and the men who could see go blind. Which one are you?
> Memory work: no new verse this unit. Keep your seven review verses sharp.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

John 9 is a full story, almost like a short play. Jesus heals a man born blind, and then the man gets interrogated over and over. Watch two things happen at the same time: the blind man sees more and more clearly who Jesus is, and the religious leaders who could see get more and more blind. Last unit Jesus said, "I am the light of the world." This unit he proves it.

## Lesson 1: the sign and the first questions (John 9:1-12)

**Read it.** John chapter 9, verses 1 through 12.

The disciples see a man born blind and ask whose sin caused it. Jesus says nobody's sin caused it, and that God is going to be shown in this man. He makes mud from spit and dirt, puts it on the man's eyes, and sends him to wash in the pool of Siloam (which means "Sent"). The man comes back seeing.

- **Observe.** In verse 3, what does Jesus say caused the man's blindness? What does he say it's for?
- **Interpret.** The pool is named "Sent," and Jesus is the one God sent. Why do you think John stops to tell us what the name means?
- **Respond.** Jesus says this man's suffering wasn't punishment for sin. If you've ever assumed your hard things are God getting back at you, how does verse 3 change that?

## Lesson 2: the prophet, and the fear of the synagogue (John 9:13-23)

**Read it.** John chapter 9, verses 13 through 23.

The leaders question the man. He keeps it simple: Jesus put mud on his eyes, he washed, now he sees. When they ask what he thinks of Jesus, he says, "He is a prophet." Then they call in his parents, who are scared, because anyone who calls Jesus the Christ gets thrown out of the synagogue.

- **Observe.** In verse 17, what does the man now call Jesus? (Notice it's a bigger claim than last time.)
- **Observe.** In verses 22 and 23, why won't the parents answer the question about Jesus?
- **Interpret.** The parents heard the name of Jesus and went quiet because saying it would cost them. When has it cost you something to be known as a Christian, and what did you do?

## Lesson 3: I was blind, now I see (John 9:24-34)

**Read it.** John chapter 9, verses 24 through 34. This lesson has an optional memory verse.

The leaders drag the man back in and pressure him to call Jesus a sinner. He won't. He gives them the one thing he's sure of: "I was blind, now I see." He even gets a little bold with them, and they throw him out for it.

- **Observe.** In verse 25, what's the one thing the man says he knows for sure?
- **Interpret.** The man can't win a theology debate with trained experts, and he doesn't need to. Why is "I was blind, now I see" so hard for them to argue with?
- **Respond.** You don't need all the answers to tell someone what Jesus has done for you. What's one true thing you could say about him from your own experience?
- **Optional stretch verse.** John 9:25: "One thing I do know, that though I was blind, now I see."

## Lesson 4: found, worship, and two kinds of blindness (John 9:35-41)

**Read it.** John chapter 9, verses 35 through 41.

Jesus hears the man got thrown out, and goes and finds him. This is the first time the man actually sees Jesus' face. Jesus asks if he believes in the Son of Man, and the man says, "Lord, I believe," and worships him. Then Jesus says something that sorts the whole crowd: he came so the blind would see and the seeing would go blind.

- **Observe.** In verse 35, what does Jesus do when he hears the man was cast out?
- **Observe.** In verse 38, what does the man do after Jesus reveals himself?
- **Interpret.** The man worships Jesus, and Jesus lets him. In the Bible, angels and apostles refuse worship. What is Jesus claiming by accepting it?
- **Respond.** Jesus says the people who admit they're blind get to see, and the people who insist "we see" stay blind. Which is harder for you: admitting you don't see, or pretending you do? Write honestly.
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 35 says Jesus "found him" after he was thrown out. If you've ever paid a price for following Jesus, what does it mean that he comes looking for the one who got cast out for his sake?

## Key words to know

- **Sign.** John's word for Jesus' miracles. A sign points past itself to who Jesus is. This one is a picture of Jesus opening the eyes of the heart.
- **Siloam.** The pool where the man washed. Its name means "Sent," and Jesus is the one God sent.
- **Put out of the synagogue.** Being cut off from your whole community and its worship. It's what the leaders threatened for anyone who confessed Jesus as the Christ.
- **Son of Man.** A title Jesus uses for himself. When the man believes in him and worships him, he's treating Jesus as God.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus is the light of the world, and he proves it by giving sight to a man born blind. As the man sees more, he believes more, until he's on his knees calling Jesus Lord. The leaders who were sure they could see refuse to admit they need anything, and they stay in the dark. And when the man gets thrown out for Jesus, Jesus comes and finds him. That's the shepherd. He opens blind eyes, and he goes after the ones cast out for his name.
