# Reading guide, Unit 20: Abide

> Passage: John 15:1-27
> Big question: what's the secret to actually living the Christian life? Jesus says it in one word: stay joined to me.
> New memory verse: John 15:5. You'll learn it in lesson 2.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

We're still in the Farewell Discourse, the same night, the same room. Last unit Jesus said he's the way and promised the Helper. Now he draws a picture: he's a grapevine, his people are the branches, and the branch only ever does one thing to bear fruit. It stays connected. Watch him hand exhausted people the most freeing sentence in the chapter: "apart from me you can do nothing."

## Lesson 1: the true vine (John 15:1-3)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 1 through 3.

Jesus says, "I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser." In the Old Testament, Israel was God's vineyard, and it kept failing to bear fruit (look at Isaiah 5). Jesus steps in as the true vine, the one who does what the old vineyard never could. The Father tends the branches, and Jesus tells his friends they're "already clean" because of his word.

- **Observe.** In verse 1, what does Jesus call himself, and what does he call the Father? In verse 3, why are the disciples already clean?
- **Interpret.** Jesus calls himself the "true" vine. True compared to what? What does it mean that he succeeds where Israel failed?
- **Respond.** The disciples are "already clean because of the word." They didn't scrub themselves clean; his word did it. Where do you try to clean yourself up for God instead of letting him do it?

## Lesson 2: abide in me (John 15:4-5)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 4 and 5. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Here's the heart of the whole chapter: "abide in me, and I in you." A branch can't make fruit on its own. It just stays connected to the vine, and the vine's life flows through it. Then Jesus says the line that changes everything: "apart from me you can do nothing."

- **Observe.** In verse 4, what can a branch not do by itself? In verse 5, what happens to the branch that stays connected, and what can you do apart from Jesus?
- **Interpret.** "Abide" means stay, remain, keep connected. Notice a branch doesn't strain to make grapes. It stays joined and fruit happens. How is that different from how people usually try to be good Christians?
- **Respond.** "Apart from me you can do nothing." That can feel harsh, or it can feel like the biggest relief you've ever heard. Which is it for you, and why?
- **Memory verse.** John 15:5: "I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."

## Lesson 3: the two branches (John 15:6-8)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 6 through 8.

Jesus says the branch that doesn't stay connected withers and gets burned. Then he turns to the branches that do stay: they can ask what they wish, they bear much fruit, and the Father is glorified in it. Two kinds of branch, two very different ends.

- **Observe.** In verse 6, what happens to the branch that doesn't abide? In verse 8, what glorifies the Father?
- **Interpret.** The burned branch is a picture of someone who looked connected but never really was (think of Judas, who sat right there). This isn't Jesus saying a real believer gets tossed out. Back in John 10:28 he said no one can snatch his sheep from his hand. So what's the warning actually about, and who should it make examine themselves?
- **Go deeper (optional).** If bearing fruit is the sign of a real branch, and fruit is the vine's own work through you (verse 5), then what should a Christian do when they look at their life and worry about fruit? Where do they look?

## Lesson 4: abide in my love, joy made full (John 15:9-11)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 9 through 11.

Jesus says he loves his friends with the exact love the Father has for him: "as the Father has loved me, so have I loved you." He tells them to stay in that love by keeping his commandments, the way he stayed in the Father's love. And he says why he's telling them all this: so his joy would be in them and their joy would be full.

- **Observe.** In verse 9, how much does Jesus love his disciples? In verse 11, why does Jesus say all this?
- **Interpret.** People often think obeying God drains the joy out of life. Jesus says the opposite: he tells them to obey and stay in his love so that their joy would be full. How does staying close to Jesus lead to more joy, not less?
- **Respond.** Jesus loves you with the same love the Father has for him. Sit with that a second. Does that feel true to you, or hard to believe? Write honestly.

## Lesson 5: greater love (John 15:12-13)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 12 and 13.

Jesus gives the command again, the one from last unit: "love one another as I have loved you." Then he names the highest love there is: "greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends." He's about to do exactly that.

- **Observe.** In verse 12, what's the command, and what's the measure ("as...")? In verse 13, what's the greatest love?
- **Interpret.** Jesus names the greatest love as laying down your life for your friends. Who shows that love first, and when? What makes his even greater than the definition (hint: look at who he died for in Romans 5:8)?
- **Respond.** "Love one another as I have loved you." The measure is the cross. That's an impossibly high bar on your own. How does the vine (verse 5) make it possible?

## Lesson 6: friends, chosen, sent (John 15:14-17)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 14 through 17.

Jesus tells his disciples something staggering: "no longer do I call you servants... I have called you friends." Then he reminds them who started it: "you did not choose me, but I chose you." And he sends them: chosen and appointed to go and bear fruit that lasts.

- **Observe.** In verse 15, what does Jesus stop calling them, and what does he call them instead? In verse 16, who chose whom?
- **Interpret.** Being Jesus' friend doesn't mean he stops being Lord. A friend of Jesus still does what he says (verse 14). So what changed when servants became friends? What do friends get that servants don't (verse 15)?
- **Go deeper (optional).** "You did not choose me, but I chose you." How is that meant to comfort a nervous disciple, and how does the rest of verse 16 turn it into a job to do?

## Lesson 7: the world's hatred (John 15:18-21)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 18 through 21.

Jesus gets honest about what's coming. If the world hates his followers, it's because it hated him first. He reminds them, "a servant is not greater than his master." They'll be treated the way he was treated, and it'll be "on account of my name."

- **Observe.** In verse 18, why does the world hate Jesus' followers? In verse 20, what reason does Jesus give from the servant-and-master line?
- **Interpret.** Jesus says his people get hated because they belong to him, "on account of my name." That's different from being disliked because you're rude or obnoxious. What's the difference between being hated for Jesus and getting pushback for acting like a jerk?
- **Respond.** Following Jesus can cost you socially. Where's a place you've felt that, or where you're afraid to be known as his? Write honestly.

## Lesson 8: no excuse, and the Spirit's witness (John 15:22-27)

**Read it.** John chapter 15, verses 22 through 27.

Jesus says those who've seen him and his works and still hate him have "no excuse for their sin." Their hatred fulfills the Scripture, "they hated me without a cause." Then he ends on hope: the Helper, the Spirit of truth, will come and "bear witness about me," and his disciples will bear witness too.

- **Observe.** In verse 25, what does Jesus say the world did to him "without a cause"? In verse 26, what will the Helper do?
- **Interpret.** "They hated me without a cause" means Jesus did nothing to deserve it. He's the innocent one. How does that make the world's hatred a picture of sin itself, and not just a rough crowd?
- **Go deeper (optional).** The Spirit and the disciples both "bear witness" to Jesus (verses 26-27). Two witnesses, one message: Christ. How is that the job of the church, right down to you?

## Key words to know

- **The true vine.** Jesus, the real thing Israel's failed vineyard only pictured. His people are the branches, and his life flows into them.
- **Abide.** To stay, remain, keep connected. The branch's whole job. It's being joined to Jesus, and fruit flows from that.
- **Fruit.** The life Jesus produces through a connected branch: love that looks like his, a changed life. Not a checklist you grind out on your own.
- **The Helper (the Paraclete).** The Holy Spirit, the Spirit of truth. Here his job is to bear witness to Christ and to make the church's witness work.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus is the vine, and every good thing his people ever do grows out of staying joined to him. He loves them with the Father's own love, he calls them friends, he chose them before they chose him, and he lays down his life, the greatest love there is. Even the world's hatred and the cost of following him get folded into being his. And the Spirit's whole job is to keep pointing back to him. The secret to the Christian life turns out to be a Person you stay connected to.
