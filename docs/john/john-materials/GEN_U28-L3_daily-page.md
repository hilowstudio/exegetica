# Daily page, Unit 28 Lesson 3: The hour, glory through the cross and the empty tomb (John 13-20)

> Keep this page next to you while we work. Write on it. It is yours to keep, and it is today's Reading-Journal page.

## 1. Quiet time (the first 7 minutes)

> "When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, 'It is finished,' and he bowed his head and gave up his spirit." (John 19:30, ESV)

Having studied the cross closely, what's one thing about it I don't want to forget? Write it.

## 2. Hear the Word

**Read: John 19:28-30 and John 20:26-31** (out loud if you can).

Where we are: the whole book has been pointing at this, the hour, the cross, and the empty tomb.

## 3. Work the text (find it yourself, in class)

John calls the cross "the hour," and he pointed at it from chapter 2 on. Gather what you learned about the cross this year that made it more than a tragedy. Write all you can (start with the Lamb of God, 1:29, and "It is finished," 19:30).

______________________________________________________________

______________________________________________________________

In the Prologue John said, "we have seen his glory" (1:14). Write down where in the book John finally saw that glory most clearly.

______________________________________________________________

The tomb is empty, and Thomas says, "My Lord and my God" (20:28). Write what John said about Jesus in the very first verse of the book (1:1).

______________________________________________________________

*Go deeper (optional):* John the Baptist called Jesus "the Lamb of God" on page one (1:29), and at the cross not one of his bones was broken (19:36), exactly as the Passover lamb required. Hold chapter 1 next to chapter 19, and sit with what the whole book was pointing at. Leave it open.

## 4. The one thing from today

The glory John saw shone brightest at a cross, and "It is finished" means paid in full. Write the one thing about the cross you don't want to forget.

______________________________________________________________

## 5. Respond

"It is finished." The whole debt paid, nothing left for you to add. What does the cross stir or ask in you? Write honestly, even if it's "I'm still not sure, and here's why." No one grades what you believe, and there is nothing here to earn.
