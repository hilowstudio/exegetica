# Reading guide, Unit 3: Sign 1 and the temple

> Unit 3 of the Gospel of John: Yorkville Christian School, Sophomore Bible  
> Passage range: John 2:1-25  
> Lessons: John 2:1-11 · 2:12-17 · 2:18-22 · 2:23-25  
> The big question this unit asks: When Jesus turns water into wine and clears out the temple, what is he showing us about who he is?  
> Use with: your Reading Journal and our in-class discussion  
> Scripture quoted in the ESV

---

## How to use this guide

- This is a **follow-along** guide, not homework. You'll open it in class as we work through each passage.
- Read the passage first. Then use the prompts here to notice things and think out loud with the class.
- The real writing happens in your **Reading Journal**. This guide just points you where to look.
- **New to the Bible? Welcome.** You don't need to know anything ahead of time. The little "finding it" notes will get you to the right page fast.
- The **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

> **What a good observation looks like.** An observation is something you can actually see in the words on the page, before you decide what it means. "There are six stone jars" is an observation. "Jesus is the true temple" is an interpretation. Start with what the text says, then move to what it means.

## Lesson 1: the wedding at Cana (John 2:1-11)

**Read it:** John 2:1-11.

> **Finding it:** John is the fourth book in the New Testament, right after Matthew, Mark, and Luke. Flip to John, then find the big **2** for chapter 2, and the small **1** for verse 1.

The scene: Jesus is at a wedding, the wine runs out (an embarrassment for the host), and Jesus quietly turns about 150 gallons of water into the best wine anyone there had tasted. John calls this the **first of Jesus' signs**.

1. Count the water jars and read what they were for (verse 6). How much water did each one hold?
2. Look at what the master of the feast says in verse 10. What surprises him about this wine?
3. **Interpret:** Verse 11 says Jesus "manifested his glory, and his disciples believed in him." A **sign** is like a window: you look at what Jesus does and you see who he is. What does this sign show you about Jesus?
4. **Respond:** Mary tells the servants, "Do whatever he tells you" (verse 5). Those are the last recorded words of Jesus' mother in this Gospel. What would it look like to take that line seriously this week?

> **Go deeper (optional):** In verse 4 Jesus says, "My hour has not yet come." Later he says, "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (John 12:23), and in John that "hour" is the cross. Even at a wedding, Jesus is already looking ahead to why he came. Trace the word "hour" if you want a thread to follow across the whole book.

## Lesson 2: Jesus clears the temple (John 2:12-17)

**Read it:** John 2:12-17.

> **Finding it:** Same chapter as last time, just keep reading down the page to verse 12.

The scene: It's Passover, the biggest feast of the year, and Jerusalem is packed. Jesus walks into the temple and finds a livestock market and money-changers set up in the one outer court where non-Jewish outsiders were allowed to come near God. He makes a whip of cords and drives it all out.

5. What exactly does Jesus find going on in the temple (verses 14-16)?
6. What does Jesus call the temple in verse 16, and what does that tell you about why the market makes him angry?
7. **Interpret:** The disciples remember a line from Scripture: "Zeal for your house will consume me" (verse 17, quoting Psalm 69:9). To "consume" means to eat up or devour. Jesus cares about real worship so much that it will one day cost him his life. How does that change the way you read this scene?
8. **Respond:** This passage is easy to turn into a permission slip for losing your temper. It isn't one. Jesus made that whip on purpose, out of love for his Father's house and for the outsiders shut out of it. Where do you tend to confuse "being angry" with "caring about the right thing"?

> **Go deeper (optional):** Read Psalm 69:9 in full. It's a psalm about a righteous person suffering for God's sake. Why might John want you to hear that whole psalm humming behind this scene?

## Lesson 3: "destroy this temple" (John 2:18-22)

**Read it:** John 2:18-22. This is a short passage and the peak of the whole unit, so we'll slow way down.

> **Finding it:** Keep reading in chapter 2, verses 18 to 22.

The scene: The leaders demand a sign to prove Jesus has the right to do what he just did. Jesus answers with a riddle: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up." They think he means Herod's building, which had been under construction for 46 years. John tells us plainly what Jesus meant.

9. What do the leaders think Jesus is talking about (verse 20)?
10. What does John say Jesus was actually talking about (verse 21)?
11. **Interpret:** The temple was the place where God met his people. Jesus points at his own body and says the real meeting place with God is standing right there. He'll prove it by dying and rising in three days. Read this next to John 1:14, "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us." How do those two verses fit together?
12. **Respond:** Verse 22 says the disciples didn't understand this until "he was raised from the dead." They followed Jesus for years with this saying unexplained. If following Jesus means living with some questions still open, how does that land on you right now?

> **Go deeper (optional):** John frames this whole chapter with resurrection language. The wedding is "on the third day" (2:1), and the temple riddle promises "in three days I will raise it up" (2:19). What might John be hinting at by bracketing chapter 2 that way?

## Lesson 4: the belief Jesus can read (John 2:23-25)

**Read it:** John 2:23-25. Three verses, and they're the sting in the tail of the whole chapter.

> **Finding it:** The last three verses of chapter 2, right before chapter 3 begins.

The scene: Lots of people at Passover "believed in his name when they saw the signs" (verse 23). In the very next breath, John says Jesus "did not entrust himself to them" (verse 24). They trusted him. He did not hand himself over to their kind of trust, because he could see straight into every heart.

13. What made the crowd believe, according to verse 23?
14. What reason does John give for Jesus holding back in verses 24-25?
15. **Interpret:** There's a difference between being **impressed** by Jesus from a distance and actually **receiving** him. Looking at these verses, how would you describe the kind of faith Jesus is looking for?
16. **Respond:** This one is honest, so take it honestly. Jesus knows what's in every heart in that crowd, and he knows what's in yours, and he still holds himself out to be received. You don't come to him with a résumé. You come empty-handed. What would it mean for you to stop trying to impress him and just come?

> **Read this before you get anxious.** Verse 24 can feel like a test you might fail: "maybe my faith is fake." That's not the point. The good news here is that Jesus already knows everything about you and still offers himself to you. The cure for a shallow faith isn't trying harder. It's coming to him with nothing in your hands.

> **Go deeper (optional):** Chapter 2 ends, "he knew what was in man." Chapter 3 opens, "Now there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus" (John 3:1). John set that up on purpose. What kind of man do you expect Jesus to meet next, and what might he say to him?

## Key words to know

- **Sign** (Greek *sēmeion*): a mark that points beyond itself. John never calls Jesus' miracles "wonders." They're signs, clues that show you who Jesus is. Water to wine is the first of seven in this book.
- **Hour** (Greek *hōra*): in John, the appointed time when Jesus is glorified, which happens at the cross. When Jesus says "my hour has not yet come" (2:4), he's already pointing there.
- **Purification**: the Jewish washing rites the six stone jars were for (2:6). Ceremonial washing to be clean before God.
- **Passover**: the yearly feast remembering how God rescued Israel out of Egypt, marked by a lamb's blood. John sets the temple scene here (2:13), and he'll set the cross here too (19:14).
- **The temple**: the building in Jerusalem where God's presence met his people and sacrifices were offered.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus walks into the two centers of Jewish religion, a wedding-and-washing and the temple, and shows he's the reality both were always pointing at. At Cana he brings the good wine of the feast the prophets promised (Isaiah 25:6), and in Jerusalem he names himself the true temple, torn down at the cross and raised on the third day. The risen body of Jesus is now where God and sinners meet. And the last three verses guard it all: you can be dazzled by the miracles and still be a stranger to the Christ they point to, so come to him empty-handed and be received.
