# Project guide, Q4: The Passion and a Personal Response

> **Quarter:** 4 (spring) · **Mode:** individual
> **Introduced:** as the class enters the Passion (John 18) · **Due:** at the capstone unit, "The whole Christ of John" (mid-May). Your teacher will give you the exact milestone dates.
> Read everything in the ESV.

## The assignment

> Walk someone through the death and resurrection of Jesus in John, and tell the truth about where you land on the reason John gives for writing it (20:31).

This last project has two halves, and they're graded differently. In the first half you *explain*: you walk a real person through the cross and the empty tomb in John 18 to 20. In the second half you *respond*: you tell the truth, honestly and on your own terms, about where you stand on why John says he wrote the whole book.

## Half one: the analysis (graded on understanding)

Walk your reader through what happens and why John tells it the way he does. The center of John's Passion is this: the cross is where John's word for Jesus, *glory*, shines brightest. Watch for it.

- **The King reigns from the cross.** Pilate hangs a sign over his head in three languages, "the King of the Jews," and refuses to change it (19:19-22). John wants you to see a coronation.
- **The Lamb dies at the Passover.** They don't break his legs, "that the Scripture might be fulfilled," the Passover lamb with no bone broken (19:36; 1:29).
- **"It is finished" (19:30).** The Greek word is *tetelestai*, a bill stamped paid in full. It's a victory shout, not a gasp of defeat. The work the Father gave him to do is complete, and he yields up his spirit by his own choice.
- **The empty tomb confirms it.** The folded grave clothes, Mary hearing her name, and Thomas's "My Lord and my God" (20:28), which confesses at the end what John declared at the start: "the Word was God" (1:1).
- **The eyewitness stakes his testimony on it.** "He who saw it has borne witness, his testimony is true" (19:35).

Explain these the way you'd explain them to someone who wasn't there. That's fully gradable, and everyone can do it well, whatever they believe.

## Half two: the personal response (credited for honesty, never graded for belief)

John says why he wrote the whole book:

> "these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." (20:31)

The second half of your project is you telling the truth about where you land on that. And here is the promise, in writing, so you can trust it:

**Your conclusion is not graded. Your honesty is.** A student who writes "I've studied this closely and I'm not sure I believe it, and here's why" has done this assignment fully and well, and earns exactly the same credit as a student who lands in worship. There is no right answer to arrive at here and no standing to earn by your response. The reason is the gospel itself: John sets the finished work in front of you already complete. "It is finished" was said before you did anything. So no one is pressed to manufacture a response to pass a class. You're asked for one thing only: the truth about where you actually stand before a Christ who is offered freely. Say it plainly, either way, and think it through with care.

## Voice and choice: pick your format

The skills are fixed by the rubric. The shape is yours:

- a walkthrough video or short documentary
- an illustrated Passion narrative with commentary
- a written piece (an essay, a letter, a guided walk-through)
- a piece of art with written explanation, plus your response
- a spoken-word or monologue piece

Whatever the format, both halves are present: the analysis, then your honest response.

## The milestones (the process is graded too)

Your teacher will give you the exact dates. The order is fixed:

1. **Pitch.** Your audience, your format, and how you'll walk them through the Passion.
2. **Plan.** Your evidence map: every passage and quote across John 18 to 20, and how it's structured.
3. **Draft or rehearsal,** with peer feedback (on the analysis; your personal response is yours).
4. **Present** the analysis to the class, with live questions. Your personal response is turned in, and it's yours to share or keep private as your teacher directs.

A fifteen-minute in-class check-in is there for roadblocks.

## The Maker's Note (required, turned in with the final)

A few honest sentences: why this format and angle, what was hard, what you'd do differently.

## How to make it actually land

- **Let the cross carry its own weight.** You don't need to inflate it. Walk your reader through what John actually shows and let "It is finished" mean what it means.
- **Keep the glory in view.** John's strange claim is that the cross is Jesus at his most glorious. Show your reader where you see it.
- **Tell the truth in the response.** Honest is the whole assignment. Don't perform faith you don't have, and don't hide faith you do. Either way, think it through.

## Rubric

The **analysis** is scored on the rubric below, each row 1 (beginning) to 4 (exemplary), multiplied by its weight, summed, and divided by 4 for the percentage.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| **Grasp of the Passion** | 30% | John 18 to 20 is read accurately and in context, no misreadings; you understand what happens and why John tells it this way. |
| **Christ at the center** | 20% | The analysis shows where John's glory shines at the cross, and what "It is finished" and the empty tomb mean. |
| **Independent insight** | 20% | Your own honest thinking and a real angle for a real audience. |
| **Communication** | 15% | Clear, organized, prepared, and pitched to your reader. |
| **Live Q&A** | 15% | You answer follow-ups with real understanding of the Passion. |

**Your project grade also includes** the graded milestones, the Maker's Note, and the **personal response, credited for honest, careful engagement and never judged for the conclusion you reach.**

**A word on the grade and your heart.** The analysis grades whether you understood and explained the Passion, which is content. The response is between you and the truth, and it's safe here. We grade the analysis and coach the heart. We never grade the heart.
