**Talk budget:** this is your sermon's home ground, and the Land is yours. Still hold the Work block to questions so the students climb the poem themselves. Sustained voice concentrated in Move 4.

---

## Move 1 — Quiet time with the Lord · 7 min · ungraded
- **Settle (1):** read **John 1:14** slowly.
- **Journal (5):** *"'The Word became flesh and dwelt among us.' God, one of us. Write what that would mean, if it were true."*
- **Gather (1):** close the journals.

## Move 2 — Hear the Word · 4 min
- Students read **John 1:14-18** aloud, slowly. This is where the poem lands, so read it like a summit, not a footnote.

## Move 3 — Work the text · 22 min · questions, not lecture
- **Entry Q (all):** "If God wanted you to know he understands what your life is actually like, what would he have to do?" Turn-and-talk. Bridge to 1:14: he did the one thing that answers it. He became flesh and moved in.
- **Core Q sequence (John groups, Reading Journal):**
  - 1:14a, "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us." Not looked human, not visited briefly, but **became** flesh. What does that let us say about whether God understands us? (He is not a stranger to any of it.)
  - 1:14b, "we have seen his glory." Recall the memory from Exodus: Moses asked to see God's glory and was told no one can see my face and live. What is John claiming here? (We have seen it, in Jesus.)
  - 1:16, "from his fullness we have all received grace upon grace." Two words again: **fullness** and **received**. If Jesus is full, what does that say about whether God is rationing his grace to you?
  - 1:17-18, "grace and truth came through Jesus Christ... the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known." What is the boldest claim in these two verses? (That in Jesus we have seen God himself.)
- **Deeper Q (D):** "The poem ends by saying the only Son, who is God, has 'made the Father known.' That is the whole reason John calls Jesus 'the Word.' Why is that the right word for him?" (A word carries the invisible inside of a person out where it can be known.)
- **Cold-call checks (named):** What did the Word become? What have we seen? From his fullness we receive what? Who has made the Father known?
- **Plant the memory verse:** read John 1:14 together; it goes on the rolling list.

## Move 4 — Land and minister · 7 min · your protected pulpit
- The eternal God through whom you were made did not stay far off. He became flesh, moved into the neighborhood, bled and wept and grew tired, and today he still stands as one of us at the Father's side, able to sympathize with everything you carry.
- And from his fullness, which never runs dry, we receive grace upon grace. Not earned, not rationed, not locked away. Received. As your sermon put it: sit under the waterfall.
- **Consider closing in the posture from your pulpit:** invite students, if they are willing, to a quiet moment, and read **Ephesians 3:14-19** over them, that they would be filled with all the fullness of God. Keep it invitational, no one is required, and let it land.

## Move 5 — Respond · 4 min · graded participation
- **Reading Journal:** *"'From his fullness we have all received grace upon grace.' Where do you try to earn what God is offering to give you for free? What would it look like to just receive it today?"*

## After the bell
- **Homework:** read **John 1:19-34** for next class. The poem ends; the story begins with the witness of John the Baptist.
- **Feeds the gradebook:** Participation, and memory verse 1:14 now enters the rolling review.
- **Sets up:** Unit 2, the witness and the first disciples (1:19-51).

## Teacher notes
- This is the climax of the Prologue and the theological center of the book stated as worship. It carries the fresh-build passages you starred later; get the incarnation and the deity claims clear and warm here, because the whole year leans on them.
- The Ephesians 3 landing is your own sermon's move. Keep it invitational in a mixed room; a student who is not ready to pray can sit quietly and still hear the words read over the class.
