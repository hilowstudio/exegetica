## Move 1 — Quiet time with the Lord · 7 min · ungraded
- **Settle (1):** read **John 1:1** slowly.
- **Journal (4):** *"You are about to hear a poem about Jesus written by a man who knew him. Come ready to listen. Write one thing you are hoping to hear."*
- **Gather (1):** close the journals.

## Move 2 — Hear the Word · 6 min · the heart of today
- Read the **whole Prologue, John 1:1-18, aloud, slowly**, start to finish, without stopping to explain. Let students hear it as one piece. Read it a second time if there is room, a different student.
- This is the poem before the history. Let it wash over the room the way a song does before you study the lyrics.

## Move 3 — Work the text · 13 min · one question
- **The question (John groups, then the room):** "This is a poem, not a story yet. What images jump out at you? And what do you think John is doing by opening this way, before he tells us anything Jesus did?"
- Collect the images on the board as they come (Word, light, darkness, life, glory, grace, truth, children of God). Note that these are the threads we will pull all year.
- Name the shape briefly (your voice, under 4 min): the poem opens "in the beginning" and closes by saying the Son has made the Father known. It is an overture. John fixes our eyes on who Jesus is before we watch him do anything.

## Move 4 — Land and minister · 4 min
- John could have started with a manger or a miracle. Instead he kneels us down in front of Jesus and asks us to look at who he really is first. That is what worship does. This week we will find out why John thought that was worth a whole poem.

## Move 5 — Respond · 3 min · graded participation
- **Reading Journal:** *"Which image from the poem stuck with you, and why that one?"*

---

## After the bell
- **Homework:** read **John 1:1-5** again, slowly, for tomorrow. We slow down and start walking through the poem.
- **Feeds the gradebook:** Participation.
- **Sets up:** Lesson 4, the Word (1:1-5), on a full Friday period.

## Teacher notes
- This is a light day by design, which is exactly why it sits on the 36-minute Thursday. Do not turn the reading into a lecture. The goal is that the room has heard the whole poem once before we take it apart.
