# John — Assessment Menu
## The varied set of graded work, and how it is weighted and spread

This is the full toolbox of assessments for the year, built to three commitments: keep every kind of learner able to show real understanding in the room, protect the line between content and heart, and keep you honest about student output.

### Two graded worlds, kept separate

- **Content mastery, graded on correctness.** Memory verses, quizzes, the marquee projects' understanding, and the finals. This is the floor. It is where we make sure every student knows the book, which is the part of the job you named as yours.
- **Formation and participation, graded on effort and service, with theology coached and never scored.** Daily engagement, quiet-time hosting, the Respond entries, and the student-led devotionals. A student is never marked down for an immature theology while they are still learning. You correct it live, in your Land moment.

Naming the line matters for one place where it could blur: a marquee project grades whether a student **accurately understood and explained the passage** (content comprehension), which is different from grading their personal faith or theology. That distinction is the one call I want you to confirm below.

## Gradebook categories

Built to the handbook: the semester grade is coursework plus a final worth 15 percent, homework alone stays under 20 percent (here it is near zero), and homework-plus-participation may run higher.

| Category | Weight | What it holds | Graded on |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Participation & Discipleship** | 30% | Daily engagement in John groups, think-pair-share, cold-call readiness, Respond entries, quiet-time hosting, student-led devotionals, seminar contribution, Reading Journal | Effort, engagement, service |
| **Assessments: Memory & Quizzes** | 30% | Rolling memory verses (written and oral), checkpoint quizzes, navigation/reading quizzes | Correctness |
| **Projects** | 25% | The marquee pieces, roughly one per quarter | Understanding, communication, live Q&A |
| **Semester Final** | 15% | Cumulative exam (Book of Signs / Book of Glory) | Correctness |

Coursework (the first three) sums to 85 percent, and the final is the school's mandated 15 percent. The earlier 30 / 30 / 40 sketch simply split its 40 into Projects 25 and Final 15. Homework is not a category, so its cap is satisfied trivially.

## Rubrics

### 1. Participation & Discipleship (holistic, 0 to 4, every 1 to 2 weeks)

Rewards preparation, engagement, and honest thinking. A thoughtful wrong answer earns full credit; honest doubts count as participation.

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **4 Exemplary** | Prepared, Bible open and on the passage. Volunteers and responds with real thought. Listens and builds on others, gracious in disagreement. Hosts and leads willingly when it is their turn. In-room reflections done with genuine effort. |
| **3 Proficient** | Usually prepared and engaged. Responds when called on with reasonable thought. Respectful. Reflections completed. |
| **2 Developing** | Inconsistent. Takes part mainly when prompted; responses thin. Reflections partial. |
| **1 Beginning** | Rarely prepared or engaged. Reluctant or off task. Reflections missing. |
| **0** | Not present to the day's thinking, or disruptive to others' thinking. |

### 2. Student-led devotional (participation grade, effort and service)

Scored 1 to 4 on each, no theology-accuracy line. Correction happens live and off the grade.

- **Prepared** the passage and a plan (read, big idea, Christ connection, a question, a close).
- **Led the arc** and kept the group's roles filled so each member had a real speaking part.
- **Served the class**: invited response, facilitated a real exchange, handled questions graciously, encouraged rather than performed.
- **Engaged** the room (drew peers in, watched the time).

Listening groups owe one written affirmation and one honest question, which feeds their own participation.

### 3. Project & Oral Presentation (marquee projects, understanding graded)

Each criterion 1 to 4, weighted. "Grasp" and "Christ at the center" grade accurate comprehension and communication of the passage, which is content, and are distinct from grading a student's personal faith.

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Grasp of the passage (accurate, in context, no misreadings) | 30% |
| Christ at the center (shows how the passage reveals Jesus and the gospel) | 20% |
| Independent insight (the student's own honest thinking) | 20% |
| Communication (clear, organized, prepared) | 15% |
| Live Q&A (answers follow-ups in real time with real understanding) | 15% |

Scoring: multiply each by its weight, sum, divide by 4 for the percentage.

## Projects — four, one per quarter, built to the sophomore bar

Every project shares five design qualities, so each asks students to **create something of value**, not regurgitate a chapter. Age note: sophomores can reason abstractly and want autonomy, but their executive functioning (planning, time management) is still under construction, so the structure below is the hidden guardrail.

- **Voice and choice (structured autonomy).** The rubric fixes the *skills*; the student chooses the *format* (video, infographic, first-person monologue, documentary, art with commentary, or a written piece) and their angle within the topic.
- **Authentic audience and a real "so what."** Presented to peers, and framed for a real purpose (help a newcomer see it, minister to classmates, move someone), not a report for the teacher. Stretch options: a younger middle-school class or chapel.
- **Scaffolded milestones, graded — the process counts, not just the product.** A **Pitch** (topic, audience, format, why) → a **Plan** (evidence map: every passage and quote, the structure) → a **Draft or rehearsal** with peer feedback → the **final, presented with live Q&A**. Project weeks include a dedicated ~15-minute in-class **check-in** for roadblocks. In-class check-ins keep the build honest (the anti-take-home-AI logic) and teach project management.
- **Interdependent roles + individual accountability (group work).** Distinct graded roles so no one carries it alone or coasts; the grade combines the group product, each member's own contribution, and a confidential peer evaluation.
- **Metacognitive reflection (required).** A short **Maker's Note** with each project, in the student's own words: why this format and angle, what was hard, what they would do differently. Graded as a skill (self-awareness), and hard to fake.

**Project grade = graded milestones (process) + the final on the rubric above + the Maker's Note + (group only) individual contribution and peer evaluation.**

| Q | Project | Prompt (create, don't regurgitate) | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 fall | **The Signs** | "Someone you know has never opened a Bible and thinks Jesus' miracles were magic tricks. Take one of John's seven signs and make something that shows them why John calls it a *sign*, and what it proves about who Jesus is." | individual |
| Q2 fall | **Character Study** | "Pick a person who met Jesus in John and put us inside their story. Show who they were, how the encounter changed them or didn't, and what John wants us to see through them." | individual |
| Q3 spring | **Teaching the Farewell Discourse** | "Your group is the teacher. Take your section of Jesus' last night with his friends and teach it so the class understands it and meets Christ in it." | group (roles + peer eval) |
| Q4 spring | **The Passion & a Personal Response** | "Walk someone through the death and resurrection of Jesus in John, and tell the truth about where you land on the reason John gives for writing it (20:31)." | individual |

The Q4 **analysis** is graded on the rubric; its **personal response** is credited for honest engagement and never judged for arriving at belief (we coach the heart, never grade it). Dramatized reading and a creative "I AM" piece remain alternates for any slot.

## How it spreads across your calendar

| When | What lands |
|---|---|
| **Weeks 1 to 2** | Navigation quizzes begin; first memory verses planted; John groups formed; quiet-time rhythm modeled by you before students host |
| **Ongoing** | Daily participation and Respond entries; rolling memory verses; devotional rotation begins once the room is ready (every group leads several times) |
| **Half-days (Oct 9, Dec 18, Mar 19, May 7)** | Good for a memory-verse quiz or oral recitation |
| **Checkpoint days** | Checkpoint quiz plus catch-up; a project introduced or due |
| **Once or twice a term** | A graded seminar |
| **Per quarter** | One project with graded milestones (pitch → plan → draft → present) and an in-class check-in: Q1 Signs, Q2 Character Study, Q3 Farewell teaching, Q4 Passion & Response |
| **Pre-finals review (Jan 8, May 24)** | Review; final study guide doubles as the handout |
| **Finals (Jan 11-14, May 25-28)** | Semester final, memory verses carried forward |

## Locked decisions

1. **Content-versus-heart line:** projects grade comprehension and analysis; devotionals and the personal-response half of Q4 stay effort-credited, theology coached not scored.
2. **Four projects,** one per quarter, as above (The Signs, Character Study, Teaching the Farewell Discourse, The Passion & a Personal Response).
3. **Category weights 30 / 30 / 25 / 15** (the final applied per the school's `(coursework × .85) + (final × .15)`).
