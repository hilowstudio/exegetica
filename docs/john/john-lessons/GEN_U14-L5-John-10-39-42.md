# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 14 Lesson 5 of 5: vindicated at the Jordan (John 10:39-42)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U14 The Good Shepherd |
| Lesson | 5 of 5 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Dec 10 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus shape |
| Passage | John 10:39-42 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus slips away to the place where John the Baptist first pointed to him, and there many believe, proving the Baptist told the truth. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the one the first witness told the truth about, believed where the whole story began. |
| Memory verse | John 10:11 was planted at L2. Review it, do not re-plant. Keep the seven earlier anchors sharp too. |
| Resources | No sermon covers this passage. Reference: John the Baptist's witness back in John 1. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe what happens when the crowd tries to arrest Jesus (10:39).
2. Students will explain what the people say about John the Baptist (10:41).
3. Students will explain the contrast between Jerusalem (stones) and the Jordan (belief).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the chapter that opened with stones ends with people believing.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): John the Baptist told the truth about Jesus, and it's now confirmed.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Baptist's witness from John 1 vindicated by events.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Everything that John said about this man was true" (John 10:41).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Who first pointed me toward Jesus?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 10:39-42 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 10, verses 39 through 42.
- One-sentence setup: after all the arguing and stones, Jesus leaves the city and goes back to where it all started.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verse 39, the crowd tries to arrest Jesus again. What happens?
   - He escapes from their hands. No one takes him before his time. He leaves on his own terms.
2. **Read-check.** In verses 40 through 42, where does Jesus go, and what do the people there say and do?
   - He goes back across the Jordan, where John the Baptist first baptized. The people say "everything John said about this man was true," and many believe.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** This whole chapter has been a fight over who Jesus is, and it's been ugly, division, insults, stones. Now look at where it lands. Work this: the chapter opens in Jerusalem with people grabbing rocks, and it closes at the Jordan with people believing. What's the difference between the two crowds, and whose word finally gets proven true?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out: in Jerusalem, the powerful, religious insiders reached for stones. At the Jordan, ordinary people looked honestly and believed. And the word that gets proven true is John the Baptist's. Way back in chapter 1, John pointed at Jesus and said "Behold, the Lamb of God." Now, at the very spot where he said it, people say "everything John said about this man was true." The first witness of the whole book is confirmed by how the story turns out.
   - For the disciple tier: notice John "did no sign" (verse 41) and still told the truth. You don't have to be flashy to point people to Jesus. You just have to point at the right person.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. Framed fresh.

- Trace the two crowds. The chapter opens with insiders holding stones and closes with ordinary people believing. Same Jesus, two responses. The difference wasn't the evidence. It was the heart that looked at it.
- Point back to the Baptist. This is where John first said "Behold, the Lamb of God," and now the crowd says he was right about everything. The very first witness in the book gets vindicated. God's word about his Son holds up.
- Aim it at the room. You're somewhere in this chapter right now. Some people harden and reach for stones. Some people look honestly and believe. Nobody stays neutral about the Good Shepherd for long. The question is which way you're moving.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the Lamb of God the Baptist pointed to, and where people looked with honest eyes, they believed.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The chapter goes from stones to belief. Where are you in it right now, and which direction are you moving with Jesus?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing John 10:11 and your other memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 10:11 stays in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 15: Jesus' friend Lazarus dies, and Jesus says, "I am the resurrection and the life." The next new memory verse (John 11:25) lands there.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- This is a self-contained closing scene. Open at verse 39 so the failed arrest launches the withdrawal (they reach, he slips away, he crosses the Jordan). It gives the Thursday a clean single focus.
- Land the Baptist's vindication (Units 1 and 2). John pointed to Jesus at the very start ("Behold, the Lamb of God," 1:29), and now, at the spot where he said it, people confirm he told the truth.
- Draw the Jerusalem-versus-Jordan contrast: the religious insiders reached for stones, the ordinary people at the Jordan believed. Same Jesus, different hearts.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. This closes Unit 14.
