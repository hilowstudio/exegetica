**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain Pilate's claim to authority (19:10).
2. Students will explain "you would have no authority... unless it had been given from above" (19:11).
3. Students will explain the "greater sin" as real degrees of guilt (19:11).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the prisoner is calmer and more in control than the governor.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): all human power is borrowed from God.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the "greater sin," guilt is real and has degrees, guarded against excusing anyone.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "You would have no authority over me... unless it had been given you from above" (John 19:11).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I feel powerless right now? Where do I think I'm more in control than I am?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:8-11 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 8 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: Pilate, rattled, plays the power card, and Jesus answers him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 10, what does Pilate claim he has the authority to do?
   - Cold-call check: to release Jesus or to crucify him. He's flexing his power.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 11. Jesus doesn't argue or beg. How does he answer Pilate's power boast? Where does Jesus say Pilate's authority actually comes from?
   - Guide them: he says Pilate would have no authority at all unless it were "given from above," from God. Pilate thinks the power is his. Jesus tells him it's on loan. Even the man who's about to order his execution only holds that power because God allows it. Jesus, the prisoner, is the calmest, most clear-eyed person in the room.
3. **Core.** Look at who's really in control in this scene. Pilate is nervous, pacing, playing power games. Jesus is bound, beaten, and calm. Who's actually running this?
   - Land it: Jesus is. He's not a victim of Pilate's power. He's submitting to a plan he's completely aware of. Nobody takes his life; he's laying it down, and he told us so (10:18). The governor holds the gavel, and the prisoner holds the peace.
4. **Core.** Read the last line of verse 11: "he who delivered me over to you has the greater sin." Jesus says one party is MORE guilty than another. What does that tell you about how guilt works? Is anybody in this scene innocent?
   - Draw out: guilt is real, and it has degrees. Pilate is guilty (he's about to condemn a man he called innocent). But the ones who handed Jesus over, who had the Scriptures and should have known their Messiah, carry the greater sin. So nobody's off the hook, and some are more responsible than others. Jesus, even here, speaks the truth about sin with total clarity.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Some people misuse "his authority was given from above" to say Pilate wasn't really responsible, or that nobody is, since God's in control. How does verse 11 itself shut that down?
   - Draw out: the same verse that says Pilate's power is "from above" also says he and the leaders are guilty, one more than the other. So God's sovereignty and human guilt are both fully true. God being in control doesn't erase anyone's responsibility. You can't hide behind "it was all God's plan" to dodge your own sin. Both are real at once.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: where does Jesus say Pilate's authority comes from?
   - From above, from God. It's borrowed, not his own.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Show them who's in charge. Watch this scene again. The governor of the region, with soldiers and the power to kill, is nervous and grasping. The man in chains, whipped and bleeding, is perfectly calm. Because Jesus knows the truth Pilate doesn't: all of Pilate's power is on loan from God, and Jesus is walking a road he chose himself.
- Comfort the powerless one. If you feel small and powerless in front of forces bigger than you, look at Jesus here. Every authority over you, every system, every person with power, holds it only because God allows it. The most powerful man in the story couldn't touch Jesus one second before God permitted it. God is over it all.
- Keep responsibility real. And don't miss the other edge. "Greater sin" means guilt is real. God being in control never means your choices don't matter or your sin isn't yours. You can't blame the plan for your part in it. Both are true: God rules everything, and you answer for what you do.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus stands calm and sovereign before Pilate, because even his executioner's power is only borrowed from the God who rules it all.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Every power over you is borrowed from God, who rules it all. Where do you need that to be true this week?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: the leaders pressure Pilate, and say "we have no king but Caesar."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: Christ's calm sovereignty (Pilate's power is "given from above," borrowed; Jesus in command, 10:18), and the "greater sin" as real degrees of guilt (Chrysostom). Guard hard against BOTH misreadings of 19:11: it does not excuse Pilate, and it does not excuse anyone. God's sovereignty and human responsibility both fully true (Adam's sermon 35: the king's heart is a stream in the Lord's hand).
- Anti-Judaism guard: "the one who delivered me over" is the specific leadership, indicting insider unbelief, not a people.
- Derive the frame from sermon 35; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
