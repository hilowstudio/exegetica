**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what the person who hears and believes has now (5:24).
2. Students will tell the two resurrections apart by their time markers (5:25 now, 5:28-29 coming).
3. Students will explain why the Son can give life and judge (5:26-27).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus promises life that starts now to anyone who hears him and believes.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "has passed from death to life" is present tense, already done, settled.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the two time-stamped resurrections (5:25 "now here," 5:28 "coming"); the Son having "life in himself" (5:26).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "has passed from death to life" (John 5:24).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What would it feel like to know I'm safe on the last day?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write what you'd want to be true about what happens after we die.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 5:24-30 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 5, verses 24 through 30.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus just claimed the Father's authority, and now he says what he does with it, he gives life and raises the dead.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 24, what does the person who hears Jesus' word and believes have? And what do they not come into?
   - Cold-call check: they "have eternal life" and do "not come into judgment." They've "passed from death to life."
2. **Entry.** Notice the tense in verse 24. Is that life something you get later, or something you have now? How can you tell?
   - "Has passed" is present. It's already true, done, settled.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 25 and verse 28 side by side. Verse 25 says the hour "is now here." Verse 28 says the hour "is coming." Jesus is talking about two different resurrections. Write what each one is.
   - Guide them: verse 25 is happening now, dead hearts coming alive when they hear Jesus (spiritual resurrection). Verse 28-29 is still ahead, everyone in the graves raised bodily at the last day.
4. **Core.** In verse 26, why can the Son give life at all? Where does his life-giving power come from?
   - Land it: "as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself." The Son holds life the way the Father does. He gives it because he has it.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 29 mentions "the resurrection of judgment." That could scare a person. Look back at verse 24. What does Jesus say about judgment for the one who hears and believes?
   - Draw out: the believer "does not come into judgment." The same passage that names the danger holds out the rescue in the same breath. The answer to the fear of the last day is hearing Jesus and believing.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what are the two resurrections in this passage?
   - One now (dead hearts made alive), one at the end (bodies raised from the grave).

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit) and offer the stretch verse

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the sermon's second question, "What happens when we die?"

- Lead with the gift, verse 24. Hear Jesus' word, believe the Father who sent him, and you "have eternal life" now and have "passed from death to life." Present tense. It starts the day you trust him.
- Hold the two resurrections together, honestly and gently. There's a raising happening now, dead hearts coming alive. And there's a day coming when every grave opens. Don't use that day as a scare tactic. The passage's own answer to the fear is verse 24: the one who believes does not come into judgment.
- Offer the stretch verse. John 5:24 is worth carrying if a student wants it, off the quiz. Put it on the board: "Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life." Spurgeon said this line was worth writing in gold across the whole earth.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus raises dead hearts to life now and will raise every body at the end, and the one who hears him and believes is already safe.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says a believer has already 'passed from death to life.' If that were settled and true for you, what would you stop being afraid of?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- To the gradebook: today's participation. No new required memory verse. John 5:24 is an optional stretch.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 06's second movement, the already and the not yet. Verse 25 is the resurrection now, verse 28-29 the bodily resurrection at the last day. Keep the two clearly separate.
- Guard against fear-leverage. The resurrection of judgment (5:29) is sober, and the passage answers the fear itself in 5:24. Lead with the rescue. Never fear as the engine of faith.
- Memory: review-only unit. Run a light cumulative pass on the five anchors, and let 20:31 ("that by believing you may have life") sit next to 5:24, since 5:24 is the same promise at full strength. Offer 5:24 as an optional stretch, off the graded line.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
