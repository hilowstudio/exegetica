**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what happens to the branch that does not abide (15:6).
2. Students will explain the burned branch as a branch never truly united to Christ, held with John 10:28.
3. Students will explain what glorifies the Father (bearing much fruit, 15:8).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a branch that never connects can't live on its own.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the warning is for self-examination, warmly, not a threat that a real believer gets tossed out.
- **Stretch** (disciple): holding the warning (15:6) with eternal security (10:28-29, 1 John 2:19) without terror.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson holds warning and comfort together, so let the students do the connecting.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "By this my Father is glorified, that you bear much fruit" (John 15:8).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Do I actually want to be Jesus' branch, connected to him? Write honestly."
- On-ramp for the doubter: an honest "I'm not sure" is a real answer.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 15:6-8 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 6 through 8.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus describes two very different branches, and two very different ends.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 6, what happens to the branch that does not abide in Jesus?
   - Cold-call check: it's thrown away, withers, is gathered up, thrown into the fire, and burned.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** This sounds scary, like a real Christian could get tossed out and burned. But hold it next to John 10:28, which you already know. What did Jesus promise there about his sheep?
   - Guide them: in 10:28 he said no one will snatch his sheep out of his hand, ever. So Jesus isn't contradicting himself here. The burned branch is one that looked connected but never truly was, like Judas, who sat at the table and got his feet washed and was never really Jesus'. First John 2:19 says it plainly: "they went out from us, but they were not of us."
3. **Core.** So who is this warning actually for? Who should it make stop and examine themselves?
   - Land it: it's for the person who looks the part, goes through the motions, knows the words, but has never actually trusted Jesus and stayed joined to him. It's a warning against faking it, not a threat to a real believer. If you're genuinely holding onto Jesus, this verse isn't aimed at scaring you.
4. **Core.** Now the other branch. Read verses 7 and 8. What does the abiding branch get to do, and what does its fruit prove?
   - Draw out: it can ask what it wishes and it'll be done (prayer shaped by his word), it bears much fruit, and that fruit glorifies the Father and shows the branch really is Jesus' disciple. Fruit is the evidence of a real connection.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Suppose a sincere Christian reads verse 6 and gets scared: "what if I'm the branch that gets burned?" Remember verse 5: fruit is the vine's work through the branch. So where should that scared person look, and what should they do?
   - Draw out: they look to Jesus, not to their own performance. The very fact that they want to be found in him, that they're grieved by sin and drawn to Christ, is fruit already growing. A person who wants to abide is abiding. The answer to fear about fruit is to stay close to the vine.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what glorifies the Father, according to verse 8?
   - Bearing much fruit, which proves you're really Jesus' disciple.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Take the warning seriously and gently. There really are branches that look attached and aren't. Judas was one. The warning is a loving question: are you actually connected to Jesus, or just near the vine? That's worth sitting with honestly.
- Then close the fear down for the sincere. If you're genuinely holding onto Jesus, verse 6 was never aimed at you. He said in chapter 10 that no one can pull his sheep out of his hand. Your connection is held by his grip, not yours.
- Point the anxious to the vine. If you look at your life and worry there's not enough fruit, don't stare harder at yourself. Look at Jesus and stay close to him. Fruit is his work through you. The wanting to be his is already the sap rising.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus holds his real branches secure, and the fruit he grows through them is what glorifies the Father.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Fruit is Jesus' work through a connected branch. If you ever worry about your own fruit, where does this passage tell you to look?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 15:5. Read John 15:9-11 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: abide in my love, and the joy Jesus wants to be full in them.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the densest pastoral lesson in the unit. The whole task is holding warning (15:6) and assurance (10:28-29) together without terror. Guard both errors: easy-believism (outward nearness is not union) and works-terror (never scare a trembling believer).
- The burned branch was never grafted in (Cyril names Judas; 1 John 2:19). Aim it at self-examination, warmly.
- Adam's pastoral move belongs in the room, in spirit: a sincere desire to be found in Christ is a really good sign. Fruit is confidence-building evidence, and not the price of salvation.
- Derive the frame from sermon 26; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
