**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain who Jesus prays for in this prayer, and why (17:9).
2. Students will explain "keep them in your name" as God preserving his people (17:11).
3. Students will explain the "son of destruction" (Judas) as the foretold exception, not a lost sheep (17:12).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus prays for his people by name.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): being a Christian is secure because God keeps you, not because you keep yourself.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the preservation of the saints held with the Judas exception (10:28, 6:39).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Holy Father, keep them in your name" (John 17:11).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I think about staying a Christian long-term, does it feel like it's up to me? How does that feel?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: honesty about the pressure is welcome.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 17:9-13 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 17, verses 9 through 13.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus prays specifically for his own disciples now, and asks the Father to keep them safe when he's gone.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 9, who does Jesus say he's praying for right now?
   - Cold-call check: his own people, the ones the Father gave him, not the world in this particular prayer.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Verse 9 says Jesus is "not praying for the world" here. Does that mean Jesus doesn't care about the world? Look ahead at verses 21 and 23, and remember John 3:16.
   - Guide them: no. In this one prayer, Jesus focuses on praying for his own, like a dad praying for his own kids by name. But the same prayer says twice that the goal is for the world to believe (21, 23), and 3:16 says God loved the whole world. Jesus prays for his people so that his people can be sent to reach the world. The world's hope is that Jesus has people he keeps and sends.
3. **Core.** Read verse 11: "Holy Father, keep them." Jesus is leaving, so he asks the Father to keep his people safe. Whose job is it to keep a Christian from falling away, according to this verse?
   - Land it: God's. Jesus doesn't say "tell them to hold on tight." He asks the Father to keep them. Back in John 10:28 he said no one can snatch his sheep out of his hand. Your security as a Christian rests on God's grip on you, not your grip on him. That's the most freeing thing in this passage.
4. **Core.** Read verse 12. Jesus says he guarded them, and "not one of them has been lost except the son of destruction." That's Judas. Does Judas prove that a real believer can be lost?
   - Draw out: no. Judas is called the exception, and his fall "fulfilled the Scripture," it was foretold (back at 13:18). Judas walked with Jesus, looked like a disciple, and was never truly the Father's. He was never a kept sheep who slipped away. He's a warning to check whether you really know Jesus (verse 3), not proof that a true believer gets dropped.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Hold two things together: Jesus keeps every one the Father gave him (verse 12, and 10:28), and Judas was lost (verse 12). How do both stay true?
   - Draw out: Judas was never one of the ones the Father gave and Jesus kept. He was among the twelve outwardly, but he was never truly Jesus'. So "I kept all you gave me" and "Judas was lost" both stand: Judas wasn't in the kept group to begin with. Nearness to Jesus isn't the same as belonging to him.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: who keeps a Christian safe, according to verse 11?
   - The Father. Jesus asks him to keep them.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Lift the weight off. Some of you carry a quiet fear: what if I don't make it, what if my faith runs out, what if I mess up too badly. Listen to what Jesus prays. He doesn't tell them to grip harder. He asks the Father to keep them. If you're his, you are held by God, and God doesn't lose his grip.
- Handle Judas honestly and gently. Judas is a real warning, and it's the right one: check whether you actually know Jesus, or just hang around him. It's not a reason for someone who loves Jesus to lie awake terrified. The warning is against faking it, and the comfort is for the real thing.
- Land the security. Being a Christian was never about you being strong enough to hold on. It's about a Savior strong enough to hold you. He prayed for exactly that the night before he died.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus prays for his own by name and asks the Father to keep every one, and God keeps who he keeps.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Your security rests on God's grip on you, not your grip on him. What would change about your week if you really believed that?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 17:3. Read John 17:14-16 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4 (short Thursday): Jesus asks the Father to keep his people in the world without letting the world claim them.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: 17:9's scope (Jesus prays for his own by name; guard against "he doesn't love the world," with 17:21, 23 and 3:16), and 17:11's preservation of the saints (God keeps you, not you keeping yourself; the confessions cite 17:11).
- The Judas exception (17:12): the foretold exception (13:18), never a true kept sheep. Held with 10:28 and 6:39. Guard hard against a believer fearing lost salvation; aim it at self-examination, warmly.
- Derive the frame from sermon 30; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
