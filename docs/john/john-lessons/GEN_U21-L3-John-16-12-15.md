**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what the Spirit of truth does (guides into all truth, 16:13).
2. Students will explain the Spirit's aim: to glorify Christ (16:14).
3. Students will explain that "all truth" is the finished teaching about Christ, not private new revelation.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the Spirit helps the church understand Jesus.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the Spirit always points to Christ, and won't speak on his own.
- **Stretch** (disciple): how "guide into all truth" grounds the apostles' teaching and the Bible, guarded against new-revelation claims.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He will glorify me" (John 16:14).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I get confused about what God wants, and where do I go to find out?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: name the confusion honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 16:12-15 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verses 12 through 15.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus has more to teach than they can hold right now, so he promises the Spirit to finish the job.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 12, why does Jesus stop short of telling them everything?
   - Cold-call check: they can't bear it now; they aren't ready for all of it yet.
2. **Entry.** In verse 13, who will finish the teaching, and what will he do?
   - Draw out: the Spirit of truth, who will guide them into all the truth.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 13 again: the Spirit "will not speak on his own authority." And verse 14: "he will glorify me." What does the Spirit always aim at? Whose spotlight does he run?
   - Guide them: the Spirit doesn't go off on his own agenda or point to himself. He points to Jesus. He takes what belongs to Christ and makes it known. Try to look at the Spirit, and you end up seeing Jesus.
4. **Core.** People sometimes say "the Spirit told me" to justify something the Bible never taught, or claim brand-new revelations that go past Scripture. How do verses 13 and 14 guard against that?
   - Land it: the Spirit "will not speak on his own authority" and his job is to "glorify me," Jesus. So any voice claiming to be the Spirit while contradicting or bypassing Jesus and his word isn't the Spirit. The "all truth" he leads into is the finished truth about Christ, which the apostles taught and wrote down. The Spirit lights up the Bible; he doesn't replace it.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 15: "all that the Father has is mine." The Spirit takes what is Jesus', and Jesus says everything is shared with the Father. What does that show about how Father, Son, and Spirit work together?
   - Draw out: one God, three persons, working in perfect harmony. The Spirit takes what belongs to the Son, which belongs to the Father, and delivers it to the church. No competition, no separate agendas. All of it aimed at making Christ known.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what is the Spirit's main aim, according to verse 14?
   - To glorify Christ, by taking what is his and making it known.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the Spirit's aim. The Holy Spirit is not a spotlight that shines on himself. He's a spotlight that shines on Jesus. Everything he does is to make Christ bigger and clearer to you. If something claims to be the Spirit but pulls you away from Jesus, it isn't him.
- Guard the truth. This is why "the Spirit told me" can never mean something that contradicts the Bible. The Spirit who guided the apostles into all truth is the same Spirit who inspired the Scripture you're holding. He leads you deeper into the word, never around it.
- Give them confidence. When you open this Gospel, you're reading truth the Spirit himself secured and made clear. You're not guessing about Jesus. The Spirit made sure you'd know him.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the Spirit's whole work is to take what is Christ's and make it known, so that his people see Jesus clearly.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The Spirit's job is to make Jesus clearer to you. Where do you most need him to do that right now?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: the Spirit's aim is to glorify Christ (16:14, the U17/U19 point), and "all truth" is the finished apostolic teaching about Christ, guarded against private new revelation (Ignatius: the Spirit speaks Christ's things; the confessions ground it in the Spirit's witness to Scripture, 1689 1.5).
- The Trinitarian ordering (16:14-15): the Spirit takes what is the Son's, which is the Father's. Keep it for the disciple tier.
- Handoff note: teach 16:12-15 in place. The grand Paraclete synthesis is the next unit (U22, "The Holy Spirit, gathered"). Don't preempt it here.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly.
