**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Mary's anointing was for (12:7) and why Judas really objected (12:6).
2. Students will contrast the crowd's political-king expectation with the humble King of Zechariah 9:9.
3. Students will explain what "the world has gone after him" (12:19) sets up.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a woman pours out a fortune on Jesus because she loves him.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the crowd wanted a King to fix their problems, and Jesus came to die.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Zechariah 9:9, the humble King; the disciples understood only after the resurrection.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students. Welcome them back from break.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Leave her alone, so that she may keep it for the day of my burial" (John 12:7).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What do I treat like it's worth everything?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest answer, whatever it is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 12:1-19 aloud, one reads 1 through 11, the other 12 through 19.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 12, verses 1 through 19.
- Set the scene in one sentence: it's six days before Passover, and Jesus is at a dinner in Bethany with Mary, Martha, and the raised Lazarus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 3, what does Mary do, and how expensive is it (see verse 5)?
   - Cold-call check: she pours a pound of pure nard on Jesus' feet and wipes them with her hair. It's worth three hundred denarii, about a year's wages.
2. **Entry.** In verse 5 Judas says it should have gone to the poor. In verse 6, what does John tell us was really going on with Judas?
   - He didn't care about the poor. He was a thief who stole from the moneybag. His concern was a mask.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 7. What does Jesus say Mary's gift was for? What does she understand that the others miss?
   - Guide them: she's anointing him for his burial. Mary, who watched Jesus raise her brother, believes Jesus is going to die, and she pours out everything to love him while he's here. She sees who he is, and it makes her worship extravagant.
4. **Core.** Read verses 12 through 15. The crowd welcomes Jesus as King, waving palm branches, expecting him to throw off Rome. What animal does Jesus ride in on, and what does that say about the kind of King he is?
   - Land it: a donkey, not a warhorse. A king on a warhorse comes to fight. A king on a donkey comes in peace. Jesus is fulfilling Zechariah 9:9. He's a real King, and he's riding in to die, not to raise an army. They wanted the wrong kind of king.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 16 says the disciples didn't understand this until after Jesus was glorified. And verse 19 has the Pharisees complain, "the world has gone after him." Look at the very next verse (12:20). Who shows up?
   - Draw out: Greeks, non-Jews. The Pharisees said "the whole world" as a complaint, and it turns out to be true. The world really is starting to come. The disciples understood the donkey later, in hindsight, by the Spirit, the same way they understood the temple saying back in chapter 2.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what kind of King did the crowd want, and what kind did Jesus show himself to be?
   - They wanted a conquering political king, and Jesus came as a humble King riding to his death.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 18.

- Start with Mary. She saw who Jesus really was, and it made her pour out a fortune without a second thought. Sermon 18's point: how you see Jesus decides how you worship him. If he's a nice teacher, you give him leftovers. If he's the Son of God going to a cross for you, you give him everything.
- Hold up Judas as the warning. He'd been following Jesus for years, and he was doing math the whole time, calculating what he could get. It's possible to be right next to Jesus and never actually love him.
- Name the King. The crowd wanted Jesus to fix their politics and beat their enemies. He rode in on a donkey, headed for a cross. A lot of us still want that Jesus, the one who solves our problems and takes our side. The real one is better and harder: a King who came to die for you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the King who was anointed for his burial and rode into his city humble to die for you.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Mary treated Jesus like he was worth everything. Do you want a Jesus who fixes your problems, or the real King who died for you? Write honestly."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 12:20-36 once before Tuesday. That lesson has an optional memory verse.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: the Greeks come, and Jesus says the hour has finally arrived.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%. Welcome students back from break.
- Build on sermon 18 for the anointing: how you see Jesus determines how you worship him, with Judas as the counter-example (a calculating heart, not care for the poor).
- Guard the anointing from becoming "be generous." The point is worship poured out on the Christ who dies, faith that counts him worth everything.
- Frame the triumphal entry fresh (no sermon). The humble King of Zechariah 9:9 comes to die, not to lead a revolt. "The world has gone after him" (12:19) is the seam into the Greeks next lesson.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
