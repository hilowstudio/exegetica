**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus says the crowd's real motive is (6:26).
2. Students will contrast the food that perishes with the food that endures (6:27).
3. Students will explain why "believe in him whom he has sent" is the one work God requires (6:29).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus calls out the crowd for chasing him just for the food.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): it's possible to want Jesus for what he gives and miss him.
- **Stretch** (disciple): faith as the one "work" of God, the end of works-righteousness.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent" (John 6:29).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Why do I actually come to God?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 6:25-29 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 6, verses 25 through 29.
- One-sentence setup: the crowd finds Jesus the next day, and he tells them why they're really looking for him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verse 26, why does Jesus say the crowd is looking for him?
   - Not because they understood the sign, but because they "ate your fill of the loaves." They want more free food.
2. **Read-check.** In verse 27, what should they work for instead?
   - "The food that endures to eternal life," which the Son of Man gives.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** In verse 28 the crowd asks what they must "do" to work the works of God. In verse 29 Jesus answers, "believe in him whom he has sent." Work this: the crowd wants a task to complete. What does Jesus hand them instead, and why is that good news?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out: Jesus takes the "work" off their shoulders and puts it back on God. The one thing God asks is that you trust the One he sent. It's a gift to receive, and that's the relief. No matter how many times we hear salvation is free, our next question is "so what do I have to do?" Jesus answers, believe.
   - Push the disciple tier: believing is a "work" you can't take credit for, because it's leaning your whole weight on someone else. It's the opposite of earning.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the opening of sermon 08.

- Name the diagnostic gently. Jesus reads the crowd's heart: you're here for the bread, not for me. There's a real difference between wanting Jesus as your treasure and wanting Jesus to get you something else you treasure more.
- Ask the room the honest version. When you pray, when you show up to church, when you call on God, are you coming for him or for what he can hand you? No shame in the question. It's the door into everything he says next.
- Land on the gift. The work God wants isn't a to-do list. It's to trust the One he sent. You bring empty hands and receive him. That's the whole thing.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus gives the food that lasts, and the one work God asks is to receive him by faith.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus asks whether you want him or just what he gives. Answer honestly, and write what it would look like to come to him for himself."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 6:30-40 once before Friday. That's the memory-verse lesson.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: the crowd brings up the manna, and Jesus says plainly, "I am the bread of life."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving.
- Build on sermon 08's opening: treasuring Jesus versus using him. This is the diagnostic that runs through the whole discourse.
- Keep 6:29 grace-first. Faith is a gift to receive, the end of works-righteousness. Watch the student who hears "believe" and turns it into one more thing to be good enough at.
- This passage does not contain the memory verse, which keeps 6:35 off the short day and lets it plant fresh on Friday.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. Protect Move 4.
