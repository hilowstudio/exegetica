**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain how the sheep know their shepherd (10:3-5).
2. Students will explain what "I am the door" claims (10:7-9).
3. Students will explain what "life abundantly" means in context (10:10).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the sheep follow the voice they know and run from a stranger's.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus is still talking to the leaders who threw the blind man out.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Ezekiel 34, God against the bad shepherds; "abundant life" guarded from prosperity.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "The sheep hear his voice, and he calls his own sheep by name" (John 10:3).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Whose voice do I let steer me most?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest answer, even if it isn't God.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 10:1-10 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 10, verses 1 through 10.
- Set the scene in one sentence: there's no break here from chapter 9, so Jesus is still talking to the leaders who threw the healed blind man out.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 3 through 5, how do the sheep know which shepherd to follow? What do they do when a stranger calls?
   - Cold-call check: they know their shepherd's voice. He calls them by name and they follow. A stranger's voice, they flee.
2. **Entry.** You've got a hundred voices trying to steer you (feeds, friends, ads, your own head). How do you tell which ones actually care about you?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus is telling this to the men who just threw a healed man out of the synagogue. Back in the Old Testament (Ezekiel 34), God said the leaders of Israel were bad shepherds who fed themselves and neglected the sheep. What's Jesus quietly saying about these leaders, and about himself?
   - Guide them: he's saying they're the bad shepherds Ezekiel warned about, and he's the true one. The tell is right in the text: these leaders can't understand him (verse 6). They don't know the shepherd's voice, which is the proof they're not his sheep.
4. **Core.** Read verses 7 and 9. Jesus says, "I am the door." What's he claiming to be the way into?
   - Land it: he's the one way in to being saved and to finding pasture, real life with God. Not one option among many. The door.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 10. Jesus says he came so people can "have life and have it abundantly." A thief is in the same sentence. Given that, what kind of "abundant life" is Jesus promising, and what is he not promising?
   - Draw out: the abundant life stands against a thief who steals, kills, and destroys, so it means rescue, safety, and real life in the shepherd's care, the eternal life he names in verse 28. It's not a promise of money, comfort, or an easy life. Cross before crown.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does Jesus mean when he calls himself "the door"?
   - He's the one way in to being saved and having life with God.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 15.

- Start with the voice. A real shepherd knows his sheep by name and leads them, and the sheep learn to trust his voice over every stranger's. Ask the room plainly: the voices steering you, do they want you, or do they want something from you? Most of them are ranchers fattening you for market. Jesus is a shepherd.
- Name the door, and its narrowness as mercy. Jesus says he's the one way in. That can sound narrow, and it's actually the kindest thing. He's not one option in a lineup. He's the open door, and anyone who comes through him is saved.
- Guard the "abundant life." Jesus isn't promising you a comfortable life. He's promising you himself, and real life in his keeping, which is worth more than comfort. The thief offers the easy version and steals everything. The shepherd offers his own life and gives you real life.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the door, the one way in, and the shepherd whose voice is worth following over every other.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "A lot of voices want to lead you. Whose are you following, and what would it look like to follow the Shepherd's voice this week?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 10:11-18 once before Monday. That's the memory-verse lesson.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: Jesus says what makes him the good shepherd, that he lays down his life for the sheep.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Set the Ezekiel 34 background here (God against the self-feeding shepherds, God himself coming to shepherd). It ties the whole unit together and it's the text the priests used at the Feast of Dedication later in the chapter.
- Keep the door saying whole in this lesson. Guard "abundant life" (10:10) hard against prosperity: its content is rescue and eternal life, not health or wealth.
- Remind students there's no real break from chapter 9. The audience is the leaders who cast out the blind man. Jesus is calling them the bad shepherds.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
