**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe what the soldiers did with Jesus' garments (19:23-24).
2. Students will identify the fulfillment of Psalm 22:18 (19:24).
3. Students will explain how the fulfilled detail shows God's sovereignty over the cross.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): even the soldiers' gambling was written about ahead of time.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): God's control reaches down to the smallest detail of the cross.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Psalm 22, written 1,000 years before, fulfilled to the letter.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "They divided my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots" (John 19:24, quoting Psalm 22).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where does life feel random or out of control to me right now?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:23-24 aloud, slowly. It helps to also read Psalm 22:18.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 23 and 24; and Psalm 22, verse 18.
- One-sentence setup: while Jesus hangs on the cross, the soldiers below him split up his clothes.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 23 and 24, what do the soldiers do with Jesus' clothes? What do they do with the seamless tunic?
   - Cold-call check: they divide his garments into four parts, and rather than tear the seamless tunic, they gamble (cast lots) for it.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read John 19:24 and Psalm 22:18 side by side. What did the psalm, written about a thousand years before Jesus, say would happen?
   - Guide them: Psalm 22:18 says, "they divided my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots." That's exactly what the soldiers do, down to the detail: dividing the clothes AND gambling for one piece. A thousand years early, written to the letter.
3. **Core.** The soldiers have no idea they're fulfilling Scripture. They're just bored guards splitting up a dead man's stuff. What does it tell you that even their random gambling landed exactly on prophecy?
   - Land it: it tells you nothing at the cross was an accident. God was in control of every detail, right down to a dice game over clothing. The soldiers thought they were making their own choices, and they were, and God had written it a thousand years before. If God rules the soldiers' dice, he rules everything.
4. **Core.** Why does John bother to point this out? What's the comfort in knowing the cross went exactly according to a plan written centuries earlier?
   - Draw out: because it means the cross wasn't God's plan falling apart. It was God's plan being carried out, perfectly, down to the smallest detail. Jesus wasn't a good man whose mission failed. He was the promised one, dying exactly as it was written, right on schedule. The cross was under complete control the whole time.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Psalm 22 opens with "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?", which Jesus quotes from the cross in the other Gospels, and it goes on to describe the crucifixion in detail. What does it mean that David wrote a psalm a thousand years early that reads like an eyewitness account of Golgotha?
   - Draw out: it means God authored the whole story. He put the cross into the songs of Israel a thousand years before it happened, so that when it came, his people would know it was him. The Bible is one book with one Author, and it all points to this hill.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did the soldiers' actions in verse 24 fulfill?
   - Psalm 22:18, written a thousand years before, about dividing his garments and casting lots.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Zoom in on the dice. Down at the foot of the cross, a few soldiers are gambling for a dead man's shirt. It's the most random, careless thing in the scene. And it's written in the Bible, word for word, a thousand years before it happened. God's control reaches all the way down to a dice game.
- Land the comfort. If God is that in control, right down to whose hand won the tunic, then the cross was never chaos. It was a plan, written centuries early, carried out to the letter. Jesus didn't die because things spun out of control. He died exactly as it was written, exactly on time.
- Bring it to their lives. And here's the reach for you: the God who ruled the soldiers' dice rules the parts of your life that feel random and out of control. Nothing about you is outside his hand. If he scripted a gambling game a thousand years ahead, he's not surprised by your life.
- Christ connection to say out loud: even the smallest, most careless detail of the cross was written ahead of time, because God ruled every inch of it.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "God controlled the cross down to a dice game. Where do you need to trust that he's in control of the parts of your life that feel random?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell
