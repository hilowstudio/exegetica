**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain Peter's comparison and Jesus' answer, "you follow me" (21:20-22).
2. Students will explain the correcting of the rumor about the beloved disciple (21:23).
3. Students will explain the eyewitness ending and the hyperbole of 21:25.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus tells Peter to mind his own calling and follow.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): comparison distracts from following Jesus.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the eyewitness authentication of the Gospel; the inexhaustible Christ (21:25).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This closes the whole verse-by-verse walk; leave room to land the book and look back over the year.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "You follow me!" (John 21:22).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I compare my life to someone else's instead of following Jesus?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 21:20-25 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 21, verses 20 through 25.
- One-sentence setup: right after being told to follow, Peter looks over at another disciple and asks about him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 21, Peter sees the disciple John and asks Jesus a question. What is it?
   - Cold-call check: "Lord, what about this man?" He wants to know what will happen to John.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Peter has just been restored, given a job, and told his future. And immediately he looks over at John and asks, "what about him?" What's Peter doing? Do you ever do the same?
   - Guide them: he's comparing. The second he gets his own calling, he's checking on someone else's, maybe wondering if John gets an easier road. It's the comparison reflex. We do it constantly: measuring our life, our gifts, our path against other people's instead of just following Jesus with the one we've got.
3. **Core.** Read verse 22. How does Jesus answer Peter's "what about him?" Is it harsh or gracious?
   - Land it: "if I want him to stay until I come, what is that to you? You follow me." It sounds blunt, but it's gracious. Jesus isn't brushing Peter off; he's freeing him. He's saying: John's story is between me and John. Your job is to follow me with your life, not to compare it to his. Keep your eyes on me, and follow. Comparison is a thief; Jesus is taking it away from Peter so he can just follow.
4. **Core.** Read verses 24 and 25. John finally identifies himself: he's the disciple who saw these things and wrote them down, and he swears his testimony is true. Then he ends with verse 25. What does verse 25 say, and why is it a fitting way to end?
   - Draw out: John says Jesus did so many things that if they were all written down, the world itself couldn't hold the books. It's a beautiful exaggeration that's also true: Jesus is bigger than any book. After a whole Gospel of signs and sayings, John says he's only scratched the surface. There's more of Jesus than could ever fit on a page. That's the right note to end on: an inexhaustible Christ.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Look back over the whole year. John told you in 20:31 why he wrote the book: so you'd believe and have life. He ends here, on a beach, with a restored failure and "do you love me" and "follow me." Why is THIS the right place to end the story of Jesus?
   - Draw out: because it's personal. John could have ended with the resurrection appearances or a grand statement. Instead he ends with Jesus restoring one failing friend and asking him "do you love me" and "follow me." That's what the whole book is for: to bring you, personally, to love and follow Jesus. It ends where it's aiming, at your heart.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how does Jesus answer Peter's comparison, and how does John end the book?
   - "You follow me"; and John ends saying Jesus did more than the world's books could hold.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ, close the unit, and close the whole walk through John.

- Land the comparison cure. Peter gets restored, gets a mission, and immediately looks sideways: "what about him?" And Jesus, kindly, takes his chin and turns his face back: "what is that to you? You follow me." Some of you spend so much energy comparing your life to everyone else's that you forget to follow Jesus with your own. Hear him say it to you: mind your call, and follow me.
- Land the inexhaustible Christ. And then John lays down his pen and says: honestly, there's more of Jesus than any book could hold. We spent a whole year in this Gospel, and John says we've barely begun. Jesus is bigger than everything we studied. You'll never run out of him.
- Look back over the year, and land the book. Think about where we started: "the Word became flesh." And where we've come: the cross, "it is finished," the empty tomb, and now a beach where the risen Lord restores a failure. John wrote every word of it so that you would believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name (20:31). And he ends the whole thing with the question that holds it all together, spoken to a man who failed and was loved anyway: do you love me. Follow me.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Christ, bigger than any book, restores failures and calls them to follow him, and that's where John ends the whole story.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "We've walked all the way through John, from 'the Word became flesh' to 'do you love me.' What's one thing about Jesus you'll carry with you? Write honestly."
- Give it quiet, and let it be reflective. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. We've finished the Gospel of John. Next we step back and look at the whole Christ of the whole book.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 21:17 stays in the rolling memory quizzes, completing the set of sixteen.
- Sets up Unit 28: pulling the whole Gospel together, the whole Christ of John, and the final project.
