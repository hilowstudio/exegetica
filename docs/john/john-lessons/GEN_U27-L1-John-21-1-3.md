**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Peter's decision to go fishing and the empty night (21:3).
2. Students will explain fishing as Peter's old life, drifted back to after failure.
3. Students will connect the empty night to "apart from me you can do nothing" (15:5).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): after failing, Peter went back to his old job.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): drifting to the familiar after failure, and coming up empty.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the empty night as the old life run dry (15:5).

---

**Talk budget:** short half-day. Keep it light and warm. One clear center, and let students do the noticing.

## Move 1: Quiet time and a brief memory warm-touch (6 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away. Note the moment: this is the last chapter of John.
- Quick warm pass: have students say two anchors together, 10:11 and 15:5, both of which come alive in this unit. Light, no quiz.
- Centering line on the board: "that night they caught nothing" (John 21:3).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I've messed up, where do I drift back to?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 21:1-3 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 21, verses 1 through 3.
- One-sentence setup: some of the disciples are back home in Galilee, and Peter decides to go fishing.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 12 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus for the short day: the empty night. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 3, what does Peter decide to do, and how does the fishing go?
   - Cold-call check: he says "I'm going fishing," the others go with him, and they catch nothing all night.
2. **The driving question (whole class).** Fishing wasn't just a hobby for Peter. It was his old job, the life Jesus first called him away from years ago. He's just failed badly, denying Jesus three times. Why does a failing, disillusioned Peter drift back to the old boat?
   - Guide them: because it's familiar, and he doesn't know what else to do. When you've blown it and you feel lost, you go back to what you know. Peter retreats into his old identity, back before Jesus, back to nets and boats. It's what people do when they think they've disqualified themselves.
3. **Core.** They fish all night, expert fishermen, and catch nothing. Remember John 15:5, "apart from me you can do nothing." How is that empty net a picture of Peter's situation?
   - Land it: the old life, without Christ, comes up empty. Peter's back where he started, and the nets are empty all night, because the life he's drifting back to has nothing in it. It's a picture: apart from Jesus, even the thing you're good at comes up empty. And Jesus is about to show up on that shore.
4. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did Peter go back to, and what did he catch?
   - His old fishing life; he caught nothing all night.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (4 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name the drift. Peter blew it, badly, and now he's back in the boat, back to the old life, because he doesn't know where else to go. Some of you know that feeling. You mess up, you feel like you've ruined it, and you drift back to the old you, the old habits, the old crowd, because it's familiar and it doesn't ask anything of you.
- Land the empty net. And it comes up empty. All night, nothing. The old life, without Jesus, always does. It promises comfort and delivers an empty net at dawn.
- Point to the shore. But here's the thing to hold onto over the weekend: Jesus is already standing on the shore. Peter doesn't know it yet. He's drifted away, and the risen Lord has come to find him. That's who Jesus is toward people who wander off after failing.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Christ comes to the shore to find his drifting friend, and the empty net is about to be filled at his word.

## Move 5: Respond (short, graded participation)

- One line in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Peter drifted back to the old life, and it came up empty. Where have you drifted, and is it coming up empty?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 21:4-8 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: a stranger on the shore fills the net, and Peter jumps in.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: half-day, 26 minutes, and the opening of the last unit. Keep it light and warm; the calendar names it a memory-quiz-and-recap beat, so keep the memory pass brief and plant nothing new today.
- One center: the empty night as the old life run dry (15:5). Peter drifts back to the old identity after failure; Jesus later calls him by his old name, "Simon, son of John" (21:15).
- Guard against reading this as a rebuke of ordinary work; the point is a disillusioned man drifting to the familiar, and a Lord who comes to find him.
- Set the warm, restorative tone for the whole final unit here.
- Fresh-build; no sermon.
