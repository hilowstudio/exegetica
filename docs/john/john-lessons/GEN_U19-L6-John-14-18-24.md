**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the promise "I will not leave you as orphans" (14:18).
2. Students will connect loving Jesus and keeping his word (14:21, 23).
3. Students will explain what it means that the Father and Son "make our home" with the believer (14:23).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus promises never to abandon his people.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): God doesn't just prepare a place for you; he comes to live in you.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the triune indwelling (14:23) and its link to the Father's house of 14:2.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you" (John 14:18).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When do I feel most alone?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: name it plainly, even if you're not sure anyone's listening.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:18-24 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 18 through 24.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus is about to leave, and he promises the opposite of abandonment.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 18, what does Jesus promise he will not do? What does he promise he will do?
   - Cold-call check: he will not leave them as orphans; he will come to them.
2. **Entry.** In verse 19, why will the disciples live? Read the reason.
   - Draw out: "Because I live, you also will live." His life is the source of theirs.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Verses 21 and 23 both tie loving Jesus to keeping his word. Is keeping his word how you earn his love, or is it what love naturally does? Where do you see that in the verses?
   - Guide them: it's what love does. Verse 21: "whoever has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me." Love shows itself in obedience. And the promise attached is huge: the Father loves him, and Jesus manifests himself to him.
4. **Core.** Read verse 23 carefully: "we will come to him and make our home with him." Who is "we," and what do they do?
   - Land it: the Father and the Son come and make their home in the person who loves Jesus. Not just a visit. They move in. Back in verse 2, Jesus prepares a place for you in the Father's house. Here, God makes his home in you. Same picture, both directions.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Judas (not Iscariot) asks in verse 22 why Jesus will show himself to them and not to the world. How does Jesus' answer in verse 23 explain the difference?
   - Draw out: the difference is love and the word kept. God makes his home in those who love him and hold his word. The world doesn't see him because it won't receive him. It's not favoritism; it's the door of love and faith standing open.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 23, what do the Father and Son do for the one who loves Jesus and keeps his word?
   - They come to him and make their home with him.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Meet the fear head-on. Jesus is leaving, and the deepest fear in that room is abandonment. His first word is "orphans," and his promise is: not you. Never you. "I will come to you."
- Show them how big the promise is. He doesn't just say he'll visit. Verse 23: he and the Father come and make their home in you. Back in verse 2 he goes to prepare a place for you in the Father's house. Now he says the Father's house also comes to live in you. You're that wanted.
- Answer the loneliness. If God himself lives in you, "alone" changes meaning. You can feel lonely and still not be abandoned, because the One who made you has moved in.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus doesn't leave his people as orphans; he and the Father make their home in everyone who loves him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus promises never to leave his people as orphans. What would it change about your loneliest moments to believe that?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 14:6. Read John 14:25-27 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L7: the Spirit who teaches and reminds, and the peace Jesus leaves that the world can't give.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The heart of the lesson: Jesus doesn't abandon his own (14:18), and God makes his home in them (14:23). Connect 14:23 back to 14:2, the same picture from both ends.
- Keep 14:21 and 14:23 grace-first: love shows itself in obedience; it doesn't purchase God's presence. Same order as L5.
- 14:22-23 (Judas's question) answers "why us and not the world" with love and the word kept, not favoritism. Keep it warm, not exclusive-sounding.
- Derive the "his presence in you" frame from sermon 25; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
