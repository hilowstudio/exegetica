# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 26 Lesson 5 of 8: peace, and the wounds (John 20:19-20)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U26 The empty tomb |
| Lesson | 5 of 8 |
| Date / Day type | Mon May 3 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 20:19-20 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus appears to the frightened disciples who abandoned him, and his first word is "peace," and they know him by the wounds he still carries. |
| Christ connection | The crucified-and-risen Lord bringing peace to the ones who abandoned him, known by his wounds. |
| Memory verse | No new verse this unit (review-only). Keep the fifteen sharp, including 16:33 (peace; take heart), delivered in person here. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the disciples were behind locked doors (20:19).
2. Students will explain Jesus' first word to them, "Peace be with you" (20:19).
3. Students will explain how the disciples recognized him (his wounds, 20:20).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus came to the friends who ran, and said "peace."
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the risen Jesus meets fear and failure with peace.
- **Stretch** (disciple): recognized by his wounds; the glorified body still bears the marks.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Peace be with you" (John 20:19).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I most afraid of right now, or where have I failed lately?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 20:19-20 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 19 and 20.
- One-sentence setup: it's Sunday evening, and the disciples are hiding, terrified.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 19, where are the disciples, and why? Why are the doors locked?
   - Cold-call check: they're together behind locked doors, hiding, because they're afraid (they think they might be arrested next).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Remember what these disciples just did: they ran away when Jesus was arrested, and Peter denied even knowing him. Now the risen Jesus appears to them. What's the very first thing he says? Is it what you'd expect?
   - Guide them: his first word is "Peace be with you." Not "how could you abandon me?" Not "where were you?" Not a word of blame. Peace. He comes to the men who failed him at his worst moment, and his opening line is peace. That's grace they did not earn and could not have expected.
3. **Core.** Read verse 20. What does Jesus show them, and how do they react?
   - Land it: he shows them his hands and his side, the wounds. And they're overjoyed when they see the Lord. They recognize him by the marks of the crucifixion. The risen body still carries the scars.
4. **Core (the wounds).** Jesus is risen and glorified, and he STILL has the wounds. He could have come back with a perfect, unmarked body. Why does he keep the scars? What do the wounds say to the men looking at them?
   - Draw out: the wounds are proof it's really him, the same Jesus who was crucified, now alive. And they're a permanent reminder of what he did for them. He carries the marks of his love forever. The scars say: this is the price I paid, and I'd pay it again. He wears his love for them on his hands.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Put together the failure (they ran) and the first word (peace) and the wounds (shown, not hidden). What's the whole picture saying about the kind of Savior Jesus is?
   - Draw out: he seeks out the ones who failed him, leads with peace, and shows them the very wounds their sin helped cause, and it's all mercy. He doesn't rub their failure in their faces. He shows them the wounds that paid for it, and says peace. That's the gospel: the ones who ran are met with peace by the one whose scars saved them.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's Jesus' first word to the disciples who abandoned him, and how do they recognize him?
   - "Peace be with you," and they recognize him by his wounds.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the first word. Picture it. The disciples are hiding, ashamed, terrified, having abandoned Jesus at the cross. And he walks through the locked door and looks at the very men who ran, and the first thing he says is "peace." Not a word of blame. Peace. That's who he is toward people who fail him.
- Land the wounds. And then he shows them his hands and side. He's glorified now, he could look however he wants, and he keeps the scars. Forever. Because those wounds are the proof of his love, and he's not ashamed of them. He shows the failing disciples the very marks that paid for their failure.
- Bring it home. Some of you are hiding behind a locked door of your own, ashamed of a failure, afraid of being found out. Hear the risen Jesus walk right through it and say to you what he said to them: peace. He doesn't come to shame you. He comes with peace, and he shows you the wounds that make it possible.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Lord meets the ones who failed him with peace, carrying forever the wounds that saved them.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus met the friends who ran with 'peace,' not blame. Where do you need to hear him say 'peace' to you, in a fear or a failure?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell
