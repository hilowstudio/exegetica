**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why Jesus warns them about persecution ahead of time (16:1, 16:4).
2. Students will explain what's coming: expulsion and killing done as "service to God" (16:2).
3. Students will explain why Jesus' going is to their advantage (16:7).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus tells his friends the hard truth so it won't blindside them.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): being warned honestly is a form of care, not a threat.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Spirit's coming is genuine gain over Jesus' bodily presence, not a downgrade.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "It is to your advantage that I go away" (John 16:7).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When has someone told me a hard truth I needed to hear? Was it kind or cruel?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it straight.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 16:1-7 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verses 1 through 7.
- One-sentence setup: still the same night, Jesus gets honest about what following him will cost, and why his leaving is actually good.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 1, why does Jesus say he's telling them all these hard things?
   - Cold-call check: to keep them from falling away when the trouble hits.
2. **Entry.** In verse 2, what specifically does Jesus say is coming for them?
   - Draw out: they'll be put out of the synagogues, and a time is coming when people who kill them will think they're doing God a favor.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 4: "that when their hour comes you may remember that I told them to you." Why would being warned ahead of time keep their faith from breaking?
   - Guide them: if suffering came out of nowhere, they might think Jesus was wrong, or that God had abandoned them. But he told them in advance, so when it lands they can say, "he knew, he said so, this is not a surprise to him." Foreknowledge turns a faith-shattering shock into evidence that Jesus was telling the truth.
4. **Core.** Read verse 7: "it is to your advantage that I go away." How can Jesus leaving possibly be good for them? What does his going make possible?
   - Land it: his going sends the Helper. If he stays in one body in one place, he can only be with them there. Once he goes to the Father and sends the Spirit, he's with all his people everywhere, living inside them. His leaving is an upgrade, not a loss.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Be honest: most of us would say we'd rather have Jesus physically standing here than have the Holy Spirit. Jesus says the opposite. Why is the Spirit's coming actually better, not second-best?
   - Draw out: the bodily Jesus could be in one room at a time. The Spirit indwells every believer, in every place, at once, forever. And the Spirit's whole job is to make Christ real to you from the inside. It's more of Jesus, not less.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why does Jesus say it's good that he's leaving?
   - Because his going sends the Helper, who will be with all his people everywhere.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name the honesty. Jesus doesn't sell his friends a fantasy. On his last night he tells them plainly that following him could cost them everything, even their lives. He loves them too much to lie. A faith that only works when life is easy isn't worth much, and he won't hand them one.
- Turn the warning into care. He tells them ahead of time so that when it comes, it won't wreck them. That's what love does with hard news. It prepares you.
- Land the advantage. And then, the surprise: my leaving is good for you. He goes so the Spirit comes, and the Spirit puts Jesus inside you, everywhere, forever, closer than he could stand beside you. You didn't get a lesser deal. You got more of him.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus warns his own to keep them, and goes to the Father so his Spirit can be with them always.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says his Spirit living in you is better than having him physically next to you. Do you believe that? What would change if you did?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 16:8-11 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2 (short Thursday): when the Helper comes, he puts the world on trial.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is a sermon-less fresh-build. The persecution warning (16:1-4) continues 15:18-25 directly; the killer who thinks he serves God is Paul before Damascus (Acts 26:9-11; Romans 10:2-3), and synagogue expulsion is the fear already at work in John 9:22.
- The key guard: "it is to your advantage that I go" is genuine gain, not a downgrade of the Spirit (Chrysostom on the Spirit's honor; Heidelberg Q49 on the ascension's benefit). Guard against making the Spirit sound second-best.
- Keep the tone honest and warm, not alarmist.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
