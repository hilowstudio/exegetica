**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the "little while" (the cross and grief, then the resurrection joy) (16:16-19).
2. Students will explain the childbirth picture: joy that comes through the pain (16:21).
3. Students will explain that the coming joy is one no one can take away (16:22).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus promises grieving people that joy is coming.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the joy comes through the sorrow, not around it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the cross-shaped pattern (real anguish producing a joy that can't be stolen).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Your sorrow will turn into joy" (John 16:20).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's a hard thing I'm carrying right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: you don't have to tie it up neatly. Just name it.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 16:16-22 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verses 16 through 22.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus says something confusing about "a little while," and then explains it with a picture every family understands.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 16, what does Jesus say about seeing him? What are the disciples confused about (verses 17-18)?
   - Cold-call check: "a little while" and they won't see him, "a little while" and they will; they don't understand what he means.
2. **Entry.** The first "little while" is the cross and their grief. The second is the resurrection, when they see him again. So what's the short, dark gap Jesus is preparing them for?
   - Draw out: the time between his death and his rising, Friday's grief before Sunday's joy.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 20: "you will be sorrowful, but your sorrow will turn into joy." Does Jesus tell them their sorrow isn't real, or that they should just cheer up?
   - Guide them: no. He says they really will weep and grieve, and the world will celebrate while they hurt. He doesn't deny the pain. He promises it will turn into joy. Real grief, real turn.
4. **Core.** Read the childbirth picture in verse 21 carefully. Does the joy come after the pain is over, like the pain was just an annoying delay? Or does the joy come through the pain?
   - Land it: through it. The labor pain isn't a pointless obstacle before the baby. The labor is how the baby comes. The pain produces the joy. That's the pattern of the cross: the suffering wasn't a detour on the way to joy. It was the road to it. Death became the doorway to life.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 22: "no one will take your joy from you." How is a joy that comes through suffering more durable than a happiness that depends on nothing going wrong?
   - Draw out: happiness built on good circumstances dies the second circumstances change. But joy that was born out of the worst thing, the cross, and came out the other side in resurrection, can't be threatened by hard times. It already survived the worst. Nobody can take it, because it doesn't depend on things going well.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to the childbirth picture, how does the joy come?
   - Through the pain, the way a baby comes through labor.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Honor the grief first. Jesus doesn't hand grieving people a slogan. He looks the disciples in the eye the night before he dies and tells them, yes, you're going to weep, this is going to hurt. He's honest about the pain. So are we.
- Then give them the picture. He compares it to a woman in labor. The pain is real and it's severe, and it's not wasted. It's how the baby comes. That's the cross. The suffering wasn't God losing control. It was the labor that brought resurrection life into the world.
- Land the durable joy. And the joy that comes out the far side of the worst thing, no one can take from you. A happiness that needs everything to go right is fragile. A joy that was born out of a cross is unbreakable.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus turned the worst day into the doorway of joy, and the joy he gives can't be stolen.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says sorrow can turn into joy, and even come through the pain. Where do you need to believe your hard season isn't the end of the story?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 16:23-28 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: praying in Jesus' name, and the stunning line "the Father himself loves you."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The whole lesson turns on the childbirth image: joy comes through the pain, not around it (Calvin: the cross always contains the victory). Adam's sermon 28 guards it hard: the blood of Christ doesn't numb or replace the pain; the baby is produced through the pain.
- Guard against cheapening it into "just be positive" or denying real grief. The tears are named (16:20). Honor the grief, then give the promise.
- Sermon 28's "chutes and ladders versus chess" picture (God intimately at work in the suffering itself, not a distant clockmaker or a frantic reactor) fits the Land here. Derive it from the sermon; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
