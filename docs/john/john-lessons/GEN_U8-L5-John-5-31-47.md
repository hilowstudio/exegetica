# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 8 Lesson 5 of 5: everything points to Jesus (John 5:31-47)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U8 Sign 3 and the Son's authority |
| Lesson | 5 of 5 |
| Date / Day type | Mon Oct 26 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 5:31-47 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Four witnesses back Jesus up, and the Scriptures themselves point to him, so the people who studied the Bible hardest walked right past its subject. |
| Christ connection | The whole Old Testament testifies to Christ, and coming to him is where the Scriptures were leading the entire time. |
| Memory verse | No new verse this unit. Review the five: John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16, 4:24. Next new one is Unit 9 (John 6:35). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will name the four witnesses Jesus points to (5:33-39).
2. Students will explain what Jesus means that they "search the Scriptures" but "refuse to come to me" (5:39-40).
3. Students will explain what "Moses wrote of me" (5:46) claims about the Old Testament.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus gives his evidence, four witnesses that back up who he is.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you can know the Bible cold and miss the Person it's about.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the christotelic claim, that the whole Old Testament was pointing to Christ (5:46; Luke 24:27).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "it is they that bear witness about me" (John 5:39).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I read the Bible, am I looking for Jesus or for something else?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write what you actually go to the Bible for, if anything.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 5:31-47 aloud, one reads 31 through 40, the other 41 through 47.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 5, verses 31 through 47.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus lays out his witnesses, then names the surprising problem, that the Bible experts missed the whole point of the Book.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Their own law said you needed more than one witness for a claim to stand. In verses 33 through 39, count Jesus' witnesses. Name all four.
   - Cold-call check: John the Baptist (5:33-35), Jesus' own works (5:36), the Father (5:37), and the Scriptures (5:39).
2. **Entry.** Have you ever known a lot about something and still totally missed the point of it? What was it?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 39 and 40. These men studied the Scriptures more carefully than anyone alive. Jesus says they did it "because you think that in them you have eternal life," and then he drops the hammer. What does he say they refuse to do?
   - Guide them: they "refuse to come to me that you may have life." They read the Bible for life and walked right past the One the Bible is about. Knowing the words is not the same as coming to the Word.
4. **Core.** In verse 46 Jesus says, "If you believed Moses, you would believe me; for he wrote of me." What is Jesus claiming the Old Testament is really about?
   - Land it: himself. Moses, the prophets, the whole Old Testament was pointing forward to Christ. The people who hoped in Moses missed that Moses was writing about Jesus.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Turn to Luke 24:27. After he rose, Jesus walked two disciples through "all the Scriptures" about himself. Put John 5:46 next to Luke 24:27. What does Jesus say is the point of the whole Old Testament?
   - Draw out: it's all about him. Every promise, every sign, every shadow was leading here. This is how the whole course reads the Bible, front to back, with Jesus as the point.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did these Bible experts have right, and what did they miss?
   - They studied the Scriptures carefully and missed the Jesus the Scriptures point to.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. This is the sermon's third movement.

- Name the christotelic point plainly. The whole Old Testament was pointing to Jesus the entire time. When you read the parts before the Gospels, you're reading the setup for him. Moses wrote about him.
- Speak the sermon's sharpest line to the churched student. On the last day, one of two records gets read over each person: the record of your deeds, or the record of Christ's. The only difference is whether you came to Jesus or came on your own merit. The good news is that his record is enough, and it's offered to you.
- Keep it grace-first. Everything, the whole Bible, the four witnesses, all of it, is God pointing you to his Son and saying come to him and live.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the whole Old Testament testifies to Christ, and coming to him is where the Scriptures were leading all along.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says you can know the Bible and still miss him. What would it look like for you to read Scripture looking for Jesus this week?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing your five memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. No new required memory verse this unit.
- Sets up Unit 9: Jesus feeds five thousand people and calls himself the bread of life. The next new memory verse (John 6:35) lands there.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 06's third movement and its verdict: your record or Christ's record on the last day. Keep it grace-first, the merit is Christ's alone. Don't let it curdle into "try harder to believe."
- Land the christotelic capstone here (5:39, 46; Luke 24:27). This is the course's whole method in Jesus' own words: the Old Testament points to him. Give it the weight it deserves as the unit's payoff.
- Guard 5:39 hard. Jesus never faults the study. He faults reading the Scriptures for life while refusing to come to the Life they point to. Keep it off the diligent student who's doing exactly what this course asks.
- Guard against anti-Jewish readings. "The Jews" here names the hostile leadership in this scene. Jesus, the healed man, and every early witness are Jewish. The warning of 5:39-44 lands on any careful Bible reader who misses Christ, including the churched student in the room.
- If the period runs short, verses 31 through 40 (the four witnesses) are the load-bearing half. Verses 41 through 44 can compress.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
