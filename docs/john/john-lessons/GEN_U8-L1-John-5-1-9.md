**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the man's situation (5:5, 7) and what Jesus asks him (5:6).
2. Students will explain why "Do you want to be healed?" draws the man out rather than scolds him.
3. Students will explain how the healing shows grace to a man with no faith-credentials.
4. Students will explain, plainly, why verse 4 is footnoted in most Bibles.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a man waits thirty-eight years for help that never comes, and Jesus heals him in one sentence.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus gives before the man believes, knows him, or thanks him. Pure grace.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the textual note at 5:3-4, and how it builds trust in the text; the healing as a "work" that will witness in the discourse (5:36).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Do you want to be healed?" (John 5:6).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What have I been waiting a long time for?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the wait honestly, even the parts you're tired of.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 5:1-9 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 5, verses 1 through 9. If a student notices the numbers skip verse 4, tell them you'll explain it in a few minutes.
- Set the scene in one sentence: a crowd of sick people wait by a pool in Jerusalem, and one man has been there a very long time.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Look at verse 5. How long has this man been an invalid? Put a number on it.
   - Cold-call check: thirty-eight years. Longer than most of their parents have been alive.
2. **Entry.** In verse 7, when Jesus asks if he wants to be healed, what does the man say is stopping him?
   - He has no one to help him into the pool. Someone always gets there first.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus asks a man who's been sick for thirty-eight years, "Do you want to be healed?" That seems obvious. Why would Jesus ask it? What does the question pull out of the man?
   - Guide them: it draws out how stuck he is. His answer isn't "yes," it's "I have no one." The question surfaces his total helplessness, and that's exactly where grace meets him.
4. **Core.** Look at what the man does and doesn't do in this scene. Does he ask Jesus to heal him? Does he know who Jesus is (see verse 13)? What does that tell you about why he got healed?
   - Land it: he never asks, and he doesn't even know Jesus' name. Jesus heals him purely out of grace, before the man believes or thanks him.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Your Bible jumps from verse 3 to verse 5, with verse 4 in a footnote. The oldest copies of John don't include verse 4 (the angel stirring the water). Why is it actually good news that our Bibles are honest about that?
   - Draw out: we can trust the text because scholars track every line and flag what the earliest copies say. Nothing in the story depends on the pool's magic. Jesus heals with a word and never touches the water.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In verse 9, what does the man do the moment Jesus speaks, and what one detail does John add at the very end of the verse?
   - He gets up, picks up his bed, and walks. And it was the Sabbath, which sets up next class.

Circulate while groups work. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This one is framed fresh, no sermon.

- Start with the wait. Thirty-eight years by a pool, watching other people get helped in ahead of him. Some student in the room knows what it is to want something for years and have no one reach for them.
- Name the grace. The man doesn't call out to Jesus. He doesn't know who Jesus is. He brings nothing. Jesus walks up, asks a question that surfaces how helpless he is, and heals him anyway. Grace reaches first, before we ask, before we clean ourselves up.
- Keep pointing through the miracle to the person. Jesus heals and then slips away into the crowd (5:13), no audience, no credit. The healing is a window onto who he is, and next class it turns into the biggest claim he's made yet.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus reaches the helpless first, and gives by his word what no pool and no waiting ever could.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The man brought nothing and Jesus healed him anyway. Where do you feel like you have to earn help before you deserve it? What would it mean that Jesus reaches first?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 5:10-18 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation (Quiet-Time Journal and the Move 5 response).
- Sets up L2: Jesus healed on the Sabbath on purpose, and the fight that starts pushes him to say who he really is.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Handle the "Do you want to be healed?" question as Christ drawing out a hopeless man, never as a scolding. His answer, "I have no one," is the point the whole scene turns on.
- The textual note builds trust in the Bible. Treat it that way. Verse 4 (the angel) is missing from the earliest manuscripts, so most Bibles footnote it. Say it plainly and move on. Nothing in the passage depends on it, and Jesus never touches the water.
- Keep the grace-first frame clean. This man has no faith-credentials, no request, no thanks. The healing lands on him purely as gift. Don't moralize his silence.
- Avoid quoting verse 3's list of the sick word-for-word (its punctuation is awkward on a handout); describe the crowd of blind, lame, and paralyzed people instead.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block. If Move 3 runs long, trim the Deeper question first, keep the cold-call checks.
