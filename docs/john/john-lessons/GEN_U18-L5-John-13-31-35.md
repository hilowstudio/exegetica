# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 18 Lesson 5 of 7: the new commandment (John 13:31-35)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U18 The towel and the basin |
| Lesson | 5 of 7 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Jan 27 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 13:31-35 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The moment the cross is set in motion, Jesus gives a new command: love one another as I have loved you, the towel and the cross made into a command. |
| Christ connection | Jesus glorified in the cross gives love as the pattern, the power, and the mark of his people. |
| Memory verse | PLANT John 13:34-35 here, the unit's new graded verse: "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. | Resources | No paired sermon. Fresh-build. Reference: Leviticus 19:18 (the old command to love your neighbor). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus means by being "glorified" (13:31-32, the cross).
2. Students will state the new commandment and its measure, "as I have loved you" (13:34).
3. Students will explain why it is "new" and that love is the mark of a disciple (13:35).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus says love is how the world will know his people.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the command is measured by his own self-giving love.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "new" in kind, not in time; the old Leviticus command given a christological measure.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson plants the new memory verse, so protect Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another" (John 13:34).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Who is hardest for me to love right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest name or situation.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 13:31-35 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 13, verses 31 through 35.
- One-sentence setup: the second Judas leaves to set the betrayal going, Jesus starts talking about being glorified.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 31, right after Judas leaves, Jesus says he's "now glorified." Judas is leaving to get Jesus killed. So what does Jesus mean he's about to be glorified by?
   - Cold-call check: the cross. His glory is being lifted up to die (the same thing he said at 12:23). The betrayal starting is the cross starting, which is his glory.
2. **Entry.** In verse 34, what's the new commandment? Read the whole thing, including the "as" part.
   - "Love one another, just as I have loved you." Love, measured by how Jesus loved.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** "Love your neighbor" was already an old command (Leviticus 19:18). So what's actually new here? Look hard at the "as I have loved you."
   - Guide them: the command to love isn't brand new. What's new is the measure. Jesus adds himself as the standard: love "as I have loved you." The word "new" means new in kind, a whole new level. It's not "love people a normal amount." It's "love the way I love."
4. **Core.** Think about what "as I have loved you" points to. They just watched him wash their feet (this same night), and he's about to go to the cross. So how high is the bar he's setting?
   - Land it: the measure of the command is a Lord on the floor with a towel and a Savior on a cross. That's the "as." He's not asking them to like each other. He's asking them to love with the self-giving, serving, dying kind of love he shows them. And here's the grace: his love came first. He loved them before he commanded them to love. The command flows out of the love they've already received.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 35. Jesus says this love is how "all people will know" you're his disciples. Not doctrine, not intensity, not being right. Why would love be the badge?
   - Draw out: anybody can claim to follow Jesus. The proof the world can actually see is love for one another that looks like his, the serving, forgiving, sacrificial kind. It's the family resemblance. A church full of that love is the clearest evidence Jesus is real.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Say verse 34 back in your own words. What's the new part?
   - Love one another the way Jesus loved us, which is a whole new level.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit) and plant the memory verse

Bring it to Christ and plant the new verse. Framed fresh.

- Plant John 13:34-35 as the unit's new memory verse. Put it on the board and say it twice: "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another."
- Teach why it's "new." Loving people is old news. The new thing is the standard: "as I have loved you." And you just spent four lessons watching what that love looks like, the Lord of everything on his knees with a towel, about to go to a cross.
- Lead with grace, because the order matters. He loved them first. He washed their feet before he commanded them to wash each other's. His love came first and became both the command and the power to keep it. You don't manufacture this love. You receive his, and it flows out.
- Land on the mark. Jesus says love is how the whole world knows you're his. Ask the room gently: if someone watched how you treat people, would they guess you follow Jesus? That's the badge he chose.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus loved his own to the end, and he made that love the command and the mark of everyone who belongs to him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says love is the badge of his people. Who is hard for you to love right now, and what would loving them 'as he loved you' look like?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 13:34-35. Read John 13:36-38 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 13:34-35 joins the rolling memory-verse quizzes going forward.
- Sets up L6: Peter promises to die for Jesus, and Jesus tells him he'll deny him instead.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Plant 13:34-35 in Move 4. The fresh angle has three parts: why it's "new" (new in kind, the measure is "as I have loved you"), the measure is the towel and the cross (point back to L2 and L3), and love is the mark of a disciple (13:35).
- Keep it grace-first: his love came first and becomes the command and its power (the same engine as 6:35 and 10:11). Guard against moralizing it into generic niceness by keeping "as I have loved you" front and center.
- The glorification (13:31-32) restates 12:23: the cross is the glory. The command to love comes out of the glory of the cross.
- Set the trajectory: "love one another" returns in the vine (15:12), which the Q3 project groups may teach.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. Protect Move 4, where the memory verse plants.
