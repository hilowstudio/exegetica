# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 1 Lesson 3 of 4: received or rejected (John 1:6-13)

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state John the Baptist's job (verses 7-8) and what he was careful to say he was not.
2. Students will name the two groups (verses 10-11) that did not receive him.
3. Students will explain, from verse 13, who does the "birthing" that makes someone a child of God.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the Baptist's whole job was to point at someone better than himself, and anyone can grasp a signpost; and God does not ask you to earn your way into his family, he brings you in.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): notice that John names the rejection out loud before the offer, and let that honesty land.
- **Stretch** (disciple): trace verse 13 to the new birth of chapter 3, and hold together the real call to believe (1:12) and the real work of God behind it (1:13), without collapsing either side.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. Turn the content into questions and let students find it.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God" (John 1:12).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "To be given the right to become God's child is a gift, not something you arrange. What is your honest reaction to that right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: a real question is a real prayer.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 1:6-13 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 6 through 13.
- Settle the names in the first two minutes: the "John" in verses 6-8 is John the Baptist, a witness who points to Jesus. He is a different person from John who wrote this Gospel.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 7-8, what was John the Baptist's job? What was he careful to say he was not?
   - Draw out: he came as a witness, to point to the light so that others would believe. He was careful to say he "was not the light." His whole job was to point away from himself.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Verses 10-11 name two groups who did not receive him. Who are they, and why is it striking?
   - Guide them: "the world did not know him," even though the world was made through him; and "his own people did not receive him." The one who made everything came to his own and was turned away. John names the rejection out loud, before he tells you anyone received him.
3. **Core.** Verse 12 offers something huge: to all who received him, he gave the right to become children of God. Then verse 13 explains how that happens. According to verse 13, who does the "birthing" that makes someone a child of God?
   - Land it: "born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God." Not your family line, not any natural drive, not a human decision. God is the one who does the birthing. You did not arrange your first birth, and you cannot arrange this one. It is a gift.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Hold verse 12 and verse 13 together. Verse 12 says receive him and believe. Verse 13 says the birth is God's work. How do both stay true at once?
   - Draw out: the call to believe is real, and so is the God who works the new birth behind it. You come, and God is the one who brought you. Grace runs underneath the whole thing. We will meet this again in three weeks, when Jesus says it to a man named Nicodemus at night (3:3-8).
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What was the Baptist careful to say he was not? Who does verse 13 credit with the new birth?
   - He was not the light; God does the birthing.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Say the honest part first. John is blunt: the world he made did not know him, and his own people turned him away. That is where the good news lands hardest, right in the middle of rejection.
- Say the gift. To everyone who receives this rejected light, God hands the right to be called his child. And verse 13 tells you who did the work. You did not talk your way into the family. God gave you a new birth.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the true light, and receiving him makes you God's own child, by God's own doing, all the way down.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "John names the rejection out loud (verse 11) before he tells you some received him (verse 12). Does it change anything for you that the Bible is honest that many did not receive him? Write honestly."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 1:14-18 once before next class. That is the peak the whole Prologue has been climbing toward.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry.
- Sets up L4: the Word becomes flesh and moves in with us, and we plant the year's anchor verse, John 1:14.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Settle the two Johns in the first two minutes so no one is confused for the rest of the hour. The Baptist points away from himself, which is the posture the course asks of its own teacher, and it seeds "he must increase, but I must decrease" (3:30) in Unit 5.
- The load-bearing ruling is verse 13. Name it as regeneration: becoming God's child is a birth, and God does the birthing. Call for faith (verse 12) and give God the credit for the birth (verse 13). Guard against reducing verse 12 to "make a decision," which steals the Unit 4 payoff. Keep the confessional language (Westminster and 1689 on effectual calling and the new birth) in your background, off the student handout.
- Do not exposit chapter 3 today. Name the new birth, then stop and point ahead: "Hold this. In three weeks Jesus says it to a Pharisee at night."
- Hold the memory. 1:12 sits on the page today, but it plants in Unit 2, so preview it lightly and leave it. 1:14 plants next lesson.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
