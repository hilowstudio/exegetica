# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 26 Lesson 4 of 8: my Father and your Father (John 20:17-18)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U26 The empty tomb |
| Lesson | 4 of 8 |
| Date / Day type | Fri Apr 30 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 20:17-18 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus tells Mary a greater nearness is coming, and sends her to his "brothers" with the news that his Father is now their Father, then she becomes the first person to preach the resurrection. |
| Christ connection | The ascending Lord who makes his God our God and his Father our Father. |
| Memory verse | No new verse this unit (review-only). Keep the fifteen sharp, including 1:12 (the right to become children of God), which pays off here. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: 38, "The First Witnesses" (John 20:1-18). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "do not cling to me... I have not yet ascended" (20:17).
2. Students will explain "my Father and your Father, my God and your God" as adoption (20:17).
3. Students will identify Mary as the first witness of the resurrection (20:18).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus calls his followers his brothers, and his Father their Father.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the resurrection makes God our Father, by adoption.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "do not cling" as a greater nearness coming (the Spirit), not coldness.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "my Father and your Father... my God and your God" (John 20:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I think of God as a Father, what comes to mind?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest picture.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 20:17-18 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 17 and 18.
- One-sentence setup: Mary has just recognized the risen Jesus, and now he gives her something to do.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 17, what does Jesus tell Mary not to do? What reason does he give?
   - Cold-call check: "do not cling to me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father."
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** "Do not cling to me" can sound cold, like Jesus is pushing Mary away. But he's not brushing her off; he's promising something better. The word means "stop holding onto me," to someone already gripping him tight. Given what's coming (his Spirit poured out on all his people), why does she not need to cling to his physical body anymore?
   - Guide them: because an even closer nearness is coming. Right now Mary can only hold his body in one spot. Soon Jesus will ascend and send his Spirit, and then he'll be with all his people everywhere, all the time, living inside them. He's saying: you don't have to grab me and keep me here, Mary. Something greater is coming. Go tell the others.
3. **Core (the big one).** Read the message Jesus sends. He calls the disciples "my brothers," and says he's ascending "to my Father AND your Father, to my God AND your God." Before this, Jesus always said "my Father." Now it's "your Father" too. What did Jesus do in that tomb that makes his Father our Father?
   - Land it: he died and rose to make us God's children. Through his death and resurrection, we're adopted into God's family. His Father becomes our Father. His God becomes our God. That's not a small thing. It means when you pray, you're not talking to a distant judge, you're talking to your Father, the same Father Jesus has. The resurrection didn't just raise Jesus; it brought us into the family.
4. **Core.** Read verse 18. Who becomes the very first person to preach the resurrection, to tell the disciples "I have seen the Lord"?
   - Draw out: Mary Magdalene. A grieving woman with a hard past is the first witness and the first preacher of the greatest news in history. Jesus entrusted the first resurrection sermon to her.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In John 1:12, way back at the start, John said Jesus gives "the right to become children of God." Here in 20:17, the risen Jesus calls the disciples his brothers and their Father his Father. How is 20:17 that promise from chapter 1 actually happening?
   - Draw out: 1:12 promised that those who receive Jesus become God's children. 20:17 is that promise landing: the risen Christ names his people his brothers and shares his own Father with them. What was promised at the beginning of the book is delivered at the resurrection. Adoption is a done deal now, secured by the empty tomb.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does Jesus call the disciples in verse 17, and whose Father is now their Father?
   - His brothers; his Father is now their Father too.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Clear up the "do not cling." Jesus isn't cold to Mary. He's promising her more, not less. Right now she can only hold his body in one place. Soon he'll send his Spirit, and he'll be closer than she could ever hold him, living inside every one of his people. He's saying: don't try to keep me here, something greater is coming.
- Land the family. And then the staggering part. He calls them "my brothers," and says his Father is now their Father. Before the cross, it was always "my Father." Now it's yours too. That's what he was doing in that tomb: making you God's child. If you belong to Jesus, God is not a distant boss you have to impress. He's your Father, the same Father Jesus has.
- Honor Mary. And who carries this news first? Mary. A woman the world overlooked, grieving, with a rough past, is the first preacher of the resurrection. That's who Jesus trusts with the best news ever told. Nobody is too small or too broken to be sent by him.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Jesus makes his Father our Father, and sends the lowly to tell it.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Because Jesus rose, his Father is now your Father. If God is really your Father, not a distant judge, what changes about how you come to him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 20:19-20 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: Jesus appears to the frightened disciples and brings them peace.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: "do not cling" as a greater nearness coming (the Spirit), guarded against reading it as coldness; and the adoption of 20:17 ("my Father and your Father"), the resurrection making God our Father. Tie to 1:12 (the right to become children of God) for the disciple tier.
- Note Mary as the first witness and first preacher of the resurrection (a woman with a hard past). Keep it pastoral.
- 20:17 also teaches the ascension (a greater nearness by the Spirit is coming); keep it warm, not a subtraction.
- Derive the frame from sermon 38; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
