**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "the hour" as the cross, the goal the whole Gospel moved toward.
2. Students will explain "It is finished" (19:30) and the Lamb of God (1:29, 19:36).
3. Students will explain the resurrection and Thomas's confession (20:28) closing the deity frame.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus died on purpose and rose again.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the cross is the glory, and "It is finished" means paid in full.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Lamb (1:29 paid at 19:36); the 1:1/20:28 deity inclusio closing.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. Synthesis and reverence. Leave room for the Passion project to come due. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. This is the cross; keep it reverent.
- Centering line on the board: "It is finished" (John 19:30).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Having studied the cross closely, what's one thing about it I don't want to forget?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Read the heart of the Book of Glory. Assign readers:
  1. John 19:28-30 (the cross, "It is finished")
  2. John 20:26-31 (Thomas, "My Lord and my God," and why John wrote)
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 28 through 30; and chapter 20, verses 26 through 31.
- One-sentence setup: the whole book has been pointing at this, the hour, the cross, and the empty tomb.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 22 min, the governed block, gather and sort)

Pull it from the room. One thread today: glory through the cross and the risen Lord.

1. **Gather (John groups).** John calls the cross "the hour," and he's been pointing at it since chapter 2. Gather what you learned about the cross this year. What made it more than a tragedy? Fill the board.
   - Expect: the Lamb of God (1:29); the King crowned with thorns and enthroned on a cross; "It is finished" (19:30); no bone broken, the true Passover Lamb (19:36); he gave up his spirit himself (nobody took it, 10:18); the innocent one in the place of the guilty (Barabbas).
2. **Sort: the glory is the cross.** Back in the Prologue, John said "we have seen his glory" (1:14). Where did John finally see that glory most clearly? What kind of glory is it?
   - Land it: at the cross. The glory a person expects is lightning and power. The glory John actually saw was grace and truth in a human face, and it shone brightest when that face was bleeding for us. The cross is the throne. "It is finished" (19:30) is the victory shout, the debt paid in full.
3. **Gather: the Lamb.** John the Baptist called Jesus "the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world" (1:29) on page one. How did the cross pay that off (think of the unbroken bones, 19:36, and Passover)?
   - Draw out: Jesus died as the true Passover Lamb, at Passover, with not one bone broken, exactly as the law required for the lamb (19:36). The Lamb announced in chapter 1 is sacrificed in chapter 19. The whole book was pointing there.
4. **Sort: the empty tomb closes the frame.** Then the tomb is empty, the grave clothes folded, and Thomas falls down and says "My Lord and my God" (20:28). Connect that back to 1:1. What just happened to the whole book?
   - Land it: the book that opened with "the Word was God" (1:1) closes with a disciple worshiping the risen Jesus as "my Lord and my God" (20:28). The frame snaps shut. Everything John claimed at the start, Thomas confesses at the end, looking at a risen body. The cross wasn't the end. The resurrection proved every word of it true.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does "It is finished" mean, and what did the resurrection prove?
   - The work of salvation is complete, the debt paid in full; and the resurrection proved Jesus was who he said, God in the flesh.

Circulate reverently. Short probes.

## Move 4: The Passion project comes due (about 8 min, and see After the bell)

- Today the "Passion and a Personal Response" project is due. Collect it, or run brief presentations as your plan allows.
- Frame what the project was: study the cross honestly (what happened, why John tells it as he does, what "It is finished" means), and then say, in your own voice, what it stirs or asks in you.
- The response half is credited for honesty, and never graded for belief. A student who honestly wrestled has done the assignment fully.
- Keep it warm and safe. The cross is set before them; they're free to respond in their own words.

## Move 5: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Reverently.

- Land the glory. For a whole year John has been showing you the glory of Jesus. And where did it shine brightest? Not on a mountain. On a cross, with a crown of thorns, bleeding for people who ran. That's the glory of God: grace and truth, poured out to the last drop for you.
- Land the finished work. And his last word was one word: finished. Paid in full. The whole debt of your sin, settled. There's nothing left for you to add. He did it all.
- Land the empty tomb. And then the grave couldn't hold him. The book that started "the Word was God" ends with a man on his knees saying "my Lord and my God" to the risen Jesus. It's all true. He died, he finished it, and he rose.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the Lamb of God finished the work at the cross and rose from the grave, and the whole book bows with Thomas: my Lord and my God.
