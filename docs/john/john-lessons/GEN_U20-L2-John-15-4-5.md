**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 15:5 and identify it as Jesus' seventh "I am" saying.
2. Students will explain what "abide" means (stay, remain, keep connected).
3. Students will explain "apart from me you can do nothing" as total dependence, and why that's good news.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a branch bears fruit by staying connected, not by straining.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): fruit flows from being joined to Jesus, and it isn't a grind you produce on your own.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "abide" (meno) as union with Christ, Marshall's grace-first order in a picture; the two guards on 15:5.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant, so give it the full period.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Apart from me you can do nothing" (John 15:5).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where am I worn out from trying to be good on my own?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest about the exhaustion.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 15:4-5 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 4 and 5.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus gives the whole secret of the Christian life in one word, "abide."

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 4, what can a branch not do by itself? What does it have to do to bear fruit?
   - Cold-call check: it can't bear fruit by itself; it has to abide in (stay connected to) the vine.
2. **Entry.** "Abide" is the key word, and it shows up over and over. What does it mean in plain English?
   - Draw out: stay, remain, keep connected, don't leave. It's a picture of just staying joined to Jesus.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Watch what a branch actually does to make grapes. Does it strain and grunt and produce fruit by willpower? What does it do?
   - Guide them: nothing but stay attached. The vine's life flows into it, and fruit shows up. The branch's whole job is to stay connected. Fruit is what the vine produces through a connected branch. That's how the Christian life works: you stay joined to Jesus, and he grows the fruit.
4. **Core.** Read the last line of verse 5: "apart from me you can do nothing." Some people hear that as harsh. Flip it around: if the fruit was never yours to force, whose job is it? Is that a burden or a relief?
   - Land it: it's the most freeing line in the chapter for a worn-out try-hard. The pressure you've been carrying, to be good enough on your own steam, was never yours to carry. Jesus grows the fruit. Your job is to stay close to him. That's rest, not defeat.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** "Apart from me you can do nothing" is total. But it doesn't mean sit on the couch and do nothing. Effort is real in the Christian life. So how do you hold "you can do nothing" and "work hard" together?
   - Draw out: you work hard, but on borrowed life. The branch is genuinely active, growing and bearing, but every bit of it runs on the vine's sap. You put in real effort, and the power for it comes from staying joined to Jesus, not from yourself. Dependence and effort, together.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Recite verse 5 from memory. Then: what's the branch's one job, and who grows the fruit?
   - The branch abides (stays connected); the vine (Jesus) grows the fruit.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Say the relief out loud. A lot of you are tired. You've been trying to be a good enough Christian, a good enough kid, keeping score, running on fumes. Jesus looks at that and says: apart from me you can do nothing, so stop trying to grow fruit you were never meant to grow on your own. Stay joined to me, and I'll grow it.
- Name what abiding is. It's not another task to grind at. It's staying close to the person of Jesus, trusting him, depending on him, day by day. Being joined to him comes first. The good stuff grows out of that.
- Close the back door. This isn't a call to do nothing. A branch joined to a vine is busy growing. You'll work, you'll obey, you'll love, and all of it will run on his life, not yours. That's the difference between striving and abiding.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the vine, and every good thing you'll ever do grows out of staying joined to him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'Apart from me you can do nothing.' Is that harsh or freeing to you right now? What would change if you believed the fruit was Jesus' job to grow through you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 15:5. Read John 15:6-8 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 15:5 enters the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up L3: the two kinds of branch, the one that abides and bears fruit, and the one that withers.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the plant. 15:5 is the unit's new graded anchor and the seventh and last "I am" (bread 6:35, light 8:12, the door + the good shepherd 10, resurrection 11:25, way 14:6, now the vine; the set of seven is complete). Name the "I am" line.
- The load-bearing idea: "abide" (meno) is union with Christ; fruit flows from union (Marshall's grace-first order, the tree made good then the fruit). Adam's guard, from sermon 26: "your activities flow out of your abiding; your activities are not your abiding."
- Hold both guards on 15:5: kills self-reliant moralism (the branch produces nothing alone) AND doesn't license passivity (the confession that cites 15:5 warns believers not to grow negligent). Teach the relief, then close the back door.
- Derive the frame from sermon 26; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block for the plant.
