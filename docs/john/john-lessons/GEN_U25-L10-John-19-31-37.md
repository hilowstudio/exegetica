**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why Jesus' legs were not broken (19:33, 36).
2. Students will explain the pierced side and the certainty of Jesus' death (19:34).
3. Students will explain John's eyewitness testimony (19:35).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus really, fully died, and someone saw it.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the unbroken bones mark him as the true Passover Lamb.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the fulfilled Scriptures (Exodus 12:46; Zechariah 12:10) and the eyewitness ground of faith.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep the reverence. One clear center: he really died, and it was witnessed.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He who saw it has borne witness... that you also may believe" (John 19:35).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Do I actually believe Jesus really died? Why does it matter?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 19:31-37 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 31 through 37.
- One-sentence setup: after Jesus dies, the soldiers make sure of it, and John tells us he watched it happen.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: he really died, and John saw it. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 32, what do the soldiers do to the two men crucified with Jesus? In verse 33, why don't they do it to Jesus?
   - Cold-call check: they break the legs of the other two (to hasten death); they don't break Jesus' legs, because he's already dead.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 36. John says the unbroken bones fulfilled a Scripture: "not one of his bones will be broken." Look at Exodus 12:46, the rule for the Passover lamb. What is John telling you Jesus is?
   - Guide them: the Passover lamb was killed to rescue Israel, and the rule was that none of its bones could be broken. John is showing that Jesus is the true Passover Lamb, the real one all those lambs pointed to. The Lamb of God (1:29) dies as the Passover Lamb, with no bone broken, exactly as the law required. His death is the true Passover that rescues God's people from death.
3. **Core.** Read verse 34. A soldier pierces Jesus' side, and blood and water flow out. What does that prove about Jesus' condition?
   - Land it: that he was really, completely dead. Not fainted, not mostly dead, not in a coma. Dead. The blood and water from a spear to the heart is the sign of a real corpse. He was all the way dead. This matters, because if Jesus didn't really die, he couldn't really rise, and the whole thing falls apart. He truly died.
4. **Core.** Read verse 35. John steps into the story and says something unusual for a narrator. What does he claim, and why does he say he's telling us?
   - Draw out: he says, "He who saw it has borne witness, and his testimony is true," so that "you also may believe." John is saying: I was there. I watched the spear go in. I saw the blood and water. I'm not passing on a rumor, I saw it with my own eyes, and I'm telling you so you'll believe. He staked his life on what he witnessed.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does the pierced side prove, and why does John tell us he saw it?
   - It proves Jesus really died; John saw it and reports it so we will believe.

Circulate quietly. Keep it reverent.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the real death. Some people want to say Jesus just passed out and woke up later. John, who was there, kills that idea. He watched a soldier drive a spear into Jesus' side, and blood and water poured out. Jesus was dead. All the way dead. And that matters, because a Savior who only fainted saves no one. He really died, in your place.
- Land the Lamb. And they didn't break his bones, because he's the true Passover Lamb. For 1,500 years Israel killed lambs at Passover to be rescued from death, never breaking a bone. Every one of those lambs was pointing here, to the Lamb of God, dying to rescue you from death for good.
- Land the eyewitness. And notice John steps out and says: I saw this. I'm telling you the truth so you'll believe. This isn't a legend that grew over centuries. It's the testimony of a man who stood at the cross and watched. You can trust it, because he was there.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus really died as the true Passover Lamb, and the man who watched it tells you so, that you may believe.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "John saw it and wrote it so you'd believe. What's one honest question or one honest step of faith the eyewitness stirs in you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 19:30. Read John 19:38-42 once before Friday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L11: Joseph and Nicodemus bury Jesus.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. Keep the reverence.
- Three things to land: the unbroken bones = the true Passover Lamb (Exodus 12:46; 1:29); the pierced side = the certainty of his real death (against every swoon/passed-out theory; sermon 36's "dead dead"); and John's eyewitness (19:35) = the ground of faith, tied to 20:31.
- On the blood and water: keep the primary point the certainty of death. The cleansing/life overtone (the tradition hears the sacraments here) may be noted with restraint; do not build a doctrine the text states only in figure.
- Zechariah 12:10 ("they will look on him whom they have pierced") is the second fulfillment; keep it for the disciple tier.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly.
