# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 4 Lesson 2 of 4: how can this be, and the bronze serpent (John 3:9-15)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U4 The new birth |
| Lesson | 2 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Tue Sep 29 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 3:9-15 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The smartest religious teacher is completely stuck, and Jesus answers by pointing to a snake on a pole: look and live. |
| Christ connection | The cross is that pole, and Jesus lifted up is the sole remedy for people dying of sin, received by a look of faith. |
| Memory verse | Say John 3:16 aloud once today (it plants next lesson). Note that 3:15, "whoever believes in him may have eternal life," is the seedbed of tomorrow's verse. Keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12. |
| Resources | Fresh-build (no Adam sermon covers John 3:1-21). Have a class Bible open, and the Numbers 21 account ready to read. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will retell the bronze-serpent event (Numbers 21) and what the dying Israelites had to do to live.
2. Students will explain "so must the Son of Man be lifted up" (3:14) as pointing to the cross.
3. Students will explain that faith here is a look: trusting the one lifted up, not doing a work.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): people were dying, God's cure was to look at a pole and live, and Jesus says the cross is that pole; you are the one bitten.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): looking is not doing, so this is not one more religious task to perform; it is trust turned toward Christ.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the serpent typology as substitution (the emblem of the curse becomes the cure), and "lifted up" as John's word for the cross-as-exaltation across 3:14; 8:28; 12:32.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. Build the Work block around the single act of the look.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "So must the Son of Man be lifted up, that whoever believes in him may have eternal life" (John 3:14-15).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Is there something in you that you cannot fix on your own? Sit with that honestly for a minute."

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 3:9-15 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 3, verses 9 through 15.
- One-sentence setup: Nicodemus asks "How can this be?" and Jesus answers with an old, strange story about a snake on a pole.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 9 Nicodemus asks, "How can these things be?" The teacher of Israel is stuck. Then Jesus reaches back to a story in Numbers 21. Read it (Numbers 21:4-9). What was happening, and what was the cure?
   - Draw out: Israel sinned, God sent venomous snakes, people were dying. God told Moses to put a bronze snake on a pole; anyone bitten who looked at it lived. No medicine, no climbing, no work. Just a look.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** In verse 14 Jesus says, "as Moses lifted up the serpent... so must the Son of Man be lifted up." What is Jesus saying will happen to him, and what is the "pole"?
   - Guide them: Jesus will be lifted up on the cross. The cross is the pole. He is the one raised up so that dying people can look and live.
3. **Core.** Think about the strangeness. The thing that was killing them was a snake, and the cure was a snake lifted up. What does that say about the cross?
   - Land it: the emblem of the curse became the cure. On the cross, Christ was made the curse that the bitten deserved, so that looking to him heals the very thing that is killing you.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** The words "lifted up" carry two meanings: raised up on the wood, and raised up in glory. Watch that word return in 8:28 and 12:32. What is John doing?
   - Draw out: for John, the cross is the exaltation. One event at two heights. Jesus is lifted up on the cross and lifted up in glory in the same act. Show the double sense once, then move.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What did the dying Israelites do to live? What is the pole for us?
   - They looked at the bronze snake; the cross, where the Son of Man is lifted up.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Preach the look. The people in the wilderness were healed by looking away from their wound to what God had raised up. They did not fix the bite. They looked.
- Make it personal. The cross is God's pole, and the crucified Jesus is God's cure for a poison you cannot get out of your own blood. You do not fix the bite. You look, and you live.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus lifted up on the cross is the one remedy for people dying of sin, and faith is simply looking to him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Looking at the snake was not something impressive; it was just trusting God's cure. What would it mean for you to 'look' to Jesus rather than fix yourself?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 3:16-17 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12.
- Sets up L3: the most famous verse in the Bible, and we plant it, John 3:16.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The bronze serpent (Numbers 21:7-9) is the top cross-reference for 3:14. Press the scandal for the disciple tier: the emblem of the curse is what heals, because Christ was made the curse the bitten deserved.
- Do not flatten the serpent into "look up to Jesus when life is hard." It is substitutionary rescue from death, not a coping tip. And keep faith from becoming a work: the weight is on the object looked at, not on an impressive look the student achieves.
- Grounding for your own prep: "lifted up" is hupsoo, both "lift up" and "exalt," running 3:14 to 8:28 to 12:32-34; verse 13's descent-ascent (Jesus can speak of heaven because he came down from it) is a stretch-tier note.
- One Greek note on "lifted up" is the whole dose.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
