**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the shift from the Book of Signs to the Book of Glory (public signs to the crowds, then his last night with his own).
2. Students will name the five chapters of the Farewell Discourse (John 13-17) and one thing in each.
3. Students will understand the Q3 group project (teaching the Farewell Discourse) and its milestones.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the tender, personal half of John is ahead, a dying man's last words to his friends.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the same Jesus who did the signs now kneels with a towel.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the six through-lines that bind the Farewell Discourse into one whole.

---

**Talk budget:** light day, first class back. Keep your own voice short. The BibleProject video in Move 3 carries the map of the second half, so the room does the crossing without you lecturing it, and your voice is freed for the project launch.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Welcome them back.
- Centering line on the board: "having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end" (John 13:1).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What do I most want to learn about Jesus this semester?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 13:1 aloud, slowly. Read it twice.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 13, verse 1.
- One-sentence setup: this one verse is the door into the whole second half of John.

## Move 3: Re-enter the book (about 20 min)

Draw the map. Ask, don't lecture. Keep it a fast, warm orientation.

1. **Where we've been.** Last semester we did the Book of Signs, chapters 1 to 12. What was it mostly about? (Who is Jesus. Seven signs, the "I am" sayings, all answering that one question, ending with "the hour has come.")
2. **Draw the seam.** Put two columns on the board. On the left: "Book of Signs (1-12): public signs, to the crowds." On the right: "Book of Glory (13-21): his last night, to his own." Ask: what changes when you go from a public miracle in front of thousands to a private dinner with twelve friends?
   - Draw out: it gets close, quiet, personal. The crowds thin out so Jesus can talk to the people he loves most, the night before he dies. The tender part of the book is ahead.
3. **Read the door again.** John 13:1. What does it tell you Jesus does with his last night? ("He loved them to the end." The whole second half is love spent on his own.)
4. **Watch the map, then fill it in (BibleProject "Overview: John 13-21," 8:32).** Show the video. It maps the whole second half in about eight minutes. Then have students fill in the Farewell Discourse (John 13-17), one line per chapter, from what they just saw:
   - John 13, the towel and the basin. Jesus washes his disciples' feet and gives a new command to love.
   - John 14, the way home. "Let not your hearts be troubled." "I am the way." The promise of the Helper, the Holy Spirit.
   - John 15, the vine. "I am the true vine." Stay connected to me. Apart from me you can do nothing.
   - John 16, sorrow into joy. The Spirit's work. "Your sorrow will turn into joy." "I have overcome the world."
   - John 17, the prayer. Jesus prays out loud, for his friends and for everyone who will ever believe, including this class.
   The video runs past the discourse into the cross and resurrection, the whole Book of Glory. Keep today's fill-in on 13-17 (the project's territory) and let the rest sit as a preview of the road ahead.
5. **Cold-call check.** In one sentence: how is the second half of John different from the first?
   - It moves from public signs for the crowds to Jesus' intimate last night with his own friends.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ. Keep it warm, and sell the intimacy ahead.

- Carry the answer across the seam. The whole first half asked "who is this Jesus," and the answer got huge: the Word made flesh, the light of the world, the resurrection and the life, the one whose glory Isaiah saw. Now watch what that same Jesus does with his last night. He gets down on the floor with a towel and washes his friends' feet.
- Hold the two together. The Lord the signs revealed is the servant on the floor. His glory and his humility are the same person. That's the wonder of the whole second half.
- Set the mood right. Don't let anyone think the exciting part is over now that the miracles slow down. The opposite is true. The most personal, comforting, and tender words in the whole Bible are in front of us, spoken by a man who knew he was about to die and kept comforting the people who were about to run.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the King who did the signs is the friend who kneels with a towel, and he loved his own to the end.

## Move 5: Launch the project (10-12 min)

Hand over the Q3 group project. Frame it as a trust handed to them.

- Name the assignment plainly. This quarter, you are the teachers. Your group takes one section of Jesus' last night with his friends (a chapter of John 13 to 17) and teaches it to the class, so everyone understands it and meets Jesus in it. You get to teach your friends the last words of Jesus to his friends.
- Walk the milestones (put them on the board): Pitch (topic, audience, format, why), Plan (an evidence map of every passage and quote), Draft or rehearsal with peer feedback, then the live Present with Q&A. Each project week has a fifteen-minute in-class check-in. Everyone writes a short Maker's Note at the end.
- Give the four principles for teaching it well: land on Jesus and say what he asks of the room so the lesson ends on him, use the actual open text and read it out loud, reach the whole room (a low floor and a high ceiling), and read it as a dying man's care for scared friends.
- Form groups and assign or let them choose their section (13, 14, 15, 16, 17). Take questions about roles and format.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 13:1-17 once before next class. That's the foot-washing, our first stop in the Book of Glory.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. The project milestones will be graded as they come.
- Sets up Unit 18: the towel and the basin, and the new command to love one another (the next memory verse, 13:34-35).

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: light bridge day and the first class back. Keep your own voice short. The room does the crossing, and the second half of the class hands over the project.
- Sell the intimacy of the Book of Glory as the reward. The most tender words in the Bible are ahead, and the crowds thinning out is what makes room for them.
- Carry the "who is Jesus" answer across the seam. The servant with the towel is the Lord the signs revealed. His glory and humility are one person.
- The six through-lines that bind the Farewell Discourse (for your own framing and for the groups): love on Jesus' own pattern, the Helper/Spirit, abiding in the vine, the Father and Jesus as the way to him, joy in the middle of grief, and glory through the cross with prayer. Point groups to these so different chapters connect to the whole.
- For the project, guard the biggest student pitfalls up front: moralizing it into "be nice," making the Spirit vague or spooky, flattening "I am the way," and turning John 17 into abstract theology. See the reading guide, which students keep as their project handout.
- Resource note: the BibleProject video frames the cross ahead as glory-through-the-cross, which is John's own frame (12:23, 13:1, 17:1). Today's Land carries the servant-King. When we reach John 19 (Unit 25), name plainly that the glory is the Lamb bearing sin in our place (1:29), so the video's story frame is completed by the substitution John also teaches.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day at light density. This is a single bridge lesson.
