| Big idea | Jesus gives the command to love one another as he loved them, and names the greatest love there is: laying down your life for your friends, which he's about to do. |
| Christ connection | Jesus whose laid-down life is the greatest love and the measure of the command. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state the command and its measure (15:12).
2. Students will explain "greater love has no one than this" (15:13).
3. Students will identify Christ as the one who shows that love first, at the cross.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the greatest love is giving your life for someone.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the command's measure is the cross, and Jesus does it first.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Christ dying for enemies (Romans 5:8), his cross as the ground and our love as the fruit.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep your sustained voice light. One clear question drives the block, and it lands on the cross.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends" (John 15:13).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Who's someone I'd take a hit for?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 15:12-13 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 12 and 13.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus gives the command again and then names the highest love there is, hours before he shows it.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: what the greatest love is, and who shows it first. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 12, what's the command? What's the measure, the "as" part?
   - Cold-call check: love one another, as I have loved you. This is the same command from John 13:34.
2. **The driving question (whole class, then small groups).** In verse 13, Jesus names the greatest love there is. What is it? And here's the key question: who's about to show that exact love, and when?
   - Guide them: the greatest love is laying down your life for your friends. And Jesus is about to do it, on the cross, within a day. He's not describing a nice idea. He's describing what he's walking toward.
3. **Core.** Jesus says lay down your life "for his friends." But look at Romans 5:8: Christ died for us while we were still sinners, still enemies. So how is Jesus' love even greater than the definition he just gave?
   - Land it: the greatest human love gives its life for friends. Jesus gave his for enemies, for people who wanted him dead. His love goes past the highest bar he named. That's the measure of "as I have loved you."
4. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's the greatest love, and where did Jesus show it?
   - Laying down your life for someone, which Jesus did at the cross, even for his enemies.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Put the command next to the cross. "Love one another as I have loved you." The measure isn't being polite. It's a man laying his life down. He said it the night before he did it.
- Show them how far his love goes. The best love the world knows dies for its friends. Jesus died for the people who were nailing him up. Romans 5:8: while we were still sinners, Christ died for us. His love outran the highest definition of love there is.
- Bring it home. That's the love you're commanded to copy, and it's impossibly high on your own. Which is why this command lives inside the vine. Stay joined to him, and his kind of love starts growing through you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus laid down his life, the greatest love there is, and that love is both the measure of the command and the power to keep it.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus loved you all the way to the cross, even as an enemy. What's one way you could love someone this week the way he loved you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 15:5. Read John 15:14-17 once before Friday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: no longer servants but friends, and "you did not choose me, but I chose you."

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One idea, taught well, landing on the cross: the greatest love, shown first by Christ.
- The christological order is the whole point: his cross first as the ground, our love second as the fruit. Keep them in that order.
- Guard against moralizing 15:13 into mere self-sacrifice with the cross cut out. Romans 5:8 (he died for enemies) keeps Christ at the center.
- Ties to the U18 plant 13:34-35 (the same command, its measure now shown as the cross).
- Derive the frame from sermon 27; cite it, don't read it aloud.
