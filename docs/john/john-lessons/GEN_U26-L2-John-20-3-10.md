**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the grave clothes and the folded face cloth (20:6-7).
2. Students will explain how the orderly scene argues against grave robbery.
3. Students will explain "he saw and believed" and "did not yet understand the Scripture" (20:8-9).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the grave clothes were left behind, folded, not stolen.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the calm, ordered tomb is evidence of a resurrection.
- **Stretch** (disciple): faith before understanding (20:8-9); the after-the-fact pattern (2:22, 12:16).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "he saw and believed" (John 20:8).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What kind of evidence would it take for me to believe something huge?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 20:3-10 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 3 through 10.
- One-sentence setup: Mary's report sends Peter and John sprinting to the tomb to see for themselves.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 3 and 4, what do Peter and John do? Who gets there first?
   - Cold-call check: they run to the tomb; John (the other disciple) outruns Peter and gets there first.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 6 and 7 carefully. What exactly does Peter see inside? Pay attention to the face cloth.
   - Guide them: the linen cloths lying there, and the face cloth (that had covered Jesus' head) NOT lying with the others, but folded up in a place by itself. The scene is calm and orderly. The grave clothes are left behind, and the face cloth is neatly folded and set aside.
3. **Core (the key detail).** Picture a grave robber. He's stealing a body wrapped in expensive linen and seventy-five pounds of spices, in a hurry, in the dark, committing a crime. Does he stop to unwrap the body and fold the face cloth neatly? What does the orderly tomb tell you actually happened?
   - Land it: no robber does that. A thief takes the whole wrapped body and runs, or takes the valuable linens too. He does not carefully undress a corpse and fold the head cloth. The calm, orderly scene says nobody stole anything. Jesus rose, left the grave clothes lying where his body had been, and even the face cloth was set aside with care. This is the evidence of a resurrection, not a robbery. (Sermon 37: whatever happened here did not happen in a rush.)
4. **Core.** Read verse 8: John "saw and believed." What did the folded grave clothes do for him?
   - Draw out: they convinced him. He looked at the empty, orderly tomb and believed Jesus was alive. He didn't see the risen Jesus yet, just the evidence he left behind, and it was enough. The first Easter faith came from an empty tomb read rightly.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 9: "they did not yet understand the Scripture, that he must rise." John believed BEFORE he fully understood the Bible said this would happen. What does the empty tomb do to how they'll read their Old Testament from now on?
   - Draw out: it sends them back to the Scriptures with new eyes. Suddenly the psalms and prophets that spoke of the Messiah rising make sense. This is a pattern John's shown before (2:22 and 12:16): they didn't get it at the time, and after Jesus was raised and glorified, they remembered and understood. The empty tomb unlocked the whole Bible.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how do the folded grave clothes argue against grave robbery?
   - A thief wouldn't undress the body and neatly fold the face cloth; the calm, orderly scene is evidence of a resurrection.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Zoom in on the folded cloth. John gives you a strange, specific detail: the face cloth, folded up, set in its own place. Why bother telling us that? Because it's the tell. Robbers don't fold laundry. Panicked thieves don't tidy up a crime scene. The neat, unhurried tomb says loud and clear: nobody stole this body. He got up and left.
- Land the calm. And there's something beautiful in the calm of it. No struggle, no mess, no rush. Death did everything it could to Jesus, and he rose so completely in control that he folded the cloth on his way out. Death met its match, and it wasn't even close.
- Land the faith. John looked at that empty, orderly tomb and believed, before he saw Jesus, before he fully understood the Scriptures. Sometimes the evidence comes before the understanding. He believed what the tomb was telling him. And then it all started to make sense.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Christ left his grave clothes folded, and the empty tomb was enough to spark the first Easter faith.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "John believed the empty tomb before he fully understood it. Where do you have enough evidence to trust Jesus, even with questions you haven't answered yet?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 20:11-16 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3 (short Thursday): the risen Jesus calls Mary by name.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The load-bearing detail: the folded face cloth (20:6-7) as John's deliberate counter to grave robbery (a thief wouldn't undress a body and fold the linens; the orderliness is the argument). Sermon 37 presses this.
- 20:8-9: first Easter faith before full understanding; the after-the-fact pattern (2:22, 12:16); the resurrection unlocked the Scriptures (Ps 16:10; Isa 53).
- Keep the resurrection bodily and evidenced. This is history with physical detail, not a metaphor.
- Derive the frame from sermon 37; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
