**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the council decides to kill Jesus (11:47-48, 53).
2. Students will explain Caiaphas's unwitting prophecy (11:49-52).
3. Students will explain the irony that the sign of life sets Jesus' death in motion.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the miracle of life is exactly what gets Jesus killed.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the leaders wanted a safe nation without Jesus as Lord.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Caiaphas's cold politics used by God to announce the substitution.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "It is better... that one man should die for the people" (John 11:50).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What would I be tempted to protect if following Jesus threatened it?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 11:45-57 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 11, verses 45 through 57.
- One-sentence setup: after the greatest miracle yet, the leaders hold an emergency meeting.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verses 47 and 48, why is the council worried about Jesus? What are they afraid of losing?
   - He's doing too many signs, and they fear that if everyone believes in him, the Romans will come and take away "our place and our nation." They're protecting their power.
2. **Read-check.** In verse 50, what does Caiaphas say is "better"? In verse 53, what does the council decide?
   - Better that one man die for the people than the whole nation perish. And from that day, they plan to kill Jesus.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Here's the strange heart of it. Caiaphas means "let's kill Jesus to save our own skins." But verses 51 and 52 say he was actually prophesying something true without knowing it. Work this: what did Caiaphas mean, what did God mean by the same words, and why is it stunning that the gospel gets announced by the man arranging the murder?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out: Caiaphas meant cold politics, sacrifice one troublemaker to keep the peace. God meant the gospel: Jesus would die "for the nation," in the place of his people, and to gather God's scattered children into one. The high priest, whose job was to offer sacrifices, unknowingly named the true sacrifice. God is so in control that he can speak the truth through a wicked man's mouth.
   - For the disciple tier: notice the deep irony. The greatest sign of life, raising the dead, is the exact thing that makes them decide to kill Jesus (verse 53). He gave Lazarus his life back knowing it would cost him his own.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. Build on the close of sermon 17.

- Name the irony out loud. Jesus raises a dead man, and that's the miracle that gets him killed. He knew it would. He walked toward his own death to give life to his friend, and to you. The sign of life cost him his life.
- Unpack Caiaphas. This man was trying to arrange a murder, and God put the gospel in his mouth. "One man should die for the people." That's the whole thing: Jesus dying in the place of his people, taking what they had coming. Even the people plotting against Jesus ended up announcing why he came.
- Speak to the churched student in the council. The leaders wanted their comfortable religious life and their safe nation, without Jesus actually being in charge. It's possible to want God's stuff and not want God. The question is whether you'll let him be Lord, even when it costs you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the one man who died for the people, on purpose, to gather God's scattered children into one, and he saw the cost when he called Lazarus out.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus gave Lazarus life knowing it would cost him his own. What does that tell you about how far he was willing to go for people, including you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing John 11:25 and your other memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 11:25 stays in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 16: it's almost Passover, and the road now leads into Jesus' final week.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- Build on the close of sermon 17. This is the hinge of the whole Gospel: the sign of life is what sends Jesus to the cross.
- Handle Caiaphas carefully. His words were cold politics, and God turned them into a true prophecy of the substitution (Jesus dying in the place of his people). It teaches God's sovereignty over even wicked speech, on the pattern of Balaam. It does not mean everyone's words are secretly prophecy, and it does not excuse Caiaphas.
- Carry the anti-Jewish guard. Note that "many of the Jews believed" (verse 45) in the same scene. The council names the hostile leadership, and the sin on display (wanting God's favor without his lordship) falls on the churched student too.
- Tie the gathering (verse 52) back to "one flock" (10:16, Unit 14). Jew and Gentile gathered into one by his death.
- This lesson opens the door to the Passion. Passover is near (verse 55). Close the unit pointing forward to Jesus' final week.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. This closes Unit 15.
