**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Martha's partial faith (11:21-24).
2. Students will state what Jesus claims in 11:25 and explain the two promises (11:25-26).
3. Students will explain Martha's confession (11:27).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus says hope isn't just an event someday. It's him.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): a doctrine about "the last day" can sit next to a small expectation right now.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "I am" present tense at a sealed tomb; the two promises of 11:25-26.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson plants the new memory verse, so protect Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am the resurrection and the life" (John 11:25).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I really hoping is true about death?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write your honest question about what happens when we die.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 11:17-27 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 11, verses 17 through 27.
- One-sentence setup: Lazarus has been dead four days, and grieving Martha comes out to meet Jesus on the road.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 21, what's the first thing Martha says to Jesus? What's the ache under it?
   - Cold-call check: "Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died." It's grief and a little blame, and still real trust.
2. **Entry.** In verse 24, Jesus says her brother will rise again, and Martha agrees, "at the last day." What does she believe, and when does she think it happens?
   - She believes in a resurrection at the end of time. It's a real doctrine, and it's far away.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 25 slowly. Martha has a resurrection filed under "someday." What does Jesus say he himself is? How is that a bigger claim than agreeing with her about the last day?
   - Guide them: "I am the resurrection and the life." Not "there will be a resurrection someday." He says he is it, present tense, standing in front of her while her brother is still in the tomb. Hope isn't just an event on a calendar. It's a Person you can know now.
4. **Core.** Read verses 25 and 26 together. There are two promises here. What happens to the believer who dies? What happens to the believer who's alive?
   - Land it: the believer who dies physically will still live ("though he die, yet shall he live"). The believer who's alive and trusting Jesus will never ultimately die ("shall never die"). Death doesn't get the last word either way. Both promises rest on believing in him.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 27 is Martha's answer to "Do you believe this?" What does she confess? And notice, at verse 39 she still says her brother stinks. What does that tell you about real faith?
   - Draw out: she confesses Jesus is "the Christ, the Son of God," a huge, true confession, the same kind Peter gave (6:69). And she still doesn't expect a raising today. Real faith can be genuine and still have small expectations. Jesus honors the faith she has.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Say verse 25 back in your own words. What does Jesus claim to be?
   - He himself is the resurrection and the life, standing right there in the present tense, a Person to have now.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit) and plant the memory verse

Bring it to Christ and plant the new verse. This is the heart of sermon 16.

- Plant John 11:25 as the unit's new memory verse. Put it on the board and say it twice: "I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live."
- Teach the fresh angle: present tense, and a Person. Every other "I am" verse students know tells them what Jesus gives in life, bread, light, a shepherd. This is the "I am" that answers the grave. And the verb is "I am," right now, while the stone is still in place. The answer to death isn't a theory or a someday. It's a Person you can know today.
- Give them the two promises, warmly. If you belong to Jesus and you die, you'll live. If you're alive and belong to him, death will never really have you. Either way, the grave doesn't get the final word over you.
- End on Martha's question, because it's the whole chapter's question. Jesus turns to her and asks, "Do you believe this?" He's asking the room too. You don't have to have big feelings about it. You just have to answer honestly.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the resurrection and the life in person, and to have him is to have life that death can't end.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus asks Martha, 'Do you believe this?' Write your honest answer, and what makes it hard or easy for you to believe."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 11:25. Read John 11:28-37 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 11:25 joins the rolling memory-verse quizzes going forward.
- Sets up L3: Mary comes to Jesus, and Jesus weeps at the grave.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Plant 11:25 in Move 4. The fresh angle is present tense and Person: resurrection is not only an event on the last day, it's Jesus himself, here now, before the miracle. This is the fifth "I am" saying (after bread, light, the door, and the good shepherd), the one that answers the grave.
- Teach the two clauses of 11:25-26 clearly. The believer who dies will live; the living believer will never ultimately die.
- Note Martha's faith is real and small at once (she confesses him as the Christ in verse 27, and still says "he stinks" in verse 39). Grace honors the faith she has.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. Protect Move 4, where the memory verse plants.
