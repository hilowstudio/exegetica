**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the crowd's confusion about where Jesus is from (7:27).
2. Students will explain why no one could arrest Jesus (7:30).
3. Students will explain what Jesus means by "you will seek me and you will not find me" (7:33-36).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the crowd can't agree on who Jesus is.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you can be sure you've got Jesus figured out and be wrong.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Jesus' origin is the Father; "his hour had not yet come" as sovereignty.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He who sent me is true, and him you do not know" (John 7:28).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "How well do I actually know God, versus know about him?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write what you'd want to know for sure about who Jesus is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 7:25-36 aloud, one reads 25 through 31, the other 32 through 36.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 7, verses 25 through 36.
- One-sentence setup: the crowd in Jerusalem is arguing about whether Jesus could really be the Christ, and the leaders send guards to arrest him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 27, why do some people think Jesus can't be the Christ? What do they claim to know?
   - Cold-call check: "we know where this man comes from." They think they've got him placed as an ordinary local, and they figured the Christ would appear mysteriously.
2. **Entry.** When have you been totally sure you knew all about someone, and it turned out you didn't?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 28 and 29. The crowd says they know where Jesus is from. How does Jesus turn that around? What do they actually not know?
   - Guide them: Jesus says his real origin is the Father, who sent him, and "him you do not know." They know the town he grew up in and think that settles it. They don't know the God who sent him, so they've got Jesus completely wrong.
4. **Core.** Read verse 30. They wanted to arrest Jesus, and they couldn't. Why not? What does the verse say?
   - Land it: "because his hour had not yet come." Nobody could lay a hand on Jesus before the Father's appointed time. He's not a victim of the crowd. He's on a schedule only God controls.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verses 33 and 34 Jesus says he's "going to him who sent me," and "you will seek me and you will not find me." The crowd thinks he means moving to another country (verse 35). What did Jesus really mean?
   - Draw out: he's talking about going back to the Father, through his death and resurrection and ascension. There's a warning under it too: there's a time to seek him and find him, and a person can put it off until the door closes. Take the invitation while it's open.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: the crowd was sure they knew where Jesus came from. What did they miss?
   - His real origin is the Father, and they didn't know God.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on the second half of sermon 11.

- Name the irony. The people most confident they had Jesus figured out had him most wrong. They knew his hometown and missed that he came from God. Knowing facts about Jesus isn't the same as knowing him.
- Turn it toward the room. It's possible to grow up with all the Bible facts, be able to answer every quiz question about Jesus, and still not actually know him. The question isn't how much you know about him. It's whether you know him.
- Point to his control. Even with a crowd wanting him dead and guards sent to grab him, no one could touch Jesus until his hour came. He walks to the cross on his own timing, on purpose, for you. He was never cornered.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus comes from the Father, no one could seize him before his hour, and he's going back to the One who sent him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "There's a difference between knowing about Jesus and knowing him. Which is more true of you right now, and what would move you toward actually knowing him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 7:37-44 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: on the last day of the feast, Jesus stands up and shouts an invitation to anyone who's thirsty.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 11's second half: the questions of where Jesus comes from, who sent him, and where he's going. His origin and destination are both the Father.
- Handle "you will seek me and you will not find me" (7:34) as his return to the Father, misheard as travel. Carry the gentle warning (there's a time to seek and find) without turning it into a scare. Lead with the open invitation coming next class.
- "His hour had not yet come" (7:30) is the sovereignty note. Tie it back to L1's "my time has not yet come." Nothing touches Jesus off-schedule.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
