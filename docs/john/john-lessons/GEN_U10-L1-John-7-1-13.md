**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what John says about Jesus' brothers (7:5).
2. Students will explain what Jesus means by "my time has not yet come" (7:6-8).
3. Students will describe the crowd's divided muttering about Jesus (7:12).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): even Jesus' own family didn't believe him at first.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus moves on God's timing, not on pressure from people.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Feast of Booths frame, and the Word (1:14) tabernacling at the Feast of Tabernacles.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "My time has not yet come" (John 7:6).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I feel pressure to prove myself to people?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest pressure you feel, from family or anyone.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 7:1-13 aloud, one reads 1 through 9, the other 10 through 13.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 7, verses 1 through 13.
- Set the scene in one sentence: it's the Feast of Booths, a big week-long celebration in Jerusalem, and Jesus' brothers are pushing him to go make a splash there.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 3 and 4, what do Jesus' brothers tell him to do? What's their advice?
   - Cold-call check: go to Judea, show off your works, "show yourself to the world." They want him to go big and public.
2. **Entry.** Read verse 5. What does John tell us about the brothers, right after their advice? Why is that surprising?
   - "Not even his brothers believed in him." His own family, who grew up with him, didn't believe. That's a lot to sit with.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** In verses 6 and 8 Jesus says "my time has not yet come." His brothers want him to move now, on their schedule. What's Jesus refusing to do, and whose clock is he keeping?
   - Guide them: he refuses to let his family rush him. He moves on the Father's timing, toward his "hour" (the cross). He goes to the feast, but quietly and in his own way (verse 10). He won't be pressured into performing.
4. **Core.** Jesus' own brothers didn't believe him after knowing him for thirty years. Back in chapter 6 Jesus said "no one can come to me unless the Father draws him" (6:44). How does his own family's unbelief actually prove that point?
   - Land it: if just being around Jesus were enough, his brothers would have believed first. They didn't. Faith is the Father's gift, not something you catch by proximity. And here's the hope: his brothers did believe later, after the resurrection. James ended up leading the church in Jerusalem.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 2 says this is the Feast of Booths, a feast where Israel remembered God living among them and providing in the wilderness. Back in John 1:14, "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (literally, tabernacled). What's rich about Jesus keeping the Feast of Tabernacles?
   - Draw out: the God who commanded the feast is now keeping it in the flesh. The one the whole feast was about is standing in the temple. Watch what he does with it by the end of the chapter.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why wouldn't Jesus go up to the feast the way his brothers wanted?
   - Because he moves on the Father's timing, not on his family's pressure.

Circulate while groups work. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 10.

- Start with the hard, tender fact. Jesus' own brothers didn't believe him. The Son of God grew up in a house where his family thought he was, at best, confused. If your home doesn't believe, you are in the company of the Lord himself.
- Name the hope. Their unbelief wasn't the end. After the cross and resurrection, his brothers believed. James and Jude, who wrote books in your Bible, are two of them. Grace reached the very family that once doubted. Pray for your house and don't give up on it.
- Point to his steadiness. Jesus didn't cave to family pressure to prove himself. He kept the Father's clock. You can love your family and still not let them set your course away from Jesus.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus lived on the Father's timing, was rejected by his own, and still went to the cross for them.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus knows what it's like when your own family doesn't get your faith. Write honestly about your home, and one way you could keep trusting Jesus there."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 7:14-24 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation (Quiet-Time Journal and the Move 5 response).
- Sets up L2: Jesus teaches openly in the temple and gives a test for knowing when teaching is really from God.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 10, "Lost Brothers." Its center is the comfort for anyone from an unbelieving home: Jesus was rejected by his own family and loved them all the way to the cross, and grace later saved them.
- Guard against family-shaming. Never turn this into "your family should be better." Make it comfort for the student whose household doesn't believe.
- Set the Feast of Booths frame here (the wilderness water, the tabernacling Word). It pays off big at L4 when Jesus offers the living water. Plant it lightly now.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block. If Move 3 runs long, trim the Deeper question first.
