**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will list the three things the Spirit convicts the world about (16:8).
2. Students will explain each clause: sin (unbelief), righteousness (Christ vindicated), judgment (the ruler condemned).
3. Students will explain what "convict" means (to prove guilty and wrong).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the Spirit shows the world it's wrong about Jesus.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the three clauses aren't random; each one is about Christ.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "convict" (elencho) as a courtroom verb, and the trial reversed (the world judged Jesus, now stands trial itself).

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep your sustained voice light. One dense passage, taught clearly.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He will convict the world concerning sin and righteousness and judgment" (John 16:8).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When have I known deep down I was wrong about something?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 16:8-11 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verses 8 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus says when the Spirit comes, he'll prove the world wrong about three big things.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: the three clauses, each pointing to Christ. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 8, what three things will the Spirit convict the world about?
   - Cold-call check: sin, righteousness, and judgment.
2. **Frame the verb.** "Convict" is a courtroom word. It means to prove someone guilty and wrong, to press the verdict home so they can't shrug it off. Who put Jesus on trial earlier in the story, and what's flipping now?
   - Guide them: the world (the leaders, the crowd) put Jesus on trial and condemned him. Now the tables turn: the Spirit puts the world on trial and proves it guilty. Same courtroom, other side.
3. **Core (small groups, take each clause).** Read verses 9, 10, and 11. Jesus explains all three. Match each to its reason, and say how each one is about Jesus.
   - Guide them, one at a time:
     - Sin, "because they do not believe in me" (verse 9). The world's deepest sin isn't a list of bad deeds. It's refusing to believe in the sinless one who stood right in front of them. The root sin is unbelief in Christ.
     - Righteousness, "because I go to the Father" (verse 10). The world called Jesus a guilty criminal. The Father raised him and welcomed him home. That's the verdict overturned: Jesus is proven right by going to the Father.
     - Judgment, "because the ruler of this world is judged" (verse 11). Satan, the ruler of this world, was sentenced at the cross. The Spirit presses on the world that its master is already a defeated, condemned power.
4. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence each: what's the sin, what's the righteousness, what's the judgment?
   - Sin: not believing in Jesus. Righteousness: Jesus proven right by going to the Father. Judgment: the ruler of this world already condemned.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Show them the courtroom turn. The world dragged Jesus into court, called him guilty, and killed him. And Jesus says: wait for the Spirit. He's going to reopen the case, and this time the world is in the dock. He'll prove the world was wrong about all of it.
- Walk the three, and land each on Jesus. The world's real sin was refusing him. The proof he was right is that the Father took him home. And the enemy behind it all is already sentenced. Every charge circles back to Christ.
- Make it personal and hopeful. This isn't the Spirit going around making people feel worthless. When the Spirit convicts, he's doing it to bring people to Jesus, to show them they were wrong so they can come home. The verdict is meant to lead to mercy.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the Spirit reopens the world's trial and proves that Jesus was right all along.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "The Spirit convicts the world to bring people to Jesus, not to crush them. Where have you felt him showing you something you needed to see?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 16:12-15 once before Friday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: the Spirit of truth guides the church into all truth, and glorifies Christ.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One dense passage, kept whole. Don't split the three clauses; that's the whole point of teaching them together here.
- The verb "convict" (elencho) is a legal term: prove guilty, press the verdict home (contrast a rebuke that may fail). The trial is reversed (the world judged Jesus; now it stands trial).
- Each clause is ruled from the text's own reason: sin = unbelief in Christ; righteousness = Christ vindicated by going to the Father; judgment = the ruler of this world condemned (tie to 12:31, 14:30).
- Guard against turning "convict" into a proof-text for guilt-tripping students. The Spirit convicts the world to bring people to Christ. Keep the Spirit concrete and Christ-centered (the U17/U19 point), never vague.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly. Calvin: this conviction runs through the church's preaching, not secret revelation.
