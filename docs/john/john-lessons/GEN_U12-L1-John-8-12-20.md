**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what Jesus claims about himself in 8:12 and what he promises his followers.
2. Students will explain the witness dispute and the two-witness law (8:13-18).
3. Students will explain what the temple lamp ceremony has to do with 8:12.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus says he's the light, and following him means you're not lost in the dark.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "I am the light of the world" is a claim to be God, said with the whole weight of it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Booths lamp staging; the two-witness law (Deuteronomy 19:15) again.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson plants the new memory verse, so protect Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am the light of the world" (John 8:12).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's something I keep in the dark, hoping no one sees?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly. No one reads this.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 8:12-20 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 8, verses 12 through 20.
- Set the scene in one sentence: last unit we learned the Feast of Booths lit huge lamps in the temple, and now Jesus stands right there and says something about light.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 12, what does Jesus call himself? What does he promise the person who follows him?
   - Cold-call check: "the light of the world." Follow him and you "will not walk in darkness" but "will have the light of life."
2. **Set the scene (30 seconds).** During the Feast of Booths, priests lit sixteen giant golden lamps in the temple courts, so bright they say the whole city glowed. When the lamps went dark after the feast, Jesus stood in that same court and said, "I am the light of the world."
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus doesn't say he brings a light or teaches about light. He says he is the light, standing where the biggest lamps in Israel had just burned. What's he actually claiming about himself?
   - Guide them: he's claiming to be God's own light come into the world. Tie it back to the very start of the book (1:4-5), where John said the life was "the light of men," and the darkness couldn't put it out.
4. **Core.** In verse 13 the Pharisees say Jesus can't just testify about himself. How does Jesus answer in verses 17 and 18? Who's his second witness?
   - Land it: their own law says you need two witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). Jesus says he and the Father are the two. Then he cuts deeper (verse 19): they don't actually know him or his Father.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Light does two things: it guides you, and it exposes what was hidden. How are both good news, even the exposing part?
   - Draw out: the light shows you the way out of the dark, and it also shines on the stuff you've been hiding. That exposure feels scary, and it's mercy, because the light kills the darkness and brings dead things to life. You bring your dark corners into it on purpose.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Say verse 12 back in your own words. What does Jesus promise?
   - He's the light of the world, and following him means you're not lost in the dark.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit) and plant the memory verse

Bring it to Christ and plant the new verse. This is the heart of sermon 13.

- Plant John 8:12 as the unit's new memory verse. Put it on the board and say it twice: "I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life."
- Teach it as a claim to be God. Jesus stands under the spot where Israel's biggest lamps just burned and says he is the light those lamps were only imitating. This is the second time he's finished "I am" with what he is, after "I am the bread of life" (6:35).
- Name the two halves. The claim: he is God's light in a dark world (1:4-5). The promise: follow him and you're not stumbling around in sin and death anymore, you have "the light of life."
- Lead with grace and speak to the room. The light came to you. You didn't light it. And the light isn't here to shame you. It's here to rescue you. Whatever you keep in the dark, drag it into his light, because his light kills the dark and raises dead things.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the light of the world, and following him is how you walk out of the dark.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says following him means not walking in the dark anymore. What's one dark corner you'd want his light on, and what would change if you let him in?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 8:12. Read John 8:21-30 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 8:12 joins the rolling memory-verse quizzes going forward.
- Sets up L2: Jesus starts using the name "I am" and points to the cross as where they'll finally know it's true.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Plant 8:12 in Move 4, after the lamp-ceremony scene sets it up. The staging (Booths, the sixteen lamps, the dark court) makes the claim land. Unit 10 pointed forward to this moment.
- This whole unit is one escalating claim to deity. Sermon 13's drumbeat is "he is God, we are not." Start the climb here at "light of the world" and keep raising it each lesson.
- Guard the light-and-darkness theme against us-versus-them superiority. Everyone was in the dark, and the light came to all. The light exposes the teacher first.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. Protect Move 4, where the memory verse plants.
