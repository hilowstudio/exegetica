# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 14 Lesson 4 of 5: the stoning and the Psalm 82 argument (John 10:31-38)

| Date / Day type | Wed Dec 9 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 10:31-38 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The crowd tries to stone Jesus for making himself God, and he answers with an argument from their own Scripture, then points them to his works. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the Son the Father set apart and sent into the world, one with the Father. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the crowd picks up stones (10:33).
2. Students will explain how the Psalm 82 argument works (from lesser to greater).
3. Students will explain "believe the works" (10:38) as an invitation to someone who can't believe yet.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the crowd understood Jesus perfectly, and that's why they reached for rocks.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus never took back the claim. He defended it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Psalm 82 argument as a lesser-to-greater move, ruling out two misreadings.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson has one dense argument, so protect the Work block.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "The Father is in me and I am in the Father" (John 10:38).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What would it change if Jesus really is God?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write your honest question about who Jesus is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 10:31-38 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 10, verses 31 through 38.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus just said "I and the Father are one," and now the crowd reaches for stones.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 31 and 33, why does the crowd want to stone Jesus? What do they say he's doing?
   - Cold-call check: for blasphemy, because "you, being a man, make yourself God." They understood exactly what he claimed.
2. **Entry.** Notice something important: the crowd didn't misunderstand Jesus and overreact. What does their reaction tell you about what Jesus claimed in verse 30?
   - They heard him claim to be God, and that's a stoning offense. Their anger is proof they got the message. Jesus doesn't take it back.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus quotes Psalm 82, where God called Israel's human judges "gods" because they held God's office of judging. Work this: if Scripture could call those men "gods," how does that answer the charge that it's blasphemy for Jesus to call himself God's Son?
   - Guide them carefully. It's an argument from the smaller to the bigger. If the Bible itself could call ordinary judges "gods" just because God gave them a job, then the word "god" isn't automatically blasphemy. So how much more can the one the Father actually set apart and sent into the world call himself God's Son? Jesus isn't lowering himself to their level. He's showing their blasphemy charge doesn't hold, and then he goes higher.
4. **Core.** Two wrong ways to read this argument. One: "See, we're all gods." Two: "See, Jesus was just a man and denied being God." Read verses 37 and 38. How does Jesus' own next sentence rule out both of those?
   - Land it: the psalm actually condemns those judges to die (they were "gods" in name and office only). And Jesus doesn't stop at the argument. He goes straight to the biggest claim yet: "the Father is in me and I am in the Father." He's not backing off his deity. He's pressing it.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 38 again. Jesus offers a lower rung to people who can't believe him yet: "even though you do not believe me, believe the works." Why is that a kind thing to offer?
   - Draw out: he meets people where they are. If you can't take his word yet, start with what he actually did, the healings, the sign of the blind man. Look at the evidence and let it lead you. Belief has an on-ramp. You don't have to start at full certainty.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does Jesus' Psalm 82 argument prove, and what does it not prove?
   - It proves the blasphemy charge doesn't stick, and it does not prove that we're all gods or that Jesus denied being God.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name what the stones prove. The crowd didn't overreact to a misunderstanding. They heard a man claim to be God, and in their world that was a crime worth death. Their rage is evidence they understood him. And Jesus never took it back. He defended it.
- Handle the clever argument simply. Jesus reasoned from their own Bible: if Scripture could call human judges "gods" because God gave them an office, then the one God actually sent can surely call himself God's Son. It shames their charge and it points to something far bigger, that he and the Father live in each other.
- Offer the on-ramp to the searching student. If you're not sure about Jesus yet, he gives you a starting place: look at what he did. Believe the works. Let the evidence walk you toward him. You don't have to have it all settled to take the first honest look.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the Son the Father set apart and sent, one with the Father, and he never once backed off that claim.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus told people who couldn't believe him yet to 'believe the works.' What's one thing Jesus did that you could look at honestly this week?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: Jesus slips away across the Jordan, back to where John the Baptist first pointed to him.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Handle the Psalm 82 argument carefully and keep it whole in this lesson. It's an argument from lesser to greater: if Scripture could call human judges "gods" (by office), how much more the one the Father sent. Rule out both misreadings by name: it does not teach "we are all gods" (the psalm sentences those judges to death), and it does not mean "Jesus denied being God" (he moves straight to mutual indwelling with the Father, 10:38).
- Use the crowd's reaction as the proof of the deity claim. They understood him and reached for stones. Jesus confirmed the claim rather than correcting it. Give this to the disciple tier.
- Use "believe the works" (10:38) as a real invitation for the searching student. Belief in John has an on-ramp.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
