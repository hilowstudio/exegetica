**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will write John 19:30 from memory.
2. Students will recall the christotelic heart of the Passion (the arrest, the trial, and the cross).

---

**Talk budget:** this is a checkpoint and a graded quiz, not new teaching. Keep your voice light and reverent, and let students do the recalling and the writing. This is the Passion, so keep the tone worshipful even in a quiz. Leave the board line up the whole period: "The King reigned from the cross."

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "It is finished" (John 19:30).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Out of the arrest, the trial, and the cross, what's one thing about Jesus I don't want to forget?"

## Move 2: Recap the Passion (10-12 min)

Quick, warm, reverent. Ask, don't lecture. Walk the two chapters as one road, and let students supply the answers. This is the gather-from-the-room move.

1. **"I am he" (18:5-6).** Ask: "When the soldiers come to arrest Jesus and he says 'I am he,' what happens to them, and who's really in charge of this arrest?" (They fall to the ground. Jesus is in command and goes by his own choice, 10:18.)
2. **The cup (18:11).** Ask: "Peter draws a sword, and Jesus says, 'Shall I not drink the cup the Father has given me?' What is the cup, and why does he take it?" (The cup of wrath sinners deserved, taken willingly from the Father's hand.)
3. **Peter's denial (18:15-27).** Ask: "Jesus says 'I am he' while Peter says 'I am not.' Peter fails just as Jesus foretold. Is that the end of Peter?" (No. Grace waits at the charcoal fire, John 21. Keep it as grace, never shame.)
4. **"My kingdom is not of this world" (18:36).** Ask: "What kind of king is Jesus, and how does his kingdom spread?" (A king whose kingdom comes from God, present now, spread by the gospel and not the sword.)
5. **"What is truth?" (18:38).** Ask: "Pilate asks 'What is truth?' and walks away. Who's standing right in front of him?" (The Truth himself, 14:6.)
6. **Barabbas (18:40).** Ask: "A guilty, violent man walks free and the innocent man is condemned in his place. What does that swap show us about the cross?" (Substitution, the gospel in miniature.)
7. **The crown of thorns (19:1-3).** Ask: "The soldiers crown him with thorns to mock him. How is their mockery telling the exact truth?" (He really is the King, and the thorns are the curse he bears for us.)
8. **"It is finished" (19:30).** Ask: "Jesus' last word is one Greek word, tetelestai, what merchants stamped on a paid bill: paid in full. What is finished, and did the cross take his life or did he give it?" (The whole work of salvation, complete, the debt paid. He gave up his spirit himself, 10:18.)

Say one sentence: arrest, trial, cross, one road, and it runs to a throne.

## Move 3: Passion checkpoint quiz (26-28 min, graded)

Phones and Bibles away. This feeds the gradebook. Two parts.

**Part A, memory (about 10 min).** Put the references on the board.
1. Write John 19:30 from memory, in the ESV (the graded verse).
Then, for a cumulative touch, write the sense or the key phrase of these four (short, by reference):
2. John 14:6
3. John 10:11
4. John 3:16
5. John 1:14

Grade for the sense and the load-bearing phrases, not word-perfect punctuation. On 19:30, the phrases that must be there are "It is finished" and "gave up his spirit." The verse texts are in the Answer Key below.

**Part B, content recall (about 16 min).** Students answer briefly in writing.
6. When Jesus said "I am he" at his arrest, what did the soldiers do, and what does it show about him?
7. What is "the cup" Jesus chose to drink (18:11)?
8. Why is Barabbas being released a picture of the gospel (18:40)?
9. What kind of king is Jesus, and how does his kingdom spread (18:36)?
10. How is the crown of thorns a true coronation (19:2-3)?
11. What does "It is finished" (tetelestai) mean (19:30)?
12. Did the cross take Jesus' life, or did he give it (19:30)?
13. Why does John stress that Jesus really died (19:34-35)?

Model answers are in the Answer Key.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and close the Passion. Off the grade, quiet, reverent.

- The King reigned from the cross. When Jesus said "It is finished," he did not mean "I am finished." He meant the work of saving you is done, complete, paid in full, and there is nothing left for you to add to it. He laid his life down; no one took it.
- The tomb is a door. And the grave where they laid him is not the end of the story. It is the door to Easter, which is exactly where we go next.
- For the room that's measured and ranked all day: the one verdict that matters was settled by someone else and stamped paid. That's the last word of the cross, and it's the word to carry.
- Christ connection to say out loud: arrest, trial, and cross were one road to a throne, and the King who reigned there finished the work of saving you.

## Move 5: Respond (short, graded participation)

- One line in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'It is finished.' If the work of saving you is already done, what does that free you from?"
- Give it quiet, and let the reverence hold. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 20:1-10 once before next class. Easter is coming.
- To the gradebook: today's checkpoint quiz (content), and the participation line. John 19:30 stays in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 26: the empty tomb. On the third day, everything changes.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: this is a scheduled checkpoint on chapters 18-19. It's a graded quiz plus a warm recap, not new exposition. The John 20 exposition begins next unit.
- Keep the recap fast, warm, and reverent. Ask the questions and let students supply the answers. Cap your voice; this is their recall.
- Grade the memory quiz for sense and load-bearing phrases, never word-perfection. On 19:30, credit "It is finished" and "gave up his spirit."
- Content questions (Part B) are recall of what was taught, answerable by every tier, not new reasoning.
- ANTI-JUDAISM GUARD (carry it into the checkpoint, since it peaked at 19:15). If Barabbas, "no king but Caesar," or "the Jews" comes up in the recap or an answer, name it the way the units did: "the Jews" in these chapters means the specific hostile leaders, the chief priests and their officers. Jesus, his mother and John at the cross, and Joseph and Nicodemus who bury him are all Jews. The guilt of the cross is human sin and God's saving plan, and never a people. Let "no king but Caesar" fall on every heart whose real allegiance is elsewhere, including the churched student. No quiz answer or comment should undo this guard.
- Keep it reverent and low-anxiety. This is the Passion, worship before instruction. Keep the thesis line ("the King reigned from the cross") audible all hour so the content questions stay tethered to Christ, not trivia.
- Content is gradable; belief is invitational. A student can write the verse and answer every question without being asked to profess.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.

## Answer Key

**Part A, the memory verses (ESV). Grade for sense and key wording.**

1. **John 19:30**: "When Jesus had received the sour wine, he said, 'It is finished,' and he bowed his head and gave up his spirit." (Must include "It is finished" and "gave up his spirit.")
2. **John 14:6**: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me."
3. **John 10:11**: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."
4. **John 3:16**: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."
5. **John 1:14**: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."

**Part B, content recall. Model answers; accept any response that carries the sense.**

6. **"I am he" (18:5-6):** The soldiers drew back and fell to the ground. It shows Jesus is in command and goes to the cross by his own choice, not overpowered (10:18).
7. **The cup (18:11):** The cup of God's wrath that sinners deserved, which Jesus takes willingly from the Father's hand. (John's Gethsemane.)
8. **Barabbas (18:40):** The guilty, violent man goes free because the innocent Jesus is condemned in his place. That exchange is substitution, the heart of the cross (Acts 3:14). "You are Barabbas."
9. **The kingdom (18:36):** A king whose kingdom comes from God, present and active now, spread by the gospel and truth, not by armies or force.
10. **The crown of thorns (19:2-3):** The soldiers mock Jesus as a fake king, and he really is the King. The thorns are the curse (from Genesis 3) pressed onto the brow of the one who bears the curse for us.
11. **"It is finished" (19:30):** "Paid in full." The whole work of salvation is complete, the debt is paid, and there is nothing left to add. A shout of victory, not a groan of defeat.
12. **His life given (19:30):** He gave it. He bowed his head and handed over his spirit by his own act. He is Lord even of the moment he dies (10:18).
13. **The real death (19:34-35):** A soldier pierced his side and blood and water flowed, proving he was truly dead. John saw it and tells us so we will believe (20:31). A real death means a real resurrection.
