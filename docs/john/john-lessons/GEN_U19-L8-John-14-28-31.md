**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "the Father is greater than I" as the Son's role in his mission, held with "I and the Father are one" (14:28 with 10:30).
2. Students will explain "the ruler of this world... has no claim on me" (14:30) as Jesus' sinlessness.
3. Students will explain why Jesus goes to the cross willingly (14:31).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus wasn't trapped; he chose the cross.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "the Father is greater than I" doesn't make Jesus a lesser God.
- **Stretch** (disciple): holding 14:28 with 10:30 and 5:18; Christ's sinlessness and willing obedience.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the unit's closer, so leave room to gather the chapter.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I do as the Father has commanded me, so that the world may know that I love the Father" (John 14:31).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's one thing from John 14 I don't want to forget?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:28-31 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 28 through 31.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus closes this part of the talk, and one line in it has confused people for centuries.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 28, why does Jesus say they should rejoice?
   - Cold-call check: because he's going to the Father. His going is good for them.
2. **The hard verse (whole class, carefully).** Same verse ends: "for the Father is greater than I." Does that mean Jesus is a lower, lesser God? Look back at John 10:30, which they already hold. What did Jesus say there?
   - Guide them: at 10:30 Jesus said "I and the Father are one." Both are true. "The Father is greater than I" is about role in the mission: the Son was sent, and he came low to obey and to die. A general and a soldier can be equal in worth while one takes orders in the mission. Jesus is fully God (10:30) and, in his mission, the sent and obedient Son (14:28). Hold both.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 30: "the ruler of this world is coming. He has no claim on me." Satan is coming through Judas and the cross. What does Jesus mean that Satan has "no claim" on him?
   - Land it: Satan finds nothing in Jesus to grab. Jesus is sinless. Death has no legal hold on him, because he owes it nothing. He goes to the cross as a spotless man, which is exactly why his death can pay for others.
4. **Core.** Read verse 31. Jesus says he does what the Father commanded "so that the world may know that I love the Father." Was Jesus dragged to the cross, or did he go? What does that tell you about why he went?
   - Draw out: he went on purpose. "Rise, let us go from here." He walks toward it. He goes because he loves the Father and obeys him. The cross is Jesus' free act of love, not a trap that closed on him.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Put verses 28 and 30 together. Jesus is equal with the Father, sinless, and untouchable by Satan's claim. Yet he goes to die. Why would someone with all that go to a cross?
   - Draw out: love and obedience. He didn't have to. Nothing forced him. He went because he loves the Father and, as the next chapters show, loves his own to the end. The willingness is the wonder.
6. **Memory verse cement.** Have students recite John 14:6 together, then in pairs. Make both halves solid: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life," and "No one comes to the Father except through me."

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the chapter?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and close the unit. Framed fresh.

- Take the hard verse head-on. Some people use "the Father is greater than I" to shrink Jesus into a junior god. Read it with 10:30, where he says "I and the Father are one." He's fully God, and in his rescue mission he took the low place of the sent, obedient Son. That's not weakness. That's love bending all the way down.
- Show them the sinless one walking to the cross. Satan comes and finds nothing to grip. Jesus owes death nothing. And he goes anyway, on purpose, "so that the world may know that I love the Father." He wasn't cornered. He chose it.
- Gather the chapter. In one night Jesus told scared friends that the way home is himself, that seeing him is seeing the Father, that he'd send his own Spirit so they're never alone, that he leaves them peace, and then he stood up and walked toward the cross for them. That's who John 14 shows you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus, equal with the Father and sinless, went willingly to the cross out of love, and he's still the way to the Father he opened.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus wasn't trapped. He chose the cross on purpose, out of love. What's one way that changes how you see him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep John 14:6 sharp; it stays in the rolling memory quizzes. Read John 15:1-3 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 14:6 joins the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 20: Jesus says "I am the vine; you are the branches," the sixth "I am" and the next memory verse (15:5).

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Guard 14:28 hard against Arianism. "The Father is greater than I" is about role in the mission, held together with 10:30 ("I and the Father are one") and the equality of 5:18. State the positive plainly: fully God, and the sent, obedient Son in his mission.
- 14:30 is Christ's sinlessness (Satan finds nothing in him), which is why his death can save. 14:31 is his willing obedience, walking to the cross out of love for the Father.
- Cement 14:6 here (planted at L2). This is the unit's graded anchor.
- Close by handing off to the vine (Unit 20, plants 15:5, the sixth "I am"). This lesson also models the Q3 project skill: land a chapter on Christ, not on a moral.
- Derive the closing frame from sermon 25; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
