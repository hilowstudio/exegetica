**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 1:14 and explain "the Word became flesh" (God became truly human while remaining fully God).
2. Students will explain "dwelt among us" as the tabernacle picture, God coming to live in the middle of his people.
3. Students will explain, from verse 18, what no one has ever done and who makes God known.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): "became flesh" is God moving into the neighborhood in a body; "grace upon grace" is one gift after another that never runs out; if God came this close, no one is too far.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): slow down and press what "flesh" cost, so the familiar verse lands with its real weight.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the tabernacle echo behind "dwelt," the Exodus 34:6 background under "grace and truth," "the only God" of verse 18, and the "exegete" word; watch the Prologue lock shut, deity in 1:1 answered by the eyewitness glory of 1:14 and the Son who makes the Father known in 1:18.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant and the warm peak of the unit, so give it the full period.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If God really moved in this close, in a body you could see, what difference could that make for someone who feels far from him?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest version.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 1:14-18 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 14 through 18.
- One-sentence setup: everything the Prologue has been climbing toward arrives here. The Word who was God in verse 1 becomes a man you could see.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Verse 14 says "the Word became flesh." In plain words, what happened? What did the one who was God in verse 1 do?
   - Draw out: he became truly and fully human, a real body you could touch, while staying fully God. Not a costume, not a ghost, not God pretending. Fully God and fully man, one person.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Verse 14 says he "dwelt among us." The word behind "dwelt" means to pitch a tent. Back in Exodus, God's glory came down and filled the tent so he could live in the middle of his people. What is John telling you Jesus is?
   - Guide them: Jesus is that tent, in person. God moved into the neighborhood in a body. The tent was a picture; Jesus is the reality. He came close enough to be seen, heard, and killed.
3. **Core.** Verse 14 also says "we have seen his glory." Who is the "we," and what does it tell you about how John knew this? What did his glory look like up close, according to the verse?
   - Land it: the "we" is John and the others who watched him; this is eyewitness testimony. And his glory looked like "grace and truth" in a human face. The glory a person expects is lightning. What John saw was grace and truth in a carpenter from Galilee.
4. **Core.** Read verse 17: "the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ." This is not an insult to Moses. What is it saying?
   - Draw out: the same God gave both. The law was a good and gracious gift through a faithful servant. What Moses served in shadow, Jesus brought in person and in fullness. "Grace upon grace" (verse 16) is one gift after another out of a fullness that never runs dry.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 18 says no one has ever seen God, and then says the Son "has made him known." The word behind that is where we get "exegete," to explain. What is John claiming Jesus is?
   - Draw out: Jesus is God's own explanation of himself. You cannot see God directly. You can look hard at Jesus and see him. That is the reason this whole course exists.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Recite verse 14 from memory. Then: what does "became flesh" mean, and who makes God known (verse 18)?
   - The Word became truly human while staying God; the Son at the Father's side makes God known.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the warm peak, so preach it.

- Name the glory. The glory a fifteen-year-old expects is lightning and thunder. The glory John saw was a human face, full of grace and truth, and it was brightest at a cross.
- Say what God did. God did not send a message about himself. He moved in, in a body, so you could look at him and see the Father. No one has ever seen God. Look hard at Jesus, and you have.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the Word made flesh, God with a human face, and the whole year is learning to look at him and see God.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- Plant the memory verse first. Teach John 1:14 in four beats, and have students say it one phrase at a time so the structure teaches the doctrine: "The Word became flesh" (the God of verse 1 became human) / "and dwelt among us" (he pitched his tent with us, God came close) / "we have seen his glory" (real eyewitnesses, real faces) / "full of grace and truth" (what his glory looked like up close). Then say the whole verse together.
- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'He came close enough to be seen and heard.' If that is true, what is one thing you would want to ask him, or say to him?"
- Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 1:14. Read John 1:19-28 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. John 1:14 enters the rolling memory quizzes as the year's anchor.
- Sets up Unit 2: the Baptist's public witness, "behold, the Lamb of God," and the first disciples who come and see.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block, and this is the plant. Turn content into questions, then plant 1:14 warmly. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the plant. 1:14 is the year's anchor verse. Plant it in four beats so the sentence structure carries the doctrine, and name it as the verse they will carry the whole year.
- Grounding for your own prep: "dwelt" is skenoo, the tent verb, tied to the Exodus tabernacle (Exodus 25:8-9; 40:34-38) and to God dwelling with his people forever (Revelation 21:3). The eyewitness "we" is echoed in 1 John 1:1-2. "Grace and truth" carries the Exodus 34:6 note of God's self-description, his steadfast love and faithfulness (hesed we'emet); Chrysostom read "grace upon grace" as wave after wave from Christ's fullness.
- Guard the mean-God, nice-Jesus cartoon at verse 17. Same God, one gift after another, Moses honored.
- Do not let the incarnation become a fact to file. Slow down at verse 14 and let it be heavy: he came close enough to be killed.
- Verse 18 is the Gospel's thesis and the course's method in one line: you cannot see God directly, so look at Jesus. Give the disciple tier one clean beat on it.
- Derive the frame from sermon 01; cite it, do not read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full period for the plant.
