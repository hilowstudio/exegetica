**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Caiaphas had said about one man dying (18:14).
2. Students will explain how Caiaphas's cold politics accidentally stated the gospel.
3. Students will describe Peter's first denial (18:17).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the leaders wanted Jesus dead, and God used even that.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Caiaphas's words unknowingly described exactly what Jesus came to do.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the unwitting prophecy (18:14 with 11:49-52); Peter's "I am not" against Jesus' "I am he."

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "It would be expedient that one man should die for the people" (John 18:14).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When have I said or done something that turned out to mean more than I realized?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 18:12-18 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 12 through 18.
- One-sentence setup: they bind Jesus and lead him to the high priest, and Peter follows at a distance.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 12 and 13, what do the soldiers do to Jesus, and where do they take him first?
   - Cold-call check: they bind him and lead him to Annas (the father-in-law of Caiaphas, the high priest).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 14. John reminds us of something Caiaphas said back in chapter 11. What did he say? What did he MEAN by it?
   - Guide them: Caiaphas said it was better for one man to die for the people. He meant it as cold politics: kill Jesus to keep the Romans off our backs and protect our position. It was a plan to commit murder.
3. **Core.** Now the irony. Caiaphas meant "kill him to protect us." But look at the actual words: "one man should die for the people." How did the high priest, trying to arrange a murder, accidentally say the gospel?
   - Land it: "one man dying for the people" is exactly what Jesus came to do, dying in the place of his people to save them. Caiaphas was scheming, and God put the truth of the cross in his mouth without him knowing it. Even the man plotting Jesus' death ended up preaching why Jesus died.
4. **Core.** Read verses 15 through 18. Peter follows Jesus into the courtyard, and a servant girl asks if he's a disciple. What does Peter say?
   - Draw out: "I am not." His first denial. Notice where he is: warming himself at a fire, trying to blend in, while Jesus is inside being bound.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Hold Peter's "I am not" (verse 17) next to Jesus' "I am he" from the garden (18:5). Same night, same few hours. What's the contrast John wants you to see?
   - Draw out: Jesus boldly owns who he is and hands himself over to save his people. Peter, terrified, can't even admit he knows Jesus to a servant girl. John braids these together on purpose. One is faithful to the end; the other fails, and will be restored.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did Caiaphas's words in verse 14 accidentally describe?
   - The gospel: one man (Jesus) dying in the place of the people.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the irony of Caiaphas. Here's the high priest, the man in charge of arranging Jesus' death, and God reaches into his scheming mouth and pulls out the gospel: one man will die for the people. He thought he was protecting his power. He was announcing the cross. God is so in control that even his enemies end up telling the truth about him.
- Land the substitution early. Hold onto that phrase, "one man should die for the people." That's the whole unit in a sentence, and you'll see it again with Barabbas at the end. Jesus dies in the place of others. That's why he came.
- Handle Peter gently. And don't miss Peter, cold and scared at the fire, saying "I am not." We'll watch him fall completely in a few lessons. Hold your judgment. Every one of us has gone quiet when it cost something to be known as Jesus'. Peter's story doesn't end at the fire.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the one man who dies for the people, and even the priest who plots his death says so.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "One man dying for the people is why Jesus came. What does it mean to you that he died in your place?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 18:19-24 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: Jesus is questioned in a night trial and struck by an officer.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: Caiaphas's unwitting prophecy (18:14 with 11:49-52), the substitution stated by the man plotting the murder; and Peter's first denial introduced (18:17), held gently, the thread that resolves at the charcoal fire (chapter 21).
- Anti-Judaism guard: "the officers of the Jews" (18:12) means the specific arresting authorities. Keep the guilt on human sin and God's plan, never a people. (Full guard introduced in the reading guide.)
- Derive the frame from sermon 33; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
