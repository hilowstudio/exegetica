**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the irony of Pilate asking "what is truth?" before Jesus (18:38, with 14:6).
2. Students will explain the difference between honestly seeking truth and using doubt as an excuse.
3. Students will explain "I find no guilt in him" as the innocence of Christ (18:38).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the question "what is truth?" is asked to the one person who is the answer.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "who can really know?" can be an excuse to avoid dealing with Jesus.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the innocence motif (three verdicts, 18:38; 19:4, 6), the spotless Lamb.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is a single verse with two big beats, so give the discussion room. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "What is truth?" (John 18:38).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Do I actually want to know the truth about Jesus, or is it easier to keep it an open question?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: total honesty welcome.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 18:38 aloud, slowly. Then read John 14:6 right after it.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verse 38, and John 14, verse 6.
- One-sentence setup: Pilate asks the biggest question in the room, and then doesn't stay to hear the answer.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it. (One verse, so go deep on it.)

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 38, what does Pilate ask Jesus? And what does he do right after asking?
   - Cold-call check: "What is truth?" And then he walks back outside without waiting for an answer.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Now put Pilate's question next to John 14:6, which you have memorized. What did Jesus say he is in 14:6? So who is Pilate asking "what is truth?" to?
   - Guide them: in 14:6 Jesus said, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life." So Pilate is standing inches from the Truth himself, looking right at him, and asking "what is truth?" The answer is standing in the room, wearing skin, and Pilate walks away from it.
3. **Core.** Pilate asks a real question and then doesn't wait for the answer. What's the difference between honestly searching for the truth and using "well, who can really know?" as a way to avoid dealing with something?
   - Land it: an honest seeker actually wants an answer and will stay for it. Pilate uses the question as an exit. "What is truth? Who's to say?" and out the door he goes. Treating truth as unknowable can be a genuine struggle, or it can be a convenient way to dodge a Jesus you don't want to deal with. Pilate does the second. He cared enough to ask, and not enough to stay.
4. **Core.** Read the second half of verse 38: Pilate goes out and says, "I find no guilt in him." What has the judge just declared about Jesus?
   - Draw out: that Jesus is innocent. The Roman governor, the man with the power of life and death, examines Jesus and finds no crime, no guilt, nothing. And he'll say it three separate times in John (here, 19:4, and 19:6). Jesus is declared innocent by the very man about to send him to die.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Why does John keep hammering that Jesus is innocent, three times over? How does Jesus being guiltless change what his death means?
   - Draw out: because a guilty man dying for his own crimes saves no one. But an innocent man, with no sin of his own, can die in the place of the guilty. Jesus' innocence is what makes the cross work. He's the spotless Lamb (1:29), condemned though he did nothing wrong, so that his death can count for others instead of for himself.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's the irony of Pilate asking "what is truth?"
   - He asks it standing in front of Jesus, who is the truth (14:6), and walks away without the answer.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Sit them in the irony. Pilate asks the deepest question a person can ask, "what is truth?", and the Truth is standing right in front of him, close enough to touch. And he turns around and walks out. He was inches from the answer and wouldn't wait five seconds for it.
- Make it personal. Some of you do exactly what Pilate did. "Who can really know? Everybody has their own truth." And it sounds thoughtful, but sometimes it's just a door you use to walk away from a Jesus you'd rather not deal with. The question is fine. The walking away is the problem. Jesus is right here. Will you stay for the answer?
- Land the innocence. And watch what Pilate himself says: "I find no guilt in him." The judge declares Jesus innocent, three times, and hands him over to die anyway. That innocence is everything. A guilty man can only die for himself. An innocent man can die in your place. Jesus is the spotless one, condemned though he did nothing wrong, so his death could count for you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the Truth that Pilate wouldn't stay for, and the innocent Lamb condemned in the place of the guilty.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Pilate asked the question and walked away from the answer. Are you actually seeking the truth about Jesus, or keeping it an open question so you don't have to deal with him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is a single verse with two big beats; give the discussion the full period. Beat one: "what is truth?" asked before the Truth himself (14:6, the memorized anchor paying off) is tragic blindness, not a cool open question. Beat two: "I find no guilt in him," the first of three innocence verdicts (with 19:4, 6), the spotless Lamb.
- Review 14:6 as the scene itself (no separate drill). This is where it lands.
- Guard against letting "what is truth?" read as admirable open-mindedness. Pilate asked and would not wait (Chrysostom). Distinguish honest searching from using doubt as an exit.
- Derive the frame from sermon 34; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block, single verse worked deeply.
