**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the raising of Lazarus (11:43-44).
2. Students will connect Jesus' call to "the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God" (John 5:28-29).
3. Students will explain the difference between Lazarus's raising and Jesus' resurrection.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus speaks to a dead man, and the dead man walks out.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the voice that made the world calls one corpse by name.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the raising as a resuscitation pointing to Jesus' own empty tomb.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the raising, so protect the Work block and Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Lazarus, come out" (John 11:43).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What in me feels dead or stuck right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly, whatever it is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 11:38-44 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 11, verses 38 through 44.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus comes to the tomb, a cave with a stone across the front.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 39, Jesus says to take away the stone. What does Martha warn him about?
   - Cold-call check: there will be a smell, because Lazarus has been dead four days. This is a real, decomposing body. The situation is hopeless by any normal measure.
2. **Entry.** Four days dead, sealed in a tomb, and starting to decay. On a scale of fixable problems, where does this rank? Why does that matter for the size of what's about to happen?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 43 and verse 44. What does Jesus do, and what happens? Be specific about the details.
   - Guide them: he cries out with a loud voice, "Lazarus, come out." And the dead man comes out, still wrapped hand and foot in grave clothes, face covered. A corpse hears its name and obeys. Jesus doesn't do CPR or medicine. He speaks, and death lets go.
4. **Core.** Back in John 5, Jesus said, "the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live." Here he calls one dead man by name. What does this scene prove about Jesus' voice?
   - Land it: his voice gives life to the dead. It's the same voice that spoke the world into being. When Jesus calls, even death has to obey. This one raising is a preview of the day he calls every grave open.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Notice something: Lazarus comes out still wrapped in grave clothes, and he would die again someday. Compare that to Jesus, who rose and left his grave clothes folded in an empty tomb, alive forever (John 20:6-7). Why is that difference the whole point?
   - Draw out: Lazarus was brought back to the same life, and he'd need to be buried again. This is a resuscitation, a sign that points past itself. Jesus rose to a new life that never ends. What Jesus did for Lazarus was a picture. What Jesus did at his own tomb is the real thing, and it's what he gives you.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what happened when Jesus called Lazarus's name?
   - A man who'd been dead four days heard his name and walked out of the tomb alive.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 17.

- Set the scene honestly. This wasn't a close call. Four days dead, in a tomb, decaying. There was nothing left to fix. And Jesus just speaks a name, and death lets go. The voice that made the universe calls one dead man, and the dead man comes.
- Point back and forward. Jesus said in chapter 5 that a day is coming when all the dead hear his voice and rise. Here's the trailer. If Jesus can call one man out of a sealed tomb, he can call you out of yours on the last day.
- Land the difference that matters. Lazarus came out still tangled in his grave clothes, and he'd die again. Jesus walked out of his own tomb and folded the grave clothes up, alive forever. Lazarus got more of the same life. Jesus gives you life that death never touches again. That's the resurrection he is.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the one whose voice the dead obey, and his own empty tomb is the life he holds out to you.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus called a dead man out by name. What in you feels dead or stuck, and what would it mean for Jesus to speak life into it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 11:45-57 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: the sign of life is exactly what makes the leaders decide to kill Jesus.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 17. Its center is that the dead obey the voice of the Lord, the same voice that spoke creation into being. Tie it to John 5:25-29 (Unit 8), the dead hearing his voice.
- Hold the resuscitation-versus-resurrection distinction clearly (sermon 17's four-year-old question: "did he ever die again?"). Lazarus was raised to die again, in grave clothes. Jesus rose to die no more, leaving his grave clothes behind. Lazarus's raising points to the greater thing.
- Keep the raising as a window (a sign), not a bare miracle. The glory of God frames it at both ends (11:4 and 11:40).
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
