**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will connect the division in 10:19-21 back to the healing of the blind man (John 9).
2. Students will explain the two hands and the security of the sheep (10:27-29).
3. Students will explain "I and the Father are one" (10:30) and how it grounds that security.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus promises his sheep can never be lost.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the security rests on his grip, not on how tightly you hold on.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the double hand sealed by "I and the Father are one"; the Feast of Dedication.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "No one will snatch them out of my hand" (John 10:28).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I most afraid of losing?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest fear, whatever it is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 10:19-30 aloud, one reads 19 through 24, the other 25 through 30.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 10, verses 19 through 30.
- One-sentence setup: it's now winter, the Feast of Dedication, and the leaders corner Jesus and demand a straight answer.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 20 and 21, the crowd is split about Jesus. One side says he has a demon. What does the other side say to shut that down?
   - Cold-call check: "Can a demon open the eyes of the blind?" They point back to the man Jesus healed last chapter. That miracle is still doing work.
2. **Entry.** When have you felt safest in your life? What made you feel like nothing could touch you?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 28 and 29 slowly. Jesus makes a huge promise about his sheep. What does he promise, and how many hands are holding the sheep?
   - Guide them: his sheep "will never perish," and "no one will snatch them out of my hand," and "no one is able to snatch them out of the Father's hand." Two hands hold the sheep at once, Jesus' hand and the Father's hand. Nobody has the strength to pull them out.
4. **Core.** Read verse 30: "I and the Father are one." Why does Jesus say this right after talking about two hands holding the sheep?
   - Land it: he just said the sheep are safe in his hand and safe in the Father's hand. Verse 30 explains how one flock can be safe in two hands: because he and the Father are one. The two hands aren't two separate grips fighting over you. They're one grip, because he and the Father are one.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Look at verse 27: his sheep "hear my voice... and follow me." How does following show that a person really is one of his sheep, without turning the following into the thing that earns the safety?
   - Draw out: sheep follow their shepherd. That's what sheep do. So following is the sign that you belong to him, the proof of it. The safety comes from his grip, and the following flows out of being held. You don't hold on tightly enough to save yourself. He holds you, and so you follow.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: whose hands hold the sheep, and what does Jesus say about anyone trying to snatch them?
   - His hand and the Father's hand, and no one is able to pull the sheep out.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh, no sermon.

- Name the fear this speaks to. Lots of students carry a quiet terror that they'll lose it, that they'll drift or fail or blow it and God will let go. Jesus speaks straight to that.
- Give them the two hands. If you're his sheep, you're held in Jesus' hand and the Father's hand at the same time, and nobody has the strength to pull you out. Nothing does: not your worst day, your worst sin, the enemy, or death. Two hands, one grip.
- Move the question off their strength. The question was never "is my grip on God strong enough." It's "whose hand am I in." And the answer is the strongest hands there are, held together as one. You don't keep yourself. The Shepherd keeps you.
- Guard both ditches, warmly. This isn't "so I can live however I want," because his sheep follow his voice (verse 27). And it isn't a reason to panic about whether you're really a sheep, because the security rests on his hand, not on how you feel today. If you're following his voice, you're held.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is one with the Father, and their two hands hold you so nothing can snatch you away.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says your safety rests on his grip and the Father's, not on yours. What fear could you set down if you actually believed that?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 10:31-38 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: Jesus' claim to be one with the Father makes the crowd reach for stones.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Teach the double hand (10:28-29) and the oneness (10:30) as one argument, not two topics. Verse 30 is the answer to how two hands keep one flock. That's the structural gift of this lesson.
- This is the payoff of John 6:37-39 (Unit 9), where Jesus said he loses none the Father gives him. Now it's doubled: two hands.
- Guard the security on both sides. Against presumption: the secure ones follow his voice (verse 27), so a life that follows no shepherd has no claim on the hand. Against anxiety: the security rests on his grip, so the anxious student's real question is "whose hand am I in," and the answer is the strongest hands there are.
- Note the Feast of Dedication (winter, Hanukkah). Priests examined themselves against Ezekiel 34 (the shepherds chapter) at this feast, so Jesus makes his shepherd claim at exactly the right festival.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
