**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain how much Jesus loves his disciples (with the Father's love for him, 15:9).
2. Students will explain what it means to "abide in my love" (15:9-10).
3. Students will explain the goal Jesus names: joy made full (15:11).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus wants his friends to be full of joy.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): obeying Jesus is how you stay in his love, and it leads to more joy.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the chain of love, Father to Son to disciples, and joy as the stated goal of the vine teaching.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "As the Father has loved me, so have I loved you" (John 15:9).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I go looking for joy? Does it last?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: name honestly what you actually chase.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 15:9-11 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 9 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus tells his friends how much he loves them and why he's saying all this.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 9, how much does Jesus say he loves his disciples? What's the comparison?
   - Cold-call check: "as the Father has loved me, so have I loved you." He loves them with the same love the Father has for him.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Sit on that comparison. The Father's love for the Son is perfect, total, from before the world began. Jesus says he loves his disciples like that. What does that do to the idea that God only sort of tolerates you?
   - Guide them: it means Jesus doesn't love his people a little, or on a good day, or grudgingly. He loves them with the fullest love there is. You're not tolerated. You're loved the way the Father loves the Son.
3. **Core.** Read verse 10. How do you "abide in" or stay in that love? What example does Jesus give?
   - Land it: keep his commandments, the way he kept the Father's commandments and stayed in the Father's love. Obeying isn't how you earn the love. It's how you stay close to it, the way staying near a fire keeps you warm. He's already loved you; keeping his word is how you live inside that love.
4. **Core.** Read verse 11. Why does Jesus say he's telling them all this? What's the goal?
   - Draw out: "that my joy may be in you, and that your joy may be full." The whole point of the vine, the abiding, the obeying, is joy. His own joy, in them, filled all the way up.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** A lot of people think obeying God drains the fun out of life. Jesus ties obeying, staying in his love, and full joy all together. How does staying close to Jesus and doing what he says produce more joy, not less?
   - Draw out: joy comes from being loved and staying near the one who loves you. Sin pulls you away from that and always over-promises. Staying in Jesus' love, which includes doing what he says, keeps you in the one place real joy actually lives. Obedience is the path deeper into joy, not away from it.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 11, why did Jesus say all of this?
   - So that his joy would be in them and their joy would be full.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the size of the love. Jesus doesn't say he likes you or puts up with you. He says he loves you the way the Father loves him, and that's the biggest, oldest, most perfect love there is. Let that be bigger than the voice in your head that says God's disappointed in you.
- Reframe obedience. Staying in his love means keeping his word, and that sounds like a leash until you see it's a place to live. He's a fire, and his commands keep you near the warmth. You don't obey to get loved. You obey to stay where the love is.
- Aim at joy. He said all of this so his joy would be in you and fill you up. The Christian life was never meant to be grim duty. It's meant to end in the fullest joy there is, his own.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus loves his people with the Father's own love, and he gives them his own joy.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus loves you with the same love the Father has for him. If that's true, what's one thing it changes about today?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 15:5. Read John 15:12-13 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5 (short Thursday): the command to love one another, and the greatest love, laying down your life.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: the size of Christ's love (the Father's own love, 15:9), and joy as the stated goal (15:11). Guard against hearing 15:1-11 as a burden; its end is fullness of joy.
- Keep obedience grace-first: keeping his word is how you stay in a love already given, not how you earn it. Ties to L2 and to 14:15.
- This closes sermon 26's span (15:1-11). Derive the "received by the joy of Christ" frame from sermon 26; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
