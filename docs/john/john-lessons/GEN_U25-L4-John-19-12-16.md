**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the pressure put on Pilate (19:12).
2. Students will explain "Behold your King!" and the crowd's response (19:14-15).
3. Students will explain "we have no king but Caesar" as self-condemnation (19:15).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the leaders traded their true King for a foreign emperor.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): claiming God while your real loyalty is elsewhere.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Passover irony of rejecting the King at the feast of God's kingship.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "We have no king but Caesar" (John 19:15).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If someone watched where my time and love actually go, who would they say is the king of my life?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: total honesty welcome.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:12-16 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 12 through 16.
- One-sentence setup: the leaders play their last card to force Pilate's hand, and it works.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it. (Anti-Judaism guard active and important, see teacher notes.)

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 12, what threat do the leaders use to pressure Pilate into condemning Jesus?
   - Cold-call check: that if Pilate frees Jesus, he's "no friend of Caesar," which could cost Pilate his career or his life.
2. **Entry.** In verse 14, what does Pilate say as he presents Jesus one last time? How does the crowd respond (verse 15)?
   - Draw out: "Behold your King!" And they scream, "Away with him, crucify him!"
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read the exchange in verse 15 slowly. Pilate asks, "Shall I crucify your King?" And the chief priests answer, "We have no king but Caesar." Now remember: what feast is happening this week, and who is that feast about?
   - Guide them: it's Passover, the feast that celebrates God rescuing Israel and being their true King. And at that exact feast, the religious leaders say they have no king but Caesar, a pagan Roman emperor. The people whose entire hope was the coming Messiah-King look at him and pick Caesar instead. It's the deepest betrayal in the book.
4. **Core.** Why is "we have no king but Caesar" a verdict they pronounce on themselves, not just on Jesus?
   - Land it: because in rejecting their King, they're disowning God himself as their King. They're saying, out loud, that their real allegiance is to worldly power, not to God. Nobody forced those words out of them. They chose Caesar over Christ, and in doing it, they condemned themselves.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** It's easy to shake our heads at those leaders. But "we have no king but Caesar" is a temptation for anyone. What does it look like today to claim God with your mouth while your real king is something else?
   - Draw out: it looks like saying you follow Jesus while your life actually bows to money, or status, or approval, or comfort, or a relationship. Your real king is whatever you'd disobey Jesus to keep. The leaders said it out loud. Most of us say it quietly, with our schedules and our wallets and what we're afraid to lose. This verse holds a mirror up to every heart, including the church kid's.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what do the chief priests say in verse 15, and why is it so tragic?
   - "We have no king but Caesar." At the feast of God's kingship, they disown their own Messiah for a pagan emperor.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the tragedy. Their whole history, every prophet, every promise, pointed to a coming King. And when he finally stood in front of them, bleeding, they said, "we have no king but Caesar." They traded the King of heaven for the emperor of Rome. It's the saddest sentence in the Gospel.
- Turn the mirror gently. And before we feel superior, hear the question honestly. Who's the real king of your life? Not who you'd say in class. Who you actually obey. What you'd disobey Jesus to hold onto, that's your Caesar. We all have one, and this verse asks us to name it.
- Point to the King they rejected. And here's the mercy: the King they threw away went to the cross anyway, for them, and for you. He was rejected by his own, and he still laid down his life to save rejecters. That's the kind of King he is.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus, disowned by his own people as King, went to the cross to reign as the King who saves even those who reject him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "What's your Caesar, the thing you'd disobey Jesus to keep? Name it honestly, and ask him to be King instead."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 19:17-18 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5 (short Thursday): they lead him out and crucify him.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- ANTI-JUDAISM GUARD AT ITS PEAK this lesson (19:15). Teach "we have no king but Caesar" as the self-condemnation of a specific set of leaders and, through them, of every heart whose real allegiance is elsewhere. Say the obvious if needed: Jesus, his mother, the disciples, and the men who bury him are all Jews. The guilt is human sin and God's plan, NEVER the Jewish people. Let the line fall on the churched student's own divided heart. No student should leave with contempt for Jews.
- The Passover irony (rejecting the King at the feast of God's kingship) is the disciple-tier depth.
- Closes the sermon 35 span (19:1-16). Derive the frame from sermon 35; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
