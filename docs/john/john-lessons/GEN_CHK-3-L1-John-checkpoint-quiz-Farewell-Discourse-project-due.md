**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will write the five Farewell Discourse memory anchors from memory (13:34-35, 14:6, 15:5, 16:33, 17:3).
2. Students will recall the christotelic heart of each chapter of the discourse (13 through 17).

---

**Talk budget:** short day. This is a checkpoint and a graded quiz, not new teaching. Keep your voice light, warm, and celebratory, and let the students do the recalling and the writing. Leave the board line up the whole period: "His last night, spent giving them himself."

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (3-4 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end" (John 13:1).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Out of the last five chapters, what's one thing about Jesus I don't want to forget?"

## Move 2: Recap the Farewell Discourse (7-8 min)

Quick and warm. Ask, don't lecture. Walk the five chapters in one sweep, and let the students supply the answers and the anchors. This is the same gather-from-the-room move from the Holy Spirit synthesis.

1. **John 13, the towel and the basin.** Ask: "What does the Lord of everything do with his hands on this night?" (He kneels and washes their feet, even Judas's, and turns it into a command.) Lands on: Christ the servant-King whose love is the pattern and the power for theirs. Anchor: 13:34-35.
2. **John 14, the way to the Father.** Ask: "Thomas says he's lost and doesn't know the way. What does Jesus hand him?" (Himself, and the promise of the Helper so they aren't left as orphans.) Lands on: Christ the only road home to the Father. Anchor: 14:6.
3. **John 15, the true vine.** Ask: "Where does a branch get everything it needs?" (From staying joined to the vine; apart from him, nothing.) Lands on: Christ the vine, the source of all their fruit, love, and life. Anchor: 15:5.
4. **John 16, sorrow into joy.** Ask: "He promises them trouble and something else in the same breath. What else?" (The Spirit who convicts and guides, a grief the cross turns to unstealable joy, and his finished victory.) Lands on: Christ who overcomes the world and sends the Spirit to make him known. Anchor: 16:33.
5. **John 17, the High Priest's prayer.** Ask: "On the worst night of his life, who does he spend his prayer asking for?" (For his own by name, and for everyone who'd believe through their word, which is this room, 17:20.) Lands on: Christ the great High Priest praying his people home into the love of God. Anchor: 17:3.

Say one sentence: five chapters, one night, and one Jesus who spent it giving his friends himself.

## Move 3: Checkpoint quiz (14-16 min, graded)

Phones and Bibles away. This feeds the gradebook. Two parts.

**Part A, the five Farewell anchors from memory (the weight of the grade).** Put the references on the board and have students write each from memory, in the ESV:
1. John 13:34-35
2. John 14:6
3. John 15:5
4. John 16:33
5. John 17:3

For a cumulative touch, add two earlier anchors by reference:
6. John 10:11
7. John 11:25

Grade for the sense and the load-bearing phrases, not word-perfect punctuation. A student who has the meaning and the main wording has it. The verse texts are in the Answer Key below.

**Part B, content recall (short, one per chapter).** Students answer briefly in writing:
8. John 13: What is the measure of the new command, the standard Jesus sets for how they're to love?
9. John 14: How is "I am the way... no one comes to the Father except through me" good news and comfort, and not only a hard line?
10. John 15: What does "abide" mean, and what flows from it?
11. John 16: What two things does Jesus promise in 16:33?
12. John 17: How does Jesus define eternal life in 17:3?
13. The Spirit (from the Holy Spirit synthesis): Name one thing the Helper does, and say where the Spirit always sends our eyes.

Model answers are in the Answer Key.

## Move 4: Land and minister, and collect the projects (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and collect the Q3 projects. Off the grade, warm.

- The closing word over the room. For five chapters you've been sitting in a room on the last night of Jesus' life, listening to a dying man comfort the friends who were about to run. Everything he gave them that night, he gave you: himself to serve you, himself as the way home, himself as the vine you live from, his Spirit to keep him near, and his prayer that carries you all the way to the Father. John 17:20 is Jesus praying for the people who would believe through the disciples' word. That reached this room. You are in that prayer.
- Collect and frame the project. As the "Teaching the Farewell Discourse" projects come in, name what the assignment actually was. John wrote his whole book so people would meet Christ and have life in his name (20:31). A group that teaches its chapter so the class meets Jesus in it is doing the very thing John did, and the very thing Jesus did on this night: taking a stretch of Scripture and landing it on Christ and the response he asks. Tell them: you got to teach your friends the last words of Jesus to his friends.
- Christ connection to say out loud: every chapter, every anchor, every project points to the same person, who spent his last night giving himself away.

## Move 5: Respond (short, graded participation)

- One line in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Which of the five chapters hit you hardest, and why?"
- A short blessing over the room closes the checkpoint well.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 18:1-11 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's checkpoint quiz (content), the Q3 project, and the participation line. The five Farewell anchors stay in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 24: the arrest. We leave the upper room and follow Jesus into the garden, where the soldiers come.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: this is a scheduled checkpoint on a short Thursday. It's a graded quiz plus a warm recap plus collecting the projects, not new exposition. The John 18 exposition begins next unit.
- Keep the recap a fast, warm sweep. Ask the five questions and let students supply the answers and the anchors. Cap your voice; this is their recall.
- Grade the memory quiz for sense and key phrases, never word-perfection. Reward the load-bearing phrase in each: the measure "as I have loved you," "no one comes to the Father except through me," "apart from me you can do nothing," "I have overcome the world," "that they know you."
- Content questions (Part B) are recall of what was taught, answerable by every tier, not new reasoning.
- Keep it celebratory and low-anxiety. The quarter's payoff, and the projects are due, so the room already carries nerves. Frame the quiz as "show me what you carry."
- Keep the christotelic thesis central so the quiz doesn't flatten five tender chapters into trivia. The facts serve the person. Leave the board line up all period, and end on the pastoral word.
- Content is gradable; belief is invitational. A student can write all five verses and answer all six questions without being asked to profess. Keep it that way.

## Answer Key

**Part A, the memory anchors (ESV). Grade for sense and key wording.**

1. **John 13:34-35**: "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another."
2. **John 14:6**: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me."
3. **John 15:5**: "I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever abides in me and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."
4. **John 16:33**: "I have said these things to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world."
5. **John 17:3**: "And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent."
6. **John 10:11**: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."
7. **John 11:25**: "I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live."

**Part B, content recall. Model answers; accept any response that carries the sense.**

8. **John 13 (the measure of the new command):** "as I have loved you." The standard is Jesus' own self-giving love, shown in the towel and headed for the cross. Accept anything that names his love as the measure. Guard against "just be nice."
9. **John 14 (14:6 as good news):** One true way home means there is a way home, and it's open to anyone. And Jesus says it to steady a scared, honest friend (Thomas), so it's spoken as comfort, warmly, not thrown as a weapon.
10. **John 15 ("abide"):** To stay joined to Christ, a state of being before it's a doing. Fruit, love, and answered prayer flow from the union, and apart from him a branch can do nothing.
11. **John 16 (the two promises of 16:33):** In the world, tribulation (trouble is guaranteed). In him, peace, grounded in "I have overcome the world" (his victory is finished).
12. **John 17 (eternal life, 17:3):** Knowing God, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom he sent. A relationship of knowing that starts now, more than a future place.
13. **The Spirit:** Any one of: teaches, reminds, bears witness, convicts the world, guides into all truth. And the Spirit always sends our eyes to Jesus: he glorifies Christ (16:14).
