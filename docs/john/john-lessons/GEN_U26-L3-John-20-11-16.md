# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 26 Lesson 3 of 8: he calls her by name (John 20:11-16)

| Field | Detail |

| Unit | U26 The empty tomb |
| Lesson | 3 of 8 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Apr 29 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus |
| Passage | John 20:11-16 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Mary is weeping at the tomb and does not recognize the risen Jesus until he says one word, her name, and she knows him by his voice. |
| Christ connection | The good shepherd who calls his own by name, known by his voice. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Mary's encounter at the tomb (20:11-15).
2. Students will explain how Mary recognizes Jesus (by his voice saying her name, 20:16).
3. Students will connect "Mary" to the good shepherd calling his own by name (10:3).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the risen Jesus knew one grieving woman by name.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus knows his own personally, by name.
- **Stretch** (disciple): 20:16 fulfilling 10:3 (the shepherd calls his sheep by name).

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep the wonder. One tender scene, taught well.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Jesus said to her, 'Mary.'" (John 20:16).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Does it feel like God knows me personally, by name? Why or why not?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 20:11-16 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 11 through 16.
- One-sentence setup: Peter and John go home, but Mary stays at the tomb, weeping.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: recognized by name. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 14 and 15, Mary turns and sees Jesus. Does she recognize him? Who does she think he is?
   - Cold-call check: no, she doesn't recognize him; she thinks he's the gardener.
2. **The driving question (whole class).** Read verse 16. Mary is looking right at Jesus and doesn't know his face. Then he says one word, and instantly she knows him. What's the word, and what does it tell you that his voice saying her name is what breaks through?
   - Guide them: the word is "Mary," her name. His face didn't give him away, and his voice speaking her name did. The moment he says "Mary," she knows exactly who he is: "Rabboni," Teacher. Something about the way he said her name, the way he'd always said it, told her everything.
3. **Core.** Now connect this to something Jesus said back in John 10:3, about the good shepherd. What did he say the shepherd does with his sheep? How is this scene that promise coming true?
   - Land it: in 10:3 Jesus said the good shepherd "calls his own sheep by name," and "the sheep follow him, for they know his voice." Here it happens, in a garden, at dawn. The risen Shepherd calls one of his own by her name, and she knows his voice instantly. He didn't call her "woman" anymore. He called her "Mary." He knows his people personally, by name.
4. **Core.** Think about who Mary is: a woman with a hard past, grieving, in the dark, at rock bottom. And she's the first person the risen Lord of the universe speaks to, and he calls her by name. What does that tell you about how Jesus sees people like her, and like us?
   - Draw out: he doesn't see a category or a nobody. He sees a person he knows and loves, by name. The risen King of everything makes his first appearance to a broken, grieving woman, and the first thing out of his mouth is her name. That's how personally he knows and loves his own.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how does Mary recognize Jesus?
   - By his voice, when he says her name, "Mary."

Circulate quietly. Keep it tender.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the name. Mary is at the lowest point of her life, crying at an empty grave, and the risen Lord of heaven and earth appears, and the first word he says is her name. Not a lecture. Not "woman." "Mary." And that one word, in that familiar voice, undoes all her grief at once.
- Make it personal. Here's what I want you to hear: he knows your name too. Not "one of the sophomores." Not a face in a crowd. Your name. The good shepherd calls his own sheep by name, and if you're his, he knows yours, and he says it the way he said "Mary," like he's known you all along.
- Land the tenderness. And notice who he came to first. Not the powerful. Not the important. A grieving woman with a rough past. That's exactly the kind of person the risen Jesus goes to first. If you've ever felt too broken or too far gone to be known by God, look at Mary in the garden.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Shepherd calls his own by name, and he knows yours.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "The risen Jesus knew Mary by name. If he knows you that personally, by name, what does that change today?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 20:17-18 once before Friday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: Jesus sends Mary with the news, and makes his Father our Father.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One tender scene: recognized by name. Keep the wonder and the personal warmth.
- The heart of the lesson: 20:16 fulfills 10:3 (the good shepherd calls his own by name). Mary knows Jesus by his voice, not his face. Land it personally: he knows each student by name.
- Note who the first witness is: a grieving woman with a hard past. The risen Lord goes to the lowly first. Keep it pastoral for the "too far gone" student.
