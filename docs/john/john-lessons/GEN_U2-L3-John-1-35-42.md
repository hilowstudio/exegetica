# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 2 Lesson 3 of 4: the first disciples follow (John 1:35-42)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U2 Witness & first disciples |
| Lesson | 3 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Fri Sep 18 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 1:35-42 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The witness passes to ordinary people, and receiving Jesus stops being an idea and becomes a scene: John points, two follow, "come and see," Andrew finds his brother, and Jesus renames Simon. This is what John 1:12 looks like when it happens to real men in one week. |
| Christ connection | The one the witnesses keep pointing at now turns and speaks. He asks the seeker's question, gives them the day, and gives a new name. Receiving him looks like coming, staying, following. |
| Memory verse | Plant and teach John 1:12 today. "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God." This is the unit's new graded anchor, and it lands here as a scene you can watch. Keep 20:31 and 1:14 sharp too. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega 02, "The First Disciples" (John 1:35-51), first half. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 1:12 and retell the scene in order (John points, two follow, "come and see," Andrew finds Simon, Jesus renames him).
2. Students will explain what "Come and see" (1:39) invites a person to do.
3. Students will explain the three moves of John 1:12 (received him, believed in his name, given the right to be God's child) using the scene.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): two ordinary men told a brother "you have to meet this man," and "come and see" is open to you, with your real questions.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the first disciples gave Jesus a whole day (1:39); coasting is possible exactly where you have never actually come and looked.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "what are you seeking?" as the Gospel's opening question; Jesus as the true finder beneath the witness chain; the renaming as a given, kept identity.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant, so give it the full period.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "But to all who did receive him... he gave the right to become children of God" (John 1:12).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Jesus' first question in this Gospel is 'What are you seeking?' If he asked you that today, what is your honest answer?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: an honest "I do not know" is a real answer.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 1:35-42 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 35 through 42.
- One-sentence setup: the pointing finally works. John points, and two men actually follow Jesus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 38, Jesus turns and speaks his first words in this Gospel. What does he ask? How do the two answer, and what does he say back (verse 39)?
   - Draw out: "What are you seeking?" They ask where he is staying, and he says, "Come and see." They spend the day with him. His first move is an invitation to come and find out.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** After Andrew spends the day with Jesus, what is the first thing he does (verses 40-41)? What does he say?
   - Guide them: he finds his own brother Simon and says, "We have found the Messiah." A witness who was pointed at Jesus turns and points someone else. The chain keeps going.
3. **Core.** Read verse 42. What does Jesus do to Simon, and when, relative to anything Simon has accomplished?
   - Land it: Jesus renames him: "You shall be called Cephas," which means Peter, a rock. He does it on the spot, before Peter has done a single thing, before any success and before any failure. The new name is given, and it is not something Peter earns or can be failed out of.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Andrew "found" Simon and said "we have found the Messiah." But look at the whole scene. Who is really doing the finding?
   - Draw out: Jesus is. The witnesses point, people come, but underneath it Jesus is the one who calls and keeps. They think they found him; the truth is he found them. That is grace, and it is why verse 13 says the new birth is "of God."
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Recite verse 12. Then: what are the three moves in it, and where do you see them in the scene?
   - Received him (they came and stayed), believed in his name (they confessed "Messiah"), given the right to be God's child (the new name and belonging Jesus gives).

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the living center of sermon 02.

- Preach the renaming. Jesus is the one with the power to name you before you have proven anything, and the name he gives holds because he holds it. He does not wait for Peter to become a rock. He calls him one, and then spends three years, and a resurrection, making it true.
- Put the verse in their hands. This is 1:12 as a scene: receiving him looks like coming, staying, following, and being given a name and a family you did not earn. And verse 13 tells you who did the work: God gave the new birth.
- Christ connection to say out loud: witnesses bring people to Jesus, and Jesus gives them a new name and a new family, by his own doing.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus renamed Simon before he had done anything to deserve it. What would it mean to have your identity given to you by Jesus, instead of earned by your record?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 1:12. Read John 1:43-51 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. John 1:12 enters the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up L4: Philip finds Nathanael, a flat-out skeptic, and Jesus turns him into a worshiper.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block, and this is the plant. Turn content into questions, then plant 1:12 warmly. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the coherent plant for 1:12. Preview it in L1 and L2, and land it here, once, where receiving becomes a scene you can watch. (Ignore any older note that planted it in L1.)
- The load-bearing sermon move is the renaming: identity given by Christ, before performance, and kept by Christ. Keep it tied to Jesus, not turned into a general self-esteem talk.
- Guard against "be bold like Andrew." The four called this week are mixed and ordinary; one is a scoffer next lesson. The point is that Jesus called them and they came. And keep "come and see" a real invitation to the doubter, not a slogan.
- Give God the credit for the new birth (1:13) even as you press the real coming and believing of 1:12. Marshall's order: the finding is his, the new name is his gift, and love follows from a heart persuaded it is held.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full period for the plant.
