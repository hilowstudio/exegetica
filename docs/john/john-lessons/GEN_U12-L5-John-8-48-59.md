**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus claims in "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58).
2. Students will explain why the crowd picks up stones (8:59).
3. Students will explain what Jesus means that Abraham "rejoiced that he would see my day" (8:56).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus makes a claim you can't shrug off, that he existed before Abraham.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the crowd understood him perfectly, which is why they reached for stones.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "I am" in the present tense as timeless being; Abraham seeing Christ's day.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the climax of the unit, so protect the Work block and Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Before Abraham was, I am" (John 8:58).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If Jesus really is God, what does that change for me?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write your honest question about whether Jesus is who he says.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 8:48-59 aloud, one reads 48 through 53, the other 54 through 59.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 8, verses 48 through 59.
- One-sentence setup: the whole argument has been building, and now Jesus says the biggest thing of all.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 51, what does Jesus promise the person who keeps his word?
   - Cold-call check: "he will never see death." A staggering promise, and the crowd is horrified by it (verse 52).
2. **Entry.** In verse 53 the crowd asks, "Who do you make yourself out to be?" It's the question the whole chapter has been circling. What do you think they're bracing for?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 56: "your father Abraham rejoiced that he would see my day. He saw it and was glad." Abraham lived two thousand years before Jesus. What is Jesus claiming about himself and about Abraham?
   - Guide them: Jesus is claiming Abraham somehow looked ahead and saw him, and that Jesus was already there to be seen. He's putting himself above and before Abraham, the great founder of the whole nation.
4. **Core.** Read verse 58 slowly: "before Abraham was, I am." Notice he doesn't say "I was." He says "I am." Then read verse 59. What does the crowd do, and why?
   - Land it: they pick up stones. "I am" is God's own name from the burning bush (Exodus 3:14), and Jesus uses it in the present tense, claiming to exist outside of time as God. Claiming to be God was blasphemy, punished by stoning. Their reaction proves they understood him perfectly.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Jesus says "I am," not "I was," about a time before Abraham was born. Why does the present tense matter?
   - Draw out: "I was" would mean he existed a long time ago. "I am" means he simply is, always, outside of time, the way only God is. It's the same "I am" from Exodus 3:14. Jesus is claiming to be the eternal God.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did the crowd understand Jesus to be claiming in verse 58, and how do you know?
   - That he is God, and you know because they picked up stones to kill him for blasphemy.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. Build on sermon 13.

- Name the summit. This whole chapter has been Jesus saying one thing louder and louder: he is God. Light of the world. The I AM. Free-er of slaves. And now the loudest of all: "Before Abraham was, I am." He takes God's own name and says it about himself in the present tense.
- Point to the stones. The crowd didn't misunderstand him and overreact. They understood him exactly. That's why they grabbed rocks. A man claiming to be God gets stoned for blasphemy, unless the claim is true.
- Close the exits, warmly. This is the claim you can't sit on the fence about. If Jesus said "before Abraham was, I am," he's either God or he's a liar or he's insane. "Nice teacher" was never on the table. He forces a verdict, and he asks you for one.
- Say the good news of the whole unit. The one who is the eternal God, the light, the great I AM, came into the dark for you and will go to a cross for you. Follow him, and you walk in the light.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the eternal I AM, God before Abraham, and he came into the dark to bring you home.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'Before Abraham was, I am' is a claim you can't ignore. Either Jesus is God or he isn't. Which way do you lean right now, and what makes you lean that way?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing John 8:12 and your other memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 8:12 stays in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 13: Jesus heals a man born blind, and the whole chapter is about spiritual sight.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 13's climax. The whole unit has climbed to this: "before Abraham was, I am." Let the escalation pay off here.
- Teach the present tense at the disciple tier. "I am," not "I was," is the point. It's the timeless being of God, the same name from Exodus 3:14. The stoning proves the crowd heard it correctly.
- Use the crowd's reaction as the argument for the deity claim. They didn't stone a good teacher. They stoned a man they understood to be claiming to be God.
- Close on the verdict the whole unit forces, warmly. Worship him or reject him. No "good teacher" middle. And then hold out the grace: this God came into the dark for them.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. This closes Unit 12.
