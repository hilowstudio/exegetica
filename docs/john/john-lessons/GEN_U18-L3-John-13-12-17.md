**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus tells his disciples to do for one another (13:14).
2. Students will explain what "wash one another's feet" means in practice.
3. Students will explain "blessed are you if you do them" (13:17).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus gives his followers an example to copy.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the blessing lands on the one who actually does it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the servant is not greater than his master; humble service as the shape of the church.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them" (John 13:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What do I know I should do but keep not doing?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 13:12-17 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 13, verses 12 through 17.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus sits back down and asks the disciples if they understand what he just did.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 13 and 14, Jesus calls himself their "Teacher and Lord." Given that, what does he say they should do for each other?
   - Cold-call check: since he, their Lord and Teacher, washed their feet, they should wash one another's feet.
2. **Entry.** Jesus isn't mainly starting a foot-washing ceremony. What do you think "wash one another's feet" means for a group of friends today?
   - Draw out: doing the low, humble, unglamorous jobs for each other. Serving in ways nobody notices or thanks you for. Taking the position Jesus took.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 16: "a servant is not greater than his master." How does that shut down the idea that some kinds of service are beneath you?
   - Guide them: if the Lord of everything washed feet, then no job is too low for his followers. You're not greater than Jesus. If he did the dirtiest task, you don't get to say "that's beneath me." Humble service isn't optional for special-tier Christians. It's the shape of the whole thing.
4. **Core.** Read verse 17 carefully: "if you know these things, blessed are you if you do them." Where does Jesus put the blessing? What does that say about just admiring this passage without changing anything?
   - Land it: the blessing is on the doing. You can understand the foot-washing perfectly, write a great essay on it, admire how humble Jesus was, and stay completely outside the blessing until you actually pick up a towel. Knowing isn't the same as doing.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Christians sometimes turn this into a literal foot-washing ritual once a year and feel like they've obeyed it. What's the risk in that, and what is Jesus actually after?
   - Draw out: a once-a-year ceremony can become a way to check the box and skip the daily humble service the passage is really about. Jesus is after a whole life of lowering yourself to serve, not one staged event. The towel is the measure, wherever the dirty job is.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 17, where does the blessing come from?
   - From doing what Jesus showed, not just knowing it.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name what Jesus is doing. He isn't just being nice. He's setting a pattern for his whole church. The Lord got on the floor, and now that's the shape of following him: you serve, especially in the low and unnoticed places.
- Close the escape hatch. "A servant is not greater than his master." You don't get to decide some work is beneath you, because Jesus, who is greater than all of you, did the lowest job in the room. There's no task too small for a follower of a foot-washing King.
- Land on the doing. This is the one where a lot of Christians nod and change nothing. Jesus says the blessing is in doing it. So the real question isn't "do I understand foot-washing." It's "whose feet am I actually washing this week."
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus the Lord served, and his people are blessed when they take the towel and do the same.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The blessing is in the doing. What's one low, unglamorous thing you could do for someone this week, and will you actually do it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 13:18-30 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: at the table, Jesus reveals that one of them will betray him.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The heart of the lesson is humble service, with the towel as its measure. Guard against literalizing this into a required foot-washing ceremony that lets people skip the daily service the passage is really about.
- Land 13:17 hard: the blessing is on the doing. This is a passage students can admire and never obey. Push them to a concrete act in Move 5.
- 13:16 ("a servant is not greater than his master") shuts down the idea that any service is beneath a follower of a foot-washing Lord.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
