# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 4 Lesson 3 of 4: God so loved the world (John 3:16-17)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U4 The new birth |
| Lesson | 3 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Sep 30 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 3:16-17 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The most familiar verse in the Bible, read slowly enough to hear again: God's love for a hostile world took the form of handing over his own Son. |
| Christ connection | The manner of God's love is the cross of verse 14, the Son given so that whoever believes has eternal life. |
| Memory verse | Plant and teach John 3:16 today. "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life." This is the unit's new graded anchor. Teach it in three chunks. Keep 20:31, 1:14, 1:12 sharp too. |
| Resources | Fresh-build (no Adam sermon covers John 3:1-21). Have a class Bible open to John 3. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 3:16.
2. Students will explain "gave his only Son" as the cost and the manner of God's love (the cross of verse 14).
3. Students will explain "whoever believes" as the wide, free way in, and "the world" as the hostile world God loved.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): God looked at people who wanted nothing to do with him and gave up his own Son to rescue them.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): name the familiarity problem out loud, then make three words strange again, "so loved," "world," "gave."
- **Stretch** (disciple): "only Son" (monogenes) across the Prologue (1:14, 1:18) and this verse; "gave" as the language of the cross; the shape of 3:16 as manner, object, cost.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant, so give it the full period, and read the famous verse slowly enough to hear it.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son" (John 3:16).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "You have probably heard John 3:16 a hundred times. Read it slowly. What is one word in it you have never really stopped on?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: honest is welcome.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 3:16-17 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 3, verses 16 and 17.
- One-sentence setup: this comes right after the snake on the pole, so "gave his only Son" means the Son lifted up on the cross.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Verse 16 says "God so loved the world." Look at verse 14 just before it. What did that love actually do? What did God "give"?
   - Draw out: he gave his only Son, and verse 14 just said the Son would be lifted up on the cross. "So loved" means loved like this, the way of the love, and the way is the cross. It is more than "loved so much." It is "loved like this."
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** "Gave his only Son." What does the word "gave" cost the Giver? And who is "the world" God loved (look ahead to verse 19)?
   - Guide them: "gave" is the language of a Father handing over a Son. And "the world" is the dark, hostile world of verse 19, the one that "loved the darkness." God loved that world, the one with its back turned, and handed over his Son for it.
3. **Core.** Who does the verse say gets eternal life, and what is the alternative it names plainly?
   - Land it: "whoever believes in him," which is wide and free, open to anyone. And the alternative is real: "should not perish." The offer is genuinely for anyone who believes, and the danger it rescues from is genuine.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 17 is the headline over everything hard that follows. What does it say God sent the Son to do?
   - Draw out: "God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but in order that the world might be saved through him." Rescue is the purpose. God's aim in sending the Son is salvation.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Recite verse 16. Then: what does "gave" cost, and who is the offer for?
   - It costs a Father his Son; the offer is for whoever believes.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Un-flatten it. The love in this verse is not a warm feeling God had at a distance. It is a Son handed over on a Roman cross, for a world that had turned away, and that world includes you on your worst day.
- Plant the verse in the payoff. The new birth you cannot make (L1) and the Son lifted up (L2) are the reason 3:16 can say "he gave his only Son." This verse is the whole unit in one sentence.
- Christ connection to say out loud: read it as news addressed to you, God gave, so that whoever believes, including you, would not perish but have eternal life.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- Plant the memory verse. Teach John 3:16 in three chunks and have students say each before the whole: (1) "For God so loved the world," (2) "that he gave his only Son," (3) "that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life." Then say the whole verse together.
- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'God so loved the world.' If that love was a Father handing over a Son for people who had turned away, what does it change to know you are in that 'world'?"
- Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 3:16. Read John 3:18-21 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. John 3:16 enters the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up L4: the verdict, the light that has come, and coming to the light.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block, and this is the plant. Turn content into questions, then plant 3:16 in three chunks. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Read "so loved" for manner ("loved like this"), pointing up to the cross of verse 14, so students hear more than "so much."
- Grounding for your own prep: "only Son" is monogenes, the unique Son of the Prologue (1:14, 1:18) now handed over; "gave" is cross language.
- Guard "the world" on both sides: it is the hostile world of 3:19 (not just the nice people), and the offer is to "whoever believes" (not everyone automatically). Hold the genuine breadth of the offer together with belief as the way in.
- Do not let 3:16 flatten into a slogan or a poster. Read it as a Father surrendering a Son and it stops being a slogan. And do not turn the response into "clean yourself up first"; God loved the hostile world as it was.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full period for the plant.
