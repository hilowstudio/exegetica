**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 21:17.
2. Students will explain the three questions as healing the three denials (21:15-17).
3. Students will explain "feed my sheep" as love for Christ expressed in caring for his people.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus gave Peter three chances to undo three denials.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "do you love me" is the question that matters, and love turns into service.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the restoration to office; the flock is Christ's own; the agape/phileo swap as stylistic variation.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant and the emotional climax of the whole unit. Give it the full period, and keep it tender.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Lord, you know everything; you know that I love you" (John 21:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If Jesus asked me, 'do you love me,' what would I honestly say?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest answer.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 21:15-17 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 21, verses 15 through 17.
- One-sentence setup: breakfast is over, and now Jesus turns to Peter by the fire.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 15 through 17, how many times does Jesus ask Peter if he loves him? What does Jesus say after each answer?
   - Cold-call check: three times. After each, he gives Peter a job: "feed my lambs," "tend my sheep," "feed my sheep."
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Why three times? What did Peter do three times, at a fire, a few weeks ago (remember 18:15-27)?
   - Guide them: Peter denied Jesus three times at a charcoal fire. Now, at another charcoal fire, Jesus asks "do you love me" three times, and gives Peter three chances to say "I love you." He's healing the exact wound, denial by denial. Each "I love you" answers an "I don't know him." By the end, the denial is completely undone.
3. **Core.** Look at the question Jesus asks. He doesn't ask "how much do you know about me," or "are you sorry enough," or "will you promise never to fail again." He asks, "do you love me." Why is that the question that matters most?
   - Land it: because the whole relationship comes down to love. Not knowledge, not performance, not a track record. Jesus wants Peter's heart. The failure didn't disqualify Peter; the question is only whether he still loves Jesus. And that's the question for every one of us: do you love him. That is what he asks, over how good you are or how much you know.
4. **Core.** Read verse 17 again: Peter is "grieved" the third time. Why does the third question hurt? And notice what Jesus does with each of Peter's answers: he gives him a job. What's the job?
   - Draw out: the third time stings because it echoes the third denial; Peter feels the weight of it. And each time Peter says "I love you," Jesus says "feed my sheep." He gives the restored failure something to do: care for Jesus' people. Love for Jesus turns into shepherding his flock. Restoration comes with a mission.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** "Feed my sheep." Whose sheep are they? And where does the restored Peter go with this charge (peek at 1 Peter 5:2, written by Peter years later)?
   - Draw out: they're Jesus' sheep, his flock, not Peter's. Peter is an under-shepherd, caring for the flock that belongs to the Good Shepherd (10:11). And Peter spent the rest of his life doing it: years later he wrote to church leaders, "shepherd the flock of God among you." The restoration wasn't just a warm moment. It launched a lifetime of feeding Jesus' sheep. (Note for anyone who's heard about the different Greek words for "love" here: the ESV rightly renders them all "love." John swaps synonyms freely in these three verses. The point is the threefold restoration, not a hidden Greek puzzle.)
6. **Memory verse plant.** Have students say John 21:17 together, then in pairs. This is the last of the sixteen. Make sure "do you love me" and "Feed my sheep" are solid, and let them feel that this closes the whole set.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the heart of the unit; let it land tenderly.

- Walk them through the healing. Peter denied Jesus three times, at a fire, in the dark. And here, at another fire, Jesus doesn't bring up the denial, doesn't shame him, doesn't make him grovel. He just asks, gently, three times, "do you love me." One for each denial. And by the third "I love you," the wound is healed. That's how Jesus restores people who fail him.
- Land the question. And notice the question he asks. Not "how much do you know." Not "how sorry are you." Not "will you promise to be perfect now." Just, "do you love me." That's the whole thing. If you've failed him and you're afraid to come back, hear the only question he's asking you: do you love me. That's it.
- Land the job. And to every "yes," he says "feed my sheep." He doesn't just forgive Peter and send him off. He gives him something to do, people to love and care for. Your love for Jesus was always meant to turn into caring for the people he loves. Restored, and sent.
- Close the set. This is your sixteenth and last memory verse. You've carried the whole story of Jesus in these verses, from "the Word became flesh" to "it is finished" to "do you love me." And the last one is a question Jesus asks you, by a fire, after you've failed: do you love me.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Jesus heals the denier completely and hands him a flock, and asks each of us the one question that matters.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus' one question is 'do you love me.' Not how good you are, or how much you know. How do you honestly answer him today?"
- Give it quiet, and let it be tender. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 21:17, your last one. Read John 21:18-19 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 21:17 enters the rolling memory quizzes, completing the set of sixteen.
- Sets up L5 (short Thursday): Jesus tells Peter how he'll die, and says "follow me."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the plant AND the emotional climax of the unit. 21:17 is the sixteenth and FINAL anchor. Give it the full period, and let the room feel the whole set closing.
- Three things to land: the three questions healing the three denials (Ambrose, Augustine); "do you love me" as the one question that matters; and "feed my sheep" as love-in-service, the flock Christ's own (Augustine), Peter as under-shepherd fulfilling it in 1 Peter 5:2.
- RULE the agape/phileo word-swap as stylistic variation (John varies three synonym pairs in three verses; the ESV renders both "love"). Do NOT let the lesson build a doctrine on the Greek swap; the point is the threefold restoration. Head off the "higher love / lower love" misreading if a student raises it.
- Keep the whole scene grace, never shame. Christ dealt with Peter as a friend (Matthew Henry).
- Derive the frame from sermon 40; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block for the plant.
