**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will trace the man's growing confession to "he is a prophet" (9:17).
2. Students will explain the parents' fear and the cost of confessing Christ (9:22).
3. Students will explain how this man's response differs from the man healed at Bethesda (John 5).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the man keeps telling the truth even when it gets uncomfortable.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the parents heard Jesus' name and went quiet to protect themselves.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the man's climbing confession, and the contrast with the Bethesda man (John 5).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He is a prophet" (John 9:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I go quiet about my faith to avoid trouble?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest place you keep your head down.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 9:13-23 aloud, one reads 13 through 17, the other 18 through 23.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 9, verses 13 through 23.
- One-sentence setup: the leaders start interrogating the healed man, and then they call in his parents.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 17, the leaders ask the man what he thinks of Jesus. What does he say?
   - Cold-call check: "He is a prophet." Notice that's a bigger claim than his first answer ("the man called Jesus," verse 11). His confession is growing.
2. **Entry.** Have you ever gotten more sure of something the more people pushed back on it? What was it?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 18 through 23. The leaders call in the parents. What two things do the parents admit, and what question do they refuse to answer? Why?
   - Guide them: they admit it's their son and he was born blind. They refuse to say anything about Jesus, and they push the question back onto their son ("ask him, he's old enough"). Verse 22 tells us why: they were afraid of being thrown out of the synagogue for confessing Jesus.
4. **Core.** Getting "put out of the synagogue" meant losing your whole community, your worship, your neighbors. The parents heard Jesus' name and went silent to protect themselves. What did their fear cost, and what did it protect?
   - Land it: it protected their place in the community, and it cost them the chance to stand with Jesus and with their own son. Fear is human and understandable, and it still keeps people quiet about the most important thing.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Think back to the man Jesus healed at the pool in John 5. He was healed, asked nothing, and then went and reported Jesus to the authorities. This man is healed, pressured hard, and gets bolder about Jesus every time. What makes the difference between the two?
   - Draw out: both got a miracle from Jesus. One walked away and even turned him in. The other's confession grew under pressure. The difference is what grace grows into when the Spirit is really at work in someone. A miracle isn't the same as a changed heart.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why wouldn't the parents answer the question about Jesus?
   - They were afraid of being thrown out of the synagogue.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 14.

- Name the cost honestly. Confessing Jesus cost these people something real. For the parents it could mean losing their whole community. Following Jesus has always cost something, and pretending it doesn't isn't honest.
- Then hold up the man. He's a nobody, a former beggar with no training, and he keeps telling the truth about Jesus while the powerful people lean on him. His confidence isn't in his arguments. It's in what Jesus did for him.
- Speak to the quiet student, gently. Most of us have a version of the parents in us. We hear Jesus' name come up and we suddenly get very interested in our shoes. That fear is understandable, and Jesus is worth naming anyway. He named you before the Father.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is worth confessing, even when his name costs you your place.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The parents went quiet to protect themselves. Where do you go quiet about Jesus, and what would it look like to name him there?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 9:24-34 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: the leaders drag the man back in, and he gives them the one thing he knows for sure.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Track the growing confession. This is the second rung ("he is a prophet," 9:17). It started at "the man called Jesus" (9:11) and climbs to "Lord, I believe" (9:38) by the end. Name the pattern.
- Carry the anti-Jewish guard on 9:22. "The Jews" here names the hostile leadership who set the synagogue-expulsion policy. Jesus, the man, his parents, and the divided Pharisees (9:16) are all Jewish. The line measures response to Christ rather than ancestry, and it falls on the churched student too.
- Set the contrast with the Bethesda man (John 5) for the disciple tier. Both were healed. One reported Jesus, this one confesses him louder. Two shapes of grace received.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
