**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 14:6 and identify it as Jesus' sixth "I am" saying.
2. Students will explain what Thomas asks and how Jesus answers him (14:5-6).
3. Students will explain both halves of 14:6: the way is a Person, and it's the one way to the Father.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus says the way to God is himself.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): 14:6 spoken to comfort a scared, honest friend, not thrown as a slogan.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the sixth "I am" in John's building case; the exclusivity as the kindest news if it's true.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant, so give it the full period.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (John 14:6).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where in my life do I feel lost or unsure of the way?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: it's okay to write that you don't know.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:5-7 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 5 through 7.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus just said "you know the way," and Thomas honestly says he doesn't.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 5, what does Thomas admit? Read his exact words.
   - Cold-call check: "Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?" He admits he's lost.
2. **Entry.** In verse 6, Jesus answers. What does he say he is?
   - Draw out: the way, and the truth, and the life. And: no one comes to the Father except through him.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Thomas asks for directions, a "how." Jesus answers with a "who." What's the difference between being handed a map to a place and having a Person be the way?
   - Guide them: a map still leaves you to walk it and maybe get it wrong. A person who is the way carries you. Jesus doesn't point at the road. He is the road, and he takes you himself.
4. **Core.** Read the second half of verse 6: "No one comes to the Father except through me." People hear that as narrow and harsh. Flip it: if there really is one true way home, and it's open to anyone, is that bad news or good news?
   - Land it: one true way means there is a way, and it's not hidden or locked. The kindest thing you can tell a lost person is the actual way out. Cutting off the second half of the verse empties it; keeping it, warmly, is the whole comfort.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** This is Jesus' sixth "I am" saying in John. You've already met the bread (6:35), the light (8:12), the door (10:7) and the good shepherd (10:11), and the resurrection and the life (11:25). What is John building by stacking these up? What might the seventh and last one be?
   - Draw out: John is building a case for who Jesus is, one claim at a time. The next is the vine (15:5), coming next unit. Each "I am" is Jesus saying he's the thing people need most.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Recite verse 6 from memory. Then: who is Jesus talking to when he says it, and what mood is that person in?
   - 14:6, said to Thomas, who's scared and admits he's lost. It's comfort, not a weapon.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Set the scene before you say the verse. This isn't Jesus winning an argument. A frightened friend just said "I'm lost," and Jesus answered him as gently as you'd answer a scared kid: I am the way. Let me carry you.
- Say both halves and mean both. He is the way, the truth, and the life, and he's the only way to the Father. That second line isn't Jesus being small-minded. It's the best news a lost person could get: there's a way home, and here he is.
- Name the "I am." This is the fifth time Jesus has said "I am" the thing you need. Bread when you're starving, light when you're in the dark, a shepherd when you're lost, life when you're dead, and now the way when you don't know how to get home. He keeps being exactly what's missing.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the road to the Father, and the road is a Person who comes and gets you.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'No one comes to the Father except through me.' Is that good news or bad news to you right now? Write honestly, either way."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 14:6. Read John 14:8-11 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 14:6 enters the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up L3: Philip asks to see the Father, and Jesus says whoever has seen him has seen the Father.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the plant. 14:6 is the unit's new graded anchor. Teach both halves together and keep it tender, spoken to a scared friend named Thomas.
- Guard both failures: don't let it flatten into an intolerance slogan (cut Thomas and it becomes a weapon), and don't let it soften into "all roads are fine" (cut the second half and it collapses). The wider witness: Acts 4:12, Ephesians 2:18.
- Set 14:6 in the "I am" line (6:35, 8:12, chapter 10, 11:25, now 14:6, vine 15:5 next). It helps the disciple tier feel John's case building.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block for the plant.
