**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Thomas's demand for proof (20:25).
2. Students will explain how Jesus met Thomas's doubt (20:26-27).
3. Students will explain "My Lord and my God" as worshiping Jesus as God (20:28), and the blessing of 20:29.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus met a doubter with patience, not shame.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): honest doubt can lead to the deepest faith.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "My Lord and my God" as the deity climax, the 1:1 inclusio; the blessing over the reader.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is a rich scene; keep the doubter welcome.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's a real doubt or question I have about Jesus? Be honest, this is safe."
- On-ramp for the doubter: doubts are welcome here. Write the real one.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 20:24-29 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 24 through 29.
- One-sentence setup: Thomas wasn't there when Jesus appeared, and he's not taking anyone's word for it.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 25, the others tell Thomas they've seen the Lord. What does Thomas say he needs before he'll believe?
   - Cold-call check: he won't believe unless he sees the nail marks and puts his finger in them and his hand in Jesus' side. He wants hard proof.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Thomas doubts. Now watch how Jesus responds (verses 26-27). Does Jesus shame him, or lecture him, or write him off? What does Jesus do?
   - Guide them: eight days later Jesus comes back, and he offers Thomas the exact thing he asked for: "put your finger here, see my hands, put your hand in my side." No shame, no scolding. He meets Thomas's doubt with patience and with the very evidence Thomas demanded. Jesus takes the doubt seriously and answers it.
3. **Core.** Read verse 28. After all his doubting, what does Thomas say to Jesus? How big is that confession?
   - Land it: "My Lord and my God!" He goes from "I won't believe" to the highest words anyone says in the whole Gospel. He calls Jesus God, and worships him. The biggest doubter ends up making the biggest confession. His honest doubt, met by Jesus, became the deepest faith in the book.
4. **Core (the deity climax).** Thomas calls Jesus "my Lord and my God." Way back in John 1:1, the very first verse, John wrote "the Word was God." How does Thomas's confession bring the whole book full circle?
   - Draw out: the Gospel opens by telling you Jesus is God (1:1), and it closes with a man looking at the risen Jesus and saying it to his face: "my God." The whole book is bracketed by the deity of Christ. Everything in between, all the signs and "I am" sayings, was building to this: Jesus is God, and the right response is to worship him, like Thomas does.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 29: "blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed." Thomas got to see and touch. Who is Jesus blessing in that sentence?
   - Draw out: everyone who would believe without seeing, which is every Christian since that room, including everyone here. You weren't in that room. You didn't touch the wounds. You're asked to believe on the witness of people who did, like Thomas and John. And Jesus calls that faith "blessed." He's speaking a blessing across the centuries, right into this classroom.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how did Jesus respond to Thomas's doubt, and what did Thomas confess?
   - He met the doubt with patience and the exact proof asked for; Thomas confessed "My Lord and my God."

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Welcome the doubter. Notice what Jesus does NOT do to Thomas. He doesn't shame him, doesn't call him weak, doesn't kick him out. He comes back a week later just for him, and offers him the exact proof he asked for. If you carry honest doubts, hear this: Jesus is not scared of your questions, and he doesn't despise you for having them. He meets doubters with patience.
- Land the confession. And where does Thomas's doubt end up? At the highest words in the whole book: "my Lord and my God." The biggest doubter makes the biggest confession. Honest doubt, brought to Jesus instead of used as an exit, can become the deepest faith. Don't let anyone tell you that questions disqualify you.
- Land the blessing. And then Jesus turns and speaks to you. "Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed." That's you. You weren't in that room, and he's calling your faith blessed across two thousand years. He had this classroom in mind when he said it.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Jesus meets doubt with patience and receives the worship of the God he is, and he blesses everyone who believes without seeing.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus met Thomas's doubt with patience, and the doubter became the biggest believer. What honest doubt do you need to bring to Jesus instead of hiding?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 20:30-31 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L8 (short Thursday): why John wrote the whole book, and your very first memory verse comes home.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Three things to land: Jesus meeting doubt with patience (never shame); "My Lord and my God" as the deity climax and the 1:1 inclusio (the book opens and closes on Christ's deity); and the blessing of 20:29 spoken over the reader (the students themselves).
- GUARD hard against using Thomas to shame doubters. "Doubting Thomas" as an insult misses that Jesus met him gently and gave him the exact proof he asked for; the same Thomas carried the gospel as far as India. Keep the room safe for honest questions.
- Derive the frame from sermon 39 ("Peace Be With the Doubter"); cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
