**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state that the Holy Spirit is a Person and fully God, from the Farewell Discourse.
2. Students will describe the Spirit's work toward the church and toward the world.
3. Students will explain the through-line: the Spirit always glorifies Christ (16:14).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the Holy Spirit isn't the weird part; he's God with you, making Jesus real.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the Spirit is a Person with a job, not a feeling you chase or hardware you ignore.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Spirit fully God (the creeds), the same title parakletos for Christ and the Spirit (1 John 2:1), and why his self-effacement proves his Godhead.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is a gathering lesson. The students already studied all five sayings, so they supply the facts and you sort. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He will glorify me" (John 16:14).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I hear 'the Holy Spirit,' what do I picture? Be honest, even if it's nothing or something weird."
- On-ramp for the doubter: an honest blank is a fine answer.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Read the five sayings back to back, so the room hears them as one conversation. Assign five readers.
  1. John 14:16-17
  2. John 14:26
  3. John 15:26-27
  4. John 16:7-11
  5. John 16:12-15
- Navigation help: all in John chapters 14, 15, and 16, the talk on the last night.
- One-sentence setup: we've studied all five of these already, one at a time. Today we gather them and ask, what do they teach together about the Holy Spirit?

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, three passes)

This is synthesis, a different skill from working one passage. You gather what they already know, sort it, and follow it home to Christ. Do not re-teach the passages. Pull the facts out of the room.

### Pass 1: Gather (about 8 min)

- In John groups, assign each group one or two of the five sayings they already studied.
1. **Group task.** Read your saying(s) again. In your own words, list everything it tells us about the Holy Spirit. Who is he, and what does he do?
   - Groups report out. Write every answer on the board as a scatter of facts, no order yet. Expect things like: he's called the Helper, he's "another" Helper, he's the Spirit of truth, he lives in them forever, he teaches, he reminds, he bears witness, he convicts the world, he guides into all truth, he glorifies Jesus, he comes because Jesus goes.

### Pass 2: Sort (about 10 min)

- Now sort the messy board into five headings. Write the headings up, and cold-call students to place each fact under one.
2. **A. The Spirit is a Person.** Which facts show he's a "he," not an "it"? (He teaches, reminds, bears witness, convicts, guides, hears, speaks. Jesus calls him "he," 16:14.)
   - Drop in the grounding hook: the word for "Helper" (parakletos) is used for the Spirit here, and it's the exact same word John uses for Jesus in 1 John 2:1, "we have an advocate (parakletos) with the Father, Jesus Christ." Same title for the Son and the Spirit. So "another Helper" (14:16) means another of the same kind as Jesus.
3. **B. The Spirit is fully God.** What in these sayings puts him on God's side, not among created things? (He "proceeds from the Father," 15:26; everything the Father has is Christ's and the Spirit takes from it, 16:15; he's named with the Father and the Son.)
   - Drop in the grounding hook: the church has always confessed this. The Nicene Creed calls him "the Lord, the giver of life, who with the Father and the Son is worshiped and glorified." One God, three Persons.
4. **C. The Spirit is given and sent, and comes because Jesus goes.** (The Father gives him, 14:16; sends him in Jesus' name, 14:26; Jesus sends him from the Father, 15:26, 16:7. "It is to your advantage that I go away," 16:7. He's "with you forever," 14:16-17.)
5. **D. His work toward the church.** (Teaches and reminds, 14:26; guides into all truth, 16:13; bears witness to Christ, 15:26; gives peace and joy across the talk.)
6. **E. His work toward the world.** (Convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment, 16:8-11. You studied all three clauses last week.)

### Pass 3: Land the through-line (about 7 min)

7. **The one question that turns the list into a doctrine.** Look at everything on the board. All of it points somewhere. Where does the Spirit always send our eyes?
   - Draw them to 16:14: "he will glorify me... he will take what is mine and declare it to you." Every heading bends back to Jesus. The Spirit teaches about Christ, witnesses to Christ, applies Christ, and presses Christ's verdict on the world. Try to look straight at the Spirit, and you end up looking at Jesus.
8. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what is the Holy Spirit's main job, according to 16:14?
   - To glorify Christ, by taking what is his and making it known.

Circulate. Short probes. "Which saying is that from?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Kill the "weird part" fear. A lot of you walked in bracing for the strange chapter, the ghost, the goosebumps. Look at what Jesus actually promised. The Spirit is God himself, come to live in you and make Jesus real to you. That's not spooky. That's the best news in the whole talk.
- Name the difference that matters. A force is a battery you use and put down. A Person is Someone who knows you, stays, and won't leave. Jesus said the Spirit would be "with you forever." God moved in. You're not on your own.
- Follow the through-line home. Here's the tell that the Spirit is God: he never points to himself. His whole mission is to make Jesus bigger and clearer to you. So if anything ever claims to be the Spirit but pulls you away from Christ or around his word, it isn't him. The real Spirit always hands you Jesus.
- Christ connection to say out loud: God gave you his own Spirit, and the Spirit's whole work is to show you his Son.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Before today, what did you picture when you heard 'the Holy Spirit'? After gathering these five sayings, what's one thing you'd now say he actually does?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged, never the belief itself.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 17:1-5 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up Unit 23: Jesus stops talking to his disciples and starts praying to the Father. We enter the high-priestly prayer of John 17 (the next memory verse, 17:3).

## Teacher notes

- This is a synthesis lesson, a distinct skill from exposition, and it doubles as the model for the Q3 project ("Teaching the Farewell Discourse"). The move to teach: gather the threads the class already studied, sort them under clear headings, and land the whole on Christ, never leaving it as a list of facts.
- The five headings are the spine: Person, fully God, given and sent, work toward the church, work toward the world, and the through-line, he glorifies Christ (16:14).
- Grounding hooks for the stretch tier: parakletos names both Jesus (1 John 2:1) and the Spirit (so "another of the same kind"); the creeds confess him fully God (Nicene, Athanasian, Heidelberg Q53).
- Trinitarian care at this level: the Spirit is a Person (a "he," not an "it"), fully God, one God in three Persons. Teach 15:26's "proceeds from the Father" as sending and origin. Keep the East-West filioque debate closed; a curious student who presses gets a one-line pointer to a later conversation, off to the side. Opening it would sink the period.
- Guard the running course point: keep the Spirit a Person with a job, never vague, spooky, or a force, and never divorced from Christ and Scripture ("the Spirit told me" untethered from the word). If a student raises tongues or gifts, name it as a real question for another day and steer back.
- Don't let it end as a whiteboard of bullet points. Follow the through-line home to Christ every time.
- Review-only memory; no new verse. Fresh-build; no sermon.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
