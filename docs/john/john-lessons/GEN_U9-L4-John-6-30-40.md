**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain how Jesus corrects the crowd's manna claim (6:32-33).
2. Students will state what Jesus promises the one who comes and believes (6:35).
3. Students will explain the comfort of "whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (6:37).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus says he's the bread, and coming to him fills a hunger food can't reach.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "I will never cast out" takes the pressure off whether you're good enough to come.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the manna typology (Exodus 16); 6:37 as the first sovereign-grace text, grace-first.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson plants the new memory verse, so protect Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am the bread of life" (John 6:35).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I running to when I feel empty?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write what you wish could actually fill you.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 6:30-40 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 6, verses 30 through 40.
- One-sentence setup: the crowd brings up the manna their ancestors ate, and Jesus tells them the real bread from heaven is standing in front of them.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 31 the crowd says their ancestors ate manna in the desert. In verses 32 and 33, how does Jesus correct them about who gave it and what the real bread is?
   - Cold-call check: it wasn't Moses who gave it, the Father did. And the true bread from heaven is a person, the one who "comes down from heaven and gives life to the world."
2. **Entry.** The manna kept Israel alive in the desert, but they still eventually died. What's Jesus claiming to offer that's different?
   - Life that lasts. Bread that satisfies forever.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 35, slowly. What does Jesus call himself? Notice the two promises. What happens to the person who "comes" to him? What happens to the one who "believes"?
   - Guide them: "I am the bread of life." Whoever comes will not hunger, whoever believes will never thirst. Point out that "coming" and "believing" are the same move, and "not hunger" and "never thirst" are the same promise. It's one claim said twice: come to Jesus and be filled forever.
4. **Core.** Read verse 37: "whoever comes to me I will never cast out." Someone worried they're not good enough for Jesus reads that. What does it tell them?
   - Land it: no one who comes gets turned away. Ever. The door doesn't have a bar high enough to keep out anyone who actually comes. And notice the first half: your coming is a gift the Father gives. So it doesn't rest on your grip.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 37 and verse 39 say the Father gives people to the Son, and the Son loses none of them and raises them up. How is "God chose to give you to Jesus" the safest thing you could hear, not the scariest?
   - Draw out: if your coming to Jesus started with the Father's gift, then staying with Jesus doesn't depend on your strength either. He loses none. Your security rests on him, not on you.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Say verse 35 back in your own words. What does Jesus promise?
   - He's the bread of life. Come to him and believe, and you're filled forever.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit) and plant the memory verse

Bring it to Christ and plant the new verse. This is the heart of sermon 08.

- Plant John 6:35 as the unit's new memory verse. Put it on the board and say it twice: "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst."
- Teach it as one claim said twice. Coming to Jesus is believing in him. Never hungering is never thirsting. It's the promise of being filled all the way, forever, by a person.
- Lead with grace. The bread is given (verse 32), and even your coming is the Father's gift (verse 37). Nobody earns this bread. You receive it, the way the crowd received loaves they didn't grow. Tie it back to 3:16 (the love that gave the Son) and 20:31 (that by believing you may have life).
- Name the hunger in the room. Everyone is trying to fill something with screens, approval, winning, being liked. Jesus says he's the only bread that reaches that deep. Come to him and stop starving.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the bread of life, and coming to him fills a hunger nothing else touches.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says he satisfies a hunger nothing else can. What have you been feeding that hunger with, and what would it look like to bring it to him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 6:35. Read John 6:41-51 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 6:35 joins the rolling memory-verse quizzes going forward.
- Sets up L5: the crowd grumbles, and Jesus says their coming to him is the Father's own work.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Plant 6:35 inside the flow that produces it, in Move 4, after the manna correction. Don't bolt it on. The verse makes sense because the crowd just asked for bread.
- Teach the manna typology plainly (Exodus 16; the manna kept them alive for a while, Jesus gives life forever). Give the disciple tier the Exodus backing.
- Introduce 6:37 grace-first, leading with the comfort (never cast out) before the security (your coming is the Father's gift). This is the first of three sovereign-grace texts in the discourse. The next two come in L5 and L7.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. Protect Move 4, where the memory verse plants.
