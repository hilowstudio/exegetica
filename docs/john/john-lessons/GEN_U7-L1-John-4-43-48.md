**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what the Galileans' welcome was based on (4:45) and why that's shallow.
2. Students will identify the official and what he asks of Jesus (4:46-47).
3. Students will explain what Jesus means by "unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe" (4:48), and who he's addressing.
4. Students will connect the sign-based welcome (4:45) to 2:23-25.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): everyone knows the difference between fans and real friends. The crowd here are fans.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): it's possible to love the Jesus stories and never actually trust him. This crowd is the warning.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the plural "you" in 4:48; the tie to 2:23-25 where Jesus "did not entrust himself" to sign-believers.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe" (John 4:48).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What kind of proof do I want from God before I'll trust him?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the proof you wish you had. Be honest about it.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 4:43-48 aloud, one reads 43 through 45, the other 46 through 48.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 4, verses 43 through 48.
- Set the scene in one sentence: Jesus comes home to Galilee, and a desperate father from Capernaum comes looking for him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Look at verse 45. Why do the Galileans welcome Jesus? What had they seen him do?
   - Cold-call check: they'd seen "all that he had done in Jerusalem at the feast." Their welcome runs on the miracles.
2. **Entry.** What's the difference between a fan of somebody and a real friend of theirs? Name it.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 46 and 47. Who comes to Jesus, and what does he ask? Write down his job and what's happening to his son.
   - Guide them: a royal official from Herod's court, a man with power, and his son is dying. He begs Jesus to come down to Capernaum.
4. **Core.** In verse 48 Jesus answers, "Unless you see signs and wonders you will not believe." Look closely: is he only talking to the dad, or to a bigger group? What's he pointing at?
   - Land it: the "you" is plural in the original, so Jesus speaks past the man to the whole crowd. He's naming a faith that only works when there's a show to watch.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Turn back to John 2:23-25. Many people "believed" when they saw Jesus' signs, and Jesus "did not entrust himself to them." How does that help you understand the welcome he gets in 4:45?
   - Draw out: Jesus knows the difference between being impressed and trusting him. A crowd can cheer the miracles and still not hand him their lives.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence, what does this crowd want from Jesus, and what is Jesus asking them for instead?
   - They want a show. He wants their trust in him.

Circulate while groups work. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Derive from the first half of sermon 05.

- Name what Jesus does on purpose. He goes home, knowing a prophet gets no honor in his hometown (4:44). He walks straight into a welcome that treats him as the local boy who does wonders. The sermon calls this the danger of "hometown folks," people who shrink Jesus down to a mascot they're comfortable with.
- Say the warning plainly to the churched student. You can grow up on the Jesus stories, love the miracles, and never once give him the honor due the Son of God. The Samaritan outsiders just called him "Savior of the world" (4:42). His own hometown gives him applause.
- Frame the rebuke in verse 48 as mercy. He's reaching for this dad and the whole crowd, to lift them off needing a show and onto trusting him. Watch how it works next class.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus serves the Father's will, refuses to be your hometown mascot, and offers you himself, which is better than any show.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The crowd wanted proof before they'd trust Jesus. Where do you do the same thing? What would it look like to trust him without the proof first?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 4:49-54 once before Monday. That's the healing.
- To the gradebook: today's participation (Quiet-Time Journal and the Move 5 response).
- Sets up L2: the dad is about to take Jesus at his word and walk home on it.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 05's first half: the hometown danger and the sign-hunger diagnostic. The sermon's central warning is about domesticating Jesus, shrinking him to something safe and local. Let that land.
- Handle 4:48 with care so it never becomes "asking God for help is unbelief." Jesus grants the request next class. The rebuke targets a faith that needs spectacle.
- This lesson ends on a cliffhanger on purpose. Leave the dad mid-story so students come back Monday for the healing.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block. If Move 3 runs long, trim the Deeper question first, keep the cold-call checks.
