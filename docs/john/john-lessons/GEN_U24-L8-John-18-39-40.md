**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the choice of Barabbas over Jesus (18:39-40).
2. Students will explain what kind of man Barabbas was.
3. Students will explain the Barabbas swap as a picture of substitution.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a guilty man went free because Jesus took his place.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the Barabbas swap is the gospel in miniature.
- **Stretch** (disciple): substitution grounded in the text (Acts 3:14; Isaiah 53:6).

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep your sustained voice light. Land the whole unit on the substitution.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Not this man, but Barabbas" (John 18:40).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Have I ever gotten away with something someone else paid for?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 18:39-40 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 39 through 40.
- One-sentence setup: Pilate offers to release one prisoner for the feast, and the crowd makes its choice.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: the Barabbas swap. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 39, what does Pilate offer? In verse 40, who does the crowd pick instead of Jesus?
   - Cold-call check: Pilate offers to release one prisoner for the Passover; the crowd picks Barabbas.
2. **Entry.** What kind of man was Barabbas (verse 40, and the other Gospels)?
   - Draw out: a robber, a violent man, an insurrectionist, and Luke and Mark tell us a murderer. A genuinely guilty, dangerous criminal.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Now watch the swap carefully. Barabbas is guilty and deserves death. Jesus is innocent (Pilate just said so). What happens to each of them?
   - Guide them: Barabbas, the guilty one, walks out free. Jesus, the innocent one, is sent to die. The guilty man goes home, and the innocent man takes his death sentence. They literally trade places.
4. **Core (the key question).** This isn't just a sad twist in the story. It's a picture of the whole gospel. How is Barabbas going free because Jesus takes his place a picture of exactly what Jesus does for you?
   - Land it: that's the gospel. You're Barabbas. You're the guilty one who deserves the sentence. And Jesus, the innocent one, takes your place and dies the death you earned, so you can walk out free. Barabbas is the first person in history to have Jesus die literally in his place, and he's a picture of every single one of us. The innocent for the guilty. That's the cross.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what happened to Barabbas, and what does it picture?
   - The guilty man went free because Jesus took his place, a picture of the cross for us.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ, close the unit, and open the door to the cross.

- Put them in the scene. There's a real man named Barabbas in a cell that morning. Guilty. Violent. A murderer. Waiting to die for what he did. And then he hears a crowd chanting his name, the door opens, and he walks out free. Somebody else is going to the cross that was built for him. That somebody is Jesus.
- Make the swap personal. Here's what I don't want you to miss. You are Barabbas. Every one of us is. We're the guilty ones who earned the sentence. And Jesus, innocent, with no crime of his own, steps into our place and takes the death we deserved, so we can walk out free. That's not a nice idea. That's what actually happened, and it's what the whole cross is about.
- Open the door to next unit. We've watched Jesus arrested, tried, and condemned in the place of a guilty man. Next, we follow him to the cross itself, where he finishes it. Come ready for the holiest ground in the book.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the innocent Jesus was condemned so the guilty could go free, and you are the one who walks out because he took your place.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "You are Barabbas: the guilty one who walks free because Jesus took your place. How do you respond to a Savior who did that for you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 19:1-16 once before next class. The next unit plants a new memory verse (19:30).
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up Unit 25: the cross. Jesus is crucified, and says, "It is finished."

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One idea, taught well, closing the whole unit: the Barabbas substitution.
- The heart of the lesson: substitution. The guilty (Barabbas, and us) goes free because the innocent (Jesus) takes his place. Ground it in Acts 3:14 (Peter's later charge) and Isaiah 53:6. Make it personal: "you are Barabbas."
- Do not let Barabbas pass as a plot detail. This is the gospel in miniature and the natural door into the cross (Unit 25).
- Anti-Judaism guard: the crowd's cry indicts a moment of human sin (and all of ours), never a people. Keep it there.
- Derive the frame from sermon 34; cite it, don't read it aloud.
