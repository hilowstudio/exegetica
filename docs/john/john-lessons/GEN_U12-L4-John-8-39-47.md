# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 12 Lesson 4 of 5: whose child are you really? (John 8:39-47)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U12 Light of the world |

| Date / Day type | Thu Nov 19 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus shape |
| Passage | John 8:39-47 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus says a person's real father shows in who they act like and obey, and only a new birth from God gives you a new Father. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the truth-speaker sent from the Father, and coming to him is how you become a child of God. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain Jesus' point that real children of Abraham would do Abraham's works (8:39-40).
2. Students will explain what "father" means in this passage (who you act like and obey, the heart's allegiance).
3. Students will explain how a person gets a new Father (new birth, John 1:12-13; 3:3).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): who you really belong to shows up in how you live.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): claiming the right family name is not the same as belonging to God.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the two fatherhoods by likeness, and why this text has been wrongly weaponized.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Whoever is of God hears the words of God" (John 8:47).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When God's word is hard, what do I do with it?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 8:39-47 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 8, verses 39 through 47.
- One-sentence setup, said carefully: this is a sharp, hard part of the argument. It's about the heart and who people belong to. It has nothing to do with anyone's ancestry or ethnicity.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question. Handle this passage with care.

1. **Read-check.** In verse 39, the people say "Abraham is our father." What does Jesus say real children of Abraham would be doing?
   - They'd "be doing the works Abraham did." Real family shows up in how you act, not just the name you claim.
2. **Read-check.** In verse 44 Jesus says something shocking, that their father is the devil. What's the one thing in this scene he points to as proof of that?
   - Their desire to kill him (verses 40, 44). Wanting to murder the one who tells them God's truth shows whose character they're mirroring.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus says whose "child" you are shows in who you act like and obey. He's not talking about anyone's family tree or nationality. He's talking about the heart. Work this: what does Jesus mean that a person's real "father" is shown by their life? And if you can't make yourself into God's child, how does a person get a new Father?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out on the meaning: "father" here means who you resemble and follow. By nature everyone is a slave to sin (from last lesson) and doesn't look like God. The test in this scene is whether you'll hear and receive Jesus. Whoever is of God hears God's words (verse 47).
   - Draw out on the new Father: you can't upgrade your own heart. You get a new Father the way John already taught, by being born again, born of God (look back at John 1:12-13 and John 3:3). God gives you a new birth and makes you his child.
   - For the disciple tier, name the history plainly: this verse has been twisted for centuries to justify hating Jewish people, and that use gets it exactly backwards. Jesus is Jewish, everyone in the room is Jewish, and some of them had just believed him (verse 30-31). The verse is about hearts that reject Christ, and it lands on any churched person who knows the words and will not come, in this room included.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 13.

- Say plainly what the passage means. It's about hearts, allegiance, and who you resemble. It has nothing to do with someone's ancestry or ethnicity. Jesus and everyone he's talking to are Jewish, and some had just believed him. When people have used this verse to hate Jewish people, they've done the opposite of what it teaches.
- Name the real question underneath. Jesus says whose child you are shows up in whether you'll receive him. Not the family you were born into, not the church you grew up in. Whether your heart hears and comes.
- Then the hope, and it's the hope of this whole course. You can't fix your own heart or pick a new father by trying harder. God gives a new birth. He makes slaves into sons and daughters. If your heart wants to come to Jesus right now, that wanting is God at work in you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus tells the truth from the Father, and coming to him is how you become a child of God.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says whose child you are shows in whether you'll hear him. When Jesus says something hard, what do you do with it, and what does that tell you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 8:48-59 once before Friday. That's the big climax.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: Jesus makes his biggest claim yet, and the crowd reaches for stones.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- This is the most sensitive passage in the unit. The anti-Jewish guard is built into the exegesis, not bolted on: "father" here means likeness and allegiance (who you resemble and obey), tested in the scene by the will to kill Jesus. Jesus, every hearer, and the "many who believed" (8:30-31) are Jewish. The line measures hearts by response to Christ rather than ancestry, so it falls on the churched student too. Name the historical weaponization for the disciple tier and rule it a contradiction of the verse.
- Turn the fatherhood question into the new-birth question from Units 1 and 4. You don't earn a new Father. God gives a new birth (1:12-13; 3:3).
- Isolating this scene on the Thursday is on purpose. It keeps 8:44 from being rushed at the tail of a packed period. Give it room and care.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. Protect Move 4.
