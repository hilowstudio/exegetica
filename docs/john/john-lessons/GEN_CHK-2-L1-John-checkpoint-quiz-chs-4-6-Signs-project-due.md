**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will write the six held memory anchors from memory (20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16, 4:24, 6:35).
2. Students will recall the christotelic heart of each of chapters 4 through 6 (living water and true worship, the Son's life-giving authority, the bread of life).

---

**Talk budget:** short day. This is a checkpoint and a graded quiz plus collecting the projects, not new teaching. Keep your voice light, warm, and celebratory, and let the students do the recalling and the writing. Leave the board line up the whole period: "Jesus goes to the outsider and gives them himself."

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (3-4 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger" (John 6:35).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Out of chapters 4 through 6, what's one thing about Jesus I don't want to forget?"

## Move 2: Recap chapters 4 through 6 (7-8 min)

Quick and warm. Ask, don't lecture. Walk the three chapters in one sweep and let the students supply the answers and the anchors.

1. **John 4, the woman at the well.** Ask: "Jesus sits down tired at a well and asks a shamed outsider for a drink. What does he offer her, and what does he tell her about worship?" (Living water, the Spirit welling up to eternal life, 4:10-14. And that worship is no longer bound to a mountain or a temple; the Father seeks worship in spirit and truth, 4:23-24. He tells her plainly, "I who speak to you am he," 4:26.) Lands on: Christ the Savior of the world, who knows everything she ever did and stays. Anchor: 4:24 (God is spirit, worship in spirit and truth).
2. **John 5, the healing at Bethesda and the Son's authority.** Ask: "A man has been down thirty-eight years and no one helps him. Jesus heals him on the Sabbath on purpose. When they come to kill him, what does he claim?" (The Son does whatever the Father does, holds the Father's own power to give life and to judge, and is owed the honor owed to God, 5:19-23. Whoever hears his word and believes has already passed from death to life, 5:24.) Lands on: Christ the Son, equal with the Father, who raises the dead to life now by his word.
3. **John 6, the feeding and the bread of life.** Ask: "Jesus feeds thousands from a boy's lunch, then walks to his friends across the sea saying 'It is I.' What does he tell the crowd that chases him down for more bread?" (That he himself is the true bread from heaven, the food that endures; whoever comes to him never hungers and whoever believes never thirsts, 6:35. And no one comes unless the Father draws him, 6:37 and 6:44.) Lands on: Christ the bread of life, the true manna, given for the life of the world. Anchor: 6:35 (I am the bread of life).

Say one sentence: three chapters, one widening offer. Jesus goes to the outsider, the helpless, and the hungry, and to each one he gives himself.

## Move 3: Checkpoint quiz (13-15 min, graded)

Phones and Bibles away. This feeds the gradebook. Two parts. Run it tight on the short day.

**Part A, the six held anchors from memory (the weight of the grade).** Put the references on the board and have students write each from memory, in the ESV:
1. John 20:31
2. John 1:14
3. John 1:12
4. John 3:16
5. John 4:24
6. John 6:35

Grade for the sense and the load-bearing phrases, not word-perfect punctuation. A student who has the meaning and the main wording has it. The verse texts are in the Answer Key below.

**Part B, content recall (short, one per chapter).** Students answer briefly in writing:
7. John 4: The woman tries to start an argument about the right place to worship, this mountain or Jerusalem (4:20). How does Jesus answer, and what reason does 4:24 give for worship not being tied to a place?
8. John 5: Jesus tells the most careful Bible readers alive that they "search the Scriptures" and still miss something (5:39-40). What do they miss, and who did Jesus say wrote about him (5:46)?
9. John 6: The crowd rows across the lake to find Jesus after the free bread. In 6:26 Jesus names why they are really looking for him. What is his diagnosis, and what does he offer instead (6:27, 6:35)?

Model answers are in the Answer Key.

## Move 4: Land and minister, and collect the projects (6-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and collect the Q1 projects. Off the grade, warm.

- The closing word over the room. For three chapters you've watched Jesus walk straight past the people who thought they were owed him and go to the ones who knew they had nothing: a Samaritan woman with five failed marriages, a man who had been on his mat for thirty-eight years, a hungry crowd. To each one he gives himself. Living water. Life by his word. Bread from heaven. And every gift is really the same gift, which is him. Whoever comes to him will never be cast out (6:37).

- Collect and frame the project. As "The Signs" projects come in, name what the assignment actually was. John wrote his whole book so people would meet Christ through his signs and have life in his name (20:31). A student who takes one sign and shows a friend why it reveals who Jesus is has done the very thing John did. Say it plainly over the room: a magic trick asks you to admire the magician, and a sign asks you to look through the miracle and see God come to give life. Your project was to open that window for someone who has never looked through it.

- Christ connection to say out loud: every sign you studied is a window on the same person, the one who came to the outsider, the helpless, and the hungry, and gave them himself.

## Move 5: Respond (short, graded participation)

- One line in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Which of these three encounters (the woman, the invalid, the crowd) do you see yourself in, and why?"
- A short blessing over the room closes the checkpoint well.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 7:1-24 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's checkpoint quiz (content), the Q1 project, and the participation line. The six anchors stay in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 10: the Feast of Booths, where the crowds argue over who Jesus is and he stands up on the last day and cries out about rivers of living water.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: this is a scheduled checkpoint on a short Thursday. It's a graded quiz plus a warm recap plus collecting the projects, not new exposition. The chapter 7 exposition begins next unit.
- Keep the recap a fast, warm sweep. Ask the three questions and let students supply the answers and the anchors. Cap your voice; this is their recall.
- Grade the memory quiz for sense and key phrases, never word-perfection. Reward the load-bearing phrase in each: "life in his name," "the Word became flesh," "children of God," "God so loved the world," "worship in spirit and truth," "I am the bread of life."
- Content questions (Part B) are recall and close reading of what was taught, answerable by every tier, not new reasoning. Watch three guards. On question 7, keep true worship from flattening into "just be sincere": it is Spirit-enabled and shaped by the truth, who is finally Christ himself. On question 8, the point is that you can know the Book and miss the Lord of the Book. On question 9, name the diagnosis honestly: they chased the bread-maker for more bread, and he offers himself instead.
- The five signs available for the project, so you can field questions as work comes in: Sign 1 water into wine (2:1-11), Sign 2 the official's son (4:46-54), Sign 3 the invalid at Bethesda (5:1-9), Sign 4 the feeding of the five thousand (6:1-15), Sign 5 walking on the water (6:16-21). The two still ahead, the man born blind (chapter 9) and Lazarus (chapter 11), were not in bounds, since students had not yet read them.
- Grade the project on the christological point the prompt asks for, "what it proves about who Jesus is," not the craft. Confirm each student chose a sign from the five they had actually read.
- Two pitfalls to keep off the recap. Do not let the healing signs become "have enough faith and God heals": the official came with weak, presence-bound faith and a rebuke, and Christ healed anyway, so the power is in his word. And do not let 5:14 turn into a sin-and-suffering scoreboard for a sick teenager; Jesus himself blocks that reading in chapter 9.
- Keep it celebratory and low-anxiety. The projects are due, so the room already carries nerves. Frame the quiz as "show me what you carry."
- Keep the christotelic thesis central so the quiz doesn't flatten three tender chapters into trivia. The facts serve the person. Leave the board line up all period and end on the pastoral word.
- Content is gradable; belief is invitational. A student can write all six verses and answer all three questions without being asked to profess. Keep it that way.
- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes.

## Answer Key

**Part A, the memory anchors (ESV). Grade for sense and key wording.**

1. **John 20:31**: "but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name."
2. **John 1:14**: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."
3. **John 1:12**: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God,"
4. **John 3:16**: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."
5. **John 4:24**: "God is spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and truth."
6. **John 6:35**: "Jesus said to them, 'I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst.'"

**Part B, content recall. Model answers; accept any response that carries the sense.**

7. **John 4 (true worship, 4:24):** Jesus answers that the hour has come to worship the Father in spirit and truth. Because God is spirit, he is not trapped in a building or on a mountain, so the worship that fits him is Spirit-enabled and shaped by the truth, which is finally Christ himself. Guard against flattening it into "just be sincere."
8. **John 5 (searching the Scriptures, 5:39-40, 46):** They search the Scriptures for eternal life yet refuse to come to the One the Scriptures are about. Jesus says Moses wrote of him. It is possible to know the Book and miss the Lord of the Book.
9. **John 6 (the crowd's real motive, 6:26):** They seek him because they ate their fill, chasing the bread-maker for more bread. He offers the food that endures to eternal life, himself, the bread of life, received by coming to him and believing (6:27, 6:35).
