**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "As the Father has sent me, so I send you" (20:21).
2. Students will explain "Receive the Holy Spirit" and the breathing (20:22).
3. Students will explain 20:23 as the church announcing the gospel's forgiveness.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus sends ordinary followers to carry his message.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the church is sent, with the Spirit, to announce forgiveness.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the "breathed" of Genesis 2:7 as new-creation life; 20:23 guarded against priestly absolution.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "As the Father has sent me, even so I am sending you" (John 20:21).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Do I think of following Jesus as being sent to do something? What would that mean?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 20:21-23 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 21 through 23.
- One-sentence setup: after "peace," Jesus turns the frightened disciples into a sent people.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 21, what does Jesus say he's doing to the disciples? What's the comparison?
   - Cold-call check: he's sending them, the same way the Father sent him. "As the Father has sent me, so I send you."
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus just met these disciples in their fear and failure with peace. Now he sends them out. What does it tell you that he turns hiding, failing people into his sent messengers?
   - Guide them: he doesn't wait for them to become impressive first. He forgives them, gives them peace, and sends them, as they are. The people he sends to carry his message are ordinary, formerly failing people. Which means being useful to Jesus isn't about being flawless; it's about being his and being sent.
3. **Core.** Read verse 22. Jesus "breathed on them" and said, "Receive the Holy Spirit." That word "breathed" only shows up one other famous place: Genesis 2:7, where God breathed life into Adam. What is the risen Jesus doing to his church?
   - Land it: he's breathing new life into them, new-creation life. Just as God breathed life into the first human, the risen Jesus breathes his Spirit's life into his church. The church is a kind of new creation, alive with his Spirit. (This is a first installment; the full outpouring comes at Pentecost, but the risen Lord is already breathing his life into them.)
4. **Core.** Read verse 23. This sounds like Jesus giving them power to forgive sins. But it's not about priests handing out pardons on their own. It's about the church announcing the forgiveness the gospel carries. How does that work?
   - Draw out: when the church tells someone the good news and they believe it, that person's sins really are forgiven, because Jesus paid for them. When someone rejects the gospel, their sins stay unforgiven. The church doesn't invent forgiveness or grant it on its own authority. It announces the forgiveness Jesus already won, and it's real for everyone who believes. Ordinary Christians get to tell people, truthfully, "your sins are forgiven in Christ."
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Put the three verses together: sent (21), given the Spirit (22), and handed the message of forgiveness (23). What's the job description of the church, and where do you fit in it?
   - Draw out: the church is sent, like Jesus was, empowered by his Spirit, to carry the message that sins are forgiven in Christ. That's the mission, and it belongs to every believer, not just pastors. You're included. If you're his, you're sent, with his Spirit, to tell people the best news there is.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does Jesus give the disciples in verse 22, and what does he send them to do?
   - His Spirit, and he sends them (like the Father sent him) to announce forgiveness in his name.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the sending. The risen Jesus doesn't just comfort his friends and leave them hiding. He turns them into a sent people: "as the Father sent me, so I send you." And these are the guys who ran. He forgives them, breathes his Spirit into them, and hands them the mission. He does the same with ordinary people like you.
- Land the breath. And that word, "breathed," is huge. It's the word from Genesis, when God breathed life into the first human. The risen Jesus is breathing new life into his church, making a new creation. If you're his, his Spirit is your life. You're not running on your own fumes.
- Land the message. And the message he sends you with is the best one there is: your sins can be forgiven, in Christ, for real. You get to tell people that. Not "clean up your act," but "the debt is paid, come and be forgiven." That's the news the whole world needs, and he's put it on your lips.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Lord breathes his Spirit into his people and sends them out to announce that sins are forgiven in his name.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus sends ordinary, forgiven people to tell others their sins can be forgiven. Who in your life needs to hear that, and what holds you back from telling them?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- To the gradebook: today's participation.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Three things to land: the church sent as the Father sent the Son (20:21); the "breathed" of 20:22 as new-creation life (Genesis 2:7; Ezekiel 37:9), a first installment ahead of Pentecost; and 20:23 as the church announcing the gospel's forgiveness.
- GUARD 20:23 against a priest-grants-absolution over-reading. The gospel forgives; the church announces it (Heidelberg Q84). Ordinary believers carry this, not just clergy.
- Derive the frame from sermon 39; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
