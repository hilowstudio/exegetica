# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 15 Lesson 3 of 5: Jesus wept (John 11:28-37)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U15 Sign 7 (Lazarus) |
| Lesson | 3 of 5 |
| Date / Day type | Tue Dec 15 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 11:28-37 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus meets Mary's grief, and though he's about to raise Lazarus, he weeps at the grave and burns with anger at death. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the God who enters our grief with real tears and hates death enough to defeat it. |
| Memory verse | John 11:25 was planted last lesson. Review it, do not re-plant. Keep the eight earlier anchors sharp too. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega, "The Death of a Friend" (John 11:1-35). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Mary's response to Jesus (11:32).
2. Students will explain the two things Jesus feels at the grave (11:33 and 11:35).
3. Students will explain what the tears and the anger together show about Jesus.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus cries at the grave, even though he's about to fix everything.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus doesn't rush past your grief on his way to the miracle.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "deeply moved" (anger at death) held together with real, quiet tears.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Jesus wept" (John 11:35).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When was the last time I cried, and did I feel alone in it?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly. A God who cries is worth knowing about.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 11:28-37 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 11, verses 28 through 37.
- One-sentence setup: now it's Mary's turn to come to Jesus, and everyone around her is weeping.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 32, what does Mary do when she reaches Jesus, and what does she say?
   - Cold-call check: she falls at his feet and says the same thing Martha did, "Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died." Same grief, different sister.
2. **Entry.** Verse 35 is the shortest verse in the whole Bible: "Jesus wept." Jesus knows he's about to raise Lazarus in a few minutes. So why does he cry?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 33 and verse 35 together. Jesus feels two things at the grave. He's "deeply moved" and "greatly troubled," and he weeps. What do these two things, together, tell you about who Jesus is?
   - Guide them: he's not a distant God watching from far off. He steps into the grief and cries real tears. And "deeply moved" is a strong word. It carries anger. He's furious at death itself, the thing that wrecks God's world and makes people cry. He weeps with the grievers and burns at the enemy that made them grieve.
4. **Core.** Here's the key: who or what is Jesus angry at? He's not annoyed at the people for crying. So what is he raging against?
   - Land it: death. Sin and death and everything broken that turns God's good world into a place full of tombs. He's not scolding anyone for their tears. He's angry at the enemy, and in a few minutes he's going to shout it down.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In the original language, the crowd's weeping (verse 33) is loud wailing, and Jesus' weeping (verse 35) is a different word, quiet tears in the eyes. It's the only time that word gets used in the New Testament. Why might that detail matter?
   - Draw out: Jesus doesn't put on a show of grief. He sheds quiet, real tears. This is the true humanity of God the Son. He genuinely feels it. God is not too big or too far away to cry over you.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what two things is Jesus feeling at Lazarus's grave?
   - He's grieving with real tears, and he's angry at death.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 16, and go slow.

- Sit on verse 35 for a second. The God of the universe stands at a grave and cries. He's minutes away from fixing the whole thing, and he still weeps. That means your grief isn't a problem to rush past. He enters it with you.
- Name the anger, because it's good news. Jesus isn't calm and detached about death. He hates it. Death was never supposed to be part of the world, and it makes him furious. So when you stand at a grave and something in you says "this is wrong," that instinct is right, and Jesus feels it too.
- Hold both together. He cries with you, and he's angry at the thing that hurt you, and he has the power to beat it. A God who only cried would be sad company. A God who only fixed things would feel cold. Jesus does both.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the God who weeps with you at the grave and hates death enough to defeat it.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus cried at a grave and hated death. Where do you need a God who enters your sadness instead of rushing you past it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged. Be ready for real emotion.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 11:38-44 once before Wednesday. That's the raising.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: Jesus stands at the tomb and calls Lazarus out.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Hold the tears and the anger together, grounded in the words. "Deeply moved" (embrimaomai) carries anger or indignation. The object of the anger is death itself, the whole regime of sin and death, never the mourners for grieving.
- The sharpest detail for the disciple tier: the crowd wails (klaio), Jesus sheds quiet tears (dakruo, its only use in the New Testament). Jesus feels it genuinely and doesn't perform it.
- Guard both errors: don't sentimentalize "Jesus wept" into a soft greeting-card Jesus, and don't flatten the anger out. And never read the anger as anger at the mourners' unbelief.
- Be ready for grief in the room. This lesson is tender. Don't rush Move 4 or Move 5.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
