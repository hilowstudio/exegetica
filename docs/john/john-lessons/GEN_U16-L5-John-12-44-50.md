**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus says he came to do (12:46-47).
2. Students will explain what will judge a person on the last day (12:48).
3. Students will explain how "I came to save, not to judge" fits with "my word will judge."

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus' last public words are an open invitation to come.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): to believe in Jesus is to believe in the Father who sent him.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the two comings, the Lamb who saves now and the Judge who returns.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I did not come to judge the world but to save the world" (John 12:47).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If Jesus were saying his last words to me, would I be listening?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 12:44-50 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 12, verses 44 through 50.
- One-sentence setup: these are the very last words Jesus speaks to the public in the whole book. After this, he's only with his disciples.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verses 44 and 45, what does Jesus say happens when you believe in him, or see him?
   - You're believing in and seeing the Father who sent him. To have Jesus is to have God.
2. **Read-check.** In verse 47, what does Jesus say he came to do? In verse 48, what will judge a person on the last day?
   - He came to save the world, not to judge it. And the word he has spoken will judge those who reject it, on the last day.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** These are Jesus' final public words in the whole Gospel. Work two things. First, how do "I came to save, not to condemn" (verse 47) and "my word will judge you on the last day" (verse 48) fit together? Second, if these are the last things Jesus says to the crowds, what's the one thing he most wants them to know?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. On the two ideas: think of two comings. Jesus came the first time as the Lamb, to save, and the door of mercy is wide open now. He comes back as the Judge, and the same word that offers rescue now will be the standard people are measured by then. Both are true, in that order.
   - On the last words: he most wants them to know the door is still open. He came as light so nobody has to stay in the dark. He came to save. His last public breath is an invitation, and it's still going out.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. Build on sermon 22.

- Frame the moment. These are the last words Jesus says to the crowds in the entire book. After this, he goes quiet to them and turns to his disciples. When someone's about to stop speaking, their last words carry weight. Listen.
- Give them the door. Jesus doesn't end his public ministry with a threat. He ends it with an open door. "I came to save the world." "I came as light, so no one who believes in me stays in the dark." Sermon 22's point: darkness is where everybody starts, and the light came to you because you couldn't light yourself.
- Hold the honesty too. He also says his word will judge on the last day. Mercy's door is open now, and it won't stay open forever. He came the first time to save. He comes back to judge. The kind thing is to walk through the open door while it's open.
- Close the unit. This ends the first half of John, the Book of Signs. Everything pointed to this King who came to die and to save. And his last public word is "come."
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the light who came to save, and his last public word to the world is an open door.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus' last public words are an open door. What would it look like to walk through it, honestly?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing your nine memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up Unit 17: a short bridge as we cross from the Book of Signs into the second half of John, where Jesus is alone with his disciples on the night before he dies.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- Build on sermon 22. This is a self-contained final appeal with no setting, because John means it for everyone, including the student in the room. Its two-comings frame holds together "came to save" and "the word will judge."
- Keep "remain in darkness" (12:46) grace-first: darkness is the default, and the light has to come to a heart that can't light itself.
- Mark the seam clearly. This ends the Book of Signs (Jesus' public ministry). After this the crowds are gone and Jesus is with his own. Students are standing at the turn of the whole Gospel.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. This closes Unit 16.
