**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will name the author, John the beloved disciple, an eyewitness.
2. Students will say roughly when and where it was written (late first century, Ephesus).
3. Students will say what a "Gospel" is and that there are four.
4. Students will give one reason John is trustworthy.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the "how do we even know we can trust this?" day, made safe and evidence-based.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): what a Gospel is, why there are four, the setting.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the eyewitness argument from the small embedded details John keeps dropping.

---

**Talk budget:** this is the day you will most want to lecture the evidence. The discovery task hands the reasoning to the students so you are not the only voice, and the BibleProject video in Move 3 carries the book's shape so you do not lecture the survey either. Your own input stays under 5 minutes at a stretch. After the video, spend one of those minutes naming the opening claim yourself, "the Word was God" (1:1), so the book's deity claim is heard plainly and not softened into "Jesus shows us God."

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

You host; invite a volunteer to read the centering verse.

- Settle (1 min): a student reads John 1:14a, "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory."
- Journal (5 min): "Would it change how you read a story if you knew the person who wrote it was actually there and watched it happen? Write your honest reaction."
- Gather (1 min): close the journals.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (4 min)

Students read aloud John's own fingerprints, the places he tells you he was there:

- 1:14, "we have seen his glory"
- 19:35, "he who saw it has borne witness, and his testimony is true, that you also may believe"
- 21:24, "this is the disciple who is bearing witness about these things, and we know that his testimony is true"

## Move 3: Work the text (24 min, discovery then the map, not lecture)

- Entry Q (all): "How do we normally decide whether to trust a story someone tells us?" Turn-and-talk 60 seconds.
- Discovery task (John groups, ~14 min): hand each group these small details John records: the six stone jars at Cana holding twenty or thirty gallons (2:6); the five roofed colonnades at Bethesda (5:2); a servant named Malchus and that it was his right ear (18:10); exactly 153 fish (21:11). Question: "What do these tiny, oddly specific details tell you about the person who wrote this?" Let them reason their own way to "he was there," and put one observation in the Reading Journal.
- See the whole book (BibleProject "Overview: John 1-12," 8:46): now that the room has decided the writer was there, show them the book he wrote. The video maps its shape: the seven signs, the two sets of "I am" sayings, the Word who was with God and became flesh. It carries the survey so you do not lecture it, and it gives the (S) student a picture of the whole before we walk it verse by verse.
- Core content (your voice, under 3 min): who John is (the beloved disciple, son of Zebedee, one of the Twelve); when and where (late first century, from Ephesus); what a Gospel is (a proclamation of good news about Jesus, four of them, John written last and most reflective). Then land the opening claim the video circles: the very first line is "the Word was God" (1:1). The book opens on Jesus being God, and everything the video just showed unfolds that one sentence.
- How to read a Gospel: read for who Jesus is and what response he calls for (20:31), slowly, whole scenes at a time, expecting the text to ask something of you.
- Deeper Q (D): "John wrote decades after the others, and his account reads very differently. Why might the church have wanted a fourth Gospel like this one?" Hold it; we will watch John's angle all year.
- Cold-call checks (named): who wrote it? One piece of eyewitness evidence? What is a Gospel? Why do we read one?

## Move 4: Land and minister (6 min, your protected pulpit)

- The witness points past himself. John never says "believe me." He says "I saw his glory, come and see him."
- Speak it over the room: the most honest thing a doubter can do is look at Jesus for himself, and that is exactly what this book puts in front of you. You do not have to arrive already believing. You have to be willing to look.

## Move 5: Respond (4 min, graded participation)

- Reading Journal: "John says he saw Jesus with his own eyes and wrote it down so you could decide for yourself. Are you willing to look? What would it take for you?"

## After the bell

- Homework: read John 1:1-5 slowly for next class. The Prologue opens.
- Feeds the gradebook: participation.
- Sets up: Unit 1 on the Prologue, beginning with the Word (1:1-5).

## Teacher notes

- The pull today is to deliver the whole apologetic as a lecture. The discovery task lets students reason their own way to "eyewitness," which persuades the skeptic in the room far more than being told. Hold your who-when-where to under five minutes.
- This is the safest day of the opening stretch for the student who arrives doubting. Keep it evidence-based and unpressured.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
