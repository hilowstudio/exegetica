**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what the good shepherd does (10:11) and why the hired hand runs (10:12-13).
2. Students will explain how deeply the shepherd knows his sheep (10:14-15).
3. Students will explain the voluntary death and resurrection (10:17-18).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the good shepherd dies for the sheep instead of running.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "good" means the true, beautiful shepherd, and the beauty is the dying.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the mutual knowing measured by the Father and Son; the death chosen, the life taken back up.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This lesson plants the new memory verse, so protect Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep" (John 10:11).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Who has ever put themselves on the line for me?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write about that person, or about wishing you had one.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 10:11-18 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 10, verses 11 through 18.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus says he's the good shepherd, and then tells you exactly what makes him good.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 11, what does the good shepherd do for the sheep?
   - Cold-call check: "lays down his life for the sheep." His goodness is dying for them.
2. **Entry.** Read verses 12 and 13. Why does the hired hand run when the wolf comes?
   - The sheep aren't his. He's there for a paycheck, so when it gets dangerous, he protects himself.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** The word for "good" here means true, real, beautiful, the genuine article against every fake. Notice what Jesus names as his goodness, in the same breath. What makes him the good shepherd?
   - Guide them: he dies for the sheep. His beauty is the cross. A gentle, nice shepherd who never lays anything down isn't the good shepherd of this verse. The good shepherd bleeds for his own.
4. **Core.** Read verses 14 and 15. Jesus says he knows his sheep and they know him, "just as the Father knows me and I know the Father." How deep is that knowing?
   - Land it: it's measured by the closest relationship there is, the Father and the Son. Jesus knows his sheep that personally, by name. And they know him back. That's not distant admiration. It's being fully known and loved.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verses 17 and 18. Jesus says no one takes his life from him. He lays it down and takes it back up by his own authority. Why does it matter that his death was chosen and not forced?
   - Draw out: he chose the cross with his eyes open. He walked into it on purpose, for the sheep, and he had the power to rise again. His death and resurrection were his own act under the Father's charge. It was a rescue he planned all along.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Say verse 11 back in your own words. What makes Jesus the good shepherd?
   - He's the true shepherd, and his goodness is laying down his life for his sheep.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit) and plant the memory verse

Bring it to Christ and plant the new verse. This is the heart of sermon 15.

- Plant John 10:11 as the unit's new memory verse. Put it on the board and say it twice: "I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep."
- Teach what "good" means. It means the true, beautiful, real shepherd, and the beauty is the dying. Every other "I am" verse students know tells them what Jesus gives (bread, light). This is the "I am" where Jesus dies. In one sentence, "I am" and "I die for you" are the same claim.
- Guard against a soft, sentimental picture. Jesus didn't throw himself down to look loving. A real wolf was coming for the sheep, sin and death and the enemy, and he stepped between them and it and let it kill him. That's what good means here.
- Land on the "for the sheep." He didn't die for sheep in general. He died for his own, the ones he knows by name. So this verse is a promise of belonging. If you're his, this is about you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the good shepherd, and his goodness is that he laid down his life for you and took it back up again.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The good shepherd knows his sheep by name and dies for them. What would change if you really believed Jesus knows you that personally and did that for you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 10:11. Read John 10:19-30 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 10:11 joins the rolling memory-verse quizzes going forward.
- Sets up L3: Jesus promises his sheep can never be snatched away, and says, "I and the Father are one."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Plant 10:11 in Move 4. Teach "good" (kalos) as beautiful and true, and show the beauty is the cross. This is the first "I am" that dies, after bread (6:35) and light (8:12).
- The hired-hand contrast is about ownership and love, not competence. The hired hand can do good work. He just won't die, because the sheep aren't his.
- Keep the wolf guard against sentimentality. A real danger was coming, and the shepherd took it in the sheep's place. That's sermon 15's best single move.
- 10:17-18 (the voluntary death and resurrection) is a real deity claim, made right before they try to stone him next lesson.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. Protect Move 4, where the memory verse plants.
