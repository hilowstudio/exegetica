**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the catch at Jesus' word (21:6).
2. Students will explain how the disciples recognized Jesus (his provision, 21:7).
3. Students will explain Peter's response of jumping into the sea (21:7).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus filled the net with one word.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): real fruit comes at Christ's word, not by skill alone.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the catch echoing Peter's original call (Luke 5).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "It is the Lord!" (John 21:7).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where have I worked hard on my own and come up empty?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 21:4-8 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 21, verses 4 through 8.
- One-sentence setup: at dawn, a man they don't recognize calls out to the tired, empty-handed fishermen.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 5 and 6, what does the stranger on the shore tell them to do, and what happens?
   - Cold-call check: he tells them to cast the net on the right side of the boat, and suddenly it's so full of fish they can't haul it in.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** These are professional fishermen who just spent all night catching nothing. Then one word from a stranger fills the net. What does that tell you about where real fruit comes from?
   - Guide them: not from their skill or effort. They tried all night, expert fishermen, and got nothing. One word from Jesus, and the net overflows. The fruit comes at his word and by his power. It's the same lesson as the vine (15:5): apart from him, empty nets; at his word, more than they can handle.
3. **Core.** Read verse 7. Who figures out it's Jesus, and how? What's the clue that gives him away?
   - Land it: John, the disciple Jesus loved, recognizes him: "It is the Lord!" The clue is the miraculous catch. They recognize Jesus by what only he can do. The impossible net full of fish tells them exactly who's on that shore.
4. **Core.** Still verse 7. When Peter hears "it is the Lord," what does he do? Is that a calm, measured response?
   - Draw out: he throws on his outer garment and jumps into the sea to swim to shore, rather than wait for the boat. It's not calm at all. It's impulsive, eager, headlong. This is the man who denied Jesus a few weeks ago, and the moment he knows Jesus is near, he can't get to him fast enough. He doesn't hang back in shame. He dives in.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Way back when Jesus first called Peter (Luke 5), it was almost the exact same scene: a night of catching nothing, a huge catch at Jesus' word, and Jesus saying "you'll be catching men." Why might Jesus stage the same miracle now, at the end, for a man who just failed?
   - Draw out: to remind Peter of the beginning, of who he is and what he's called to. It's like Jesus is saying, "remember? This is where it started. It's not over. I'm still calling you." The repeated miracle tells Peter his failure didn't cancel his calling. Jesus is starting him over.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how did they recognize Jesus, and what did Peter do?
   - By the miraculous catch; Peter jumped into the sea to get to him.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the empty-then-full. All night, nothing. One word from Jesus, and the net is bursting. That's the whole Christian life in one picture. On your own strength, you strive and come up empty. At his word, more than you could carry. If you're worn out from trying to make things happen on your own, this scene is for you.
- Land Peter's dive. And watch Peter. This is the guy who denied even knowing Jesus a few weeks ago. You'd think he'd hang back, ashamed, hiding in the boat. Instead, the second he hears "it is the Lord," he's in the water, swimming for shore. His love for Jesus is bigger than his shame. He can't wait.
- Make it personal. If you've failed Jesus and you're tempted to keep your distance out of embarrassment, look at Peter. The right response to grace isn't hiding. It's diving in. Get to Jesus as fast as you can. He's the one standing on the shore, cooking you breakfast.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Lord fills the empty net and stands on the shore, and Peter's love runs to him through the water.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Peter dove in to get to Jesus, even after failing him. Where are you hanging back from Jesus out of shame, and what would 'diving in' look like?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 21:9-14 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: breakfast on the beach, over a charcoal fire.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: the fruit comes at Christ's word (the empty night versus the overflowing net, 15:5), and Peter's eager love diving in even after failure (grace draws him, not shame).
- For the disciple tier, the echo of Peter's original call (Luke 5), the same miracle staged at the end: the failure did not cancel the calling.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly.
