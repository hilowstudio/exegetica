**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what Jesus tells his disciples to do with their troubled hearts (14:1).
2. Students will explain what Jesus means by going to prepare a place and coming back (14:2-3).
3. Students will explain how "believe also in me" answers fear.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): when you're scared, Jesus says look at him.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the Father's house is a promise of a permanent place with Jesus, not a moral about being brave.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the comfort of the second coming, and how 14:1 opens a chapter that closes on the same word (14:27).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Let not your hearts be troubled" (John 14:1).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's troubling my heart right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: you don't have to fix it. Just name it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:1-4 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 1 through 4.
- One-sentence setup: it's the night before the cross, the disciples are shaken by talk of betrayal and death, and Jesus turns to comfort them.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 1, what does Jesus tell them to do with their troubled hearts, and what does he tell them to do instead?
   - Cold-call check: don't let their hearts be troubled; believe in God and believe also in him.
2. **Entry.** Set the scene from the end of chapter 13. What had Jesus just told them that would make their hearts troubled?
   - Draw out: that one of them would betray him, that Peter would deny him, and that he was about to die. Real fear, not imagined.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 2 and 3. In your own words, what does Jesus promise? List each thing.
   - Guide them: in the Father's house are many rooms; he's going to prepare a place for them; he will come again; he'll take them to himself so they're where he is.
4. **Core.** The "many rooms" isn't mainly about mansions in the sky. It's a promise of a permanent place with Jesus in the Father's presence. Why is "I will come again and take you to myself" the part that actually calms a scared person?
   - Land it: the promise isn't just a nice place. It's being with him, forever, and him personally coming back to get you. You're not left to find your own way.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Jesus doesn't say "cheer up" or "be strong." He says "believe also in me." What's the difference between telling someone to stop being anxious and telling them where to look?
   - Draw out: he doesn't put the weight back on their willpower. He gives them an object for their trust: himself. Comfort comes from who he is, not from trying harder to feel calm.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 1, where does Jesus point their troubled hearts?
   - To himself. "Believe also in me."

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name what Jesus is doing. He's hours from the worst night of his life, and he spends it comforting the very people who are about to abandon him. That's who he is.
- Land the promise. He didn't tell them to calm down. He told them where he was going and that he'd come back for them. The way home isn't something they had to figure out. He was going to prepare it and personally return to carry them there.
- Make it personal. When your heart is troubled, Jesus doesn't hand you a technique. He hands you himself: "believe also in me." The cure for a shaken heart is a steady person to look at, and he's it.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus goes through the cross to prepare a place for his people, and comes back to take them home.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "When you're anxious, what do you look at? What would it change to look at Jesus instead?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: Thomas admits he's lost, and Jesus says, "I am the way, and the truth, and the life." The new memory verse plants tomorrow.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This opens Unit 19 and the middle of the Farewell Discourse. Keep the tone tender, the way sermon 23 does: this is pastoral care for scared people, not a lecture on heaven.
- Guard against making "many rooms" a real-estate promise. The treasure is being with Jesus (14:3). The same picture returns at 14:23, where God makes his home in the believer.
- Flag forward: 14:1 and 14:27 bookend the chapter on the same words, "let not your hearts be troubled." You'll close the loop at L7.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
