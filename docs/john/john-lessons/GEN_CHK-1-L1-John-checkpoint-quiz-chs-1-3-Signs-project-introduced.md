**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will write the four held memory anchors from memory (20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16).
2. Students will recall the christotelic heart of each of the first three chapters (the Word made flesh, the true temple and the first sign, the new birth and the Son lifted up).

---

**Talk budget:** this is a checkpoint and a graded quiz, not new teaching, and the project launch is the day's second half. Keep your voice light and warm. Let the students do the recalling and the writing. Leave the board line up the whole period: "John tells us who Jesus is, then how to enter the life he gives."

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Out of the first three chapters, what's one thing about Jesus I don't want to forget?"

## Move 2: Recap chapters 1 through 3 (8-9 min)

Quick and warm. Ask, don't lecture. Walk the three chapters in one sweep and let the students supply the answers and the anchors. This is the same gather-from-the-room move you'll use again at every checkpoint.

1. **John 1, the Word and the first witnesses.** Ask: "Before Jesus does a single thing, John tells us who he is. Who is the main character?" (The eternal Word, God with God, who made everything and became a man you could see, 1:1 and 1:14. Then John the Baptist and Andrew and Philip keep pointing away from themselves at him.) Lands on: Christ the Word made flesh, full of grace and truth, received one name at a time. Anchors here: 1:14 (the Word became flesh) and 1:12 (the right to become children of God).
2. **John 2, the wine and the temple.** Ask: "Jesus walks into a wedding and into the temple. What does the first sign show, and what does he say the real temple is?" (At Cana he turns water into the best wine in flood quantity, the feast the prophets promised, and "manifested his glory," 2:11. In the temple he clears his Father's house and names his own body the true temple, torn down and raised in three days, 2:19-21.) Lands on: Christ the reality every ritual and building was pointing at, whose death and resurrection is the deepest sign of all.
3. **John 3, the new birth and the serpent lifted up.** Ask: "A religious insider comes at night. What does Jesus tell him he needs, and can he produce it himself?" (You must be born again, from above, a birth worked by the sovereign Spirit like the wind, 3:3-8. Then the bronze serpent: as Moses lifted it, so the Son of Man must be lifted up, and whoever looks in faith lives, 3:14-16.) Lands on: Christ lifted up on the cross, God's gift to a world that could not save itself. Anchor: 3:16 (God so loved the world).

Say one sentence: three chapters, one doorway. John tells you who Jesus is, then that you need a birth you cannot perform, then the cross you look to and live.

## Move 3: Checkpoint quiz (15-16 min, graded)

Phones and Bibles away. This feeds the gradebook. Two parts.

**Part A, the four held anchors from memory (the weight of the grade).** Put the references on the board and have students write each from memory, in the ESV:
1. John 20:31
2. John 1:14
3. John 1:12
4. John 3:16

Grade for the sense and the load-bearing phrases, not word-perfect punctuation. A student who has the meaning and the main wording has it. The verse texts are in the Answer Key below.

**Part B, content recall (short, one per chapter).** Students answer briefly in writing:
5. John 1: In 1:12-13 becoming a child of God is called a birth. What three things does verse 13 rule out as its cause, and who does it credit?
6. John 2: The disciples "believed" at Cana (2:11), and at Passover many "believed" when they saw signs, yet 2:24 says Jesus "did not entrust himself to them." What is the difference between the two kinds of believing?
7. John 3: In 3:14 Jesus compares himself to something Moses lifted up in the wilderness. What was it (Numbers 21), and what did the dying Israelites have to do to live?

Model answers are in the Answer Key.

## Move 4: Land, then launch the Signs project (13-15 min, your protected pulpit, then the assignment)

Bring it to Christ first, then open the quarter's project. Off the grade, warm.

- The closing word over the room. In three chapters John has walked you to a door. He tells you who Jesus is before Jesus lifts a finger: the eternal Word, God himself, who made you and then became one of you. He shows you that meeting God was never about the right building, because Jesus is the true temple. And he tells you the one thing you need, which is a birth you cannot give yourself, handed to whoever looks in faith to the Son lifted up on a cross. John 3:16 is not a slogan on a poster. It is God so loving the world that he gave his only Son. That is the door you are standing in.

- Launch the project. Point the room at the coming quarter. Say what the assignment actually is. Read the prompt aloud: "Someone you know has never opened a Bible and thinks Jesus' miracles were magic tricks. Take one of John's seven signs and make something that shows them why John calls it a sign, and what it proves about who Jesus is."

- Frame the word "sign" against the objection. This is the whole point. John never calls the miracles "wonders" for the show. He calls them signs. The Greek is semeion, a mark by which a thing is known, an indication of something beyond itself. A sign is a window. You look at what Jesus does and you see who Jesus is. John says exactly that at the end of the book: the signs "are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:30-31). For the friend who thinks it was a trick, hand them this: a magician wants you to watch the trick, and he hides how it was done. John wants you to look through the miracle and see the face of God.

- The menu widens over the next three chapters. Tell them straight: so far you have met one sign, the water into wine at Cana. Four more are coming in chapters 4 through 6, before this is due. So the sign you choose is not locked in today. Read forward, and pick the one that grips you.

- Walk the milestones so the build stays honest and nobody drowns. This is a graded process, not just a final product. Pitch (your sign, your audience, your format, and why), then Plan (the passages and quotes you'll use and how it's structured), then a Draft or rehearsal with peer feedback, then the final, presented to the class with live Q&A. There is an in-class check-in along the way for roadblocks. You choose the format: video, infographic, first-person monologue, art with commentary, a written piece. The rubric fixes the skills; you choose the shape and the angle. And a required Maker's Note at the end, a few honest sentences on why you built it this way and what was hard.

- Name what earns the grade, so the room aims right. The prompt asks what the sign "proves about who Jesus is." That christological point is the heart of the grade, not the polish. A slick video that never says who Jesus is has missed the assignment. Open the window; show them the face of God.

- Christ connection to say out loud: every one of John's signs is a window on the same person you met in these three chapters, the Word made flesh who came to give the world life.

## Move 5: Respond (short, graded participation)

- One line in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Which of the first three chapters hit you hardest, and why?" And, if there's time: "Which sign are you leaning toward for the project, and who is the one friend you're making it for?"
- A short blessing over the room closes the checkpoint well.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 4:1-26 once before next class. Start thinking about your project sign and your audience.
- To the gradebook: today's checkpoint quiz (content) and the participation line. The four anchors stay in the rolling memory quizzes. The project's graded milestones begin in the coming weeks.
- Sets up Unit 5 and the walk into chapter 4: John the Baptist steps back so Christ can increase, and then Jesus sits down at a well with an outsider.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: this is a scheduled checkpoint on a regular 50-minute day. It's a graded quiz plus a warm recap plus the launch of the Q1 project, not new exposition. The chapter 4 exposition begins next unit.
- Keep the recap a fast, warm sweep. Ask the three questions and let students supply the answers and the anchors. Cap your voice; this is their recall.
- Grade the memory quiz for sense and key phrases, never word-perfection. Reward the load-bearing phrase in each: "that by believing you may have life in his name," "the Word became flesh," "the right to become children of God," "God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son."
- Content questions (Part B) are recall and close reading of what was taught, answerable by every tier, not new reasoning. Guard the new-birth question (5) from collapsing into "make a decision" or "say a prayer": verse 13 credits God, and the point is that the new birth is God's work, received, never engineered.
- The project launch is the load-bearing half of the day. The one thing to get across is that a sign is a window on Jesus, not a spectacle. That is the correction the whole assignment exists to make. Keep pointing through the deed to the person.
- Two guards on the project. First, students have met only Cana today, so tell them plainly the menu widens through chapter 6 and not to lock in a sign they haven't read yet. Second, grade the christological point the prompt asks for, not the craft; a beautiful piece with no Jesus in it has done half the work.
- Keep it celebratory and low-anxiety. This is the first checkpoint of the year, so nerves are normal. Frame the quiz as "show me what you carry."
- Keep the christotelic thesis central so the quiz doesn't flatten three rich chapters into trivia. The facts serve the person. Leave the board line up all period and end on the pastoral word before the launch.
- Content is gradable; belief is invitational. A student can write all four verses and answer all three questions without being asked to profess. Keep it that way.
- Day-type note: Friday, regular 50 minutes.

## Answer Key

**Part A, the memory anchors (ESV). Grade for sense and key wording.**

1. **John 20:31**: "but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name."
2. **John 1:14**: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."
3. **John 1:12**: "But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God,"
4. **John 3:16**: "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish but have eternal life."

**Part B, content recall. Model answers; accept any response that carries the sense.**

5. **John 1 (the cause of the new birth, 1:12-13):** Verse 13 rules out three things: bloodline ("not of blood"), natural desire ("nor of the will of the flesh"), and a human decision ("nor of the will of man"). It credits God, who does the birthing. The point is that the new birth is God's work, received, never engineered. Guard against "make a decision" or "say this prayer."
6. **John 2 (two kinds of believing):** One kind of faith rests on the person Jesus is. The other is being impressed by the miracles from a distance. Jesus reads the heart and knows what is in a man (2:25), so a sign-dazzled faith has not yet become saving trust.
7. **John 3 (the bronze serpent, 3:14):** The bronze serpent Moses lifted on a pole (Numbers 21). The dying Israelites had to look at it in faith and they lived, with no medicine and no work. Jesus says the cross is that pole: he is lifted up, and whoever looks to him in faith has eternal life (3:15).
