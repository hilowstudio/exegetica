**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "my kingdom is not of this world" (18:36).
2. Students will explain how Jesus' kingdom differs from a kingdom of force.
3. Students will explain why Jesus says he came into the world (18:37).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus is a different kind of king.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): his kingdom comes from God and spreads by truth, not by power grabs.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the kingdom of a different origin and character, guarded against escapism and against a merely political reading; 18:37 the stretch verse.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I hear 'the kingdom of God,' what do I picture?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write your honest picture.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 18:33-37 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 33 through 37.
- One-sentence setup: Pilate takes Jesus inside and asks the question that matters to Rome: are you a king?

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 33, what does Pilate ask Jesus? Why would that question worry a Roman governor?
   - Cold-call check: "Are you the King of the Jews?" A rival king means rebellion against Rome, which is what Pilate has to watch for.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 36. Jesus says his kingdom "is not of this world." He gives a piece of evidence for it in the same verse. What is it? What does it prove about his kingdom?
   - Guide them: his evidence is that his servants aren't fighting to free him. If his kingdom ran on armies and force like Rome's, his followers would be swinging swords right now. They're not (he told Peter to put his sword away). So his kingdom isn't the sword-and-army kind. It comes from somewhere else.
3. **Core.** "My kingdom is not of this world" gets misread two ways. It does NOT mean Jesus doesn't care about this world, or that his kingdom is just a floating spiritual thing with no effect here. What does it actually mean?
   - Land it: it means his kingdom comes from God (a different source) and works in a different way (by truth and love, not by force). It's very much at work in this world, right now, changing real people. It just doesn't advance the way Rome does, by conquering and coercing. It spreads as people hear the truth and follow the King.
4. **Core.** Read verse 37. Pilate says, "so you are a king?" How does Jesus describe why he came into the world?
   - Draw out: "to bear witness to the truth," and "everyone who is of the truth listens to my voice." His whole mission is bound up with truth. He's a King, and his reign and the truth are the same thing. To belong to him is to hear and follow his voice.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Christians sometimes try to advance Jesus' kingdom the way Pilate would run Rome: grab power, force everyone to comply, win by strength. How does verse 36 correct that?
   - Draw out: Jesus' kingdom doesn't spread by force, so his people don't advance it by force either. It grows as the truth is told and people's hearts are won, one at a time. When the church tries to win by worldly power, it stops looking like its King, who refused to let his servants fight for him.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does "my kingdom is not of this world" mean?
   - His kingdom comes from God and spreads by truth, not by armies or force.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Show them the strange king. Pilate is looking for a rebel with an army. What he gets is a bound, beaten man who says his kingdom doesn't run on armies at all. Every kingdom Pilate knew was built on force. This one is built on truth. Its throne is a cross, and its power is love.
- Correct the two mistakes. His kingdom is crashing into this world right now, changing real lives, including maybe yours, so "not of this world" never meant Jesus checked out of it. And it wins hearts by the truth, the way it's winning yours, so it never meant grab power and force people to obey. It comes from God, and it runs on truth and love.
- Land the truth thread. He says he came to bear witness to the truth, and that his people hear his voice. You've spent a whole year learning that Jesus is the truth (14:6). Here he says his whole reign is bound to it. Following him means listening to his voice over every other one.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the King from God, whose kingdom runs on truth and love, and whose voice his people follow.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says his people hear his voice. Where is his voice hard for you to listen to and follow right now?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Optional stretch: start learning John 18:37. Read John 18:38 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L7: Pilate asks "what is truth?" and declares Jesus innocent.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The key rulings on 18:36: a kingdom of a different ORIGIN (from God) and CHARACTER (truth and love, not force). Guard BOTH misreadings: escapism ("Jesus doesn't care about this world") and a merely political kingdom. Sermon 34's edge: the church errs when she weds herself to earthly power.
- 18:37 ties the King to the truth (10:26-27, "my sheep hear my voice"; 14:6). Offer 18:37 as the OPTIONAL off-graded stretch verse today (it's on the taught-not-memorized list). It is not the graded work; there is no graded plant this unit.
- Anti-Judaism guard: "King of the Jews" is Pilate's political charge; handle without slur.
- Derive the frame from sermon 34; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
