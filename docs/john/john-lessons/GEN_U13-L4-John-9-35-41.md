**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus does when he hears the man was cast out (9:35).
2. Students will explain the man's worship (9:38) and what it claims about Jesus.
3. Students will explain the two kinds of blindness in 9:39-41.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus goes and finds the man who got thrown out for him.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): admitting you can't see is how you get to see.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Jesus receiving worship as a claim to deity; the two blindnesses.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Lord, I believe" (John 9:38).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I need to admit I can't see on my own?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 9:35-41 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 9, verses 35 through 41.
- One-sentence setup: the man got thrown out for Jesus, and now Jesus comes looking for him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verse 35, what does Jesus do when he hears the man was cast out?
   - He goes and finds him. The man paid a price for Jesus, and Jesus comes looking for him. (This is also the first time the man actually sees Jesus' face, verse 37.)
2. **Read-check.** In verse 38, after Jesus reveals who he is, what does the man do?
   - "Lord, I believe," and he worshiped him. This is the top rung of his confession, all the way up from "the man called Jesus."
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** In verse 39 Jesus says he came so "those who do not see may see, and those who see may become blind." Two kinds of blindness are standing right there. Work this: which kind of person ends up seeing, and how? And why do the Pharisees, who have perfect eyes, end up blind (verses 40-41)?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out: the man who admitted he was blind and came to Jesus can see now, in every way. The Pharisees insist "we see," so they never come to Jesus for sight, and they stay blind. Verse 41 is the key: if they admitted they were blind, they'd be forgiven. Because they claim to see, their guilt stays.
   - For the disciple tier, guard the edge: the blindness Jesus means isn't being unintelligent. It's the pride that won't admit you need anything. Plenty of brilliant people have come to Jesus as beggars. The one thing that keeps a person blind is saying "I'm fine, I can see."
   - Also for the disciple tier: notice Jesus lets the man worship him (verse 38) and doesn't stop him. In the Bible, angels and apostles refuse worship. Jesus receives it, because he's God.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. Build on the close of sermon 14.

- Start with verse 35, aimed at the student who's paid a cost. The man got thrown out of everything for Jesus, and the very next thing that happens is Jesus comes and finds him. That's the shepherd. When you lose something for naming him, he sees it, and he comes looking for you.
- Name the worship. The man ends the chapter on his knees calling Jesus "Lord." And Jesus takes it. Angels in the Bible refuse worship. Jesus receives it, because he really is God. The whole chapter of a growing confession lands here, at worship.
- Put the two blindnesses in front of the room. Everyone's in one of two groups. Either you admit you can't see and you come to Jesus for sight, or you insist you can see and you stay in the dark. The only thing that keeps a person out is pride, saying "I'm fine." The door is wide open for anyone honest enough to say, "I was blind."
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus receives worship as God, he opens the eyes of everyone who admits they're blind, and he goes and finds the ones cast out for his name.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus gives sight to people who admit they can't see. Where do you need to stop pretending you're fine and ask him to help you see?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing your seven memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. No new required memory verse this unit.
- Sets up Unit 14: Jesus calls himself the Good Shepherd, and the next new memory verse (John 10:11) lands there.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- Build on the close of sermon 14: the two blindnesses run through every person in the room, and admitting blindness is how you receive sight.
- Carry the worship ruling (9:38). Jesus receives worship and doesn't stop it, where Peter (Acts 10:26) and the angel (Revelation 19:10) refuse it. Accepting worship is a claim only God can make. Give this to the disciple tier.
- Guard 9:41 against "smart people can't be saved." The blindness is pride that won't admit need, not intelligence. Aim it at pride, and keep it off the honor student.
- Use verse 35 for the student who's paid a cost. The shepherd goes after the one thrown out for his sake. This closes the unit warmly.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. This closes Unit 13.
