**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state the man's testimony in 9:25.
2. Students will trace his confession to "if this man were not from God, he could do nothing" (9:33).
3. Students will explain why the leaders cast him out (9:34).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the man doesn't have all the answers, and he doesn't need them.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "I was blind, now I see" is a testimony anyone can give.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the man's boldness as the fruit of grace, and getting cast out for Jesus.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "One thing I do know, that though I was blind, now I see" (John 9:25).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's one true thing Jesus has done in my life or in someone I know?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write what you'd want to be true, even if you're not sure yet.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 9:24-34 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 9, verses 24 through 34.
- One-sentence setup: the leaders call the man back a second time and pressure him hard to turn on Jesus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 24 the leaders demand the man agree that Jesus is a sinner. In verse 25, how does he answer?
   - Cold-call check: "Whether he is a sinner I do not know. One thing I do know, that though I was blind, now I see." He won't argue about things he can't settle, and he holds onto the one thing he's certain of.
2. **Entry.** Why is "I was blind, now I see" so hard for the experts to argue him out of?
   - It's not a theory or a debate. It's what happened to him. Nobody can talk him out of the fact that he can now see.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 30 through 33. The man is not a scholar, and he reasons circles around the experts. Where does his confidence come from, and what does his confession climb to by verse 33?
   - Guide them: his confidence rests on what Jesus did for him, not on his skill in debate. He reasons simply: God doesn't do this for sinners, so "if this man were not from God, he could do nothing" (verse 33). His confession has climbed from "the man called Jesus" to "a prophet" to "from God."
4. **Core.** Read verse 34. What do the leaders do to the man, and why?
   - Land it: they insult him and throw him out ("cast him out"). He loses his community for refusing to deny what Jesus did. He pays a real price for confessing Christ.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** The man is bold, and it's worth asking where that boldness comes from. Did he work up his courage to earn Jesus, or did his boldness grow out of what Jesus already did for him?
   - Draw out: he's bold because he was found and healed first. The healing came before the courage, and the courage grew out of it. That order matters. You don't perform bravely to earn Jesus. Being given sight is what makes a person able to stand.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's the one thing the man is sure of, and what did it cost him to say it?
   - That he was blind and now sees, and it cost him his place in the synagogue.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 14, whose title is this man's line.

- Free the room from needing all the answers. This man couldn't out-argue the experts, and he didn't have to. He just told them what Jesus did for him. You don't need a theology degree to tell someone about Jesus. You need one true thing you've seen him do.
- Give them the template. "I was blind, now I see" is a testimony every believer can give. Whatever Jesus has done in you, or in someone you love, that's your version of it. Plain, true, hard to argue with.
- Keep the order straight, gently. The man's boldness didn't earn him Jesus. He got bold because Jesus found him and healed him first. If you want courage about your faith, it grows from what Jesus has already done for you, not from gritting your teeth.
- Name the cost, and the promise under it. He got thrown out for Jesus. And next class you'll see what Jesus does about that.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is worth being cast out for, and the simplest true word about him is stronger than every argument against him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "You don't need all the answers to say what Jesus has done. Write your own 'I was blind, now I see,' one true thing you've seen him do."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 9:35-41 once before Thursday. That's the ending.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. No new required memory verse. John 9:25 is an optional stretch.
- Sets up L4: Jesus goes and finds the man who got thrown out, and the man worships him.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 14. Its title is 9:25, and its point is that every believer can give this testimony, the plain report of what Christ has done, without winning a debate.
- Guard the legalist side door hard at the close. The man's boldness is the fruit of grace already given. Grace came first, and the boldness grew out of it. Keep the order: the Lord found and healed him first, and the courage grew out of that. Don't let it become "be brave like this guy to prove you're a real Christian."
- Offer 9:25 as an optional stretch, off the graded line. It's on the taught-not-memorized list and it's the one line from this chapter every student could carry.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
