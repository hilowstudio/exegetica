# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 3 Lesson 3 of 4: destroy this temple (John 2:18-22)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U3 Sign 1 & the temple |
| Lesson | 3 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Sep 24 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus |
| Passage | John 2:18-22 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The leaders demand a sign. Jesus gives them the deepest one in the book and veils it: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up." He is speaking of his body. |
| Christ connection | The risen body of Jesus is now the true temple, the place where God and sinners meet. The building was a shadow; Christ is the reality. |
| Memory verse | Review only. Use a backward glance at 1:14 today, since the tabernacle language of the Prologue ("dwelt among us") is what makes "the temple of his body" land. No new memory load. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega 03, "Zeal for the Lord" (John 2:13-25), middle part. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what sign Jesus offers when the leaders demand one (2:19).
2. Students will explain, from verse 21, what Jesus meant by "this temple" (his own body).
3. Students will explain how his death and resurrection is the sign, and why the disciples understood it only later (2:22).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus points at himself and says the real place you meet God is standing right here, and he will prove it by dying and rising in three days.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): sit in verse 22. You can be near Jesus for years with a saying unexplained and still be his; understanding often comes later.
- **Stretch** (disciple): walk the true-temple line from 1:14 ("tabernacled among us") to the resurrection and on to 4:21-24, worship "in spirit and truth," neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem.

---

**Talk budget:** short Thursday, single-focus. One saying carries the whole period, so slow way down and keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up" (John 2:19).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do you go, or what do you do, to feel close to God? What if the answer were a person?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 2:18-22 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 2, verses 18 through 22.
- One-sentence setup: the leaders want proof, and Jesus hands them the deepest sign in the book, folded up in a riddle.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 18 the leaders demand a sign. What does Jesus say in verse 19? What do they think he means (verse 20)?
   - Draw out: "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up." They think he means the building, which took forty-six years to build, and they scoff.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** John steps in and tells you what Jesus actually meant. Read verse 21. What is "this temple"?
   - Guide them: "he was speaking about the temple of his body." The one place Israel met God was the temple; Jesus says the real meeting place is his own body. Back in the Prologue the Word "dwelt among us" (1:14), literally pitched his tent. Jesus is where God lives with people now.
3. **Core.** So what is the sign Jesus offers the leaders? Read the riddle again with verse 21 in mind.
   - Land it: they will in fact destroy him, and three days later he rises. His death and resurrection is the sign. On day one, Jesus folds the whole gospel into one sentence.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 22 says the disciples understood this only after he rose. What does that tell you about how understanding comes?
   - Draw out: they followed with the saying unexplained, and it clicked later, in hindsight, by the Spirit. Following Jesus with real questions still open is the normal shape of discipleship, not a failure.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What is "this temple"? What is the sign?
   - His body; his death and resurrection.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Say the shift. The building where Israel met God is finished. The body of Christ, torn down at the cross and raised on the third day, is where God and sinners meet now.
- Make it personal. You do not travel to a holy place to find God. You come to a Person, and he is risen. The meeting place has a face and a name.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the true temple, and his resurrection is the sign that proves it.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "If the place you meet God is a risen Person, not a building or a feeling, what changes about how you would come to him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 2:23-25 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12.
- Sets up L4: many believed at the signs, but Jesus "did not entrust himself to them," because he knew what was in man.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: single-focus Thursday. One saying, slowed way down. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The christotelic peak of the unit. Hold both halves together: the sign is his rising, and the point is that he himself is the temple, the true meeting place with God. Do not flatten it into a mere prediction of the resurrection.
- Verse 22 is the pastoral gift: understanding came later, so a student following with questions open is in good company.
- Tie back to 1:14 (the tabernacle language) and forward to 4:21-24 (worship in spirit and truth, off the mountain and out of Jerusalem), and let the Passover setting shake hands with the Lamb of God from Unit 2. The chapter even brackets "on the third day" (2:1) with "in three days" (2:19).
- Do not reopen the two-cleansings debate here; hold the class on the saying and its meaning.
- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus.
