**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe how Jesus fed the crowd (6:9-13) and what was left over.
2. Students will explain why Jesus refused to be made king by force (6:15).
3. Students will explain what the Passover setting (6:4) signals about how to read this chapter.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus feeds thousands from one kid's lunch. Everyone in the room knows what hunger feels like.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the crowd wanted the provision without the person, a king to keep the food coming.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the Passover frame (6:4) and the Exodus backdrop, setting up the manna and the cross.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Where are we to buy bread, so that these people may eat?" (John 6:5).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I hungry for that food doesn't fix?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the hunger honestly, whatever it is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 6:1-15 aloud, one reads 1 through 9, the other 10 through 15.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 6, verses 1 through 15.
- Set the scene in one sentence: a huge crowd follows Jesus up a mountain near Passover, and there's no food.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 9, what does the boy bring? In verse 13, what's left over after thousands eat?
   - Cold-call check: five barley loaves and two fish. Twelve baskets of leftovers. He starts with almost nothing and ends with more than they began.
2. **Entry.** If someone could always give you food, money, or whatever you wanted, would you follow them? Be honest about why.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 15. The crowd tries to grab Jesus and make him king, and he slips away alone. What kind of king did they want, and why would Jesus turn that down?
   - Guide them: they wanted a king who'd keep the bread coming and fight Rome. Jesus is a king, and he refuses to be their vending machine. He's headed for a different throne, the cross.
4. **Core.** Look at verse 4: "the Passover was at hand." Passover was when Israel remembered God rescuing them from Egypt and feeding them in the desert. Why might John point that out right before Jesus feeds a crowd in a wilderness?
   - Land it: John wants us to see the God of the Exodus at work. The one who fed Israel in the desert is standing here feeding a crowd. This sets up the whole chapter about the true bread from heaven.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Twelve disciples, twelve baskets left over. Jesus feeds the crowd, then there's enough to feed the servants too. What does that show about what Jesus has to give?
   - Draw out: his provision doesn't run out when he serves people. There's more than enough, and he takes care of the ones who served. Receive from him, then give it away.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did the crowd want from Jesus, and what did he do about it?
   - They wanted to make him their bread-king, and he withdrew from them.

Circulate while groups work. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on the first half of sermon 07.

- Start with the abundance. Jesus doesn't just meet the need. He fills it and overflows it, twelve baskets left. He sees the hunger before anyone asks (verse 5) and moves to meet it.
- Name the temptation the crowd fell into, and that we fall into. They loved what Jesus could do for them and missed who he was. We do the same thing, wanting Jesus for good grades, calm feelings, or a fixed life, and missing him.
- Point past the meal. This sign is a window. The God who fed Israel in the desert is here, and by the end of the chapter he'll say the real bread is himself, given on a cross. Keep pointing through the miracle to the person.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the true King who provides more than enough, and he's worth wanting for himself.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The crowd wanted Jesus for what he could give. Where do you do that? What would it look like to want him for himself?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 6:16-24 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: that same night, Jesus walks to his disciples across a storm.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 07's first half. Its central move is that we do not make Jesus king, he is the king, and he refuses the crowd's small throne because he's headed for the cross.
- Guard the "share your lunch" misread. The point isn't the boy's generosity. It's that Jesus multiplied almost nothing into more than enough. Keep Jesus central.
- Plant the Passover frame (6:4) lightly here. It pays off across the whole unit as the manna and the cross come into view.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block. If Move 3 runs long, trim the Deeper question first.
