**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 16:33.
2. Students will explain the two promises: tribulation in the world, and peace in Christ.
3. Students will explain "I have overcome the world" as a finished victory.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus promises peace in him even when life is hard.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the verse promises trouble AND victory, not "everything will be fine."
- **Stretch** (disciple): "I have overcome" as an already-finished, still-standing victory; the answer to 14:1.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. This is the memory-verse plant. One verse, taught with care, memorized in the room.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Take heart; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What trouble am I bracing for right now?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 16:33 aloud, slowly. Read it twice.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verse 33.
- One-sentence setup: this is the last thing Jesus says in the whole Farewell talk before he prays and goes to the cross.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: the memory verse. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 33, where does Jesus say you'll find peace?
   - Cold-call check: "in me." The peace is located in Jesus.
2. **Entry.** What two things does Jesus promise in this one sentence?
   - Draw out: "in the world you will have tribulation" (trouble is guaranteed), and "I have overcome the world" (his victory is done). Both, together.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Notice Jesus doesn't promise a smooth life. He promises trouble AND victory. Why is that more honest, and more helpful, than "if you follow Jesus everything will be fine"?
   - Guide them: because life won't always be fine, and a promise that says it will falls apart the first time it isn't. Jesus tells the truth: you'll have real trouble. And then he gives you something bigger than a trouble-free life: his finished victory over the whole thing. That promise still stands when life gets hard, because it never depended on life being easy.
4. **Core.** "I have overcome the world." Jesus says this the night before the cross, as if the win is already done. It's written in a form that means a completed victory that still stands. Why does it matter that the victory is already finished, not just hoped for?
   - Land it: because you're not waiting to see if Jesus wins. He already won. When trouble comes at you, you're not fighting to see who comes out on top. You're standing behind a Savior who already beat the world, and its ruler, at the cross. The outcome is settled. You're living inside a victory that's already been won.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Way back at the start of this whole talk (John 14:1), Jesus said "let not your hearts be troubled." Now he ends it here. How does 16:33 answer 14:1?
   - Draw out: he opened by telling their troubled hearts to rest, and he closes by giving them the reason: he has overcome the world. The whole talk runs from "don't be troubled" to "I've already won." That's why they can have peace even with trouble still coming: he beat the thing that troubles them.
6. **Memory verse plant.** Have students say John 16:33 together, then in pairs. Make sure they can hold both halves: "in the world you will have tribulation" and "I have overcome the world," and that they know the peace is "in me."

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and close the unit. Framed fresh.

- Say both halves and mean both. Jesus doesn't promise you a life without trouble. He looks you in the eye and says you'll have it. And in the same breath he says the thing that changes everything: I have overcome the world. He's honest about the storm and bigger than it.
- Land the finished victory. He said "I have overcome" the night before he died, because he already knew how it ends. The cross wasn't a defeat. It was the win. So when your life gets hard, you're not waiting to find out if Jesus is strong enough. He already won. You're just living inside the victory.
- Close the whole talk. This is where the Farewell Discourse has been heading. It started with "let not your hearts be troubled," and it lands here: take heart, I have overcome the world. The peace was never in your circumstances. It was always in him.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus has already overcome the world, so his people can have real peace in him in the middle of real trouble.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "'In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world.' Which half do you need more today, and why?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep John 16:33 sharp; it stays in the rolling memory quizzes. Read John 17:1-5 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 16:33 joins the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 22: we gather everything Jesus said about the Holy Spirit across this whole talk into one lesson.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. This is the memory-verse plant, previewed at the close of L6 so today is a second exposure, not a cold meeting. Spend the time on the fresh angle and the first memorization pass.
- Hold both halves of 16:33: tribulation promised AND victory finished. Guard hard against flattening it into "everything will be fine."
- "I have overcome" is a perfect tense: a completed victory that still stands. Ground the plant in that, and in the answer to 14:1 (the discourse's comfort bookend).
- The peace is "in me," located in Christ's person, not in circumstances.
- Derive the frame from sermon 28; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Closes the unit and hands off to U22 (the Paraclete synthesis). Also models the Q3 project skill: land the chapter on Christ.
