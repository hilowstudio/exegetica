**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the breakfast Jesus prepared (21:9-13).
2. Students will explain the charcoal-fire echo of Peter's denial (21:9 with 18:18).
3. Students will explain that Jesus cared for the disciples before addressing failure.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the risen Jesus made his friends breakfast.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus tends people's needs before their failures.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the charcoal fire (18:18 into 21:9), the place of denial becoming the place of grace.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Come and have breakfast" (John 21:12).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I've done wrong, do I expect God to be cold to me? Why?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 21:9-14 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 21, verses 9 through 14.
- One-sentence setup: the disciples wade ashore, dripping, and find breakfast already cooking.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 9, what do the disciples find waiting on the shore? In verse 12, what does Jesus invite them to do?
   - Cold-call check: a charcoal fire with fish and bread cooking; he says, "come and have breakfast."
2. **Core (the key detail).** Look carefully at "charcoal fire" in verse 9. There's only one other charcoal fire in the entire New Testament. Where was it (look back at John 18:18)?
   - Guide them: the only other charcoal fire is the one Peter stood at, warming himself, when he denied Jesus three times on the night of the arrest (18:18). John uses the exact same rare word on purpose. Jesus builds a charcoal fire, the very kind of fire Peter failed at.
3. **Core.** Why would Jesus deliberately build a fire that would remind Peter of his worst night? Is that cruel, or is something else going on?
   - Land it: it's not cruel; it's healing. Jesus takes Peter back to the scene of his failure, on purpose, so he can heal it there. The place of the denial becomes the place of the restoration. Jesus doesn't avoid Peter's wound. He walks straight to it, and he brings breakfast. He's getting ready to give Peter back everything the denial took.
4. **Core.** Before Jesus deals with Peter's failure at all (that comes next lesson), what does he do first? What does that order tell you about him?
   - Draw out: he feeds them. He cooks breakfast and serves it before he brings up a single thing about the denial. He tends their cold, wet, hungry bodies first. That's the order of his care: he meets you in your need before he deals with your failure. Grace comes to the table before the hard conversation.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** This is the third time the risen Jesus has appeared to his disciples (verse 14). Notice how he keeps showing up: filling nets, cooking breakfast, serving. What kind of risen King is this?
   - Draw out: a King who serves. The Lord of the universe, raised in glory, spends his time making breakfast for tired fishermen on a beach. He doesn't come back to be waited on; he comes back to feed his people. The risen Christ is still the one who washes feet and serves.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's the charcoal fire, and what does Jesus do before addressing Peter's failure?
   - It echoes the fire of Peter's denial (18:18); Jesus feeds them breakfast first.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the fire. John gives you one word that changes the whole scene: charcoal fire. The only other one in the New Testament is the fire where Peter said "I don't know him," three times, in the dark. And now the risen Jesus builds one on the beach. On purpose. He takes Peter right back to the smell of his worst night, so he can heal it there.
- Land the order. And before he says one word about the denial, he cooks breakfast and serves it. He feeds the men who ran. That's the order of grace: he meets your need before he deals with your sin. He doesn't make you grovel before he'll care for you. He hands you breakfast first.
- Reframe how they picture God. Some of you assume that when you've done wrong, God goes cold and distant, arms crossed, waiting for you to earn your way back. Look at this beach. The risen Lord cooks breakfast for the ones who failed him and says, "come and eat." That's who he actually is toward you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the risen Christ meets his failures at the fire of their failure, and feeds them before he heals them.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus fed the ones who failed him before he ever mentioned it. What does that change about how you think God feels about you when you've messed up?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 21:15-17 once before Wednesday. The last memory verse is coming.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: the restoration, and your final memory verse, "do you love me."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The load-bearing detail: the charcoal fire (anthrakia) appears only at 18:18 (Peter's denial) and 21:9 (the restoration). It's a grounded two-occurrence echo, not a flourish; John's most tender literary touch. Surface it plainly so it pays off in L4.
- The order of care: Jesus feeds them before addressing failure (Matthew Henry: he would not spoil their breakfast; tenderness toward penitents). Grace to the table first.
- The risen King who serves (still cooking breakfast, still washing feet).
- Fresh-build; frame it richly.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
