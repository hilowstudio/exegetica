# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 9 Lesson 7 of 7: where else would we go? (John 6:60-71)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U9 Bread of Life |
| Lesson | 7 of 7 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Nov 4 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 6:60-71 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The teaching is too hard, most of the crowd leaves, and when Jesus asks the twelve if they'll go too, Peter says there's nowhere else to go for the words of life. |
| Christ connection | Jesus has the words of eternal life, and those the Father keeps stay with him even when the crowd walks away. |
| Memory verse | John 6:35 was planted at L4. Review it, do not re-plant. Keep the five earlier anchors sharp too. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega, "Who Will Eat the Bread of Life?" (John 6:60-71). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus means that "the flesh is no help at all" and "the words are spirit and life" (6:63).
2. Students will describe the mass defection (6:66) and Peter's confession (6:68-69).
3. Students will explain how Jesus handles knowing his betrayer (6:70-71).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the teaching gets hard and most people quit. Following Jesus can cost something.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Peter stays because there's nowhere else to get the words of life.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the defection as a sifting; Judas held inside God's plan (Acts 4:28).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life" (John 6:68).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What makes following Jesus hard for me right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest reason you'd want to walk away, if you have one.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 6:60-71 aloud, one reads 60 through 65, the other 66 through 71.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 6, verses 60 through 71.
- One-sentence setup: the bread teaching was too hard for the crowd, and now we see who stays.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 60, what do many of the disciples say about Jesus' teaching? In verse 66, what do they do?
   - Cold-call check: they call it "a hard saying" and ask who can listen to it. Then many of them turn back and stop following him.
2. **Entry.** Have you ever quit something because it got harder than you expected? What made it hard?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 63. Jesus says, "the flesh is no help at all. The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life." Look back at last class. How does verse 63 solve the confusion about "eating his flesh"?
   - Guide them: Jesus says a literal, physical eating gets you nothing. His words, received by the Spirit, are where the life is. So the "eating" was always about taking his words in by faith.
4. **Core.** Read verses 67 through 69. Jesus asks the twelve if they want to leave too. Peter says, "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life." Peter doesn't fully understand the teaching yet, and he stays. Why?
   - Land it: Peter has tasted something real. He knows there's nowhere else to go for the words of life. He stays because Jesus is worth staying for, even when it's hard and confusing. That's faith, not full understanding.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verse 70 Jesus says one of the twelve is "a devil," meaning Judas. Jesus chose Judas knowing he'd betray him. Read Acts 4:27-28. How can Jesus be in complete control even over his own betrayer?
   - Draw out: nothing about the betrayal caught Jesus off guard. God folded even Judas and the cross into the plan that saves us (Acts 4:28). If the worst thing ever done, betraying and killing the Son of God, was inside God's plan to rescue us, then nothing done to you or by you is outside his reach either.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: when the crowd left, why did Peter stay?
   - Because Jesus has the words of eternal life, and there's nowhere else to go.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the whole unit. Build on sermon 09.

- Name the sifting honestly. The crowd was huge until Jesus taught the hard part, and then it got small. Following Jesus costs something, and some people walk when it does. Don't dress that up.
- Point to why Peter stayed. He didn't stay because he was scared to leave or because he understood everything. He stayed because he'd tasted the words of life and knew there was nowhere else to get them. When Jesus stops being merely useful and starts being demanding, the ones who've really tasted him stay.
- Speak the comfort under the hard doctrine. The ones who stay are the ones the Father gave to the Son (6:37, 6:65), and Jesus loses none of them. If you find yourself still wanting him when it's hard, that wanting is a gift, and it's proof you're held.
- Handle Judas without fear. Jesus wasn't blindsided. Even the betrayal was inside God's saving plan. Don't turn this into anxiety about your own heart. Turn it into confidence that nothing is outside his control.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus has the words of eternal life, and the ones the Father keeps stay with him even when the crowd leaves.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Peter stayed because there was nowhere else to go for the words of life. When following Jesus gets hard for you, what makes you want to stay?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing John 6:35 and your other memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 6:35 stays in the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up Unit 10: Jesus goes up to the Feast of Booths, and the crowds argue about whether he's the Christ.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 09. Teach the defection (6:66) as a sifting that reveals real faith, and land on Peter's "to whom shall we go." Do not use the mass exit to scare students into staying. Fear does not regenerate. Peter stays because he's tasted the words of life.
- Handle Judas (6:70-71) with sermon 09's two answers: Scripture was fulfilled, and no evil falls outside God's redemptive plan (Acts 4:28). Guard it hard against a student's paranoia about their own election. The point is God's control, not the student's anxiety.
- Let the exclusivity comfort. A God who chooses is uncomfortable until he is your comfort. The by-name love that set itself on you before you existed is what every student is actually hungry for. That's the warmth under the hard doctrine.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block. This closes Unit 9.
