# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 27 Lesson 5 of 6: follow me (John 21:18-19)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U27 Do you love me |
| Lesson | 5 of 6 |
| Date / Day type | Thu May 13 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus |
| Passage | John 21:18-19 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus tells the man who once denied him that he will one day die faithful, glorifying God, and gives him two words: "follow me." |
| Christ connection | Christ who tells the restored man he will glorify God even in death, and calls him to follow. |

| Resources | Paired sermon: 40, "Jesus and Peter" (John 21:15-25). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain Jesus' prediction of Peter's death (21:18).
2. Students will explain that Peter's death would glorify God (21:19).
3. Students will explain "follow me" as the call to discipleship, even at a cost.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the man who denied Jesus would one day die faithful.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): following Jesus can cost everything, and it's grace.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Peter's foretold martyrdom (the Spirit changed the coward); "follow me" as the last word.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep it warm. One clear passage, taught well.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Follow me." (John 21:19).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where does following Jesus cost me something right now?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 21:18-19 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 21, verses 18 and 19.
- One-sentence setup: right after restoring Peter, Jesus tells him how his story will end.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: the death foretold, and "follow me." Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 18, what does Jesus say will happen to Peter when he's old? In verse 19, what does John tell us that meant?
   - Cold-call check: Peter will stretch out his hands and be led where he doesn't want to go; John says this signified the kind of death by which Peter would glorify God (he would be martyred).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Remember what Peter once bragged (John 13:37): "I will lay down my life for you." And then he denied Jesus three times. Now Jesus tells him he really will die for him one day. How is that prediction grace, not doom?
   - Guide them: it's grace because it means Peter's failure isn't the end of his story. The man who couldn't admit he knew Jesus to a servant girl will one day die faithfully professing him. Jesus is telling the restored Peter: you really will be faithful to the end, all the way to death. The denial won't get the last word. That's not doom; that's a promise that grace wins.
3. **Core.** How does a coward who denied Jesus at a fire become a man who dies for him? What changed?
   - Land it: the Spirit changed him. At Pentecost, the risen Jesus poured out his Spirit, and the same Peter who folded in fear became bold enough to preach and eventually to die for Jesus. It wasn't Peter getting tougher on his own. It was God changing him from the inside. Which means your failures aren't the final measure of you either; God can make weak, failing people faithful.
4. **Core.** Read the last two words Jesus says in verse 19: "follow me." That's the last thing Jesus says to Peter here, and nearly the last thing in the whole Gospel. What is Jesus asking, and what might it cost?
   - Draw out: he's asking Peter to follow him, all the way, even to a cross like the one Jesus died on. Following Jesus isn't a casual thing; it can cost you comfort, reputation, and even your life. And Jesus asks for it anyway, from the man he just restored. Restored, and called to follow, whatever it costs.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did Jesus predict for Peter, and what did he call him to do?
   - That Peter would die faithful, glorifying God; and he called Peter to "follow me."

Circulate. Keep it warm.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the grace in the prediction. Jesus has just restored Peter, and then he tells him how he'll die: faithful, glorifying God, stretching out his hands like Jesus did. Think about what that says to a man haunted by his denial. Jesus is promising: you won't fail at the end. The coward at the fire will die a hero of the faith. Your worst moment does not decide your story.
- Land what changed him. And how does that happen? Not by Peter white-knuckling it into bravery. The Spirit came at Pentecost and changed him. God took a man who denied Jesus to a servant girl and made him bold enough to die for him. If God can do that with Peter, your failures are not the final word on you either.
- Land "follow me." And then Jesus says the two words that end the Gospel: follow me. Not "admire me," not "agree with me." Follow me, wherever I go, whatever it costs. That's the invitation the whole book has been building to, and it's for you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus tells the restored failure he will die faithful, and calls him, and us, to follow.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "'Follow me,' whatever it costs. Where is Jesus asking you to follow him somewhere that costs you something?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 21:17. Read John 21:20-25 once before Friday, the last passage.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: the last scene, "you follow me," and the end of the book.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One passage, taught warmly: the death foretold as grace, and "follow me."
- Two things to land: Peter's foretold faithful death is grace (the failure doesn't get the last word; the Spirit changed the coward at Pentecost), and "follow me" as the costly call to discipleship (13:37 finally made true).
- Keep it hopeful, not grim. The point of the prediction is that grace wins, not that following is bleak.
