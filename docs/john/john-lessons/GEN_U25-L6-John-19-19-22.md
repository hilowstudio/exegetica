**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what the inscription said and in how many languages (19:19-20).
2. Students will explain Pilate's refusal to change it (19:22).
3. Students will explain how the cross is Jesus' throne and the sign his royal title.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a sign over the cross announced Jesus as King.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the true title of Christ preached to the whole world from the cross.
- **Stretch** (disciple): God's sovereignty working through Pilate's spite.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews" (John 19:19).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If a sign told the truth about who Jesus is to me, what would it say?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:19-22 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 19 through 22.
- One-sentence setup: Pilate writes a sign for the cross, and the leaders don't like it.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 19 and 20, what does the sign over the cross say, and in how many languages was it written?
   - Cold-call check: "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews," in three languages (Aramaic/Hebrew, Latin, and Greek).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Why three languages? Think about who spoke each one back then (Hebrew/Aramaic, Latin, Greek). Who was meant to be able to read this sign?
   - Guide them: Hebrew was the language of the Jewish religion, Latin was the language of the Roman empire, and Greek was the common language of the whole culture. So the sign could be read by basically everyone who passed by, from every world. Pilate meant it as a taunt, but it ended up announcing Jesus as King to religion, empire, and culture all at once. The true title of Christ, preached to the whole world, from the cross.
3. **Core.** Read verses 21 and 22. The chief priests want the sign changed. What do they want it to say instead, and how does Pilate respond?
   - Land it: they want "he SAID he was king," to make it a false claim. Pilate refuses: "What I have written I have written." He won't budge. And so the true statement stands, nailed over the cross for everyone to read.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Pilate isn't trying to serve God here. He's being stubborn and petty, getting one last jab in at the leaders who pushed him around. Yet the result is that the true title of Jesus gets proclaimed to the nations. What does that show about how God works?
   - Draw out: God is so completely in control that he uses even a spiteful pagan governor's stubbornness to preach the gospel. Pilate thought he was winning a personal argument. God used him to publish "Jesus is King" to the whole world, over the very cross where the King reigned. God doesn't need willing servants to accomplish his purposes. He rules even the unwilling ones.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what was proclaimed over the cross, and to whom?
   - "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews," in three languages, to the whole world.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Read the sign as truth. Pilate nailed a mockery over the cross, and it turned out to be the plainest truth in the world: this is the King. Not "was." Is. Right there, bleeding, dying, reigning. The cross is his throne, and the sign is his royal title, and it's written so everyone can read it.
- Land the reach. And it's in three languages on purpose, so no one is left out. The religious, the powerful, the ordinary, everyone who walks by can read who Jesus is. The King is proclaimed to the whole world from the cross. That includes you.
- Marvel at the control. And notice who wrote it: a stubborn pagan who wasn't trying to help God at all. God used Pilate's pettiness to preach Christ. If God can do that with Pilate, there's no one and nothing he can't bend to his purposes, including the hard things in your life.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the King is proclaimed to the whole world from his throne, the cross, and even his enemies end up telling the truth about him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The sign called Jesus King, and it was true. Is he actually King of your life, or just a claim you'd make on paper?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: the true title proclaimed to the whole world (three languages = religion, empire, culture), and God's sovereignty working through Pilate's spite ("what I have written I have written"). The kingship-of-Christ irony continues here: the cross is the throne.
- Anti-Judaism guard: the priests' objection is a specific leadership's; keep the guilt on human sin, not a people.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly and reverently.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
