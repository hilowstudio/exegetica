**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain Jesus' invitation on the last day of the feast (7:37).
2. Students will state what the "living water" is (7:39, the Holy Spirit).
3. Students will explain how a person "drinks" (comes to Jesus and believes, by faith).
4. Students will describe the division Jesus' words cause (7:40-44).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus offers to satisfy a thirst nothing else reaches.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you come and drink by faith, no cleaning yourself up first.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the water ceremony and the eighth-day silence; the water is the Spirit (7:39).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the summit of the unit, so protect the Work block and Move 4.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink" (John 7:37).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I most thirsty for, deep down?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the thirst you've never been able to fill.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 7:37-44 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 7, verses 37 through 44.
- One-sentence setup: it's the last and biggest day of the feast, and Jesus stands up in the temple and shouts.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Set the scene (30 seconds, then ask).** All week at this feast, priests poured out water at the temple every morning, remembering how God gave Israel water in the desert and praying for the Messiah. On the last day, the pouring stopped. Into that silence, Jesus stands up.
2. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 37, what does Jesus shout to the crowd? What's the invitation?
   - Cold-call check: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink." Anyone. Come to me.
3. **Entry.** Think about a thirst that a drink of water can't fix, the ache to be loved, to matter, to be okay. Where do people try to satisfy that?
4. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 39. Jesus talked about "rivers of living water." What does John tell us the living water actually is?
   - Guide them: the Holy Spirit. Not a vague feeling, not good vibes. God himself, poured into a person. And John says it would come after Jesus was "glorified," meaning after the cross and resurrection.
5. **Core.** How does a person "drink" this water? Look at verse 38, "whoever believes in me." What does drinking turn out to be?
   - Land it: drinking is coming to Jesus and believing. You don't earn the water or qualify for it. You come thirsty and you trust him, and the Spirit fills you. It's a gift you receive with empty hands.
6. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Two kinds of people refuse this water. One keeps drinking dirty water, chasing sin to fill the thirst. The other refuses to come back to Jesus until they've cleaned themselves up first. What does Jesus say to both?
   - Draw out: the same thing. Come and drink. You don't fix yourself first and then earn a drink. You come thirsty, as you are, and keep coming. The self-punishing "I'll come back when I'm better" is its own way of refusing the water.
7. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what is the living water, and how do you get it?
   - It's the Holy Spirit, and you get it by coming to Jesus and believing.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the heart of sermon 12.

- Set the moment. All week the water poured at the temple. On the last day it went silent, and the people were aching for God to send the promised rescuer. Right then, Jesus stands up and shouts that the water they've been waiting for is him.
- Name the thirst honestly. You've been thirsty your whole life, and you've tried to fill it with screens, relationships, achievement, being liked. Jesus says the thirst has an address. Come to me and drink.
- Make the invitation as wide as Jesus made it. "If anyone thirsts." No qualifications. You don't clean up first. You come dirty and thirsty and you drink, and the Spirit fills you and starts to flow out of you. And then you keep coming, every day, to the same fountain.
- Offer the stretch verse. John 7:37-38 is worth carrying if a student wants it, off the quiz. Put it on the board: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. Whoever believes in me, out of his heart will flow rivers of living water."
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the living water, and everyone who comes to him thirsty gets the Spirit, flowing.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says the thirst you've been trying to fill has an address: him. What have you been drinking instead, and what would it look like to come to him thirsty this week?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 7:45-52 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. No new required memory verse. John 7:37-38 is an optional stretch.
- Sets up L5: the guards sent to arrest Jesus come back empty, and Nicodemus speaks up.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 12. Teach the water ceremony and the eighth-day silence (the daily pouring stops, and Jesus stands up into the quiet). It makes the whole scene land.
- Hold the ruling: the living water is the Spirit (John says so, 7:39), and Christ is the fountain. Don't let "rivers of living water" drift into a vague feelings thing.
- Guard the close hard against a legalist side door. The invitation is "come and drink," by faith alone. The believer's overflow is the Spirit's work. Sermon 12's whole burden is that you do not clean yourself up before you drink, and you keep coming daily.
- Offer 7:37-38 as an optional stretch, off the graded line.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day. Protect Move 4, the summit of the unit.
