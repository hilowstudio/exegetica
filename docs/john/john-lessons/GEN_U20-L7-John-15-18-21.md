**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the world hates Jesus' followers (union with him, 15:18-19).
2. Students will explain "a servant is not greater than his master" (15:20).
3. Students will explain that the hatred comes "on account of my name" (15:21).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): following Jesus can cost you socially.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the hatred is for belonging to Jesus, not for being difficult.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the servant-master line echoing 13:16, now turned toward suffering.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "A servant is not greater than his master" (John 15:20).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where does following Jesus feel costly or awkward for me?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest about the social cost.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 15:18-21 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 18 through 21.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus stops comforting for a moment and gets honest about what following him will cost.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 18, if the world hates Jesus' followers, what does Jesus say happened first?
   - Cold-call check: the world hated him first. The hatred lands on them because it landed on him.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 19. Why does the world love its own but hate Jesus' people? What made the difference?
   - Guide them: Jesus chose them out of the world, so they don't belong to it anymore. The world loves what's its own. Once you belong to Jesus, you stand out, and that's what draws the hatred. It's about whose you are.
3. **Core.** Read verse 20: "a servant is not greater than his master." You heard that line before, back in chapter 13 with the foot-washing. Here Jesus uses it differently. What's his point now?
   - Land it: back in 13:16 it meant no job is beneath you if Jesus did it. Here it means if they came after your master, they'll come after you too. You're not above your Lord, so you shouldn't be shocked to share his treatment. If they persecuted him, they'll persecute his people.
4. **Core.** Read verse 21. What's the reason Jesus gives for why the world treats his followers this way?
   - Draw out: "on account of my name," because they don't know the One who sent Jesus. The hatred is about him. His people catch it because they belong to him.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** There's a big difference between being hated because you belong to Jesus and getting pushback because you're being a jerk about your faith. How do you tell them apart, and which one does verse 21 talk about?
   - Draw out: verse 21 is hatred "on account of my name," for belonging to Jesus and living like him, which was gentle, humble, and loving. Getting disliked for being rude, smug, or obnoxious is just the fallout of acting that way, and Jesus is talking about something else here. The test: are they rejecting Christ in you, or reacting to something ugly in how you acted? A servant follows a master who was hated while doing nothing but good.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why does the world hate Jesus' followers, according to verse 21?
   - On account of his name, because they belong to Jesus, whom the world rejected.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Tell them the truth Jesus told. Following him can cost you. Friends, status, comfort. He didn't hide it from his disciples, and it's not fair to hide it from you. If the world turns on you for being his, you're in good company. It turned on him first.
- Explain why it stings less than it looks. A servant isn't greater than his master. Jesus was perfect, kind, and healing people, and they still hated him. So when it lands on you for belonging to him, it's not a sign something's wrong. It's a sign you're actually with him.
- Guard the honest line. This is never a license to be obnoxious and then call the pushback persecution. The hatred Jesus means comes from belonging to him and living like him, which looked like humility and love. If people are reacting to you being a jerk, that's on you, not on Jesus.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus was hated first, and his people share his rejection because they belong to a hated and gentle King.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Is there a place you hide that you follow Jesus because of what it might cost you? Write honestly about it."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 15:5. Read John 15:22-27 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L8: why the world has no excuse, and the Spirit who bears witness to Christ.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is a sermon-less fresh-build. Frame it richly, and keep the tone honest and warm, not alarmist.
- The key guard: hatred "on account of my name" (15:21), borne as Jesus bore it. Guard hard against weaponizing this into a persecution complex or culture-war posture. A servant is not greater than his master, and the master was gentle.
- 15:20 echoes 13:16 (the foot-washing line), now turned toward suffering. Name the callback for the disciple tier.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
