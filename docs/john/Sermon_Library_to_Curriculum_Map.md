# Sermon Library to Curriculum Map
## Pairing the 40 John sermons with the lesson units

This links every unit in the scope-and-sequence to the sermon or sermons that cover the same text. It records three things: the seams you actually cut when preaching (your natural passage lengths), the emphasis worth borrowing, and the passages where this set has no transcript, so we know where we build fresh.

Emphasis notes marked **(read)** come from sermons I have read in full or in part. The rest are drawn from your sermon titles, which function as your big-idea statements, and will be deepened when we read that sermon in full at the time we build the unit.

---

## Your own pacing rules, from the transcripts

- One interaction, one sitting. A single dialogue is not split across sermons, because that is how the first hearers received it. (Lifted Up, 12:27-36)
- Verse count bows to the text. Thirty-nine verses one week, two the next. A two-verse passage on prayer can hold a whole hour. (Ask, 14:12-14)
- When a chapter is too long for sentence-by-sentence, organize it around Jesus' central statements. (The Light of the World, 8:12-59)
- Recap generously. Nearly every sermon reopens by resituating the hearer in the ongoing dialogue.
- Land in worship and mission. Re-preach the gospel at the close rather than leaving an imperative hanging.

These become construction rules for the lessons: subdivide a discourse across lessons if needed, but never end a lesson on a severed thought, always reopen by resituating, and land every unit on Christ.

## Working method

When we build a unit, I read its paired sermon or sermons in full at that moment, and mine them for the big idea, the illustrations, the memorable lines, and the exact framing. That keeps your emphases sharp rather than compressed into a lossy summary now. For the starred fresh-build passages, we talk through your framing before I draft.
