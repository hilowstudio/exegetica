# Reading guide, Unit 24: Arrest and trial

> Passage: John 18:1-40
> Big question: Jesus is betrayed, arrested, and put on trial through the night. Watch closely: who's actually in control?
> No new memory verse this unit. Optional stretch verse: John 18:37.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

We're leaving the upper room and stepping into the Passion, the story of Jesus' death. The prayer of John 17 is over. Now Judas leads soldiers into the garden, and the arrest and trials begin. This is heavy, sober ground, so we walk it with reverence. And watch one thing the whole way through: everything looks like it's happening TO Jesus, but he's the one in command of all of it. A quick word before you start: when John says "the Jews" in these chapters, he means the specific leaders driving this, the chief priests and their officers. Jesus, all his disciples, and the first church were themselves Jews. This story is about human sin and God's rescue plan, and it never gives anyone a reason to look down on Jewish people.

## Lesson 1: the arrest, and "I am he" (John 18:1-11)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 1 through 11.

Judas brings a band of soldiers with torches and weapons to arrest Jesus in the garden. Jesus steps forward and asks who they want. They say "Jesus of Nazareth." He says, "I am he," and the whole armed crowd draws back and falls to the ground. Peter swings a sword, and Jesus stops him and speaks of the cup the Father has given him.

- **Observe.** In verse 6, what happens when Jesus says "I am he"? In verse 8, what does Jesus ask the soldiers to do for his disciples?
- **Interpret.** An armed squad comes to arrest one unarmed man, and his words knock them to the ground. Who's really in control of this arrest? Could Jesus have walked away?
- **Respond.** Jesus says, "shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?" He's choosing to go to the cross. What does it mean to you that Jesus wasn't trapped, that he went on purpose?

## Lesson 2: bound and led away (John 18:12-18)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 12 through 18.

The soldiers bind Jesus and take him to Annas. John reminds us that Caiaphas had said it was better for one man to die for the people. Meanwhile Peter follows at a distance, warming himself at a fire, and a servant girl asks if he's a disciple. He says, "I am not."

- **Observe.** In verse 14, what had Caiaphas said earlier about one man dying? In verse 17, what does Peter say when the servant girl asks about him?
- **Interpret.** Caiaphas meant his words as cold politics, one man killed to protect their position. But he accidentally said the gospel: one man dying in the place of the people. How is that exactly what Jesus is about to do?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Peter's first denial happens by a fire while Jesus is being bound. Hold Peter's "I am not" next to Jesus' "I am he" from the last lesson. What's the contrast?

## Lesson 3: the night trial (John 18:19-24)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 19 through 24.

Annas questions Jesus about his teaching. Jesus answers that he's always taught openly, in public, and says to go ask the people who heard him. An officer slaps him for the way he answered. Jesus asks, calmly, why he'd be struck for telling the truth.

- **Observe.** In verse 20, where does Jesus say he taught? In verse 23, how does Jesus respond to being slapped?
- **Interpret.** Jesus points out he's got nothing to hide, everything was said in the open, while this trial happens in secret in the middle of the night. What does it tell you that they need a secret, rigged trial to condemn a man who taught in public?
- **Respond.** When Jesus told the truth and they had no answer, they hit him. Where do you see that same reflex in yourself, getting defensive or nasty when someone says something true you don't want to hear?

## Lesson 4: the rooster crows (John 18:25-27)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 25 through 27.

Peter, still at the fire, is asked twice more if he's a disciple. Twice more he denies it, the last time to a relative of the man whose ear he cut off. Right then, a rooster crows, exactly as Jesus said it would.

- **Observe.** In verse 27, what happens right after Peter's third denial? What did that fulfill (look back at John 13:38)?
- **Interpret.** Peter, who swore he'd die for Jesus, can't even admit he knows him to a servant girl. Jesus knew this would happen and told Peter in advance. Is this the end of Peter's story? (Look ahead: in John 21, Jesus meets Peter at another fire and restores him.)
- **Respond.** Peter failed badly, and Jesus loved him anyway and took him back. Where have you failed in a way that makes you feel disqualified? What does Peter's story say to that?

## Lesson 5: handed to Pilate (John 18:28-32)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 28 through 32.

They lead Jesus to Pilate, the Roman governor. The leaders won't go inside Pilate's headquarters, because entering a Gentile's house would make them ceremonially unclean and unable to eat the Passover. They want Jesus dead, but they need Rome to do it, because they can't legally execute anyone themselves.

- **Observe.** In verse 28, why won't the leaders go inside? In verse 31, what do they say they aren't allowed to do?
- **Interpret.** Look at the irony in verse 28: they're so careful about staying religiously "clean" for the feast, while they're in the middle of arranging an innocent man's death. How can someone be so precise about outward religion and so blind to what their heart is actually doing?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 32 says they couldn't execute Jesus themselves "to fulfill the word Jesus had spoken about the kind of death he'd die." Stoning was the Jewish way; crucifixion was Roman. How did their hands being tied make sure Jesus was "lifted up" on a cross, exactly as he foretold (12:32-33)?

## Lesson 6: my kingdom is not of this world (John 18:33-37)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 33 through 37.

Pilate asks Jesus if he's the King of the Jews. Jesus says his kingdom "is not of this world," which is why his followers aren't fighting to free him. He tells Pilate he came into the world to bear witness to the truth, and that everyone who belongs to the truth listens to his voice.

- **Observe.** In verse 36, how does Jesus describe his kingdom? In verse 37, why does Jesus say he came into the world?
- **Interpret.** "My kingdom is not of this world" doesn't mean Jesus doesn't care about this world, or that his kingdom is just floating in the sky somewhere. It means his kingdom comes from God and doesn't run on armies and force. How is a kingdom that spreads by truth and love different from every kingdom Pilate knew?
- **Respond.** Jesus says everyone who belongs to the truth listens to his voice. Do you? Where is his voice hard for you to listen to right now?
- **Optional stretch verse.** If you want to carry one more, learn John 18:37: "For this purpose I was born and for this purpose I have come into the world, to bear witness to the truth. Everyone who is of the truth listens to my voice." It's optional and off the grade.

## Lesson 7: what is truth? (John 18:38)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verse 38.

Pilate asks Jesus, "What is truth?" And then he doesn't wait for an answer. He walks straight back outside and tells the crowd, "I find no guilt in him."

- **Observe.** In verse 38, what does Pilate ask Jesus? Does he stay for the answer? What verdict does he give about Jesus?
- **Interpret.** Pilate asks "What is truth?" while standing right in front of the one who said "I am the way, and the truth, and the life" (14:6). He's inches from the answer and won't wait for it. What's the difference between honestly searching for truth and using "who can really know?" as an excuse to not deal with Jesus?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Pilate says three separate times in John that he finds no guilt in Jesus (here, and 19:4, 6). Why does John keep stressing that Jesus is innocent? How does that matter for what the cross means?

## Lesson 8: Barabbas (John 18:39-40)

**Read it.** John chapter 18, verses 39 through 40.

Pilate offers to release one prisoner for the Passover and asks if they want "the King of the Jews." The crowd shouts for Barabbas instead, a violent man, a robber and (the other Gospels tell us) a murderer. So the guilty man goes free, and the innocent one is sent to die.

- **Observe.** In verse 40, who does the crowd choose instead of Jesus? What kind of man was Barabbas?
- **Interpret.** Watch the swap: a guilty, violent man walks out free, and the innocent Jesus takes his place under a death sentence. That's not just a sad twist. It's a picture of the whole gospel. How is Barabbas going free because Jesus takes his place a picture of what Jesus does for you?
- **Respond.** Barabbas literally walked away free because Jesus was condemned in his place. If that's what the cross does for you, an innocent Jesus taking the sentence you earned, how do you respond to that?

## Key words to know

- **The cup.** The suffering and the wrath Jesus took on the cross, handed to him by the Father. He chose to drink it (18:11).
- **"I am he."** Jesus' answer in the garden (18:5-6). The same "I am" of his earlier claims, and it drops an armed squad to the ground. He's God, and he's in control.
- **The praetorium.** The Roman governor's headquarters, where Pilate held the trial.
- **Substitution.** The guilty going free because the innocent takes their place. Barabbas (18:40) is a live picture of it, and it's the heart of the cross.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus is arrested, bound, slapped, and tried through the night, and he's the steadiest, most in-command person in every room. His word knocks the arresting squad to the ground. He goes because he chose to drink the Father's cup. He tells the truth openly while his judges plot in the dark. He owns a kingdom that comes from God and runs on truth, not swords. Pilate declares him innocent, and the crowd sends him to die in place of a guilty man. The King goes to his throne by way of a cross, and every step of it is under his own control.
