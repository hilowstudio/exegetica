# Reading guide, Unit 1: The Word

> Course: Sophomore Bible, the Gospel of John: Yorkville Christian School  
> Passage range: John 1:1-18 (read across four lessons)  
> Memory verse: John 1:14 (ESV)  
> The big question this unit asks: **Who is Jesus, and why does it matter that God became a man you could see?**  
> Scripture quoted in the ESV.

---

## How to use this guide

This is a follow-along guide, not homework to grind through at home. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we read and talk in class.

- **Read** the passage first. Every block tells you where to find it.
- **Observe, interpret, respond.** Jot short answers in your Journal. We'll build them out together in discussion.
- **Go deeper** prompts are marked *optional*. Take them if you want more.

> **What a good observation looks like:** it points to actual words in the text. "Verse 3 says everything was made through him" is an observation. "I like this part" is a reaction. Start with what the text says, then move to what it means.

**Can't find John yet?** John is the fourth book in the New Testament, right after Matthew, Mark, and Luke. Use the table of contents in the front of your Bible. Big number = chapter, small number = verse.

## Lesson 2: The Word: God, and the maker of everything (John 1:1-5)

**Read it:** John 1:1-5.

> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made. In him was life, and the life was the light of men. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." (John 1:1-5, ESV)

Verse 1 makes three claims, in order. Slow down and take them one at a time.
- "In the beginning was the Word": he already existed when the beginning began. He was never made.
- "The Word was with God": he's a distinct person, face to face with the Father.
- "The Word was God": he is himself fully God.

**Observe:**

5. According to verse 3, how much was made through him? Quote the words.
6. Verse 5 says the light shines where? And what has the darkness *not* done to it?

**Interpret:** Some people read verse 1 as "the Word was *a* god," a lesser being. Look at verse 3: everything that was made was made through him. Can the one who made all created things be a created thing himself? Why does verse 3 settle the question?

7. ____________________________________________

**Respond:** "The light shines in the darkness" is present tense. The light is shining now, in a world that's genuinely dark, and John says the darkness hasn't overcome it. Where do you most want that to be true?

8. ____________________________________________

> *Go deeper (optional):* Hold onto "light." Later Jesus says "I am the light of the world" (John 8:12). John plants the word here so you'll recognize it when Jesus claims it.

## Lesson 3: Received or rejected (John 1:6-13)

**Read it:** John 1:6-13.

> "There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. He came as a witness, to bear witness about the light, that all might believe through him. He was not the light, but came to bear witness about the light. The true light, which gives light to everyone, was coming into the world. He was in the world, and the world was made through him, yet the world did not know him. He came to his own, and his own people did not receive him. But to all who did receive him, who believed in his name, he gave the right to become children of God, who were born, not of blood nor of the will of the flesh nor of the will of man, but of God." (John 1:6-13, ESV)

Heads-up on names: the "John" in verses 6-8 is **John the Baptist**, a witness who points to Jesus. He's a different person from John who wrote this Gospel.

**Observe:**

9. What was John the Baptist's job (verses 7-8)? What was he careful to say he was *not*?
10. Verses 10-11 name two groups that didn't receive him. Who were they?

**Interpret:** Verse 12 offers something huge: to all who received him, he gave the right to become children of God. Then verse 13 explains how that happens. According to verse 13, who does the "birthing" that makes someone a child of God?

11. ____________________________________________

**Respond:** John is honest that many people rejected the light (verse 11) before he tells you some received it (verse 12). Does it change anything for you that the Bible names the rejection out loud?

12. ____________________________________________

> *Go deeper (optional):* Verse 13 says becoming God's child is a birth God gives, not something you arrange. In three weeks Jesus says this to a religious leader named Nicodemus at night (John 3:3-8). Read John 3:3-8 and watch the same idea come back.

## Lesson 4: The Word became flesh (John 1:14-18)

**Read it:** John 1:14-18. This is the peak the whole Prologue has been climbing toward.

> "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth. (John bore witness about him, and cried out, "This was he of whom I said, 'He who comes after me ranks before me, because he was before me.'") For from his fullness we have all received, grace upon grace. For the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ. No one has ever seen God; God the only Son, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known." (John 1:14-18, ESV)

Two words worth knowing here:
- **"Became flesh"** means he became truly and fully human, a body you could touch, while staying fully God.
- **"Dwelt among us"** translates a word that means "pitched a tent." Back in Exodus, God's glory came down and filled the tent so he could live in the middle of his people. Jesus is that, in person. God moved into the neighborhood in a body.

**Observe:**

13. Verse 14 says "we have seen his glory." Who is the "we," and what does it tell you about how John knew this?
14. In verse 18, what has no one ever done? And who makes God known?

**Interpret:** Verse 17 says "the law was given through Moses; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ." This isn't putting Moses down. The same God gave both, and what Moses served in shadow, Jesus brought in person. What do you think "grace upon grace" (verse 16) means about how God gives?

15. ____________________________________________

**Respond:** "He came close enough to be seen and heard" is the warm center of this unit. If God really moved in this close, in a body, what difference could that make for someone who feels far from God?

16. ____________________________________________

> *Go deeper (optional):* Verse 18 is the reason this whole course exists. You can't see God directly. You can look hard at Jesus, and see him. The word behind "made him known" is where we get "exegete," to explain. Jesus is God's own explanation of himself.

## Key words to know

- **The Word**: John's title for Jesus before he uses the name Jesus. God spoke, and the speech turned out to be a person.
- **The light**: Jesus as the one who shows God and pushes back the dark.
- **Flesh**: a real human body and life; not a costume, not a ghost.
- **Dwelt / tabernacled**: "pitched a tent." God coming to live right in the middle of his people.
- **Grace**: a gift you didn't earn. "Grace upon grace" is one gift after another, out of a fullness that never runs dry.
- **John the Baptist**: the witness in verses 6-8 who points to Jesus. Not the author of this Gospel.

## Memory verse: John 1:14 (ESV)

> "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."

Learn it in four beats. Each phrase carries one truth:
1. **"The Word became flesh."** The one who is God (verse 1) became a human being.
2. **"and dwelt among us."** He pitched his tent with us. God came close.
3. **"we have seen his glory."** Real eyewitnesses. A real event, real faces.
4. **"full of grace and truth."** What his glory looked like up close.

**Review note:** We'll say this one out loud together in Lesson 4, one phrase at a time, and review it through the whole unit. This is your anchor verse for the year, so get it solid now. The meaning gets coached in class; the words are yours to memorize.

---

## Where this unit lands on Jesus

The whole Prologue climbs to verse 14: the eternal God who made everything became a man John and his friends could watch, "full of grace and truth." To know what God is like, look at Jesus. That's the doorway you're walking through, and it's the way we'll read the rest of this Gospel.
