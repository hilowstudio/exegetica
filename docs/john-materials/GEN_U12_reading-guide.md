# Reading guide, Unit 12: Light of the world

> Passage: John 8:12-59
> Big question: who does Jesus say he is, and what happens if you follow him out of the dark?
> New memory verse: John 8:12. You'll learn it in lesson 1.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This whole unit is one long argument in the temple, and it keeps building to one point: Jesus is claiming to be God. Watch it climb, statement by statement, until the crowd picks up stones. Remember from last unit that the Feast of Booths lit huge lamps in the temple. That's the backdrop when Jesus says, "I am the light of the world."

## Lesson 1: the light of the world (John 8:12-20)

**Read it.** John chapter 8, verses 12 through 20. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Standing right where the temple's giant festival lamps had blazed, Jesus says, "I am the light of the world." The Pharisees say he can't just testify about himself. Jesus says the Father testifies with him, and that they don't really know him or his Father.

- **Observe.** In verse 12, what does Jesus call himself, and what does he promise to whoever follows him?
- **Interpret.** Jesus doesn't say he brings light or teaches about light. He says he is the light. What's he actually claiming about himself?
- **Respond.** Light shows you what's really there, including the stuff you'd rather keep hidden. What's one thing you keep in the dark? What would it be like to bring it into the light?
- **Memory verse.** John 8:12: "Again Jesus spoke to them, saying, 'I am the light of the world. Whoever follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.'"

## Lesson 2: the I AM, lifted up (John 8:21-30)

**Read it.** John chapter 8, verses 21 through 30.

Jesus says he's going somewhere they can't follow, and warns that they'll "die in their sin" if they don't believe who he is. He uses the name "I am," the name God gave himself. And he says the day they crucify him is the day they'll finally know it was true.

- **Observe.** In verse 24, what does Jesus say has to happen so a person doesn't "die in their sins"?
- **Interpret.** In verse 28 Jesus says, "when you have lifted up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am he." He means the cross. Why would the cross be the place people finally see who he is?
- **Go deeper (optional).** "I am he" (verse 24) is God's own name from Exodus 3:14. Read verse 29: "he who sent me is with me... I always do the things that are pleasing to him." How is that a comfort when everyone in the room is against you?

## Lesson 3: the truth will set you free (John 8:31-38)

**Read it.** John chapter 8, verses 31 through 38.

Jesus tells the people who believed him that if they keep living in his word, the truth will set them free. They're offended, saying they've never been slaves. Jesus tells them everyone who sins is a slave to sin, and only the Son can really free them.

- **Observe.** In verse 34, who does Jesus say is a slave? In verse 36, who can set them free?
- **Interpret.** The people thought freedom meant nobody controls them. Jesus says a person can look free and still be owned by their sin. What's the difference between the two kinds of freedom?
- **Respond.** Be honest: what habit or pattern owns you more than you'd like to admit? What would it mean for the Son to free you from it?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Read verses 34 through 36 together. Notice Jesus defines "the truth will set you free" right there. Why is it a mistake to quote "the truth will set you free" all by itself?

## Lesson 4: whose child are you really? (John 8:39-47)

**Read it.** John chapter 8, verses 39 through 47.

The argument gets sharp. The people claim Abraham and God as their fathers. Jesus says their actions tell the real story, and their desire to kill him shows a different father entirely. This is hard language about the heart. It has nothing to do with anyone's ancestry.

- **Observe.** In verse 39, what does Jesus say real children of Abraham would be doing?
- **Interpret.** Jesus says a person's "father" is shown by who they act like and obey. He's not talking about anyone's family tree or ethnicity. He's talking about the heart. So how does a person get a new Father (look back at John 1:12-13 and John 3:3)?
- **Respond.** Jesus says whose child you really are shows up in whether you'll hear his words. When you hear something Jesus says that's hard, what do you usually do with it?

## Lesson 5: before Abraham was, I am (John 8:48-59)

**Read it.** John chapter 8, verses 48 through 59.

The crowd insults Jesus, and he keeps making bigger claims: whoever keeps his word will never die, and Abraham was glad to see his day. Then Jesus says the biggest thing yet: "Before Abraham was, I am." The crowd grabs stones to kill him for it.

- **Observe.** In verse 58, what does Jesus say about himself and Abraham?
- **Observe.** In verse 59, what does the crowd do right after?
- **Interpret.** Why do they grab stones? (Hint: "I am" is God's own name, and claiming to be God was punishable by stoning.) What does their reaction tell you about what they understood Jesus to mean?
- **Respond.** "Before Abraham was, I am" is a claim you can't sit on the fence about. Either Jesus is God or he's making it up. Which way do you lean, and why?

## Key words to know

- **Light of the world.** What Jesus calls himself. He's God's own light come into a dark world, and following him means you're not lost in the dark.
- **I am.** God's personal name, from Exodus 3:14 ("I AM WHO I AM"). When Jesus says "I am" this way, he's claiming to be God. He says it three times in this chapter.
- **Slave to sin.** Jesus' picture of everyone apart from him. You can look free and still be owned by sin. Only the Son sets you truly free.
- **Lifted up.** Jesus' way of talking about the cross. It's where he's raised up to die, and where people finally see who he is.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The whole chapter is Jesus saying one thing louder and louder: he is God. He's the light the temple lamps only pictured. He's the "I am" from the burning bush. He was alive before Abraham was born. He frees slaves into sons, and he tells the truth even when it gets him stones instead of applause. Follow him, and you walk in the light.
