## Part B. The Farewell Discourse (John 13-17). For each chapter, name what Jesus gives his friends and the memory anchor that goes with it.

>> KEY (Part B):
>> - **John 13, the towel and the basin.** He gives himself as the servant-King who kneels and washes their feet, and a new command to love as he has loved. Anchor: **13:34-35**.
>> - **John 14, the way to the Father.** He gives himself as the way home ("I am the way, and the truth, and the life; no one comes to the Father except through me") and the promise of the Helper. Anchor: **14:6**.
>> - **John 15, the vine.** He gives himself as the vine they live from, "apart from me you can do nothing." Anchor: **15:5**.
>> - **John 16, sorrow into joy.** He gives the Spirit (who convicts, guides, and glorifies Christ) and his finished victory, "I have overcome the world." Anchor: **16:33**.
>> - **John 17, the high-priestly prayer.** He gives himself as the praying High Priest who carries his people to the Father, and defines eternal life as knowing the Father and the Son. Anchor: **17:3**.
>> One gift under many images: Christ himself.

## Part C. The Passion, the resurrection, and the epilogue (John 18-21). Short answer, one or two sentences each.

C1. In the garden, how does Jesus answer the soldiers (18:5-6), and what does it show about how he goes to the cross?

>> KEY: He says "I am he," and the soldiers draw back and fall to the ground (18:6). It shows he goes willingly, in full control; no one takes his life from him (10:18). "I am he" also carries the divine name.

C2. Pilate hangs a sign over the cross (19:19-22). What does it say, in how many languages, and what does John want us to see in it?

>> KEY: "Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews," written in Aramaic, Latin, and Greek. Pilate refuses to change it. John wants us to see the truth preached to the whole world: the crucified one really is the King, reigning from the cross.

C3. Why does John note that they did not break Jesus' legs (19:33, 19:36)?

>> KEY: So that the Scripture would be fulfilled, "Not one of his bones will be broken" (Exodus 12:46). Jesus dies as the true Passover Lamb (1:29), the Lamb of God, at the Passover.

C4. What does "It is finished" (19:30) mean? What is finished?

>> KEY: The Greek is *tetelestai*, a completed, standing declaration, like a debt stamped "paid in full." The work of salvation the Father gave him to do is complete (compare 17:4). It is a victory shout, and he then yields up his spirit by his own choice.

C5. At the empty tomb, what does Thomas confess (20:28), and how does it connect to the very first verse of the Gospel?

>> KEY: "My Lord and my God!" It closes the frame John opened at 1:1, "the Word was God." Thomas confesses at the end what John declared at the start: Jesus is God. (The deity inclusio.)

C6. On the beach, how does Jesus restore Peter (21:15-17), and what does he tell him to do?

>> KEY: He asks Peter "do you love me?" three times, one for each of Peter's three denials, and each time gives him his flock back: "feed my lambs," "tend my sheep," "feed my sheep." He restores the denier and sends him as an under-shepherd, and says "Follow me" (21:19).

## Part D. Short answer (the christotelic heart). One or two sentences each.

D1. What does "abide" mean in John 15, and what flows from it?

>> KEY: To abide is to stay joined to Christ, a state of union before it is an activity. Fruit, love, and answered prayer flow from staying joined to him; "apart from me you can do nothing" (15:5).

D2. How is 14:6 both a hard line and a comfort?

>> KEY: One true way home means there really is a way home, open to anyone. And Jesus says it to steady a scared, honest friend (Thomas), so it is spoken as comfort, warmly, not thrown as a weapon.

D3. In chapter 17, how does Jesus define eternal life (17:3)?

>> KEY: "That they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent." Eternal life is a relationship of knowing God and the Son, which starts now, more than a future place.

D4. What is the point of the folded grave clothes (20:6-7), and how is the resurrection different from what happened to Lazarus?

>> KEY: The clothes lie folded and ordered, a sign of a deliberate resurrection, not a grave robbery (a thief would not undress a body and fold the linens). Lazarus rose still bound in his grave clothes and would die again (11:44); Jesus left his folded and rose to die no more. Resurrection, not resuscitation.

D5. What does John say about his own testimony in 19:35, and why does it matter?

>> KEY: "He who saw it has borne witness, his testimony is true," and he knows he tells the truth, "that you also may believe." An eyewitness stakes his testimony on the real death of Jesus. It grounds the whole account in something seen.

D6. Read across the whole Gospel: how do the sixteen memory anchors together form one portrait of Christ?

>> KEY: They are not sixteen separate sayings; they are one Christ seen sixteen times, from "the Word became flesh" (1:14) to "feed my sheep" (21:17). The Word made flesh, the giver of life, the light, the shepherd, the resurrection, the way, the vine, the one who overcomes, who finishes the work, and who restores and sends. (Accept any answer that reads the anchors as one unified portrait of who Jesus is.)

## Part E. Essay (choose ONE). Write a clear, organized response that shows you understand what John is showing about who Jesus is. You are graded on comprehension, not on whether you personally believe it.

**E1.** Trace "the hour" and the theme of glory across the Book of Glory. Explain how John presents the cross as Jesus' glorification, using at least three specific moments from chapters 13 to 19.

**E2.** What does Jesus mean by "It is finished" (19:30)? Explain what is finished, and how the resurrection and Thomas's confession in chapter 20 confirm it.

**E3.** The Farewell Discourse (chapters 13 to 17) is Jesus giving his friends himself before he dies. Choose three of these chapters and explain what Jesus gives in each, and what each shows about who he is to his people.

>> KEY (Part E), load-bearing points a full-credit answer hits:
>> **E1:** the hour announced in the Book of Signs now arrives (13:1, "his hour had come"); glory is defined by the cross, not withheld from it (12:23 fulfilled in chapter 19); the King reigns from the cross with the trilingual charge over his head (19:19-20); the Lamb dies as the Passover lambs die, no bone broken (19:36); "It is finished" (19:30) declares the work complete; he lays down his life by his own authority (10:18). The student shows they see that John's word for the cross is glory.
>> **E2:** *tetelestai* is a completed, standing-finished declaration, a debt paid in full; what is finished is the work of salvation the Father gave him (17:4); it is a shout of completion, spoken as he freely yields his spirit; the empty tomb vindicates the finished work, and Thomas's "My Lord and my God" (20:28) confesses the crucified-and-risen one as God, closing the frame 1:1 opened.
>> **E3:** chapter 13, himself as servant and the pattern of love (13:34-35); chapter 14, himself as the way to the Father and the promise of the Helper (14:6); chapter 15, himself as the vine they live from (15:5); chapter 16, the Spirit and his finished victory (16:33); chapter 17, himself as the praying High Priest (17:3). The student shows they grasp the discourse is one gift, Christ himself, under many images.
>> Grade for comprehension of what John shows. A searching student who explains it accurately earns full marks. Do not require or reward personal profession.
