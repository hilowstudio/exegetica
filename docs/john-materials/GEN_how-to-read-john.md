## Start here: what's a Gospel?

A **Gospel** is a written account of the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus. The word "gospel" means *good news*.

The Bible gives us four of them, from four writers: **Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John**. Four accounts of the same Jesus, each one telling the story with its own focus.

John is the fourth. He tells us up front why he wrote it, so we're not guessing.

---

## John's own reason for writing

At the end of the book, John stops and explains the point of the whole thing:

> "but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." (John 20:31, ESV)

Two things to hold onto:

- **Believe**: that Jesus is the Christ (God's promised King) and the Son of God.
- **Have life**: real life, life with God, that comes through believing.

Every chapter is pushing toward those two words.

---

## The book has two halves

John built his Gospel in two big movements. Knowing where you are helps you read.

| Half | Chapters | What happens | Nickname |
|------|----------|--------------|----------|
| First half | John 1–12 | Jesus does seven **signs** (miracles that point to who he is) and teaches the crowds | The Book of Signs |
| Second half | John 13–21 | Jesus turns toward the cross: the last supper, his death, and his resurrection | The Book of Glory |

The first half asks, "Who is this man?" The second half shows what he came to do.

---

## Where the whole book is headed

John opens by telling us who Jesus really is before the story even starts:

> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." (John 1:1, ESV)

> "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth." (John 1:14, ESV)

The "Word" is Jesus. He was God, and he became a real human being and lived among people. That's the claim John spends 21 chapters proving. Everything in this book lands on Jesus.

---

## How we'll read it this year

- **Verse by verse, slowly.** We're not rushing to finish. We're paying attention.
- **Two questions on every passage:**
  1. **Who is Jesus here?** What does this show about him?
  2. **What response does he call for?** What does he ask of the person reading?
- **We read it as God speaking now,** not as an old document under glass. This is the living Word, and it's talking to you.

---

## How to find John and get around in it

- John is in the **New Testament**, the fourth book, right after Matthew, Mark, and Luke.
- Use the **table of contents** in the front of your Bible to find the page. No shame in that. Everybody does it.
- A reference like **John 3:16** means: the book of John, **chapter 3**, **verse 16**. Big numbers are chapters; small numbers are verses.
- Chapter and verse numbers were added later to help us find things. They're a map, not part of the original letter.

> **New to the Bible? You're in the right seat.** Nobody in this room is expected to already know where things are or what the words mean. We'll learn it together, and you'll be finding passages on your own within a few weeks.

## A word about your questions

Bring your honest questions. Bring your doubts. John is a book about a man who let a doubting disciple touch his wounds, so this classroom can handle whatever you're actually wondering.

You won't be graded on how much faith you walk in with. Asking hard questions out loud is one of the best ways to read this book well.
