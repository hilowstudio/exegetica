# Reading guide, Unit 6: Living water

> Passage: John 4:1-42
> Big question: where do you actually meet God, and does he want someone like you?
> New memory verse: John 4:24. You'll learn it in the worship lesson.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says. Who's there, what they ask, what changes.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

The whole unit is one long conversation at a well, plus what happens after. Jesus talks with a Samaritan woman that everybody else in the story would have avoided. Watch how he treats her.

## Lesson 1: Jacob's well and living water (John 4:1-15)

**Read it.** John chapter 4, verses 1 through 15. If you're finding John for the first time, use the table of contents, turn to John, then find the big 4 and the small verse numbers.

Jesus is tired and thirsty at a well at noon. A Samaritan woman comes to draw water. Jews and Samaritans didn't mix, and men didn't strike up conversations with a woman alone at a well. Jesus asks her for a drink anyway, then offers her "living water."

- **Observe.** In verse 9, why is the woman surprised that Jesus talks to her at all?
- **Observe.** In verses 13 and 14, what's the difference between the well water and the water Jesus gives?
- **Interpret.** Jesus says his water "will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life" (verse 14). Later in the book (John 7:39) John tells us the living water is the Holy Spirit. What is Jesus really offering her?
- **Respond.** Everybody's thirsty for something they keep going back to the well for. What do you keep going back to that never fills you up for long?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Read Jeremiah 2:13. God calls himself "the fountain of living waters" and calls our substitutes "broken cisterns that can hold no water." How does that line up with the well in John 4?

## Lesson 2: the five husbands and the prophet (John 4:16-19)

**Read it.** John chapter 4, verses 16 through 19.

Jesus tells her to go get her husband. She says she has none. He tells her the truth about her whole history, five husbands and a man now who isn't her husband. He names it gently, to bring the buried thing into the light so it can heal.

- **Observe.** In verse 18, what does Jesus already know about her that she never told him?
- **Interpret.** She answers, "I perceive that you are a prophet" (verse 19). Being fully known could have sent her running. Why do you think she stays in the conversation?
- **Respond.** Jesus knew the worst about her and kept talking with her. Is there something about you that you assume would make God turn away? Write it honestly.
- **Go deeper (optional).** Look back at John 1:47-48, where Jesus tells Nathanael he saw him under the fig tree. What's the same about how Jesus knows people in both scenes?

## Halfway checkpoint (Friday Oct 9, half day)

No new reading this day. We recap the well conversation so far and run a memory pass on the four verses you already know (John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16). Come ready to say them.

## Lesson 3: worship in spirit and truth (John 4:20-26)

**Read it.** John chapter 4, verses 20 through 26. This is the memory-verse lesson.

The woman brings up the old fight: the Samaritans worship on their mountain, the Jews say worship belongs in Jerusalem. Jesus lifts worship off the map of places entirely. Then he tells her plainly, for the first time in the whole book, that he's the Messiah.

- **Observe.** In verse 21, what does Jesus say about "this mountain" and Jerusalem?
- **Observe.** In verse 24, what reason does Jesus give for how we worship? (Look at the first three words.)
- **Interpret.** "God is spirit" (verse 24) means God isn't stuck in a building or a mountain or your family's way of doing church. Where, then, can you worship the Father?
- **Respond.** Verse 23 says "the Father is seeking such people to worship him." God is looking for you first. What changes if worship starts with God coming after you?
- **Go deeper (optional).** In verse 26 Jesus says "I who speak to you am he." He tells a Samaritan woman with a rough past that he's the Messiah before he ever says it plainly in Jerusalem. Why do you think John records that the outsider heard it first?

## Lesson 4: the Savior of the world (John 4:27-42)

**Read it.** John chapter 4, verses 27 through 42.

The woman leaves her water jar and runs to tell the town, "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did." The whole town comes out. Jesus talks with his confused disciples about a harvest that's already ripe. The Samaritans hear Jesus for themselves and call him "the Savior of the world."

- **Observe.** In verse 28 and 29, what does the woman do with the very thing that could have shamed her?
- **Interpret.** In verse 42 the townspeople say they believe now because they've "heard for ourselves," not just because of the woman's word. What's the difference between a borrowed faith and a firsthand one?
- **Respond.** The woman's whole testimony was "he told me all that I ever did, come and see." Who in your life could you say one true thing to about Jesus?
- **Go deeper (optional).** The Samaritans, the outsiders, are the ones who call Jesus "the Savior of the world." Read John 1:29 and John 3:16. How far does "the world" reach?

## Key words to know

- **Samaritan.** People who lived between Galilee and Judea. Jews and Samaritans shared some history and despised each other. Jesus crosses that line on purpose.
- **Living water.** Moving, fresh spring water, not water sitting in a jar. Jesus uses it for the Holy Spirit and the eternal life the Spirit brings (John 7:39).
- **Worship in spirit and truth.** Worship that the Holy Spirit makes possible, shaped by the truth God has shown us, finally by Jesus himself. It's freed from any one building or style.
- **Messiah / Christ.** God's promised Rescuer and King. Jesus tells the woman plainly that he's the one.
- **Savior of the world.** The title the Samaritan town gives Jesus. He saves people from every nation, Jew and Samaritan and everyone else.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus goes out of his way to a well in enemy country, asks a shamed outsider for a drink, and gives her himself. He's the living water, the one greater than Jacob, the Messiah who tells her plainly who he is. By the end an outsider town calls him the Savior of the world. He knows everything about you and he stays.
