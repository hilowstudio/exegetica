# Reading journal guide: the Gospel of John

> Yorkville Christian School · Sophomore Bible  
> Rhythm: every class, verse by verse through John  
> What you need: your Bible (or the class ESV), this journal, something to write with  
> You will learn to: keep a Reading Journal that records what you see in the text and how you honestly respond to it.  
> Scripture: John 20:30-31 (ESV)

---

## Why we keep this journal

John tells us straight out why he wrote his Gospel:

> "Now Jesus did many other signs in the presence of the disciples, which are not written in this book; but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name." (John 20:30-31, ESV)

John wrote so you would meet Jesus and find life in him. Your Reading Journal follows the same aim. You slow down, watch what the text actually says, and let it speak to you.

The whole Gospel points to one Person. Every entry is really you getting to know Christ better, one passage at a time.

## How the grade works

> Your journal counts inside your **participation** grade. You're graded for **honest effort**, never for landing on the "right" answer. A real question, a struggle, even an "I don't get this yet" all earn full credit when you actually engaged the text.

## The four steps (do these every entry)

Work them in order. Keep it short and real.

- **Locate**: Find the passage and write the reference. Note who's speaking and what's happening around it.
- **Observe**: Write down what the text says. Repeated words, a name for Jesus, a command, something that surprises you.
- **Respond**: Say honestly what you think or feel about it. Agreement, confusion, a question, a hard spot are all fair.
- **Apply**: Write one line: how does this change something about today or this week?

## What a good entry looks like

A good entry is specific about the text and honest about you. Here's a short one on John 1:5.

> **Locate:** John 1:5. The opening about the Word, before John the Baptist shows up.  
> **Observe:** "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it." The word "light" keeps coming back. Present tense, "shines," like it's still happening.  
> **Respond:** I like that the darkness doesn't win, but I'm not sure I always believe it when the week is bad.  
> **Apply:** When I feel stuck this week, I'll remember the light is still on and hasn't gone out.

Notice: it names the actual verse, quotes a phrase, and the response is honest instead of polished.
