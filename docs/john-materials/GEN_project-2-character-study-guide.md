# Project guide, Q2: Character Study

> **Quarter:** 2 (fall) · **Mode:** individual
> **Window:** after the Signs project, through the weeks before winter break. Your teacher will give you the exact milestone dates.
> Read everything in the ESV.

## The assignment

> Pick a person who met Jesus in John and put us inside their story. Show who they were, how the encounter changed them or didn't, and what John wants us to see through them.

You're a storyteller here. You take one real person from the Gospel, someone who stood in front of Jesus, and you help the class feel what that meeting was like and understand why John put it in the book.

## The one idea to build everything on

John doesn't give us these people so we'll admire them or scold them. He gives us people so we can watch a real human being meet Jesus and be exposed, changed, or hardened by it. And every one of these encounters is a window on Jesus. Watch how it works:

- Nicodemus comes at night, confused, and his confusion draws out the new birth (chapter 3).
- The Samaritan woman comes to a well thirsty, and her thirst draws out the living water (chapter 4).
- The man born blind gains his sight one step at a time, while the leaders who can see go blind, and the whole scene judges the room (chapter 9).

The center of your project is not the person by themselves. It's what John wants us to *see about Jesus* through this person. The character is a lens. Keep Jesus in focus through the lens.

## Who you can study

Any person who genuinely meets Jesus in John. Some strong choices:

- **Nicodemus** (3:1-21; 7:50-51; 19:39), the cautious insider who keeps showing up.
- **The Samaritan woman** (4:1-42), the outsider who becomes the first witness to her town.
- **The man born blind** (9:1-41), whose growing sight is a growing confession.
- **Martha** (11:17-27), who hears "I am the resurrection and the life."
- **Thomas** (11:16; 14:5; 20:24-29), honest doubt met by a patient Christ.
- **Peter** (1:42; 13; 18:15-27; 21:15-19), called, failing, and restored.
- **Pilate** (18:28-19:16), the man who asks "what is truth?" with the Truth in front of him.

If you want someone not on this list, clear it with your teacher at the pitch.

## Voice and choice: pick your format

The skills are fixed by the rubric. The shape is yours. Choose one:

- a first-person monologue (you become the character)
- a short video or documentary
- an illustrated story or graphic-novel page with commentary
- a diary or letters written in the character's voice
- a piece of art with a written explanation
- a written piece (a character study, a short story, a profile)

## The milestones (the process is graded too)

Your teacher will give you the exact dates. The order is fixed:

1. **Pitch.** Your character, your format, and the one thing about Jesus you think John shows through them.
2. **Plan.** Your evidence map: every passage and quote, and how your piece is structured.
3. **Draft or rehearsal,** with peer feedback.
4. **Present** it to the class, with live questions.

A fifteen-minute in-class check-in during a project week is there for roadblocks.

## The Maker's Note (required, turned in with the final)

A few honest sentences: why this character, why this format, what was hard, and what you'd do differently. Graded as a skill, self-awareness.

## How to make it actually land

- **Keep Jesus in focus.** The character is your way in. What Jesus reveals is your destination. If a viewer leaves knowing the person and not the Christ they met, the lens got cloudy.
- **Be honest about the person.** Real people, real reactions. Some believe, some resist, some are still deciding. Tell the truth about them, including the ones who walk away.
- **Watch two traps.** Don't turn the person into a hero to copy or a villain to avoid, which flattens them into a moral lesson. And don't get so deep inside their head that Jesus becomes a minor character in their story. Their story exists to reveal him.
- **Use the real text.** Quote John. Ground your character in what the passage actually says they did and heard.

## Rubric

Each row scored 1 (beginning) to 4 (exemplary), multiplied by its weight, summed, and divided by 4 for the percentage.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| **Grasp of the passage** | 30% | The character and their encounter are read accurately and in context, no misreadings. |
| **Christ at the center** | 20% | The piece shows clearly what the encounter reveals about who Jesus is; the character stays a lens on him. |
| **Independent insight** | 20% | Your own honest thinking: a real reading of this person, not a summary. |
| **Communication** | 15% | Clear, organized, prepared, and true to the character's voice or story. |
| **Live Q&A** | 15% | You answer follow-up questions with real understanding of the person and what John shows through them. |

**Your project grade also includes** the graded milestones and the Maker's Note.

**A word on the grade and your heart.** This grades whether you understood and explained the encounter, which is content. It does not grade whether you personally believe. Show me you see what John is showing through this person.
