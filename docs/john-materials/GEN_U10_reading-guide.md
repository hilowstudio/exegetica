# Reading guide, Unit 10: Rivers of living water

> Passage: John 7:1-52
> Big question: what are you thirsty for, and where do you go to drink?
> Memory work: no new verse this unit. Keep your six review verses sharp.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This whole unit happens at the Feast of Booths, a week-long celebration where priests poured out water at the temple every day, remembering how God gave Israel water in the desert. Keep that picture in mind. On the last day, when the water stopped pouring, Jesus stands up and says something huge about water.

## Lesson 1: lost brothers (John 7:1-13)

**Read it.** John chapter 7, verses 1 through 13.

Jesus' own brothers tell him to go to Judea and show off his miracles. John adds a hard line: they didn't believe in him. Jesus says his "time" hasn't come, then goes up to the feast quietly. The crowd is split, some calling him good, some calling him a fraud.

- **Observe.** In verse 5, what does John tell us about Jesus' brothers?
- **Interpret.** Jesus' own family didn't believe him yet. If even his brothers, who grew up with him, didn't get it, what does that tell you about how a person comes to believe?
- **Respond.** Some students have families that don't believe. Jesus knows exactly what that feels like. Write one honest sentence about your own home and faith.

## Lesson 2: teaching in the temple (John 7:14-24)

**Read it.** John chapter 7, verses 14 through 24.

Jesus teaches in the temple and the crowd is amazed he knows so much without formal training. He says his teaching comes from God, and he gives a test for how to know that. Then he calls out their double standard about the Sabbath.

- **Observe.** In verse 16, whose teaching does Jesus say he's giving?
- **Interpret.** In verse 17 Jesus says, "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God." Why would wanting to obey God come before understanding whether something's true?
- **Respond.** Verse 24 says, "judge with right judgment," not by appearances. Where do you judge things (or people) by how they look on the surface?

## Lesson 3: can this be the Christ? (John 7:25-36)

**Read it.** John chapter 7, verses 25 through 36.

The people argue about where Jesus is from and whether the leaders secretly think he's the Christ. Jesus says they don't actually know where he's from, because he comes from God. They try to arrest him and can't, because "his hour had not yet come."

- **Observe.** In verse 30, why couldn't they arrest Jesus, even though they wanted to?
- **Interpret.** The crowd was sure they had Jesus figured out ("we know where this man comes from"). Jesus says they don't know his real origin at all. How can people be certain about Jesus and still be completely wrong?
- **Go deeper (optional).** In verse 33 Jesus says he's "going to him who sent me," and the crowd thinks he means moving to another country. What was he really talking about?

## Lesson 4: rivers of living water (John 7:37-44)

**Read it.** John chapter 7, verses 37 through 44. This lesson has an optional memory verse.

On the last day of the feast, right when the daily water-pouring has stopped, Jesus stands up and shouts an invitation: if you're thirsty, come to me and drink. John tells us the "living water" is the Holy Spirit. The crowd splits again over who Jesus is.

- **Observe.** In verse 37, what does Jesus invite thirsty people to do?
- **Observe.** In verse 39, what does John say the "rivers of living water" actually are?
- **Interpret.** Jesus says he satisfies a thirst that water can't reach. What kind of thirst do you think he means?
- **Respond.** People try to fill their deep thirst with all kinds of things. What have you been trying to drink that never quite works?
- **Optional stretch verse.** John 7:37-38: "If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. Whoever believes in me, as the Scripture has said, 'Out of his heart will flow rivers of living water.'"

## Lesson 5: no one ever spoke like this man (John 7:45-52)

**Read it.** John chapter 7, verses 45 through 52.

The temple guards were sent to arrest Jesus and came back empty-handed, saying, "No one ever spoke like this man." The leaders are furious. Nicodemus, the man who came to Jesus at night back in chapter 3, quietly asks them to at least give Jesus a fair hearing.

- **Observe.** In verse 46, why didn't the officers arrest Jesus?
- **Interpret.** In verse 51 Nicodemus speaks up, carefully, for giving Jesus a hearing. He's still cautious, and he's edging toward the light over time. Why is it hard to speak up for Jesus when the room is against him?
- **Respond.** When has it cost you something to stand up for what you believe, even a little? What happened?

## Key words to know

- **Feast of Booths (Tabernacles).** A week-long celebration remembering God caring for Israel in the desert. Priests poured out water each day, praying for rain and remembering God's provision.
- **Living water.** What Jesus offers. John tells us it's the Holy Spirit, God's own life poured into a person (7:39).
- **His hour / his time.** Jesus lived on God's schedule. His "hour" is the appointed time of his death, and nothing could touch him before it came.
- **Nicodemus.** A Jewish leader who first came to Jesus at night (chapter 3) and shows up here defending a fair hearing. He's slowly moving toward faith.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

At a feast about God giving water in the desert, Jesus stands up and says he is the water. Come to him and drink, and the Holy Spirit flows into you like a river. The crowd argued and took sides and tried to arrest him, and his word stopped them cold. Whatever you're thirsty for, the address is Jesus.
