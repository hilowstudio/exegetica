# Reading guide, Unit 23: The prayer of the Son

> Passage: John 17:1-26
> Big question: on the night before he dies, Jesus stops teaching his friends and prays for them out loud. What does the Son ask his Father for?
> New memory verse: John 17:3. You'll learn it in lesson 1.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This is the end of the Farewell Discourse and the climax of the whole talk. Jesus has been teaching his friends. Now he turns and prays to his Father while they listen in. It's the longest prayer of Jesus we have, and it comes in three parts: he prays for himself (verses 1-5), for his disciples in the room (verses 6-19), and for everyone who will ever believe in him, which includes you (verses 20-26). Listen in on the Son talking to his Father the night before the cross.

## Lesson 1: the Son prays for himself (John 17:1-5)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 1 through 5. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Jesus says "the hour has come" and asks the Father to glorify him. He's talking about the cross. Then he says what eternal life actually is: knowing God and Jesus Christ. And he asks to return to the glory he had with the Father "before the world existed."

- **Observe.** In verse 3, how does Jesus define eternal life? In verse 5, what glory does Jesus ask to return to?
- **Interpret.** Most people think "eternal life" means going to heaven someday. Jesus defines it as "knowing" God and Jesus Christ, the way you know a person, starting now. How is that bigger than just a future address?
- **Respond.** Jesus says eternal life is knowing God, not just knowing about him. What's the difference between knowing facts about someone famous and actually knowing a close friend? Which one do you have with God right now?
- **Memory verse.** John 17:3: "And this is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent."

## Lesson 2: I have shown them your name (John 17:6-8)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 6 through 8.

Jesus tells the Father what he's done for the disciples: he made the Father known to them, gave them the Father's words, and they received them and believed that Jesus came from God.

- **Observe.** In verse 6, what has Jesus done for the people the Father gave him? In verse 8, what did they do with the words Jesus gave them?
- **Interpret.** To "manifest your name" means to make God himself known, what he's really like. Jesus is how you find out who God is. How is that the same thing as the "knowing God" from verse 3?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 6 says these people were the Father's, and he gave them to Jesus. What does that tell you about where faith starts, with us reaching for God or with God reaching for us?

## Lesson 3: kept, and not one lost (John 17:9-13)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 9 through 13.

Jesus prays specifically for his own people here, not the whole world in this prayer. He asks the Father to "keep them." He says while he was with them, he guarded them, and not one was lost except Judas, "the son of destruction," which happened to fulfill Scripture.

- **Observe.** In verse 11, what does Jesus ask the "Holy Father" to do for the disciples? In verse 12, how many were lost, and who was the exception?
- **Interpret.** Jesus asks the Father to KEEP his people, because he's leaving. Their staying safe depends on God's grip, not on their own strength. Back in John 10:28 he said no one can snatch his sheep from his hand. How does that make being a Christian more secure than white-knuckling it on your own?
- **Respond.** Judas looked like a disciple but never really belonged to Jesus. That's a warning to check whether you truly know him (verse 3), not a reason for a real believer to panic about losing their salvation. Where do you need the comfort of being "kept" by God?

## Lesson 4: in the world, not of it (John 17:14-16)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 14 through 16. This is a short-day, single-focus lesson.

Jesus says the world hates his followers because they don't belong to it. Then he makes a careful request: he does NOT ask the Father to take them out of the world. He asks the Father to keep them from the evil one while they're in it.

- **Observe.** In verse 15, what does Jesus specifically NOT ask for, and what does he ask for instead? In verse 16, why does the world hate them?
- **Interpret.** There's a difference between being IN the world and being OF the world. Christians live right in the middle of everything, and they belong to Jesus instead of to the world's values. Why doesn't Jesus just pull his people out of the world to keep them safe? What's the world for, in his plan (peek ahead at verse 18)?
- **Respond.** Being "in the world, not of it" is a real tension for a fifteen-year-old. Where do you feel the pull to just blend in and belong? Where do you feel like withdrawing completely? What would the middle path look like?

## Lesson 5: sanctify them in the truth (John 17:17-19)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 17 through 19.

Jesus asks the Father to "sanctify" the disciples, to make them holy, and he says how: "your word is truth." Then he says he's sending them into the world the way the Father sent him. And he says he sets himself apart (for the cross) so they can be made holy.

- **Observe.** In verse 17, how does Jesus ask the Father to make them holy? In verse 19, what does Jesus do "for their sake"?
- **Interpret.** "Sanctify" means to set apart and make holy. Jesus says the tool for it is God's word, the truth. This isn't a grind-harder self-improvement project. It's God growing you through his word. How does that change how you'd read your Bible?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 19: "for their sake I consecrate myself." Jesus sets himself apart for the cross so his people can be made holy. How does your growth in holiness flow from what Jesus did, not from your own effort?

## Lesson 6: that they may all be one (John 17:20-21)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 20 through 21.

Jesus widens the prayer. He says he's not just praying for the eleven, but for "those who will believe in me through their word." That's every future Christian. That's you. And he asks that they would all be one, the way the Father and Son are one, so the world would believe.

- **Observe.** In verse 20, who else is Jesus praying for besides the disciples in the room? In verse 21, what does he ask for them, and why?
- **Interpret.** Verse 20 means Jesus was praying for you, by implication, the night before he died. The gospel reached you through 2,000 years of Christians passing it on. How does it feel to know you were in this prayer?
- **Respond.** Jesus prays that Christians would be "one," and that this unity would make the world believe. Where have you seen Christians divided in ugly ways? Where have you seen a unity that actually made Jesus look real?

## Lesson 7: the glory he shares (John 17:22-23)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 22 through 23.

Jesus says he's given his people the glory the Father gave him, so they'd be one. He describes it: "I in them and you in me." And the goal again is that the world would know the Father sent Jesus and loves his people the way he loves his Son.

- **Observe.** In verse 22, what has Jesus given his people, and why? In verse 23, what will the world know when Christians are one?
- **Interpret.** The unity Jesus prays for isn't Christians just being nice or agreeing to disagree about everything. It's a real oneness because Jesus lives in his people and the Father lives in Jesus. How is that deeper than a club of people who happen to get along?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 23 says the world will know the Father "loved them even as you loved me." God loves Christians the same way he loves Jesus. Sit with that. What would change if you believed it?

## Lesson 8: bring them home, and the love in them (John 17:24-26)

**Read it.** John chapter 17, verses 24 through 26.

Jesus ends his prayer with what he wants most: that his people would "be with me where I am, to see my glory." Then he closes on the deepest note in the whole prayer: that the love the Father has for the Son would be IN his people, and that Jesus himself would be in them.

- **Observe.** In verse 24, what does Jesus want for his people? In verse 26, what does Jesus want to be "in them"?
- **Interpret.** Heaven, in verse 24, is being WITH Jesus and seeing his glory. It's not a vague sky or a harp cloud. It's being with the person you were made to know (verse 3). How does that change what you're actually hoping for?
- **Respond.** The prayer ends with the Father's own love for Jesus being placed inside his people. That's the highest thing Jesus asks for you. What would it look like to live today believing that love is in you?

## Key words to know

- **The high-priestly prayer.** The name for John 17. Jesus, our great High Priest, prays for his people before he dies, the way the priest carried Israel's names into God's presence.
- **Eternal life.** Knowing God and Jesus Christ (verse 3), a relationship that starts now and never ends. It's more than a place you go someday.
- **Sanctify.** To set apart and make holy. God does it through his word (verse 17).
- **In the world, not of it.** Christians live right in the middle of the world and belong to Jesus, sent into it without being swallowed by it.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The Son prays. On the night before the cross, we get to listen in as Jesus talks to his Father. He asks to be glorified through the cross, he tells the Father he's shown these people who God is, he asks the Father to keep them and make them holy and send them into the world, and he prays for every Christian who would ever believe, including you. Then he ends by asking that his people would be with him forever and that the Father's own love would live in them. Eternal life turns out to be knowing this God, and the Son who prays for you is the one who died to bring you in.
