# Reading guide, Unit 19: The way to the Father

> Passage: John 14:1-31
> Big question: it's the night before Jesus dies, and he spends it comforting the scared people who are about to run. What does he tell them?
> New memory verse: John 14:6. You'll learn it in lesson 2.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

We're still in the Farewell Discourse, Jesus' last long talk with his friends the night before the cross. Last unit he knelt and washed their feet and gave them a new command to love. Now he steadies them. Twice in this one chapter he says, "let not your hearts be troubled." Watch him hand frightened people the one thing that will hold them: himself.

## Lesson 1: let not your hearts be troubled (John 14:1-4)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 1 through 4.

The disciples are shaken. Jesus just said one of them will betray him and Peter will deny him, and that he's about to die. Into that fear he says, "let not your hearts be troubled. Believe in God; believe also in me." Then he points them home: in his Father's house are many rooms, and he's going to prepare a place for them and come back to take them there.

- **Observe.** In verse 1, what does Jesus tell them to do with their troubled hearts? In verses 2 and 3, where is he going, and why?
- **Interpret.** The "many rooms" isn't mainly about real estate in heaven. It's a promise of a permanent place with Jesus in the Father's presence. Why would "I go to prepare a place for you, and I will come again and take you to myself" be exactly what a scared person needs to hear?
- **Respond.** Jesus doesn't tell them to calm down and try harder. He tells them where to look: "believe also in me." When you're anxious, what do you usually look at instead? What would it change to look at him?

## Lesson 2: the way, the truth, and the life (John 14:5-7)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 5 through 7. This is the memory-verse lesson.

Thomas says out loud what everyone's thinking: "Lord, we do not know where you are going. How can we know the way?" He's lost, and he admits it. Jesus doesn't hand him a map. He hands him himself: "I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me."

- **Observe.** In verse 5, what does Thomas admit he doesn't know? In verse 6, what does Jesus say he is?
- **Interpret.** Jesus answers a lost man not with directions but with a Person. What's the difference between being handed directions to a place and having Someone come to carry you there?
- **Respond.** "No one comes to the Father except through me" sounds narrow until you flip it: it means there's a way home, and it's open to anyone. If that's true, is it good news or bad news to you? Write honestly.
- **Memory verse.** John 14:6: "Jesus said to him, 'I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.'"

## Lesson 3: whoever has seen me has seen the Father (John 14:8-11)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 8 through 11.

Philip asks for the one thing he thinks would settle everything: "Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us." Jesus is a little grieved. He's been standing right there the whole time. "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father." Then he offers a backup: if you can't take my word for it, look at the works.

- **Observe.** In verse 8, what does Philip ask for? In verse 9, how does Jesus answer him?
- **Interpret.** A lot of people carry a scary or blurry picture of God the Father. Jesus says if you want to know what the Father is actually like, look at him. How does that change the picture of God for someone who's afraid of him?
- **Go deeper (optional).** In verse 11 Jesus says if you won't believe his words, "believe on account of the works themselves." What works is he pointing at, and what's the biggest one still coming?

## Lesson 4: greater works, and ask in my name (John 14:12-14)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 12 through 14.

Jesus makes a huge claim: whoever believes in him will do the works he does, and even "greater works than these," because he's going to the Father. Then he promises that whatever they ask in his name, he'll do it, so that the Father is glorified in the Son.

- **Observe.** In verse 12, what will believers do, and why is that possible ("because...")? In verse 13, what's the goal of answered prayer?
- **Interpret.** "Greater works" doesn't mean bigger miracles than Jesus did. It means greater in reach: the church, filled with the Spirit, carrying the gospel to more people across more places than Jesus reached on foot. How does "because I am going to the Father" explain that?
- **Respond.** "Ask in my name" isn't a magic phrase you tack onto the end of a prayer. It means asking the way Jesus would, for what he wants, so the Father gets glory. How does that reshape how you'd actually pray this week?

## Lesson 5: if you love me, and the first Helper (John 14:15-17)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 15 through 17.

"If you love me, you will keep my commandments." Then, in the very next breath, Jesus promises the power to do it: he'll ask the Father, and the Father will give them "another Helper, to be with you forever, even the Spirit of truth."

- **Observe.** In verse 15, what does love for Jesus look like? In verse 16, what does Jesus promise to ask the Father for?
- **Interpret.** Notice the order: love comes first, and obedience grows out of it. And the Spirit is given right after the command, so obeying isn't on your own strength. Why does that order matter? What happens if you flip it and try to obey your way into being loved?
- **Go deeper (optional).** "Another Helper" means another of the same kind as Jesus. The Spirit is a Person with a job, not a vague force or a feeling. From verse 17, what's true of him?

## Lesson 6: I will not leave you as orphans (John 14:18-24)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 18 through 24.

"I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you." Jesus promises he won't abandon them. He'll be present with them by the Spirit. And he makes an even bigger promise: to anyone who loves him and keeps his word, he and the Father "will come to him and make our home with him."

- **Observe.** In verse 18, what does Jesus promise he will not do? In verse 23, what will Jesus and the Father do for the one who loves him?
- **Interpret.** God making his "home" in a person is the same picture as the Father's house with many rooms from verse 2, just from the other end: he prepares a place for you, and he also comes to live in you. What does it do to "feeling alone" if God himself lives in you?
- **Respond.** Verses 21 and 23 tie loving Jesus to keeping his word. Not to prove you love him, but because that's what love does. Where's one place your life shows what you actually love?

## Lesson 7: he will teach you, and peace I leave with you (John 14:25-27)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 25 through 27.

Jesus promises the Spirit will "teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you." That's how the disciples would remember and write down what Jesus taught. Then he gives them a parting gift: "peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you. Not as the world gives do I give to you."

- **Observe.** In verse 26, what two things will the Helper do? In verse 27, what does Jesus leave with them?
- **Interpret.** Verse 27 circles all the way back to verse 1, "let not your hearts be troubled." The chapter opens and closes on comfort. What kind of peace can Jesus hand to men who are about to watch him be killed? How is that different from the peace the world offers?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Jesus says the Spirit will remind the apostles of everything he said. How is that promise part of why we can trust the Gospel of John you're holding?

## Lesson 8: the Father is greater than I (John 14:28-31)

**Read it.** John chapter 14, verses 28 through 31.

Jesus says if they loved him they'd be glad he's going to the Father, "for the Father is greater than I." Then he says the ruler of this world is coming and "has no claim on me," and that he's going to the cross so the world will know he loves the Father. He ends: "Rise, let us go from here."

- **Observe.** In verse 28, why should the disciples rejoice? In verse 30, what does Jesus say the ruler of this world has on him?
- **Interpret.** "The Father is greater than I" has confused people for centuries. It doesn't mean Jesus is a lesser God. Back in John 10:30 he said, "I and the Father are one." Here he's talking about his role and mission: the Son was sent, and he came low to obey. How do you hold "the Father is greater than I" and "I and the Father are one" together?
- **Respond.** Verse 31: Jesus walks toward the cross on purpose, so the world will know he loves the Father. He wasn't trapped. He went. What does it mean to you that Jesus chose this?

## Key words to know

- **The Father's house.** Not mainly real estate in the sky. A permanent place with Jesus in God's presence. And in verse 23, God comes to make his home in you, the same picture from the other side.
- **The way, the truth, and the life.** Jesus' sixth "I am" saying. He's the one road to the Father, and the road is a Person.
- **The Helper (the Paraclete).** The Holy Spirit, "another Helper" of the same kind as Jesus. A Person with a job: he stays with the church forever, teaches, reminds, and points people to Christ.
- **The Farewell Discourse.** Jesus' last talk with his friends, John 13 through 17, the night before he dies. Tender, personal, comforting.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The night before he dies, Jesus doesn't fall apart. He comforts the people who are about to fall apart. He tells them the way home is himself, that seeing him is seeing the Father, that he'll send his own Spirit so they're never alone, and that he leaves them a peace the world can't give. He's the way, opened by the cross he's walking toward on purpose. And the whole chapter is bookended by the same tenderness: let not your hearts be troubled.
