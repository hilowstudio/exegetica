# Reading guide, Unit 18: The towel and the basin

> Passage: John 13:1-38
> Big question: the most important person in the room takes the dirtiest job. Why?
> New memory verse: John 13:34-35. You'll learn it in lesson 5.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

This is the first chapter of the second half of John, and it's the night before Jesus dies. He's alone with his friends. Before he says a word, he gets down on the floor and washes their feet, the job of the lowest servant in the house. Watch the Lord of everything kneel.

## Lesson 1: who Jesus is on this night (John 13:1-3)

**Read it.** John chapter 13, verses 1 through 3.

Before anything happens, John tells us what Jesus knows: his hour has come, the Father has put all things into his hands, and he came from God and is going back to God. He knows he holds everything. And John says he "loved his own to the end."

- **Observe.** In verses 1 and 3, list the things Jesus knows about himself on this night.
- **Interpret.** Jesus knows he holds all things and knows exactly who he is. In the very next verse, he's going to kneel and wash feet. How does being that secure make him able to go that low?
- **Respond.** People who are insecure grab for status. Jesus, totally secure, gives it up. Where would being sure of who you are in Christ free you to serve?

## Lesson 2: the washing (John 13:4-11)

**Read it.** John chapter 13, verses 4 through 11.

Jesus takes off his outer clothes, wraps a towel around his waist, and washes the disciples' feet. Peter is horrified and says, "you shall never wash my feet." Jesus says, "if I do not wash you, you have no share with me." Then he says something about being clean.

- **Observe.** In verse 8, what does Peter refuse, and what does Jesus say will happen if Peter won't let him wash him?
- **Interpret.** In verse 10 Jesus says a person who has bathed only needs their feet washed. Think of it this way: when you trust Jesus, he makes you clean all over, once. But you still get your feet dirty walking through daily life, so you come back to him to be washed again (not saved again, cleaned again). How does that help you understand confessing your sins?
- **Respond.** Peter's "never" was pride dressed up as humility. He didn't want to be served. Where is it hard for you to just let Jesus do something for you instead of earning it?

## Lesson 3: do you understand what I have done (John 13:12-17)

**Read it.** John chapter 13, verses 12 through 17.

Jesus sits back down and explains: I'm your Lord and Teacher, and I washed your feet, so you should wash one another's feet. He gave them an example to follow. And he says the blessing comes from doing it, not just knowing it.

- **Observe.** In verse 14, what does Jesus say his disciples should do for each other? In verse 17, where does the blessing come from?
- **Interpret.** "Wash one another's feet" isn't mainly about literal feet. It's about doing the low, humble, unglamorous work of serving each other. Why does Jesus put the blessing on doing it rather than just knowing about it?
- **Respond.** What's a "foot-washing" job in your life, something low and unnoticed you could do for someone? Name one.

## Lesson 4: the betrayal foretold (John 13:18-30)

**Read it.** John chapter 13, verses 18 through 30.

Jesus says one of them will betray him. The disciple John, sitting next to Jesus, asks who. Jesus hands a piece of dipped bread to Judas, which was actually a gesture of honor. Judas takes it, and Satan enters him. Jesus tells him to go do it quickly. Judas leaves, and John writes, "and it was night."

- **Observe.** In verse 26, what does Jesus give Judas? In verse 30, what does John add right after Judas leaves?
- **Interpret.** Jesus knew Judas would betray him the whole time, and he still washed Judas's feet and handed him bread in honor. In verse 19 Jesus says he's telling them ahead of time so they'll believe. How does Jesus knowing all this in advance make his betrayal easier to trust God through, not harder?
- **Go deeper (optional).** "And it was night" (verse 30) is more than the time of day. What does John mean by ending Judas's exit with darkness?

## Lesson 5: the new commandment (John 13:31-35)

**Read it.** John chapter 13, verses 31 through 35. This is the memory-verse lesson.

The moment Judas leaves to set the cross in motion, Jesus says he's now being glorified. Then he gives a new command: love one another, the way I have loved you. And he says this love is how everyone will know you belong to him.

- **Observe.** In verse 34, what's the new commandment? What's the measure, the "as" part?
- **Interpret.** Loving your neighbor was already an old command (Leviticus 19:18). What's new here is the standard: "as I have loved you." Given what he just did with the towel, and what he's about to do on the cross, how high is that standard?
- **Respond.** In verse 35 Jesus says love is the badge, how everyone will know you're his. Is that what people would guess about Christians from watching you? Write honestly.
- **Memory verse.** John 13:34-35: "A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another."

## Lesson 6: the denial foretold (John 13:36-38)

**Read it.** John chapter 13, verses 36 through 38.

Peter wants to follow Jesus wherever he's going and says, "I will lay down my life for you." Jesus tells him the truth: before the rooster crows, Peter will deny even knowing him three times.

- **Observe.** In verse 37, what does Peter promise? In verse 38, what does Jesus say will really happen?
- **Interpret.** Peter says he'll lay down his life for Jesus. But laying down your life for the sheep is what the Good Shepherd does (John 10:11). Who's the only one who actually does it? What does Peter get backwards?
- **Respond.** Jesus knew Peter would fail him that very night, and he loved him anyway (and later restored him). What does it mean to you that Jesus loves people who let him down?

## Pulling it together (Lesson 7)

Our last class on John 13 pulls the whole chapter together: the Lord who kneels, the washing that points to the cross, Jesus in control even of betrayal, and the new command to love. No new reading. We'll also connect this towel-shaped love to what's coming in chapter 15, "love one another," and step into the next unit, where Jesus tells his scared friends, "let not your hearts be troubled."

## Key words to know

- **The towel.** The tool of the lowest servant. Jesus, the Lord of all, uses it. It's the measure of humble love.
- **Bathed vs washed.** When you trust Jesus, he cleans you all over, once (like a bath). Then he keeps cleaning the daily dirt off (like washing feet). One is being saved, the other is ongoing.
- **The new commandment.** "Love one another as I have loved you." The word "new" means new in kind. The new part is the standard: his own self-giving love.
- **The Farewell Discourse.** Jesus' last words to his friends (John 13 to 17), the night before he dies. Tender, personal, comforting.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus, holding all things in his hands, gets on the floor with a towel and washes the feet of men who are about to run. The washing points to the cross, where he makes his people clean for good. He's in control even of the man who betrays him. And he hands his friends a new command: love one another the way I have loved you. The towel and the cross are the measure, and that kind of love is how the whole world will know you're his.
