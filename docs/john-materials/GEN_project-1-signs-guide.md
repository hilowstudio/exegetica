# Project guide, Q1: The Signs

> **Quarter:** 1 (fall) · **Mode:** individual
> **Introduced:** Friday, October 2 (at the chapters 1-3 checkpoint) · **Due:** Thursday, November 5 (at the chapters 4-6 checkpoint)
> Read everything in the ESV.

## The assignment

> Someone you know has never opened a Bible and thinks Jesus' miracles were magic tricks. Take one of John's seven signs and make something that shows them why John calls it a *sign*, and what it proves about who Jesus is.

That's the whole thing. You pick one sign, and you build something that opens it up for a real person who doesn't believe it yet.

## The one idea to build everything on

John never calls these miracles "wonders" for the show. He calls them signs. A sign is a window. You look at what Jesus does, and you see who Jesus is. John says so himself at the very end of the book: the signs "are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:30-31). And he tells you at the very first sign, at the wedding in Cana, that Jesus "manifested his glory. And his disciples believed in him" (2:11).

So here is the difference that makes your whole project work. A magic trick points at itself and says "look what I can do," and it hides how it was done. A sign points away from itself, at Jesus, and says "look who this is." Your job is to help a friend look *through* the miracle and see the face of God. If your project only makes the miracle look impressive, it has done half the work. Show them what it proves.

## The seven signs you can choose from

By the time this is due you'll have read five of the seven. Pick the one that grips you.

1. **Water into wine, Cana (2:1-11).** Jesus the Bridegroom bringing the joy and abundance the prophets promised. The first sign, where he "manifested his glory."
2. **Healing the official's son (4:46-54).** His bare word carries the Father's life-giving power across fifteen miles. The word does what it says.
3. **Healing the invalid at Bethesda (5:1-9).** The Son who gives life to the helpless by a word, doing the Father's own work.
4. **Feeding the five thousand (6:1-15).** The true bread from heaven, the God of the Exodus handing out bread in the wilderness, pointing to himself as the bread of life.
5. **Walking on the water (6:16-21).** The one who treads the sea speaks the divine name over his friends' fear: "It is I; do not be afraid."

Two more signs come later in the book, the man born blind (chapter 9) and the raising of Lazarus (chapter 11). Since you won't have read them by the due date, choose from the five above.

## Voice and choice: pick your format

The skills are fixed by the rubric below. The shape is yours. Choose one:

- a short video
- an infographic or a poster with commentary
- a first-person monologue (you become a witness who saw it)
- a piece of art with a written explanation
- a short documentary or interview
- a written piece (a letter to your friend, a short essay, a story)

Pick the format that fits how *you* would actually reach the friend you have in mind.

## The milestones (the process is graded, not just the final piece)

Your teacher will give you the exact dates. The order is fixed:

1. **Pitch.** Your sign, the friend you're making it for, your format, and why. A few sentences.
2. **Plan.** Your evidence map: every verse and quote you'll use, and how the piece is structured. This is where you make sure the sign actually points at Jesus.
3. **Draft or rehearsal,** with feedback from a peer. Bring something real enough to get useful notes on.
4. **Present** it to the class, with live questions.

There's a fifteen-minute in-class check-in during a project week for roadblocks. Use it.

## The Maker's Note (required, turned in with the final)

A few honest sentences, in your own words: why you chose this format and this angle, what was hard, and what you'd do differently. This is graded as a skill, self-awareness, and it's hard to fake. Write it like you mean it.

## How to make it actually land

- **Name what the sign proves.** Every sign reveals something specific about who Jesus is. Say it out loud in your piece. Don't leave your friend to guess.
- **Use the real text.** Quote John. Point at what the passage actually says. That's your evidence, and it outlasts a cool effect.
- **Answer the "magic trick" objection head-on.** Your friend thinks it was a trick. Show them why a sign is the opposite of a trick: it hides nothing and it points at someone.
- **Keep the wonder serving the revelation.** The miracle is amazing. Let the amazement carry your friend to the person, and not stop at the spectacle.

## Rubric

Each row scored 1 (beginning) to 4 (exemplary), multiplied by its weight, summed, and divided by 4 for the percentage.

| Criterion | Weight | What a 4 looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| **Grasp of the sign** | 30% | The sign is read accurately and in context, no misreadings; you clearly understand what happens and where it sits in John. |
| **Christ at the center** | 20% | The piece shows plainly what the sign reveals about who Jesus is, and points through the miracle to the person, answering the "magic trick" objection. |
| **Independent insight** | 20% | Your own honest thinking is in it: a real angle for a real audience, not a summary. |
| **Communication** | 15% | Clear, organized, prepared, and pitched to the friend you named. |
| **Live Q&A** | 15% | You answer follow-up questions with real understanding of the sign and what it shows. |

**Your project grade also includes** the graded milestones (the process) and the Maker's Note.

**A word on the grade and your heart.** This project grades whether you *understood and explained* the sign, which is content. It does not grade whether you personally believe it. You can build an excellent project and be honestly still deciding. Show me you see what John is showing.
