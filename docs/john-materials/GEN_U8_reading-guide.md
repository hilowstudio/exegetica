# Reading guide, Unit 8: Sign 3 and the Son's authority

> Passage: John 5:1-47
> Big question: when Jesus claims to do what only God can do, what will you do with him?
> Memory work: no new verse this unit. Keep your five review verses sharp.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

John 5 has two halves. First Jesus heals a man on the Sabbath (5:1-18). Then he explains who he is, in the biggest claim he's made yet: the Son does what the Father does, gives life, and judges (5:19-47). Watch the healing turn into a trial about Jesus' identity.

## Lesson 1: the man who couldn't get to the water (John 5:1-9)

**Read it.** John chapter 5, verses 1 through 9.

A man has been sick for thirty-eight years, lying by a pool where people hoped the stirred water would heal them. Jesus asks him, "Do you want to be healed?" The man explains he has no one to help him in. Jesus heals him with one sentence: "Get up, take up your bed, and walk."

- **Observe.** In verse 5, how long has the man been sick? In verse 7, what's his problem getting to the water?
- **Interpret.** Jesus asks a man who's been down for thirty-eight years if he wants to get well. Why ask? What does the man's answer ("I have no one") show about his situation?
- **Respond.** Jesus healed a man nobody else would help, and asked nothing of him first. Where do you feel stuck or overlooked? Write it honestly.
- **Go deeper (optional).** Your Bible probably skips from verse 3 to verse 5, with a footnote for verse 4. The oldest copies of John don't have verse 4 (the bit about an angel stirring the water). Why is it good news that scholars track every line like that?

## Lesson 2: the claim that got him killed (John 5:10-18)

**Read it.** John chapter 5, verses 10 through 18.

Because Jesus healed on the Sabbath, the leaders come after him. Jesus answers, "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (5:17). They understand exactly what he means, and it makes them want to kill him.

- **Observe.** In verse 16, why are the leaders going after Jesus?
- **Observe.** In verse 18, what two things does John say Jesus was doing that made them want to kill him?
- **Interpret.** Jesus calls God "my Father" and claims to work God's own work. Verse 18 says this made him "equal with God." Why would that claim be either the best news or the most dangerous thing anyone ever said?
- **Respond.** Jesus told the healed man, "Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen" (5:14). What's the "something worse" than being sick for thirty-eight years?

## Lesson 3: the Son does what the Father does (John 5:19-23)

**Read it.** John chapter 5, verses 19 through 23.

Jesus explains himself. The Son does only what he sees the Father doing, and whatever the Father does, the Son does too, including giving life and judging. He should be honored exactly the way the Father is honored.

- **Observe.** In verse 21, what two things does Jesus say the Son does that only God does?
- **Interpret.** In verse 23 Jesus says everyone should honor the Son "just as they honor the Father." That leaves you no room to call Jesus just a good teacher. Why not?
- **Respond.** Jesus is either telling the truth about himself or making the biggest false claim in history. Which do you find yourself leaning toward, and why?

## Lesson 4: life now, and life to come (John 5:24-30)

**Read it.** John chapter 5, verses 24 through 30. This lesson has an optional memory verse.

Jesus makes a promise: whoever hears his word and believes has eternal life right now and has "passed from death to life" (5:24). He talks about two resurrections, one that's already happening (dead hearts coming alive) and one still to come (everyone raised from the grave).

- **Observe.** In verse 24, what does the person who hears and believes have right now? What do they not come into?
- **Interpret.** Verse 25 says the hour "is now here" and verse 28 says the hour "is coming." How can Jesus talk about a resurrection that's already happening and one that's still ahead?
- **Respond.** Verse 24 says believers have "passed from death to life" already, done, settled. What would change for you if that were true of you right now?
- **Go deeper (optional stretch verse).** Carry John 5:24 if you want it, off the quiz: "Truly, truly, I say to you, whoever hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life. He does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life."

## Lesson 5: everything points to Jesus (John 5:31-47)

**Read it.** John chapter 5, verses 31 through 47.

Jesus lists his witnesses: John the Baptist, his own miracles, the Father, and the Scriptures. Then he says the sharpest thing: these Bible experts study the Scriptures for eternal life and walk right past the One the Scriptures are about.

- **Observe.** In verses 33 through 39, name the four witnesses Jesus points to.
- **Interpret.** In verse 39 and 40, Jesus says they "search the Scriptures" but "refuse to come to me." How can someone know the Bible really well and still miss Jesus?
- **Respond.** Verse 46 says Moses "wrote of me." The whole Old Testament points to Jesus. How does that change the way you'd read the parts of the Bible before the Gospels?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Read Luke 24:27. After he rose, Jesus walked two disciples through "all the Scriptures" about himself. What's he claiming the Old Testament is really about?

## Key words to know

- **Bethesda.** The pool in Jerusalem where sick people waited, hoping to be healed.
- **Sabbath.** The seventh day, set apart for rest. The leaders had strict rules about it, and Jesus healed on it on purpose.
- **Equal with God.** What the leaders understood Jesus to claim (5:18). He works God's own work and takes God's own honor.
- **Resurrection.** Being raised to life. Jesus talks about a spiritual one now and a bodily one at the last day.
- **Witness / testimony.** Evidence that something's true. Jesus points to four witnesses that back up who he is.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus heals a man nobody would help, then tells the world who he is: the Son who does what the Father does, gives life to the dead, judges everyone, and deserves the honor God gets. The whole Old Testament was pointing to him the whole time. Hearing his word and believing is how you pass from death to life, starting now.
