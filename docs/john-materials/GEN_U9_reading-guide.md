# Reading guide, Unit 9: Bread of Life

> Passage: John 6:1-71
> Big question: what are you really hungry for, and can Jesus be enough?
> New memory verse: John 6:35. You'll learn it in lesson 4.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

John 6 is one long chapter about bread. Jesus feeds a huge crowd, walks on water, then says something that empties the room: he himself is the bread, and the way you eat is to come to him and believe. Watch the crowd go from thousands to twelve.

## Lesson 1: the feeding of the five thousand (John 6:1-15)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 1 through 15.

A huge crowd follows Jesus up a mountain near Passover. He asks Philip where they'll buy bread, tests him, then feeds thousands from a boy's five loaves and two fish. Twelve baskets are left over. The crowd tries to grab him and make him king.

- **Observe.** In verses 9 through 13, what did Jesus start with, and what was left over when everyone had eaten?
- **Interpret.** In verse 15 the crowd tries to make Jesus king by force, and he slips away. They wanted a king who'd keep the food coming. Why would Jesus refuse that kind of crown?
- **Respond.** The crowd wanted what Jesus could give them. Where do you want God mostly for what he can do for you?

## Lesson 2: walking on the water (John 6:16-24)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 16 through 24.

That night the disciples row across the sea in the dark and a storm. They see Jesus walking on the water and get scared. He says, "It is I; do not be afraid," and gets in the boat. They reach shore at once.

- **Observe.** In verses 19 and 20, what do the disciples see, and what does Jesus say to them?
- **Interpret.** John doesn't say Jesus stopped the storm. He says Jesus got in the boat. What does it tell you about Jesus that he comes to his friends in the middle of the trouble?
- **Go deeper (optional).** "It is I" is literally "I am," the name God gave himself to Moses (Exodus 3:14). Why would Jesus say that name while walking on water in a storm?

## Lesson 3: why are you looking for me? (John 6:25-29)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 25 through 29.

The crowd tracks Jesus down. He tells them straight: you're chasing me because I fed you, not because you understood the sign. Then he tells them what to work for, and what the one real "work" is.

- **Observe.** In verse 26, why does Jesus say the crowd is really looking for him?
- **Interpret.** In verses 28 and 29 they ask what work God wants, and Jesus says, "believe in him whom he has sent." How is believing a "work" you can't take credit for?
- **Respond.** Be honest: do you come to Jesus for what he gives, or for him? What's the difference?

## Lesson 4: I am the bread of life (John 6:30-40)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 30 through 40. This is the memory-verse lesson.

The crowd brings up the manna their ancestors ate in the desert. Jesus says the real bread from heaven is a person, and then says it plainly: "I am the bread of life." Come to him and you're never hungry again. And everyone the Father gives him, he keeps.

- **Observe.** In verse 35, what does Jesus call himself? What does he promise the person who comes to him and believes?
- **Interpret.** In verse 37 Jesus says, "whoever comes to me I will never cast out." How does that take the pressure off you about whether you're good enough to come?
- **Respond.** Jesus says he satisfies a hunger bread can't reach. What are you trying to fill that hunger with right now?
- **Memory verse.** John 6:35: "Jesus said to them, 'I am the bread of life; whoever comes to me shall not hunger, and whoever believes in me shall never thirst.'"

## Lesson 5: no one comes unless the Father draws him (John 6:41-51)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 41 through 51.

The crowd grumbles: we know this guy's parents, how can he be from heaven? Jesus tells them their coming to him is a gift the Father gives. Then he says the bread he'll give "for the life of the world" is his own flesh, pointing to the cross.

- **Observe.** In verse 44, what does Jesus say has to happen before anyone can come to him?
- **Interpret.** Verse 44 could sound cold, like God picks favorites. But look at verse 37 and verse 51. How is "the Father draws you" actually the safest, kindest thing Jesus could say?
- **Respond.** In verse 51 Jesus says he'll give his flesh "for the life of the world." He's talking about the cross. What does it mean that the bread costs him his life?

## Lesson 6: eat the flesh, drink the blood (John 6:52-59)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 52 through 59.

The crowd is horrified: how can he give us his flesh to eat? Jesus doubles down. Unless you eat his flesh and drink his blood, you have no life. He means feeding on him by faith, taking all of him into yourself the way you take in food.

- **Observe.** In verse 54, what does the person who "feeds on" Jesus have?
- **Interpret.** Jesus isn't asking for cannibalism. Look back at verse 35, where "coming" and "believing" satisfy hunger and thirst. What does "eat my flesh" really mean?
- **Go deeper (optional).** This is the language we use at the Lord's Supper (communion). How does the Supper picture what Jesus is talking about here?

## Lesson 7: where else would we go? (John 6:60-71)

**Read it.** John chapter 6, verses 60 through 71.

The teaching is too hard for the crowd, and most of them leave. Jesus asks the twelve if they'll go too. Peter answers with one of the best lines in the book. Then Jesus says something sober about one of the twelve.

- **Observe.** In verse 66, what do many of the disciples do? In verses 68 and 69, how does Peter answer?
- **Interpret.** Peter says, "Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life." He doesn't fully understand yet, and he stays. Why does he stay when the crowd leaves?
- **Respond.** Following Jesus got hard and the crowd thinned out. When has following Jesus been hard for you, and what kept you (or would keep you) in?

## Key words to know

- **Bread of life.** What Jesus calls himself. He satisfies a hunger deeper than the stomach, the hunger for God.
- **Manna.** The bread God gave Israel in the desert (Exodus 16). It kept them alive for a while. Jesus is the bread that gives life forever.
- **The Father draws.** God brings people to Jesus. Your coming to him starts with him coming after you.
- **Feed on / eat my flesh.** To take all of Jesus into yourself by faith, the way food becomes part of you. It points to his death on the cross.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

Jesus fed thousands, then told them the real food is himself. He's the true bread from heaven, better than the manna, and he gives his own body on the cross so you can live forever. Coming to him and believing is how you eat. When the crowd left over that, Peter stayed, because there's nowhere else to go for the words of life.
