## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it next to your Reading Journal while we gather the whole Gospel in class. This is a one-page map of everything you've studied, and a keeper for after the course.

We've walked all the way through John, from "In the beginning was the Word" to "do you love me" on a beach. This unit doesn't teach anything new. It gathers the whole year and asks the one question the book was written to raise. John told you his aim on the first day and again at the end:

> **John 20:31:** "but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name."

Everything below is that verse, seen across the whole book.

## The whole Christ of John, in five threads

**1. The Word made flesh, and the deity of Christ.**
John opens by telling you who Jesus is: "the Word was God" (1:1), who "became flesh and dwelt among us" (1:14). All year you tracked the claim: "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58), "I and the Father are one" (10:30), "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9). And the book ends where it began: Thomas falls down and says, "My Lord and my God!" (20:28). The Gospel opens and closes on the same confession.

**2. The seven signs (John 1-12).**
Jesus showed who he is by what he did: water into wine (2), the official's son healed from a distance (4), the paralytic at Bethesda (5), feeding the five thousand (6), walking on water (6), the man born blind (9), and Lazarus raised (11). Each sign is a window onto the person, chosen so you would believe (20:30-31).

**3. The "I am" sayings.**
In his own words, Jesus told you what he is to you: the bread of life (6:35), the light of the world (8:12), the door and the good shepherd (10), the resurrection and the life (11:25), the way, the truth, and the life (14:6), and the true vine (15:5). And under them all, the divine name from the burning bush: "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58).

**4. The hour: glory through the cross.**
The whole book moved toward "the hour." Jesus is the Lamb of God (1:29), who died at Passover with no bone broken (19:36), reigning from the cross, finishing the work with one word: "It is finished" (19:30). Then the tomb was empty, and he rose. The glory John saw shone brightest at a cross.

**5. The response Jesus asks.**
The Gospel was written for a response: believe, and have life (20:31). "God so loved the world, that he gave his only Son" (3:16), and "to all who did receive him... he gave the right to become children of God" (1:12). And the risen Lord's last words are a question and a call: "do you love me... feed my sheep" (21:17), and "follow me" (21:19).

## Your sixteen memory verses, as one portrait of Jesus

Read them in order, and you get the whole story. These aren't sixteen separate sayings. They're one Christ, seen sixteen times.

1. **20:31**, why the book exists: believe, and have life.
2. **1:14**, the Word became flesh. God moved in.
3. **1:12**, those who received him became children of God.
4. **3:16**, God so loved the world that he gave his Son.
5. **4:24**, God is spirit; worship in spirit and truth.
6. **6:35**, I am the bread of life. He feeds.
7. **8:12**, I am the light of the world. He gives sight.
8. **10:11**, I am the good shepherd who lays down his life. He dies for the sheep.
9. **11:25**, I am the resurrection and the life. He conquers the grave.
10. **13:34-35**, love one another as I have loved you. He makes a people.
11. **14:6**, I am the way, the truth, the life. He is the only road home.
12. **15:5**, I am the vine; apart from me you can do nothing. He is our strength.
13. **16:33**, I have overcome the world. He wins.
14. **17:3**, eternal life is to know God and Jesus Christ. He defines the prize.
15. **19:30**, It is finished. He pays it in full.
16. **21:17**, feed my sheep. He restores and sends.

From the Word made flesh to the risen Lord handing a forgiven friend the flock: one Jesus, the whole way through.

## The Passion project

Your Q4 project, "The Passion and a Personal Response," comes due this unit. Study the cross honestly, what happened, why John tells it the way he does, what "It is finished" means, and how the eyewitness (19:35) stakes everything on it. Then say, in your own voice, what the cross stirs or asks in you. Your personal response is graded for honesty and the care of your thinking, never for what you conclude. An honest "I'm still not sure, and here's why" is a complete and good answer.

## Think it over

- **Observe.** Pick one of the five threads. What did you learn about Jesus from it this year?
- **Interpret.** John says the whole book was written so you would believe and have life (20:31). Why do you think he ends it with "do you love me" instead of a summary?
- **Respond.** A year ago, who was Jesus to you? Who is he now? Write honestly.

## Where the whole year lands

We started with "the Word became flesh," and we've ended with the risen Lord cooking breakfast for the friend who denied him and asking, "do you love me?" In between, you watched God become a man, turn water to wine, open blind eyes, raise the dead, wash feet, finish the work on a cross, and walk out of a grave. One person, the whole way through. John wrote every word so that you would believe he is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name. And the last thing he shows you is Jesus turning to a failure with a question that still comes to you: do you love me? Follow me.
