# Reading guide, Unit 26: The empty tomb

> Passage: John 20:1-31
> Big question: on the third day, the tomb is empty. Is Jesus really, bodily alive, and what does it mean if he is?
> No new memory verse this unit. But your very first verse, John 20:31, comes home here.
> Read it in the ESV.

## How to use this guide

You don't turn this in. Keep it open next to your Reading Journal while we work the passage in class.

- **Read** the passage first, slowly, before you write anything.
- **Observe** means say what the text actually says.
- **Interpret** asks what it means and who Jesus is in it.
- **Respond** asks what it's asking of you.
- **Go deeper** prompts are optional. Take them if you want more.

Last unit ended in the dark, with a dead body in a sealed tomb, and everything went quiet. This unit breaks the quiet. It's Easter. The tone lifts from grief to joy and wonder, so read it that way, with your eyes open for the living Lord. And keep one thing in mind the whole way: John didn't write a legend. He records grave clothes lying folded, real wounds you can touch, and an eyewitness who watched it. This is a real, bodily resurrection, and it changes everything.

## Lesson 1: the stone is gone (John 20:1-2)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 1 and 2.

Early on Sunday, while it's still dark, Mary Magdalene comes to the tomb and finds the stone rolled away. She runs to Peter and John, sure someone has stolen the body: "they have taken the Lord, and we don't know where they've laid him."

- **Observe.** In verse 1, what does Mary find when she gets to the tomb? In verse 2, what does she assume happened?
- **Interpret.** Mary comes in the dark to grieve at a grave, and the grave is open and empty. Her first thought is that someone stole the body. Why is that the natural, honest first reaction? What would you have thought?
- **Respond.** Mary went to the tomb to be near someone she'd lost. Where do you go, or what do you do, when you're grieving something? Write honestly.

## Lesson 2: the folded grave clothes (John 20:3-10)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 3 through 10.

Peter and John race to the tomb. John gets there first and looks in at the linen cloths. Peter goes in and sees the grave clothes lying there, and the face cloth folded up by itself in a separate place. Then John goes in, "and he saw and believed."

- **Observe.** In verses 6 and 7, what does Peter see inside the tomb? In verse 8, what happens to John when he goes in?
- **Interpret.** Think about the folded face cloth. A grave robber, in a hurry and stealing an expensive body, does not stop to undress the corpse and neatly fold the linens. What does the careful, orderly scene tell you actually happened here?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 9 says they "did not yet understand the Scripture, that he must rise." They believed before they fully understood. How does the empty tomb send them back to the Old Testament with new eyes (this is the same pattern as 2:22 and 12:16)?

## Lesson 3: he calls her by name (John 20:11-16)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 11 through 16. This is a short-day, single-focus lesson.

Mary stays at the tomb, weeping. She sees two angels, and then a man she thinks is the gardener. She begs him to tell her where the body is. And then he says one word: "Mary." And instantly she knows him. "Rabboni!", she says, which means Teacher.

- **Observe.** In verse 15, who does Mary think Jesus is? In verse 16, what does Jesus say, and how does Mary respond?
- **Interpret.** Mary is looking right at Jesus and doesn't recognize his face. She recognizes her name in his voice. Remember John 10:3, the good shepherd "calls his own sheep by name." How is this scene that promise coming true, and what does it mean that he knew her name?
- **Respond.** The risen Lord of the universe knew one grieving woman by name and called her. He knows your name too. What would change if you really believed Jesus knows you personally, by name?

## Lesson 4: my Father and your Father (John 20:17-18)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 17 and 18.

Jesus tells Mary, "do not cling to me, for I have not yet ascended." Then he gives her the first Easter message to carry: go tell my brothers, "I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God." Mary runs and announces, "I have seen the Lord."

- **Observe.** In verse 17, what does Jesus tell Mary not to do, and what message does he send her with? In verse 18, what does Mary tell the disciples?
- **Interpret.** "Do not cling to me" isn't Jesus being cold. He's telling Mary an even closer nearness is coming (his Spirit, in every believer). And look what he calls the disciples: "my brothers," and their Father is now "your Father." What did Jesus do in that tomb that makes his Father our Father too?
- **Go deeper (optional).** The first person to see the risen Jesus, and the first to preach the resurrection, was Mary Magdalene, a woman with a hard past. What does it say about Jesus that he chose her to be the first witness?

## Lesson 5: peace, and the wounds (John 20:19-20)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 19 and 20.

That evening, the disciples are hiding behind locked doors, afraid. Jesus comes and stands among them and says, "Peace be with you." Then he shows them his hands and his side, the wounds. And the disciples are overjoyed when they see the Lord.

- **Observe.** In verse 19, why are the doors locked, and what's the first thing Jesus says? In verse 20, what does he show them, and how do they respond?
- **Interpret.** These are the men who ran away and abandoned Jesus. His first word to them isn't "how could you?" It's "peace." And they recognize him by his wounds. What does it tell you about Jesus that he comes to the ones who failed him with peace, and still carries the scars of what he did for them?
- **Respond.** Jesus met his friends' fear and failure with "peace." Where do you need to hear him say "peace" to you, in a fear or a failure you're carrying?

## Lesson 6: sent, and given the Spirit (John 20:21-23)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 21 through 23.

Jesus says "peace" again, then commissions them: "As the Father has sent me, so I send you." He breathes on them and says, "Receive the Holy Spirit." And he gives them the ministry of forgiveness: the sins they forgive are forgiven.

- **Observe.** In verse 21, what does Jesus send the disciples to do? In verse 22, what does he give them?
- **Interpret.** Jesus "breathed" on them. That word takes you back to Genesis 2:7, where God breathed life into Adam. The risen Lord is breathing new life into his church. How is the church a kind of new creation, sent out with his life in it?
- **Go deeper (optional).** Verse 23 isn't about priests granting pardon on their own. It's the church announcing the forgiveness the gospel carries: when someone believes the good news, their sins really are forgiven. How does that make sharing the gospel a job Jesus gives to ordinary Christians, including you?

## Lesson 7: my Lord and my God (John 20:24-29)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 24 through 29.

Thomas missed it, and he won't believe unless he touches the wounds himself. Eight days later, Jesus comes again and offers Thomas exactly what he asked for: "put your finger here." Thomas answers with the biggest words in the whole book: "My Lord and my God!" And Jesus says, "blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed."

- **Observe.** In verse 25, what does Thomas demand before he'll believe? In verse 28, what does Thomas finally say to Jesus?
- **Interpret.** Jesus doesn't shame Thomas for doubting. He comes back and gives him the exact proof he asked for. And Thomas responds by calling Jesus "my Lord and my God," worshiping him as God. Way back in John 1:1, John said "the Word was God." How does Thomas's confession bring the whole book full circle?
- **Respond.** In verse 29, "blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed." That's you. You weren't in that room, and you're asked to believe on the witness of people who were. Where are you in that, honestly, and what's one honest question or step you have?

## Lesson 8: why this book was written (John 20:30-31)

**Read it.** John chapter 20, verses 30 and 31. This is your very first memory verse, coming home.

John steps back and tells you why he wrote everything. He picked these particular signs out of many, and he wrote them down for one reason: "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name."

- **Observe.** In verse 31, what are the two things John wants for you (what to believe, and what to have)?
- **Interpret.** You've carried this verse since the first day of the course. Now you're standing in its home, at the end of John's own account, right after Thomas worshiped the risen Lord. Everything you've read, all the signs and sayings, was written to do one thing in you. What is it? And has it happened?
- **Respond.** John wrote this whole book so you would believe and have life. That's the point of all of it. Where are you with that, today? Write honestly.
- **Memory verse (coming home).** John 20:31: "but these are written so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name."

## Key words to know

- **The grave clothes.** The linen wrappings and the folded face cloth (20:6-7), left behind in order, the first evidence of a resurrection and not a robbery.
- **Rabboni.** Aramaic for "Teacher," Mary's cry when the risen Jesus calls her name (20:16).
- **"My Lord and my God."** Thomas's confession (20:28), the highest words in the Gospel, worshiping Jesus as God.
- **The purpose statement.** John 20:30-31, where John tells you why he wrote the whole book: that you would believe and have life.

## Memory verses

No new verse this unit. But your first one comes home: John 20:31 lives right here, at the end of John's account. Keep all fifteen sharp: John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16, 4:24, 6:35, 8:12, 10:11, 11:25, 13:34-35, 14:6, 15:5, 16:33, 17:3, and 19:30. Watch how many of them light up in this chapter: 11:25 (the resurrection and the life) now stands alive; 10:11 (the good shepherd) calls Mary by name; 19:30 (It is finished) is proven true by an empty tomb; 16:33 (peace) is spoken in person. The final new verse comes in Unit 27, John 21:17.

## Where the whole unit lands on Jesus

The tomb is empty, and Jesus is bodily, gloriously alive, never to die again. He leaves his grave clothes folded like a man who wasn't in a hurry. He calls a grieving woman by her name. He makes his Father our Father. He brings peace to the friends who ran, and breathes his Spirit into his church, and sends them out. He meets a doubter with patience and gets worshiped as "my Lord and my God." And John tells you why he wrote all of it down: so that you would believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name. That's the whole point. That's been the point since day one.
