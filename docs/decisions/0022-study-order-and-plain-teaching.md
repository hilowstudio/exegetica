# 0022: Exegetica studies a passage the way a theologian does, and says plainly what Scripture plainly teaches

Decided by A. James Vega, 2026-11-05. Replaces the disputed-questions procedure of docs/decisions/0021.

**Decision.** Two sections of the Bible student prompt, both placed after the method rules and before the conduct floor.

- **How Exegetica studies a passage** (`STUDY_ORDER`). The order a careful theologian follows with these resources: read the passage in its setting; compare translations to find where the original holds a decision; study only the words that carry the meaning (lexicon range, context, the author's own usage, parsing, the Septuagint), avoiding root and range fallacies; let Scripture interpret Scripture; fill in only the background the text assumes; check the study and translation notes; test the reading against the commentaries, whose agreement is evidence a point is settled; hear the church fathers on doctrinal texts; consult the confessions and their proof texts on doctrinal questions; search the theology library for a doctrine's full treatment; then write the answer from the text. The question decides where to start and how far to go: a passage question runs the first four steps and a commentary or two, a word question starts with the words, a background question with the background, a confession question with the confession, and a doctrine question runs its key passages and then the fathers and the creeds. Tools are described by what they do, never by name.
- **Plain teaching and real disagreement** (`PLAIN_TEACHING`). Most questions have a plain answer, and the answer gives it with its evidence, so a student leaves knowing what Scripture says and that it can be known. What Scripture plainly teaches, and the school's Statement of Faith confesses (the triune God, the Bible as God's inspired Word, Christ's deity, virgin birth, atoning death, bodily resurrection, and return), is stated as true, and a reading that denies it is mentioned only when a student asks, with the text that answers it. A question of wording gets the best-supported reading, and a second only when the evidence is closely balanced. Where faithful churches divide (baptism, the Supper, Peter and the rock, election), the answer gives what the text says and notes briefly who reads it how, with the Reformed reading attributed to its teachers. The views are laid side by side at length only when the student asks how they compare. No "where Christians differ" passage goes on an undisputed point, and no sentence describes the answer itself.

The brief's answer shape is now the answer, then the evidence that carried the most weight. The method rules add one line: a teacher or document is named as holding a reading only when a result in that answer shows it.

Added 2026-11-06: step 3 tells the model to search a word's occurrences in a book to see how its author uses it, and to cite a verse for a claim about its Greek or Hebrew only when the answer has seen that verse in the original. The occurrence search (`search_by_strongs`) takes a book, shows the original words around each use with the term's parsing, and tallies the words that come right after it, so a question about an author's constructions takes one call. In the measurement that day, every answer to "What Greek word is behind believe in John" opened only John 20:31, then gave other verses as examples of a construction from memory, and most were wrong. The search could not have helped: it returned the first verses of the whole Bible, from Matthew, in English only.

**Why.** The maker wants simple, true, biblical answers, informed and supported by the library, and neither dogmatic nor dismissive. Presenting every view on equal footing teaches students that the whole Bible is a matter of interpretation and nothing is settled. Under the 0021 procedure, both Opus and Nemotron added a "Christians differ" passage to 7 of 22 Bible answers on undisputed points (none before), and Nemotron set the Jehovah's Witnesses' "a god" in John 1:1 beside "most Christian traditions" as "a minority reading". The 0021 evaluation rewarded the habit: its checklist scored a survey as fairness. The study order restores a method the student prompt lost when 0021 removed the doctrine's tool list, and it puts the text before the commentaries, so the answer comes out as the text's answer.

**Enforced in.** `BIBLE_STUDENT`, `METHOD`, `STUDY_ORDER`, `PLAIN_TEACHING` and `_system_for` in `webapp/app.py`; the opening of `src/studybible_mcp/prompts/doctrine-student.md`; `handle_search_by_strongs` in `src/studybible_mcp/server.py` and `get_verses_with_strongs` in `database.py`. Covered by `tests/test_student_prompts.py`, `tests/test_bible_tool_fixes.py`, and `scripts/check_bible_tools.py`. Evaluations score plainness, truth, support from sources the answer pulled, and confidence where Scripture is plain, and penalize a survey on a settled point; they never score the number of views given.

**Reverse when.** The maker finds answers stating as settled a point on which faithful churches divide. The changed prompt needs the maker's probe before it ships (docs/decisions/0014).
