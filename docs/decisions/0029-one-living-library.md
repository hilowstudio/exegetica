# 0029: One Living Library for everyone, with the Reading Room inside it and a shelf for each class

Decided by A. James Vega, 2026-11-10, from the cohesion review of 2026-11-09 (direction A, "one text, many views", built and deployed in v151) and the question of what the Living Library's "ready library" still meant. It extends 0007 and takes the place of the separate record planned for publishing Prep Room units (direction B). **Phase 1 built 2026-11-10, not yet deployed:** one library, the Reading Room inside it, your shelf, class shelves of the school's texts, students' classes. **Phase 2 built the same day:** class shelves that teachers fill with Reading Room books and videos (a whole video, or a moment that opens at its second), through one "Save to" menu that also lists a teacher's own shelf and units. Podcasts wait for a player students can use. **Phase 3 built the same day:** a teacher uploads a file or saves a web page, and each is filed as a book, an article, a handout or a document (the teacher can change it), kept encrypted, and read in the Reading Room's typography at `/read/doc/<id>`. Sharing one with a class copies it to the class shelf. A teacher may suggest an uploaded book that everyone may read for the Reading Room, giving the reason, and the maker decides at `/ops`. The Book search no longer reaches Google Books: a book the Reading Room lacks is uploaded. **Phase 4 built the same day:** a Prep Room resource has "Save to…" for the teacher's own shelf ("From the Prep Room") and the class shelves they teach. A class shelf keeps the resource's newest saved version, refreshed inside the same transaction as every save, so the class always sees the newest and keeps the last one if the resource or its teacher's account goes. Students are sent the student copy alone (the same code draws the Prep Room's Student view and the class's copy); an answer key, or a resource with nothing for students, is placed for teachers and can't be moved to students. In the Prep Room, a shelved resource shows a line naming the shelf and the version its students see, the revise box and Edit say that saving reaches them, an older version names what students see, and every save confirms who sees it with Undo, which brings back the version before. Make a copy puts the newest version into the same unit as a new resource on no shelf; the copy offers to take the original's place on its shelves (with Undo), and the original stays in the unit. Deleting a resource leaves each class shelf its last version until a teacher takes it off, and the delete message says so.

**Decision.**
- **One library.** The Living Library becomes a place for everyone signed in, students and teachers. The Reading Room is the room inside it for text: books, articles and documents. Videos and podcasts are its other rooms. The rail becomes Chat, Scripture Memory, Living Library and, for teachers, the Prep Room. Every Reading Room address keeps working.
- **A shelf you fill yourself.** A person's shelf starts empty. Everything in the catalog can still be opened and read by anyone it is meant for. Shelving a book the catalog already holds only places it on the shelf. The "ready library" of books built in September is retired.
- **Three places a resource can live, each with its owner.**
  1. **My shelf** belongs to me. Only I see it, and it goes when my account goes.
  2. **A class shelf** belongs to a course, such as Bible II, not to a teacher. There is one shelf per course, shared by every teacher and section of it. Students who take the course see its student items. Every teacher of the course also sees a teachers-only section, for keys and teacher editions. A class shelf is a live view: what a teacher adds reaches the class's students at once, and a student who changes classes sees different shelves.
  3. **The catalog** is the collection open to everyone. A teacher may suggest a public-domain or openly licensed work they uploaded, and the maker approves what enters it.
- **Taking something off a class shelf never destroys it.** Any teacher of the course may remove any item, with Undo, and teachers see who added each item. When someone other than its creator removes it, the original returns to the creator's own shelf. When its creator no longer has an account, there is no shelf to return it to, and it is deleted once the Undo time passes.
- **Accounts and what outlives them.** Uploads are kept as long as the account that made them. When a teacher's account is deleted, their own shelf goes with it, and what they placed on a class shelf stays with the class with nothing that names them, so the next teacher of the course inherits it. A work the maker approved into the catalog stays in the catalog.
- **Classes.** Students choose the classes they take and teachers choose the classes they teach, and either can change them at any time, since schedules change by semester and year. Choosing a class brings that course's shelf to the top of the person's library. A school-managed class list may come later, and YCS has no one to carry that work today.
- **Students.** Students read, shelve from the catalog, and see their class shelves. They do not upload, they do not search for or shelve videos and podcasts, and they reach videos and podcasts only through what a teacher placed on a class shelf. Books the school holds for teachers alone (reference and teacher editions) never reach a student's shelf or a class shelf's student section. A way for a student to request a resource may come later.
- **Teachers.** Teachers upload books, documents and links, and each is filed as the kind of thing it is (a study guide is not a book). They search for and save videos and podcasts, place anything on their own shelf or a class shelf, and suggest works for the catalog.
- **The Prep Room sends its work to shelves.** A resource can go to the teacher's own shelf, and its student copy can go to a class shelf, never a key or a teacher-only part. Google Classroom is a further way out, with its own decision.
- **A class always sees the newest version.** A Prep Room resource on a class shelf shows its newest saved version, so every revision, hand edit or restore reaches the class's students as soon as it is saved. Because the Prep Room saves every change, it must make this plain before and after each one:
  - a resource on a class shelf says so where its versions are shown, naming the shelf, and says which version students see when an older one is open
  - the places that change a resource (the revision box and Edit) say that saving reaches the class's students
  - each save on such a resource confirms which class now sees it, with Undo, which brings back the version before
  - the Student view of the resource is exactly what the class sees.
- **Make a copy.** Any Prep Room resource can be copied, newest version first, into the same unit. A copy is on no class shelf, so a teacher can rework it out of the students' sight. When it's ready, the teacher puts the copy on the class shelf in place of the original, and the original stays in the unit.

**Why.** The Reading Room and the Living Library were two doors to one collection, and the ready library a third. A teacher's work had no way to reach students in the app, and the class, which is what students and teachers line their work up behind, had no place in it. A class shelf gives the class that place, and owning resources by course keeps a class's materials when its teacher leaves, the way a shared drive keeps a team's files.

**What it touches.**
- **0007.** The Living Library opens to students for reading and shelving. Uploads, transcripts, media search, saved moments, the Prep Room and every exam key stay teacher-only.
- **0002.** A student's shelf, chosen classes and reading place are the student's own records. They are never put in a prompt or given to a model as knowledge about the student.
- **0013.** The privacy page must say, before this ships, what is kept for students (their shelf and the classes they choose), that class shelves outlive the accounts that filled them, and that a teacher's name comes off what they leave behind. The data download includes what a person placed on class shelves.
- **0026.** A unit's upload stays the teacher's own copy. Placing it on a class shelf is the teacher's act, and the teacher answers for it, as the position statement says.

Phase 2: `shelves.place`, `take_off`, `set_audience` and `class_holding` (a teacher places only on the classes they teach; a reference copy is placed for teachers alone; taking off another teacher's item returns it to their shelf), the routes under `/api/shelf/class/` and `/api/shelf/items/`, the "Save to" menu in `static/js/passage.js`, and `account.collect`. Phase 3: `webapp/shelf_docs.py` (upload, link, rename and refile, delete, who may read one, the reading view, suggestions and the maker's decision), `shelf_texts` and `catalog_suggestions` in `scripts/schema.sql`, `templates/doc.html`, `shelves.place` and `_return_doc` (a class copy keeps its own text and its `origin`; one taken off by another teacher returns to its sharer unless their own copy remains), `prep._from_shelf` (a unit holds a shelf upload once, by its `shelf` ref), and the suggestions table in `webapp/ops.py`. Students get 403 on every upload route. Covered by `tests/test_shelf_docs.py`; the SQL by `scripts/check_prep_sql.py`. Phase 4: `webapp/shelf_resources.py` (the snapshot a class shelf holds, `refresh` called from `prep.add_version`, keys kept with teachers, which shelves hold a resource, the teacher's own shelf, and putting a copy in place of its original), `prep.copy_resource` and the `copy` and `replace` routes, the resource branch of `shelves.place`, `set_audience` and `take_off` (a resource stays in its teacher's Prep Room), the class copy's reading view in `shelf_docs._resource_page`, and in the Prep Room `drawShelf` and `savedToast` (`static/js/generate.js`). The shared renderer and student copy are `static/js/prepdoc.js` and the paper is `static/css/handout.css`, loaded by both pages. Covered by `tests/test_shelf_resources.py`; the SQL by `scripts/check_prep_sql.py`.

**Reverse when.** Letting students upload, or search for videos and podcasts, needs a new decision. Changing who owns a class shelf's items needs the maker, because teachers will rely on their classes keeping them.
