**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the order in 14:15: love first, obedience follows.
2. Students will explain who the "Helper" is and what "another Helper" means (14:16).
3. Students will describe the Spirit of truth from 14:17 (a Person with a job, not a vague force).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus doesn't leave his friends on their own; he sends the Helper.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): obedience grows as the fruit of being loved.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "another of the same kind" (allos parakletos) and the Spirit's co-equality; the optional stretch verse 14:16-17.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you" (John 14:18, looking ahead).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I try hardest to be good, and how's that going on my own strength?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest about the gap between what you want to do and what you do.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:15-17 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 15 through 17.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus ties love and obedience together, then promises the One who makes obedience possible.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 15, what does Jesus say love for him will look like?
   - Cold-call check: "If you love me, you will keep my commandments." Love shows up as obedience.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Notice the order. Does verse 15 say "keep my commandments so I'll love you," or "if you love me, you'll keep them"? Which comes first?
   - Guide them: love comes first. Obedience grows out of it. This ties back to last unit's new command (13:34): he loved them first, and their love is the response. You don't obey your way into being loved. You're loved, and obedience follows.
3. **Core.** Read verse 16. What does Jesus promise to ask the Father for? Look at the word "another."
   - Land it: "another Helper." Another of the same kind as Jesus himself. So the Spirit isn't a downgrade or a vague replacement. He's God with them, like Jesus was with them, and he stays "forever." Notice too: the command in verse 15 is followed immediately by the Helper in verse 16. The power to obey comes with the command.
4. **Core.** Read verse 17. What does Jesus call the Helper, and what's true of him?
   - Draw out: the Spirit of truth. The world can't receive him because it doesn't see or know him. But the disciples know him, because he dwells with them and will be in them. He's a Person with a job, not a mood or a force.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** People sometimes treat the Holy Spirit as a vague energy or a spooky feeling. From these verses, what's the Spirit actually like, and what's his relationship to Jesus and the Father?
   - Draw out: he's sent by the Father at Jesus' asking, he's "another" of the same kind as Jesus, he's the Spirit of truth, and he lives in believers. He's fully personal and fully God, and his job points people to Christ.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's the order in verse 15, and who does Jesus promise in verse 16?
   - Love first, obedience follows; and he promises another Helper, the Spirit, to be with them forever.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Fix the order first. This verse gets read backwards all the time, as if obeying enough earns God's love. Read it forward: you're loved, and love changes what you want, and obedience is what that changed heart does. The tree is made good, then it bears fruit.
- Then give them the power. Jesus doesn't say "try harder." He says "I'll ask the Father, and he'll give you a Helper." The command in verse 15 and the gift in verse 16 come in the same breath on purpose. You were never meant to do it alone.
- Name the Spirit plainly. He's not a spooky feeling. He's God himself, another Helper of the same kind as Jesus, living in his people forever, and everything he does points you back to Christ.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus asks the Father and gives the Spirit, so that loving him and obeying him run on his power, not yours.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "What would change if you stopped trying to earn God's love and started obeying because you already have it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 14:6. Optional stretch: start learning 14:16-17. Read John 14:18-24 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: "I will not leave you as orphans," and God making his home in those who love him.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The two things to get right: the grace-first order of 14:15 (love then obedience, tied to 13:34, Marshall's order), and the Spirit as a Person with a job (14:16-17), the point Unit 17 asked the course to hold. Guard against a spooky or vague Spirit.
- Guard against reading obedience as earning salvation. Watch your own closing line so grace at the top doesn't quietly become "so try harder" at the end.
- Offer 14:16-17 as an optional stretch verse for students who want more. It is not the graded anchor; 14:6 is.
- Derive the pairing of command-and-Helper from sermon 25; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
