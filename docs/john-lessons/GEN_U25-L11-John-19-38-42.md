**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will identify Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus and what they did (19:38-40).
2. Students will explain how two secret disciples came into the open.
3. Students will explain that a real body was buried in a real tomb (19:41-42).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): two men who hid their faith finally came out into the open.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): everyone at the tomb came to bury a dead man.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the burial as the seal on his death and the setup for the resurrection.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This closes the unit; leave room to gather it and point ahead.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "They laid Jesus there" (John 19:42).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I keep my faith hidden because it feels safer?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:38-42 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 38 through 42.
- One-sentence setup: after Jesus dies, two men who had followed him in secret come forward to bury him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 38 and 39, who are the two men who come to bury Jesus? What does John tell us about each of them?
   - Cold-call check: Joseph of Arimathea (a disciple, but secretly, for fear) and Nicodemus (who first came to Jesus at night, back in chapter 3).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Both of these men had kept their faith hidden. Joseph was a secret disciple; Nicodemus came to Jesus in the dark. Now, at the moment it looks completely hopeless, with Jesus dead, they step into the open and publicly claim his body. What changed them?
   - Guide them: the cross drew them out. When it looked like there was nothing left to gain, and every reason to stay hidden, they came forward publicly instead. Something about Jesus' death made hiding impossible. These men were in the very council that condemned Jesus, and they break with it to honor him. Nobody is beyond reach, and the cross pulls secret followers into the open.
3. **Core.** Read verse 39. Nicodemus brings about seventy-five pounds of burial spices. That's an enormous, expensive amount, a king's burial. What does that lavish gift say about how they saw Jesus?
   - Land it: they buried him like royalty. Seventy-five pounds of myrrh and aloes is what you'd use for a king. Even in death, even when it looked like defeat, they honored Jesus as someone worth everything. Their extravagant burial is an act of worship.
4. **Core.** Read verses 41 and 42. A real body is wrapped and laid in a real, new tomb. Why does John make sure we know Jesus was actually buried? What does everyone at that tomb believe about Jesus at this point?
   - Draw out: everyone who came to that tomb came to bury a dead man. They wrapped a corpse and sealed it in a grave. Nobody expected anything else. Which matters enormously for what happens next: when the tomb is empty in three days, it won't be because people were expecting a resurrection or faked one. They buried Jesus dead, for good, and gave up. The real burial guards the real resurrection.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** This chapter ends with Jesus in a tomb. It looks like the end. But John has been dropping hints all along that it isn't. Why is the burial actually a doorway, not a dead end?
   - Draw out: because the one in the tomb said "It is finished," not "I am finished." Because he laid down his life himself, and said he had power to take it up again (10:18). The burial is the seal on a real death, and a real death is exactly what has to come first for a real resurrection. The stone rolling over the tomb isn't the last word. It's the setup for Sunday.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did everyone who came to Jesus' tomb believe about him at that point?
   - That he was dead, and they were burying him for good.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ, close the unit, and point ahead.

- Honor the two who came out of hiding. Joseph and Nicodemus followed Jesus in secret for years, afraid of what it would cost. And when everything looked lost, when there was nothing left to gain, they finally stepped into the open and buried their King like royalty. The cross drew them out. It has a way of doing that. Some of you have kept Jesus a secret. Watch these two, and don't wait for the worst moment to come out of hiding.
- Sit in the silence of the tomb. And then the chapter ends. They lay him in a grave and roll the stone. Silence. To everyone there, it's over. The King is dead and buried. Let the room feel the weight of that for a moment. This is the darkest place in the whole book.
- Point ahead, gently. But hold on to two things he said. He didn't say "I am finished." He said "It is finished," the work done. And he said no one takes his life, that he'd lay it down and take it up again. The tomb is not the end of the story. It's the last dark moment before the sun comes up. Come back ready for Easter.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus was truly dead and buried, honored as a king even in the grave, and the sealed tomb is the doorway to the empty one.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Two secret followers came out of hiding at the cross. Where do you need to stop hiding that you follow Jesus?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.
