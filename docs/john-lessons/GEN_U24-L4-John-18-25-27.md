# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 24 Lesson 4 of 8: the rooster crows (John 18:25-27)

| Passage | John 18:25-27 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Peter denies Jesus two more times and the rooster crows, exactly as Jesus foretold, and yet this failure is not the end of Peter's story. |
| Christ connection | Jesus whose word about Peter comes true, and whose grace waits for him at another fire. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Peter's second and third denials (18:25-27).
2. Students will connect the rooster to Jesus' foretelling (13:38).
3. Students will explain that Peter's failure is not the end of his story (chapter 21).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): even Jesus' closest friend failed him, and Jesus still loved him.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): failure isn't the end when Jesus is the one who restores you.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Peter's foretold fall (13:38) held with the charcoal-fire restoration (21:9-19).

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep your sustained voice light. This is the last class before spring break, so land it on grace.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Peter again denied it, and at once a rooster crowed" (John 18:27).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Have I ever gone quiet about Jesus because it was easier? What happened?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 18:25-27 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 25 through 27.
- One-sentence setup: back at the fire, Peter is asked two more times if he knows Jesus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: Peter's failure, and whether it's the end. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 25 through 27, how many more times does Peter deny Jesus, and what finally happens?
   - Cold-call check: two more times (three total), and then a rooster crows.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Look back at John 13:38, from a few weeks ago. What had Jesus told Peter would happen? Did it come true exactly?
   - Guide them: Jesus told Peter, "before the rooster crows, you'll deny me three times." And it happened exactly. Peter, who swore he'd lay down his life for Jesus, couldn't even admit he knew him to a servant girl and a few people at a fire. Jesus saw it coming and told him.
3. **Core (the key question).** Peter failed as badly as a friend can fail, and Jesus knew he would. So here's the question: is this the end of Peter? Is he done? (Look ahead: John 21.)
   - Land it: no. In John 21, after the resurrection, Jesus meets Peter at another charcoal fire and gently gives him three chances to say "I love you," one for each denial, and restores him completely. Peter's worst night was not his last word. Jesus already knew he'd fail, and already planned to take him back.
4. **Core.** Why does it matter that Jesus knew Peter would deny him AND still washed his feet, still prayed for him, still went to the cross for him?
   - Draw out: it means Jesus doesn't love us because we're strong or loyal enough. He loved Peter knowing Peter would fold. He loves failing people on purpose. Your failure doesn't catch him off guard or change his heart toward you.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: was Peter's denial the end of his story?
   - No. Jesus restored him at another fire in John 21.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room, and send them into break on grace.

- Tell the truth about Peter. Peter blew it. Completely. The boldest disciple, the one who said "I'll die for you," couldn't admit he knew Jesus to a servant girl. If failure disqualified you from Jesus, Peter would be finished right here.
- Then tell the truth about Jesus. Jesus saw the whole thing coming and loved Peter anyway. He washed those denying feet. He prayed for Peter by name. And after the resurrection, he found Peter at a fire and took him all the way back, one "do you love me" for each "I don't know him." Peter's worst moment became the place Jesus met him with grace.
- Send them out. Some of you are carrying a failure you think puts you out of reach. Hear this before break: the man who denied Jesus three times went on to lead the church and die for the name he once denied, because Jesus restores failures. Your worst night is not your last word.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus knew Peter would fail, loved him through it, and waited at another fire to take him back.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus restored the friend who denied him three times. What failure do you need to bring to a Savior who takes failures back?"
- Give it quiet. A short blessing over the break closes the class well.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Enjoy spring break. When we come back, Jesus stands before Pilate. Read John 18:28-32 before we return.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5 (after break): the trial moves to Pilate, the Roman governor.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus, and the last class before spring break. Land it on grace, not shame. This is the clean pre-break stopping point.
- The whole lesson turns on the restoration: Peter's foretold fall (13:38) held with the charcoal-fire restoration (21:9-19). Point ahead so students leave for break on grace.
- GUARD hard against using Peter to shame. Peter is the mirror for every student who's gone quiet about Jesus. Fear drove it. Grace answers it.
- Note the "charcoal fire" detail: the same word appears here (18:18) and at Peter's restoration (21:9). John connects the fire of denial to the fire of restoration on purpose. A gem for the disciple tier.
- Derive the frame from sermon 33; cite it, don't read it aloud.
