**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the disciples' claim to believe and Jesus' response (16:29-31).
2. Students will explain what Jesus predicts: they'll scatter and leave him alone (16:32).
3. Students will explain "yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me" (16:32).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus knew his friends would run, and loved them anyway.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): our standing with Jesus rests on his faithfulness, not our confidence.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Christ's aloneness at the cross held with the Father's presence.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me" (John 16:32).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When have I been sure I could handle something, and then folded?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: honesty about failing is welcome here.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 16:29-32 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verses 29 through 32.
- One-sentence setup: the disciples finally feel like they get it, and Jesus tells them what's really about to happen.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 29 and 30, what do the disciples say they now understand and believe?
   - Cold-call check: they say Jesus is speaking plainly now, that he knows all things, and that's why they believe he came from God.
2. **Entry.** In verse 31, how does Jesus respond to their confident "we believe"?
   - Draw out: "Do you now believe?" A gentle question, not a celebration. He knows something they don't.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 32. What does Jesus say they're about to do? Does he shame them for it?
   - Guide them: he says the hour is coming, is here, when they'll scatter, each to his own home, and leave him alone. That night, when the soldiers come, they'll run. But he doesn't rub their faces in it. He tells them ahead of time, the same way he warned them about persecution, so it won't destroy them when it happens.
4. **Core.** The disciples grounded their confidence on themselves: "now WE believe." Jesus knew they'd fold that very night. So what did he ground their future on instead, if not their steadiness?
   - Land it: on himself. Their faith would fail that night, and Jesus would still go to the cross for them and rise and come find them. Their standing with him never rested on how strong their faith felt in a good moment. It rested on his faithfulness to them. That's why failing that night didn't finish them.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read the last line of verse 32: "yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me." Jesus says he'll be left utterly alone by his friends, and in the same breath says he's not alone. Hold both. What's true?
   - Draw out: both are true. His friends really do abandon him, and that's real loneliness. And the Father is really with him, so at the deepest level he isn't abandoned. Jesus keeps trusting the Father even while everyone runs. (Handle the cross reverently: on the cross he bears something even deeper, but here he steadies the eleven by pointing to the Father's presence.)
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did Jesus ground the disciples' future on, since not on their own confidence?
   - On his own faithfulness to them.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name the mirror. The disciples were never more confident than right before they failed. "Now we believe!" And Jesus, who loved them, knew they'd scatter by midnight. If you've ever been sure of yourself and then folded, you're in this room with them.
- Show them where the ground actually is. Jesus didn't tie their future to how strong their faith felt. He tied it to himself. That's why their failure that night wasn't the end. He held on to them when they let go of him. He does the same for you.
- Hold Jesus' own steadiness. Everyone ran, and Jesus said, "yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me." He kept trusting his Father while every friend abandoned him. That's the kind of Savior walking to the cross for you.
- Preview the memory verse (look-ahead). Next class we learn this unit's memory verse, and it's the last thing Jesus says in this whole talk. Put it on the board and read it once: "I have said these things to you, that in me you may have peace. In the world you will have tribulation. But take heart; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33). Come back Thursday ready to sit with it.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus held on to his friends when they let go of him, and stayed steady in the Father's presence even when he was left alone.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Your standing with Jesus rests on his faithfulness, not on how strong your faith feels today. What would change if you really believed that?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start looking at John 16:33 (the memory verse). Read it once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L7 (short Thursday): the memory verse, "I have overcome the world."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: our standing with Christ rests on his faithfulness (16:31-32, not our confidence), and Christ alone-yet-not-alone (16:32). Adam's sermon 28 refuses to soften "you will leave me alone"; keep it honest.
- Handle the cross reverently. Here Jesus asserts the Father's presence to steady the eleven; the deeper darkness of the cross (Psalm 22) is Good Friday's mystery and belongs later, in the Passion units.
- IMPORTANT: preview 16:33 in Move 4 as a look-ahead, so L7's plant is a second exposure (not a cold meeting). This is how the short Thursday plant gets its room.
- Derive the frame from sermon 28; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
