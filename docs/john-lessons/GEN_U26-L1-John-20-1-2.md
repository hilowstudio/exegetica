**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe what Mary found at the tomb (20:1).
2. Students will explain Mary's assumption that the body was taken (20:2).
3. Students will connect the empty tomb to the real burial of Unit 25.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the tomb was empty on the third day.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the empty tomb was a shock, not something anyone expected.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Mary's grave-robbery reading as the honest first response.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. The tone lifts to Easter now; carry the wonder. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Note the shift: last unit ended in the dark at a grave; today the sun comes up.
- Centering line on the board: "the stone had been taken away from the tomb" (John 20:1).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I go, or what do I do, when I'm grieving something?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 20:1-2 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verses 1 and 2.
- One-sentence setup: last we saw, Jesus was sealed in a tomb; now it's Sunday morning, and Mary comes back.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 1, when does Mary come to the tomb, and what does she find?
   - Cold-call check: early Sunday, while it's still dark, and she finds the stone rolled away from the tomb.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Remember how last unit ended (Unit 25): everyone who came to that tomb came to bury a dead man, and the stone was rolled over it. So when Mary sees the stone GONE, what's her very first thought (verse 2)?
   - Guide them: she assumes grave robbery. "They have taken the Lord, and we don't know where they've laid him." She doesn't think "resurrection." Nobody did. An open, empty grave, in her mind, meant someone stole the body. That's the honest first reaction of a grieving woman at a rifled tomb.
3. **Core.** Why does it matter that NOBODY expected a resurrection? What does that tell you about the disciples and the women?
   - Land it: it means they weren't primed to see what they wanted to see. They came to grieve a corpse. When the tomb turns up empty, they're baffled and heartbroken, not celebrating. Which makes them terrible candidates for making up a resurrection story. They believed it only because it actually happened, and it took them by complete surprise.
4. **Core.** Mary comes "while it was still dark." John loves details like that. She's walking through grief in the dark, sure her Lord is gone for good. What's about to happen to that darkness?
   - Draw out: the sun is coming up, and so is the Son. Mary's in the deepest dark of her grief, and she has no idea she's minutes from the greatest joy of her life. The darkness is about to break. She just can't see it yet.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did Mary find, and what did she assume?
   - The stone rolled away and the tomb empty; she assumed someone had taken the body.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Carry the Easter lift.

- Set the scene. It's still dark. Mary is walking to a grave, carrying the heaviest grief of her life, to be near the body of the man she loved. And she finds the stone gone and the tomb empty, and her heart sinks even lower, because she thinks they've stolen him. Even the empty tomb, at first, felt like one more loss.
- Name the surprise coming. Nobody in this story is expecting a resurrection. They came to bury Jesus and stay buried in their grief. Which is exactly why you can trust it: they didn't invent a happy ending, they stumbled into one they never saw coming.
- Point to the light. Mary is in the dark, minutes from the greatest joy in history, and she doesn't know it yet. Hold onto that when you're in the dark. The story isn't over where you think it is. The sun is coming up.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the tomb that held a dead man is empty, and the Lord it could not hold is about to meet a grieving woman in the dark.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Mary was in the dark, minutes from the greatest joy of her life. Where do you need to trust that your story isn't over where it feels like it ended?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Mark the tonal shift: last unit closed in the dark at the grave, this unit opens onto Easter. Carry the wonder without rushing to the resurrection reveal (that builds across the unit).
- Two things to land: Mary's honest grave-robbery reading (nobody expected a resurrection, which makes the witnesses credible), and the seam with Unit 25 (everyone came to bury a dead man).
- Keep the resurrection bodily and historical from the start; John records concrete details, not a legend.
- Derive the frame from sermons 37 and 38; cite them, don't read aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
