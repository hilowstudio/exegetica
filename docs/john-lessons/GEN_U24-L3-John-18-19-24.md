**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain Jesus' answer about his teaching (18:20).
2. Students will explain the injustice of a secret night trial.
3. Students will explain Jesus' calm reply to being struck (18:23).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus had nothing to hide; he taught in the open.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the secret, rigged trial exposes that they had no real case.
- **Stretch** (disciple): when truth has no answer, the wicked reach for violence.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I have spoken openly to the world... I have said nothing in secret" (John 18:20).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "How do I usually react when someone tells me a true thing I don't want to hear?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 18:19-24 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 19 through 24.
- One-sentence setup: the high priest questions Jesus in the middle of the night, and things turn violent.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 20, when the high priest questions his teaching, what does Jesus say about how and where he taught?
   - Cold-call check: he taught openly, in the synagogues and the temple, in public, and said nothing in secret.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus basically says in verses 20 and 21, "I've got nothing to hide. Go ask the people who heard me." What does it tell you that they need a secret trial, in the middle of the night, to condemn a man who taught in broad daylight?
   - Guide them: it tells you they don't actually have a case. If Jesus were really a criminal, they could bring witnesses and try him in the open. Instead they grab him at night, question him in secret, and skip the witnesses. The whole setup shows they've already decided the verdict and just need a way to make it stick.
3. **Core.** Read verse 22. Jesus points out the trial has no real evidence, and how does an officer respond? What does that reaction reveal?
   - Land it: the officer slaps him. When Jesus said something true and they had no answer for it, the response was a fist. That's what happens when someone has no argument left: they reach for force. The slap is a confession that they've got nothing.
4. **Core.** Read verse 23. Jesus has just been struck for no reason. How does he respond?
   - Draw out: calmly and reasonably. "If what I said is wrong, show me the wrong. But if it's right, why hit me?" No panic, no shouting back, no swinging. He answers violence with the calm of a man who's telling the truth and knows it.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** There's a pattern here worth naming. When people are confronted with a true word they can't refute, and they don't want to change, what do they tend to do instead of dealing with the truth?
   - Draw out: they attack the person. They get defensive, they lash out, they change the subject, they try to shut the speaker up. It's easier to hit the messenger than to admit the message is right. And it's a reflex that lives in all of us, not just in that courtyard.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why does the officer strike Jesus, and what does it show?
   - Because Jesus told the truth and they had no answer. It shows they had no real case.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Expose the sham. Watch how they have to do this. In the dark. In secret. In the middle of the night. Skipping the witnesses. If Jesus were guilty, they could try him in the daylight with real evidence. The whole rigged setup screams that they've got nothing on him, and they know it.
- Land the slap. And when Jesus calmly points that out, an officer hits him. That's the tell. When people can't answer the truth and won't change, they reach for force. The fist is what you throw when you're out of arguments.
- Turn it on us, gently. And before we feel too superior to that courtyard, be honest: when someone brings you a true word you don't want to hear, a parent, a friend, your own conscience, what do you do? Get defensive. Snap back. Shut them down. That same reflex lives in every one of us. The gospel calls us to hear the true word instead of hitting it.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus told the truth in the open and took the blow without flinching, the innocent one calm before an unjust court.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "When a true word confronts you, do you hear it or attack it? Where do you need to just listen instead of getting defensive?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 18:25-27 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4 (short Thursday): Peter's denials finish, and the rooster crows.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The heart of the lesson: the secret night trial exposes that they had no case (18:20-21), and the officer's slap (18:22) shows what people do when truth has no answer. Sermon 33's turn: in the face of truth they can't refute, the wicked reach for violence, and the same reflex lives in us.
- Jesus' calm (18:23) is the innocent one's poise, not weakness. Keep him in command even while struck.
- Anti-Judaism guard: the injustice here is a specific court's, not a people's.
- Derive the frame from sermon 33; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
