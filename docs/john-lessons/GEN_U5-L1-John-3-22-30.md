# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 5 Lesson 1 of 2: the friend of the bridegroom (John 3:22-30)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U5 He must increase |
| Lesson | 1 of 2 |
| Date / Day type | Mon Oct 5 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 3:22-30 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus is the bridegroom, and John the Baptist shows the joy of a person who gets smaller so Jesus can get bigger. |
| Christ connection | Christ is the bridegroom who has come for his bride, and the fullest joy a person can have is a full heart making room for him. |
| Memory verse | Review only. No new verse this unit. Keep John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, and 3:16 sharp. Offer 3:30 as an optional stretch, off the graded line. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega, "They Shall Inherit the Earth" (John 3:22-30). External map: the Jordan River valley showing Aenon near Salim. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what John's disciples were upset about in verse 26 and how John answered in verses 27 through 30.
2. Students will explain what "friend of the bridegroom" meant and why John's joy was complete as his crowd left.
3. Students will restate John 3:30 in their own words and say what it means for Jesus to increase.
4. Students will connect the bridegroom picture back to Cana (John 2) and out to Christ and the church.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): everyone knows the sting of watching attention move to someone else. Start there, no assumed knowledge.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): John the Baptist's whole assignment was to point away from himself to Jesus. This scene is that assignment finished well.
- **Stretch** (disciple): verse 27 reads the whole thing grace-first, the wedding thread runs across the Bible, and the word "must" in verse 30 carries the will of God.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block of teacher talk past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away and face down.
- Centering line on the board: "He must increase, but I must decrease" (John 3:30).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I most want to be noticed?"
- Honest on-ramp for the doubter: if you're not sure God's there, write the question straight to him and keep going.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 3:22-30 aloud, slowly. One reads 22 through 26, the other 27 through 30.
- Navigation help before you start: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 3, verses 22 through 30. Walk the newcomer to the page. Nobody hunts alone.
- Set the scene in one sentence: Jesus and John the Baptist are both baptizing, the crowds are shifting to Jesus, and John's followers don't like it.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Don't lecture them. Let students find it.

3. **Entry (any student can answer).** Look at verse 26. What are John's disciples worried about? Put your finger on the exact words.
   - Cold-call check: they say Jesus "is baptizing, and all are going to him." The crowd is leaving John for Jesus.
4. **Entry.** How would you feel if you built something for years and then watched everyone walk over to someone else? Say it honestly.
5. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 27 through 29. John gives himself a title. What is it, and what's that person's actual job at a wedding?
   - Guide them to "friend of the bridegroom" (verse 29), the best man. His job is to bring the bride and groom together, then step back. Have groups write his job in one line.
6. **Core.** John says in verse 29 that his joy is "now complete." His joy fills up at the exact moment his crowd shrinks. How is that possible? What does John know about himself that keeps him steady?
   - Land it: John knows precisely who he is and what he was sent to do (verse 28, "I am not the Christ, but I have been sent before him"). His identity is settled, so a shrinking crowd can't shake him. This is confidence, and it makes him glad.
7. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 27 again: "A person cannot receive even one thing unless it is given him from heaven." How does that line take all the pressure off John about the crowd size?
   - Draw out: if every good thing, including a crowd, is a gift from God, then John has nothing to defend and nothing to grab. The people crossing to Jesus are God's gift to Jesus. Connect back to 3:8 (the wind and the Spirit) and 1:13 (born of God).
8. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In your own words, verse 30. What has to happen to Jesus? What has to happen to John?

Circulate while groups work. Ask short follow-ups. "Where do you see that in the verse?" Keep your turns under a minute.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Derive from sermon 04; don't read it.

- John calls Jesus the bridegroom, the one who has the bride. Point back to Cana in John 2, where Jesus supplied the wine at a wedding, doing the groom's own job. What Cana pictured, John now says out loud. Jesus is the true bridegroom, and the people who belong to him are his bride. The Bible carries this wedding picture from start to finish (Hosea 2:19; Isaiah 54:5; Ephesians 5:25-27; Revelation 19:7-9).
- Name the meekness in John clearly, straight from the sermon. His lowness comes from a secure standing before God. He can step down because his place with God is settled and can't be lost. Say this plainly to the student who already feels small: John's joy came from confidence, and the confidence came from God.
- Press the word "must" in verse 30. John says Jesus must increase, and "must" carries the settled will of God, older than the world.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the bridegroom who came for his bride, and the fullest joy you'll ever feel is a full heart glad to make room for him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "John's joy grew as he stepped back for Jesus. What's one place in your life where making room for Jesus would actually make you more glad, not less?"
- Give it quiet. Let the Word press. You're grading that they wrote and engaged, not what they decided.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 3:31-36 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation (Quiet-Time Journal and the Move 5 response). Content mastery lives in the memory verses, quizzes, and finals, not in this lesson.
- Sets up L2: John the Baptist stops talking at verse 30. Next class the Gospel writer picks up in his own voice with the One from above.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: this is a governed Work block. Convert content into questions and let students discover it. Your sustained voice stays under 40% of the period.
- Paired-sermon note: sermon 04 does the meekness frame from the Beatitudes. Meekness is humility born of a secure identity, the confidence to lower yourself for someone else's good. That's the read on John's decrease. Use it to protect the anxious student.
- Watch three pitfalls: (1) don't let "decrease" become self-hatred, teach it as confidence; (2) don't flatten this to "don't be jealous," the cure John gives is a settled identity as friend of the bridegroom; (3) don't turn the wedding picture into loose allegory, anchor it in Cana and the named Old Testament texts.
- Verse 30 is famous and short. Teach it, offer it as an optional stretch, keep it off the quizzed memory line.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, so the Work block gets its full 25 minutes. If you run long in Move 3, trim the Deeper question first, not the cold-call checks.
