**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what changed when Jesus called them friends instead of servants (15:15).
2. Students will explain "you did not choose me, but I chose you" (15:16).
3. Students will explain the purpose Jesus gives: go and bear fruit that abides (15:16).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus calls his followers his friends.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): being his friend still means doing what he says; friendship doesn't cancel his Lordship.
- **Stretch** (disciple): sovereign election as comfort and mission, guarded from both fatalism and boasting.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "You did not choose me, but I chose you" (John 15:16).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Have I ever felt picked last, or left out? What's that like?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it straight.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 15:14-17 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 14 through 17.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus tells his disciples what they are to him, who did the choosing, and what he's sending them to do.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 15, what does Jesus stop calling them, and what does he call them instead?
   - Cold-call check: he stops calling them servants and calls them friends.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Look carefully at verses 14 and 15. Does becoming Jesus' friend mean he's no longer the boss? What does verse 14 say a friend of Jesus does?
   - Guide them: a friend of Jesus still does what he commands (verse 14). He's still Lord. What changed is closeness: a servant just gets orders, but a friend gets let in on the plan. Jesus made known to them everything he heard from the Father. Friendship with Jesus means you obey him and he trusts you with his heart.
3. **Core.** Read verse 16: "you did not choose me, but I chose you." Who started this relationship? Why would Jesus make a point of saying it that way?
   - Land it: he did. They didn't work their way up to Jesus. He came and picked them. He wants them to know their place with him rests on his choice, not their performance, which means it's secure. You didn't earn your way in, so you can't flunk your way out.
4. **Core.** Keep reading verse 16. Being chosen isn't a couch to sit on. What did Jesus choose and appoint them to do?
   - Draw out: "go and bear fruit and that your fruit should abide." Chosen means sent. He picked them for a job: to go, and to bear fruit that lasts. Election comes with a mission.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** "I chose you" can get twisted two ways: into "so I can coast, it's all decided" or into "so I must be better than other people." Read verse 16 and the "go bear fruit" part. How does the verse itself shut down both of those?
   - Draw out: it shuts down coasting because the purpose is to go and bear fruit, an active mission, not a hammock. And it shuts down pride because he chose them, they didn't earn it, so there's nothing to brag about. Grace picked you, and grace sent you.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: who chose whom, and what were they chosen to do?
   - Jesus chose them; he appointed them to go and bear lasting fruit.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the friendship. The God of the universe looks at these ordinary men, the night before he dies, and calls them friends. Not staff. Not tools. Friends he lets in on everything. That's what he calls his people.
- Keep the Lordship. Being his friend doesn't make him less the boss. It makes obeying him personal. You do what he says because you know his heart now, and it's good.
- Land the choosing, especially for the kid who's felt picked last. You did not choose him. He chose you. Your standing with Jesus doesn't ride on your winning streak. He picked you, and what he picks, he keeps.
- Send them. And he didn't choose you to sit still. He chose you to go and grow fruit that lasts. You're picked, and you're sent.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus makes servants into friends, chose them before they chose him, and sends them out to bear fruit that lasts.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'You did not choose me, but I chose you.' If Jesus picked you and you didn't earn it, what does that change about how secure you feel with him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 15:5. Read John 15:18-21 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L7: Jesus gets honest about the world's hatred, and why his followers share it.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two guards to hold: friendship doesn't cancel Lordship (verse 14: a friend does what he commands; two verses later, 15:20, they're "servants" again), and election is comfort AND mission (guard against fatalism with "go bear fruit," against boasting with "you did not choose me").
- Keep the choosing pastoral, aimed at assurance, the way Adam does in sermon 27: it will be so, we have the confidence of assurance for those in Christ.
- Derive the frame from sermon 27; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
