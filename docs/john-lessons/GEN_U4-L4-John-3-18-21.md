# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 4 Lesson 4 of 4: light, darkness, and the verdict (John 3:18-21)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U4 The new birth |
| Lesson | 4 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Oct 1 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus |
| Passage | John 3:18-21 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The verdict is already in, and it is honest: the light has come, and people show what they love by whether they step into it. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the light who came to save, and coming to him is coming to be seen and healed, not hunted. |
| Memory verse | Students recite or write John 3:16 from memory this lesson (the two-week target lands here). Keep 20:31, 1:14, 1:12 in review. |
| Resources | Fresh-build (no Adam sermon covers John 3:1-21). Have a class Bible open to John 3. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the present verdict of 3:18 (believing is not condemned; not believing is condemned already).
2. Students will explain how the judgment of 3:19 is self-selected: the light came, and people loved the darkness.
3. Students will explain "coming to the light" (3:20-21) as letting the real self be seen, and that even the coming is God's work (3:21).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): coming to the light means being fully seen, and the light came to save you, so being seen here is safe.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "condemned already" is a present address, not a scare tactic, and the way out is open in the same breath.
- **Stretch** (disciple): self-selected judgment holds God's kindness and sin's seriousness together; 3:21's "carried out in God" makes even the coming a work of grace, and Nicodemus's later arc (7:50-51; 19:39) is the slow new birth edging out of the dark.

---

**Talk budget:** short Thursday, single-focus. Keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 36 minutes and land the unit's altar as invitation, never as scored belief.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Whoever does what is true comes to the light" (John 3:21).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Coming to the light means being fully seen. What in you would you rather keep in the dark? You do not have to share it."

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 3:18-21 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 3, verses 18 through 21.
- One-sentence setup: the light has come into the world, and these verses say what happens when it does.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Verse 18 gives a verdict. Read it. Is it about the future or about right now?
   - Draw out: "Whoever believes in him is not condemned," and "whoever does not believe is condemned already." Present tense. It is a standing right now, decided by what a person does with the light that has already come. And a present standing you can walk out of by coming to the light.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Verse 19 defines "the judgment." Is it God hunting people down, or something else?
   - Guide them: "the light has come into the world, and people loved the darkness." The judgment is self-selected. The light came, and it was refused. God is kind (verse 17, he came to save), and refusing the light is still a real, culpable choice. Both stay true.
3. **Core.** Verses 20-21: why does one person stay away from the light, and what does "whoever does what is true" do?
   - Land it: the one doing evil stays away so his works are not exposed. But "whoever does what is true comes to the light." Coming to the light means letting the real self be seen, out in the open.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** The very end of verse 21 says something surprising about the person who comes to the light. What does it say, and how does it tie back to L1?
   - Draw out: his works "have been carried out in God." Even his coming to the light was God working in him. The passage ends where the unit began: God does the saving. The new birth of L1 is what makes anyone step into the light at all.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Is verse 18 about now or later? What is "the judgment" of verse 19?
   - Now, a present standing; the light came and people loved the darkness.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Handle this as invitation, never as scored belief.

- Say what the light is for. The light did not come to expose you and leave you standing there ashamed. It came to save (verse 17), and coming to it is coming to the One who already gave his Son for whatever the light would show.
- Say the safety. You can be fully seen and fully safe in the same moment, because the light you are stepping into is the crucified and risen Christ. And even your stepping toward him is his work in you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the light who came to save, so coming to him is coming to be seen and healed.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- Students recite or write John 3:16 from memory (the two-week target lands today).
- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The light came to save, not to hunt. What is one honest thing you would bring into the light if you knew you were fully safe there?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 3:22-30 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation, the Respond entry, and the John 3:16 memory check.
- Sets up Unit 5: John the Baptist says of Jesus, "He must increase, but I must decrease."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: single-focus Thursday. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Grade the present tense. "Condemned already" (3:18) is a present standing the coming-to-the-light resolves, and it is a present address, not a future threat. Do not use it to frighten the room; fear diagnoses, it does not give new birth, and only the Son lifted up draws (3:14). Press honest coming-to-the-light.
- Keep God kind and sin serious together: the verdict is self-selected (the light came and was refused, 3:19), which holds both.
- Do not moralize 3:20-21 into "stop sinning so you can come to the light." The coming to the light is grace (3:21, "carried out in God"), the fruit of the new birth, not the entry fee for grace.
- Bookend Nicodemus: he came at night in L1, and by 19:39 he helps bury Jesus in the open with seventy-five pounds of spices, one picture of the new birth as a slow walk out of the dark. Let the trajectory preach; do not overclaim his conversion or add a "be brave like Nicodemus" moral.
- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus.
