**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the foot-washing (13:4-5) and Peter's resistance (13:6-8).
2. Students will explain "if I do not wash you, you have no share with me" (13:8).
3. Students will explain the difference between the bath and the daily washing (13:10).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus does the dirtiest job in the house for his friends.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you have to let Jesus clean you, and you can't earn it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): bathed once (justification) and feet washed daily (ongoing cleansing, 1 John 1:9).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the doctrinally densest lesson in the unit, so give the Work block its full time and teach the two washings simply.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "if I do not wash you, you have no share with me" (John 13:8).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I try to clean myself up before coming to God?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest thing you feel you have to fix first.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 13:4-11 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 13, verses 4 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus gets up, wraps a towel around his waist, and starts washing feet.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 4 and 5, what does Jesus take off, put on, and do? Whose job was foot-washing in that world?
   - Cold-call check: he takes off his outer clothes, ties on a towel, and washes feet. That was the lowest servant's job in the house.
2. **Entry.** In verse 6, Peter is shocked: "Lord, do you wash my feet?" In verse 8, what does Peter flat-out refuse?
   - "You shall never wash my feet." He can't handle the Lord serving him.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Peter's "never" sounds humble. Read Jesus' answer in verse 8: "if I do not wash you, you have no share with me." What just changed? What are the real stakes?
   - Guide them: it jumped from manners to salvation. To refuse to be washed by Jesus is to have no part in him at all. Peter's "never" was actually pride, wanting to earn his place instead of receiving it. The hardest thing for a proud heart is to stand still and let Jesus clean you.
4. **Core.** Peter flips to the other extreme (verse 9): wash all of me. Read Jesus' answer in verse 10 carefully. Here's the picture: when you first trust Jesus, he cleans you all over, once, like a full bath. That's being saved. But you still walk through a dirty world and your feet get dusty, so you come back to him to wash the daily dirt off. That's not getting saved again. It's being cleaned again. How does that help you understand confessing your sins?
   - Land it: you get cleaned once, for good, when you come to Christ (the bath). Then, day by day, you bring your sins to him and he keeps washing (the feet). That daily washing is 1 John 1:9: "if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us." You never lose the bath. You just keep coming for your feet.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 11 says "not every one of you" is clean. Jesus means Judas. Judas got his feet washed like everyone else. What does that tell you about the difference between outward religion and a truly clean heart?
   - Draw out: Judas got the water on the outside and was not clean on the inside. Being near Jesus, sitting at the table, going through the motions, is not the same as being made clean by him. Cleanness is a matter of the heart receiving him, not just the outward signs.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what's the difference between the "bath" and the daily "washing" in verse 10?
   - The bath is being made clean once when you trust Jesus. The daily washing is bringing your everyday sins to him to be forgiven.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Start with the towel. The Lord of all things is on his knees washing feet. That's how far his love stoops. And the washing isn't only about dirty feet. It points to the cross, where he makes his people clean for good.
- Hit Peter's pride, gently, because it's ours. "You shall never wash my feet" sounds humble, and it's pride. We want to clean ourselves up before we come to God, to bring something, to earn it. Jesus says the only way in is to stop refusing and let him wash you. You can't do it yourself.
- Give them the two washings as comfort. When you trust Jesus, you're clean all over, once, for good. You don't have to get re-saved every time you sin. And when you track in the dirt of the day, you come back and he washes your feet again. That's what confessing sin is: not groveling for your salvation back, but coming to the One who already made you clean and letting him wash the day off.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus stoops to wash you clean, and the only thing he asks is that you stop trying to clean yourself and let him do it.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Peter tried to refuse being served. Where do you try to clean yourself up before coming to Jesus, and what would it mean to just let him wash you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 13:12-17 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: Jesus explains why he washed their feet and tells them to do the same.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The bath-versus-feet passage (13:10) is the flagship of this unit. Teach it simply: bathed once (saved, made clean for good), feet washed daily (bringing everyday sin to Christ, 1 John 1:9), both done by Jesus and received by faith. Don't bury the room in Greek. One clear picture.
- Guard two errors: this is not baptism making you saved (Jesus grounds cleanness in his word at 15:3, not a rite), and it is not a washing you perform on yourself. The whole action is Christ's, received by faith.
- Peter's "never" (13:8) is pride wearing humility's mask. The gospel's hardest demand on a proud heart is to be served and made clean by Another.
- Handle Judas (13:11) soberly, not with terror: outward nearness is not a clean heart.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
