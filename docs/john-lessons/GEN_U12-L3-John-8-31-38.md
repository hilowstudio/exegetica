# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 12 Lesson 3 of 5: the truth will set you free (John 8:31-38)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U12 Light of the world |
| Lesson | 3 of 5 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Nov 18 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 8:31-38 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus says staying in his word frees people from slavery to sin, and only the Son can make a slave into a son. |
| Christ connection | Jesus the Son sets slaves free indeed, bringing them into the family forever. |
| Memory verse | John 8:12 was planted at L1. Review it, do not re-plant. Keep the six earlier anchors sharp too. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega, "The Light of the World" (John 8:12-59). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what "the truth will set you free" means, reading 8:31-36 as one unit.
2. Students will explain "everyone who practices sin is a slave to sin" (8:34).
3. Students will explain how the Son frees a slave into a son (8:35-36).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): everyone is owned by something, and Jesus offers real freedom.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you can look free and still be a slave to sin.
- **Stretch** (disciple): freedom as freedom from sin's slavery, given by the Son, not a self-help slogan.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "If the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed" (John 8:36).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What owns more of me than I'd like to admit?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest answer, whatever it is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 8:31-38 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 8, verses 31 through 38.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus offers freedom to the people who believed him, and they're insulted, because they think they're already free.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 31 and 32, what does Jesus say leads to knowing the truth and being set free?
   - Cold-call check: "if you abide in my word." Staying in what Jesus says leads to the truth and to freedom.
2. **Entry.** In verse 33 the people say they've "never been enslaved to anyone." What do they think freedom means?
   - Nobody bosses them around. They think free means independent, doing what they want.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 34: "everyone who practices sin is a slave to sin." Jesus says a person can feel free and still be owned. What's the difference between the freedom the people are bragging about and the slavery Jesus is describing?
   - Guide them: they mean "nobody controls me." Jesus means "your sin controls you." You can do whatever you want and still be dragged around by a habit, a craving, a pattern you can't quit. That's real slavery, and you can't see it while you're calling it freedom.
4. **Core.** Read verses 35 and 36. A slave and a son have different futures in a household. Who can actually free a slave and make them family?
   - Land it: "if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed." Only Jesus can break sin's grip and bring you into God's family as a son or daughter, forever. You can't free yourself. The Son does it.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** People love to quote "the truth will set you free" (verse 32) all by itself, like it's about getting good information. Read verses 31 through 36 together. What does Jesus actually mean by it, and why is quoting verse 32 alone a mistake?
   - Draw out: Jesus defines it himself in the next verses. The freedom is freedom from slavery to sin, given by the Son. It's not a slogan about facts or self-discovery. Pulled out of context, "the truth will set you free" becomes the opposite of what Jesus meant.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: who can set a slave to sin truly free, and how sure is that freedom?
   - The Son, and it's free "indeed," all the way, forever.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 13.

- Name the slavery honestly. Everyone in the room is owned by something: a screen, a substance, approval, anger, a habit you hate and keep going back to. You can do whatever you want and still be a slave to it. That's the human condition, and calling it freedom doesn't change it.
- Then the good news. You can't free yourself. If you could, you'd have done it already. Jesus says the Son can, and when the Son frees you, you're free "indeed," all the way down.
- Name the sonship. Jesus doesn't just unlock the cell. He brings you into the family. A freed slave becomes a son or daughter who belongs forever. That's a bigger freedom than "nobody tells me what to do." It's belonging to God.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the Son sets slaves free and makes them family, and that freedom holds forever.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says everyone's a slave to something and only the Son can free you. What owns you, and what would it be like to be free of it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 8:39-47 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4: the argument turns to the hardest question yet, whose child you really are.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 13. Freedom here is freedom from slavery to sin, given by the Son (8:36), grounded in union with Christ before any effort of ours. Marshall's order: the tree is made good, then it bears fruit.
- Teach 8:36 as the christological punchline, and always inside its 8:34-36 context. Do NOT hand students "the truth will set you free" (8:32) on its own. Pulled from context, 8:32 becomes the exact misreading (a slogan about information or self-discovery) that this lesson corrects. There is no optional stretch verse this unit; the graded plant is 8:12.
- Guard the freedom against "do whatever you want," which is exactly the error the crowd makes in 8:33.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
