# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 9 Lesson 5 of 7: no one comes unless the Father draws him (John 6:41-51)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U9 Bread of Life |
| Lesson | 5 of 7 |
| Date / Day type | Mon Nov 2 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 6:41-51 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The crowd grumbles that they know Jesus' family, and he tells them their coming to him is the Father's gift, then points to the cross where he gives his flesh for the world. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the living bread the Father draws people to, given for the life of the world. |
| Memory verse | John 6:35 was planted last lesson. Review it, do not re-plant. Keep the five earlier anchors sharp too. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega, "The Bread of Life" (John 6:22-59). |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what the crowd grumbles about (6:41-42).
2. Students will explain "no one can come to me unless the Father draws him" (6:44) as good news, grace-first.
3. Students will explain what Jesus means that the bread he gives "for the life of the world" is his flesh (6:51).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the crowd can't believe a hometown guy is from heaven.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): your coming to Jesus started with God coming after you.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the "draw" of 6:44 and effectual calling, taught warmly, not fatalistically.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him" (John 6:44).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If God came looking for me first, how does that change how I see myself?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write your honest question about whether God wants you.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 6:41-51 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 6, verses 41 through 51.
- One-sentence setup: the crowd starts grumbling, and Jesus tells them why some people can't seem to believe.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 41 and 42, what's the crowd's complaint? What do they think they know about Jesus?
   - Cold-call check: they know his parents ("Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph?"), so they can't accept that he came down from heaven. They've boxed him in as a local.
2. **Entry.** When have you decided you already knew all about someone, and it kept you from seeing them clearly?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 44: "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him." At first that can sound like God picks favorites. Now read it next to verse 37 ("whoever comes to me I will never cast out"). Work this: how is "the Father draws you" actually the kindest, safest thing Jesus could say?
   - Guide the groups. Draw out: if your coming to Jesus depended only on you, it would rest on your strength and could fail. Jesus says it starts with the Father drawing you, so it rests on him. And nobody who's drawn gets turned away (verse 37). God drawing you is God wanting you first. Read it as comfort.
   - Guard against fatalism from the text itself: the same Jesus says "come," "believe," "look" (6:35, 40, 47). Nobody is told to sit back. If you want to come, that wanting is the drawing. If you're not sure, you can ask him to draw you.
4. **Core.** Jump to verse 51. Jesus says the bread he'll give "for the life of the world" is his flesh. He's pointing ahead to something. What?
   - Land it: the cross. His body given, his life laid down, so the world can live. The bread costs him everything.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** God "drawing" people is the same idea as the wind-Spirit of new birth (3:8) and the Son giving life "to whom he will" (5:21). Why is it comforting that your salvation is God's doing from start to finish?
   - Draw out: if you didn't start it, you can't lose it by running out of effort. Your security in Christ comes from the fact that you didn't earn your standing in the first place.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does verse 44 say has to happen before anyone comes to Jesus, and how is that good news?
   - The Father has to draw them, and that means God wants and pursues them first.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the sovereign-grace core of sermon 08.

- Lead with the comfort. "Whoever comes to me I will never cast out" (verse 37). Say that first, plainly. Nobody who comes is turned away.
- Then put the security under it. Your coming to Jesus started with the Father drawing you. That means your standing with him doesn't rest on your grip. It rests on his. You didn't earn your way in, so you can't fail your way out.
- Guard it warmly. Nobody gets dragged to a Jesus they hate. God gives a dead heart a new taste, and the person comes gladly, because they finally see he's worth it. If you're not there yet, the honest prayer is "God, draw me."
- Point to the cross. The bread he gives is his own flesh, "for the life of the world" (verse 51). This is where the whole chapter is heading. He feeds you by dying for you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the living bread the Father brings people to, and he gives his own body so the world can live.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says your coming to him starts with God drawing you first. What changes if your faith rests on God's grip and not yours?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 6:35. Read John 6:52-59 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: the crowd is horrified by "eat my flesh," and Jesus explains what he means.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 08's sovereign-grace core. Teach election the way Unit 4 taught 3:8: explicit and glad. Order matters: comfort first (6:37b, never cast out), then security (your coming is the Father's gift), then guard against fatalism with the chapter's own call to come (6:35, 40, 47) and the prayer "draw me."
- Never let 6:44 turn cold. God draws people by giving them a delight in Christ, not by hauling the unwilling. The proof you're drawn is that you come.
- Land 6:51 on the cross (the Passover frame from L1). The bread is his crucified body.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
