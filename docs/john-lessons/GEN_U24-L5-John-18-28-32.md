**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the leaders would not enter Pilate's headquarters (18:28).
2. Students will name the irony of guarding outward cleanness during a judicial murder.
3. Students will explain how 18:32 fulfilled the kind of death Jesus foretold.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): you can be very religious on the outside and blind on the inside.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): outward cleanness is not the same as a clean heart.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the providence fixing crucifixion (Roman) over stoning (Jewish), fulfilling "lifted up" (12:32-33).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. Welcome them back from break warmly, then into the text.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Welcome them back from break.
- Centering line on the board: "They themselves did not enter... so that they would not be defiled, but could eat the Passover" (John 18:28).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where am I careful about looking right on the outside while something's off underneath?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 18:28-32 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 28 through 32.
- One-sentence setup: the trial moves from the high priest to Pilate, the Roman governor, because only Rome could order an execution.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it. (Keep the anti-Judaism guard active, see teacher notes.)

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 28, why won't the leaders go inside Pilate's headquarters?
   - Cold-call check: entering a Gentile's house would make them ceremonially unclean and unable to eat the Passover.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Sit on the irony in verse 28. They're extremely careful not to get religiously "unclean" for the feast. And what are they doing at that exact moment?
   - Guide them: arranging the death of an innocent man. They won't step through a Gentile's doorway because it might defile them, while they work to have Jesus killed. They're straining out a technicality and swallowing a murder.
3. **Core.** How can a person be that precise about outward religion and that blind to what their heart is actually doing?
   - Land it: because outward religion is easy to measure and a heart is easy to hide from yourself. You can keep every rule, hit every service, look completely clean, and never once let God near the actual condition of your heart. These men had made righteousness into a checklist, and a checklist can be perfect while the person keeping it is arranging a murder. That's a warning aimed straight at religious people, including church kids.
4. **Core.** Read verses 31 and 32. Pilate tells them to judge Jesus themselves, and they say they can't legally execute anyone. John says this happened "to fulfill" something Jesus said. What was fixed by this?
   - Draw out: the KIND of death. The Jewish penalty was stoning. But because they had to hand Jesus to Rome, he'd die the Roman way: crucifixion, lifted up on a cross. And Jesus had already said he'd be "lifted up" (12:32-33). So even the legal detail of who could execute made sure Jesus died exactly the death he foretold. God was running the whole thing.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Nobody in this scene is trying to fulfill prophecy. The leaders are just working the legal system to get what they want. Yet it lands precisely on what Jesus predicted. What does that tell you about how God works through even unjust human choices?
   - Draw out: God's plan runs right through people's free, sinful decisions without forcing them or excusing them. The leaders freely chose evil and are responsible for it. And God wove their choices into the exact death that would save the world. Sovereignty and human responsibility, both fully true.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what was the irony of verse 28?
   - They guarded their outward cleanness while arranging an innocent man's death.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Aim the mirror at the religious heart. This one's for the church kids especially. It is entirely possible to keep every rule, look completely put-together, never miss a service, and be spiritually asleep. These men wouldn't step through a doorway, and they were arranging a murder. Clean on the outside, blind on the inside. Ask yourself honestly if your religion is a checklist you keep while keeping God at arm's length.
- Land the providence. And look what God is doing under all their scheming. They're just working the system, and every move lands Jesus exactly on the cross he predicted. God is so in control that even his enemies, freely doing their worst, carry out his plan to the letter.
- Point to the cross. Jesus said he'd be "lifted up." And here, in a legal technicality about who's allowed to execute, God makes sure of it. The cross was never plan B. It was the plan, and nothing could knock it off course.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus goes to the exact death he foretold, his Roman cross secured by a God who rules even an unjust court.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Is your faith a checklist you keep, or a heart open to God? Be honest. Where might you be clean outside and closed inside?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 18:33-37 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: Pilate asks if Jesus is a king, and Jesus describes a kingdom not of this world.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- ANTI-JUDAISM GUARD (active this lesson especially): 18:28 must be taught as hypocrisy in the religious HEART, aimed at the churched student, never as a slur on Jewish people. "The Jews" here means these specific leaders. Jesus, the disciples, and the first church were all Jews. Say it plainly if it helps. The sin named is the sin of every religious hypocrite.
- Two things to land: the irony of 18:28 (outward cleanness during a judicial murder), and the providence of 18:32 (crucifixion fixed, fulfilling "lifted up," 12:32-33; sovereignty and human responsibility both true).
- Derive the frame from sermon 34; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, first day back from spring break. Full Work block.
