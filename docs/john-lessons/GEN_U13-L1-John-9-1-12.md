**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus says did and did not cause the man's blindness (9:3).
2. Students will connect "I am the light of the world" (9:5) to 8:12 and to this healing.
3. Students will explain the meaning of Siloam ("Sent") and how it points to Jesus.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus heals a man born blind, and says his suffering wasn't a punishment.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): last unit Jesus claimed to be the light. Here he proves it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the clay as a Genesis echo; Siloam "Sent" and Jesus the Sent One.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world" (John 9:5).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Is there something hard in my life I've assumed is God punishing me?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest question about your own hard things.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 9:1-12 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 9, verses 1 through 12.
- Set the scene in one sentence: Jesus and his disciples pass a man who's been blind his whole life.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 2, what do the disciples assume caused the man's blindness? In verse 3, how does Jesus answer?
   - Cold-call check: they assume somebody sinned, either the man or his parents. Jesus says neither. It happened "that the works of God might be displayed in him."
2. **Entry.** People still assume that when something bad happens, someone must have deserved it. Where have you seen that assumption?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus flatly rejects the idea that the man's blindness was payback for sin. That's huge for anyone carrying pain. What question does verse 3 replace "what did I do wrong" with?
   - Guide them: instead of "whose fault is this," the question becomes "how does God want to show himself here." God didn't blind a baby to make a point. He redeems the whole thing when Christ opens the man's eyes. Suffering isn't God settling a score with you.
4. **Core.** Read verse 5, then verses 6 and 7. Jesus says "I am the light of the world," the same thing he said last unit (8:12). Then what does he do to prove it?
   - Land it: he gives sight to a man born in total darkness. Last unit it was a claim. This unit it's a healing. He makes mud, puts it on the man's eyes, and sends him to wash.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verse 7 John stops to tell us the pool's name, Siloam, means "Sent." Jesus is the one God sent (remember all the "him who sent me" language). Why would John point that out?
   - Draw out: Jesus sends the blind man to a pool named for the very thing Jesus is, the Sent One. The pool just carries the name. The real power is Christ, who was sent from the Father to open blind eyes. And notice: making eyes out of dirt is what the God of Genesis did when he made the first man from dust.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did Jesus say the man's blindness was for?
   - So that the works of God, God's own power and glory, would be shown in him.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 14.

- Start with verse 3, gently, for the suffering student. Jesus looks at a man who's suffered his whole life and says plainly, this is not punishment for anyone's sin. If you've been carrying something hard and quietly assuming God's getting back at you, hear him: you didn't owe God your suffering for your sin.
- Then the turn. God has a purpose in this man's pain, and it's kind. His whole life of blindness gets gathered up and redeemed the moment Jesus opens his eyes. The best thing that ever happened to him grew right out of the hardest thing.
- Connect it to last unit. Jesus said, "I am the light of the world." Here he walks up to a man born in the dark and gives him light. He proves the claim on a real person's real eyes.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the light of the world and the one God sent, and he opens eyes that were born in the dark.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says this man's suffering wasn't punishment, and that God had a good purpose in it. If God could bring something good out of your hardest thing, what would you want him to do with it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 9:13-23 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: the leaders start interrogating the man, and his parents get scared.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The 9:3 ruling is the payoff of Unit 8's handling of 5:14. Guard it on both sides: God did not blind a baby to show off (the cruel misread), and this isn't a promise that God always heals (the prosperity misread). God has a kind purpose in suffering, and it landed for this man in the moment Christ opened his eyes.
- Tie 9:5 to 8:12 explicitly. The claim students memorized last unit is acted out here. Let 8:12 sit next to 9:5 in the cumulative review.
- Teach Siloam ("Sent") plainly for the whole room, and give the disciple tier the Genesis echo (eyes from dust, like the first man).
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
