**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what happens when Jesus says "I am he" (18:6).
2. Students will explain that Jesus goes to the cross by his own choice (18:8, 18:11).
3. Students will explain "the cup that the Father has given me" (18:11).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the man being arrested is the one really in control.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus isn't a helpless victim; he chooses the cross.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "I am he" (ego eimi) as the divine name (tie to 8:58); the cup as John's Gethsemane.

---

**Talk budget:** short half-day. Keep it tight and reverent. One clear center. Let students do the noticing.

## Move 1: Quiet time and a brief memory warm-touch (6 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away. This is the day after the checkpoint, so keep the memory beat light.
- Quick warm pass: have students say two anchors aloud together, 10:11 and 14:6, since both come alive in this unit. No quiz today.
- Centering line on the board: "Shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?" (John 18:11).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When everything falls apart around me, how do I usually react?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 18:1-11 aloud, slowly and reverently.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 18, verses 1 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: the prayer is over, and Judas leads soldiers into the garden to arrest Jesus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 12 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus for the short day: who's in control of this arrest. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 3, how many people and how much gear come to arrest Jesus? (A band of soldiers and officers, with lanterns, torches, and weapons, to take one unarmed man.)
   - Cold-call check: a whole armed squad, for one man.
2. **The driving question (whole class).** Read verses 5 and 6. When Jesus says "I am he," what happens to the arresting squad? What does that show?
   - Guide them: they draw back and fall to the ground. His words alone, no weapon, knock them down. It shows he's not being overpowered. He's completely in control. If he wanted to walk away, he could.
3. **Core.** Read verse 8. In the middle of being arrested, what is Jesus concerned about? Who is he protecting?
   - Land it: his disciples. "If you seek me, let these men go." He's the good shepherd (10:11) putting himself between the danger and his sheep, even as they arrest him.
4. **Core.** Peter grabs a sword and cuts off a man's ear. Read verse 11. What does Jesus say to Peter, and what does he say about the cup?
   - Draw out: he tells Peter to put the sword away. And he says, "shall I not drink the cup the Father has given me?" The cup is the suffering of the cross. Jesus isn't being dragged to it. He's choosing to drink it, because the Father gave it to him.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: is Jesus a helpless victim here, or in control? How do you know?
   - In control. His word knocks the squad down, he protects his friends, and he chooses to drink the cup.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (4 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name what you're watching. This looks like the worst thing happening TO Jesus. An armed mob, torches in the dark, a betrayer, a friend swinging a sword. And underneath it, Jesus is the calmest, most in-command person in the garden. One word from him and the soldiers hit the dirt.
- Land the choice. He wasn't caught. He wasn't cornered. He said it himself: "shall I not drink the cup the Father has given me?" He walked into this on purpose, for you. Nobody took his life. He laid it down.
- Set the tone for the whole unit. We're stepping into the hardest, most sacred part of the story. Watch for it the whole way: everything happens to him, and he is running all of it, out of love.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus goes to the cross as the King in command, choosing the Father's cup and shielding his own.

## Move 5: Respond (short, graded participation)

- One line in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus wasn't trapped. He chose the cross on purpose, for you. What's one thing that changes about how you see him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 18:12-18 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: Jesus is bound and led to the high priest, and Peter's denials begin.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: half-day, 26 minutes. Light and tight. Keep the memory beat brief (the checkpoint the day before carried the heavy recap). One center: Jesus is sovereign over his own arrest.
- The load-bearing point: "I am he" (18:5-6) drops the squad, proving Jesus goes willingly (10:18, no one takes his life). For the disciple tier, "I am he" is ego eimi, the divine name (tie to 8:58).
- The cup (18:11) is John's Gethsemane: Jesus embracing the Father's will. Peter's sword is rebuked (the kingdom doesn't advance by force, sets up 18:36).
- Set the reverent tone for the whole Passion here. This is sacred, sober ground.
- Anti-Judaism guard (carried all unit): where "the Jews" appears in these chapters it means the hostile leaders; the cross is human sin and God's plan, never a people. Introduce it gently in the reading guide and hold it through the unit.
- Fresh-build; no sermon.
