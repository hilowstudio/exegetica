**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what Jesus knew about the woman that she never told him (4:18).
2. Students will explain why being fully known led her to call him a prophet (4:19).
3. Students will connect Jesus' knowledge here to how he knew Nathanael (1:47-48).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): everyone has something they hope nobody finds out. Jesus already knows hers, and he stays.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): this is the same knowing that saw Nathanael under the fig tree (1:47-48). Jesus sees people fully.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Jesus exposes the wound to heal it, the way hard ground gets broken before it can bear fruit.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, so the Work block is one key question. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Come, see a man who told me all that I ever did" (John 4:29).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What's one thing about me I'd rather nobody knew?" No one reads this out.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 4:16-19 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 4, verses 16 through 19.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus asks her to go get her husband, and what he says next shows he already knows her whole story.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with a quick read-check, then work the one question in groups.

1. **Read-check (any student can answer).** In verse 18, what does Jesus tell her about her own life that she never mentioned?
   - Cold-call check: she's had five husbands, and the man she's with now is not her husband.
2. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus just named the most painful part of her life out loud. She could have shut the conversation down or walked off. Instead she says, "I perceive that you are a prophet" (verse 19). Work this: why does being completely known make her lean in rather than run?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out: he named her history without contempt. There's no sneer in it. He knows the worst and he's still talking with her, still offering her living water. That kind of knowing feels safe, not dangerous. Being seen all the way through and not thrown away is what she'd been missing.
   - Push the disciple tier: Jesus doesn't dig this up to embarrass her. He brings the buried thing into daylight because hidden things can't heal. Hard ground has to be broken before it can grow anything.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Handle this passage with care.

- Say plainly what the text says and no more. She's a woman marked by loss and broken relationships and a present situation that isn't right. The text doesn't call her names, and neither do we. Guard her dignity the way Jesus does.
- Name the knowing. Jesus knew every hidden thing about her before she said a word, the same way he knew Nathanael sitting under the fig tree (1:47-48). He knows you like that too, all the way down.
- Then the good news the whole scene turns on: he knows the worst about her and he stays. He keeps offering her living water. Being fully known by Jesus is safe.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus knows everything you've ever done, and he stays.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus knew the hardest thing about the woman and kept talking with her. What would change for you if you really believed he knows the hardest thing about you and stays?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged, not what they wrote.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Friday is a half-day recap and memory check, so review your four verses (John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16).
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: Friday's half-day recaps the well conversation and checks the four memory verses.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups, not a lecture about her marriages.
- Handle the five husbands the way the framing rules: real history, exposed to heal. No speculation about how the marriages ended, no leering, no using her shame as a lever. Do not call her a prostitute. The text will not carry that word, and the point is mercy.
- Watch the anxious student in the room who's sure their secret would end things with God. This passage is for them. Lead with the knowing that stays.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. Keep the Work block to the one key question and protect Move 4.
