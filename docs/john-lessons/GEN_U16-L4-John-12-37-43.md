**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the two Isaiah quotations John uses (12:38 and 12:40).
2. Students will explain how "would not believe" and "could not believe" are both true (12:37, 12:39-40).
3. Students will explain what "Isaiah saw his glory" (12:41) claims about Jesus.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): people saw miracle after miracle and still didn't believe.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): God is in control even of unbelief, and people are still responsible.
- **Stretch** (disciple): judicial hardening held together with human responsibility; Isaiah saw the Son.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is dense theology, so give the Work block its full time.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory" (John 12:41).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Is there something about God that's hard for me to accept?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest hard thing.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 12:37-43 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 12, verses 37 through 43.
- One-sentence setup: John stops the story to explain something troubling, why so many people saw Jesus' signs and still would not believe.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it. This is a hard passage, so go slow and let students wrestle.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 37, what had the people seen, and what did they do about it?
   - Cold-call check: they'd seen so many signs, and they still did not believe. Evidence wasn't the problem.
2. **Entry.** Have you ever seen someone shown clear evidence and still refuse to change their mind? What was going on in them?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** John quotes Isaiah twice. Read verse 37, then verse 39. Verse 37 says they "did not believe." Verse 39 says they "could not believe" because God hardened them. Work this: how can both be true, that they would not believe (their choice) and could not believe (God's doing)?
   - Guide them carefully, and don't resolve it too fast. Draw out: John puts both right next to each other, so both are true. The people genuinely refused, and it's really their fault (verse 37). And God, in judgment, gave hard hearts over to their hardness, so they couldn't come (verses 39-40). The Bible holds God's control and human responsibility together without picking one. It's okay for this to feel like a lot.
4. **Core.** This can sound scary, so here's the guard: the hardening isn't God zapping innocent people who wanted to believe. It's God's judgment handing people over to a "no" they already chose. And look what God did with all this rejection: the message went out to the whole world (the Greeks are already coming). How is God still in control, and still good, even in the unbelief?
   - Land it: God wasn't caught off guard by the rejection. He foretold it through Isaiah, and he worked it into his plan, so that mercy spilled out to the nations. Nothing about the cross was an accident. God's sovereignty is a comfort here, for the person whose heart is soft toward Jesus.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 41: "Isaiah said these things because he saw his glory." Isaiah 6 says Isaiah saw the LORD enthroned in the temple, seven hundred years before Jesus. Whose glory does John say Isaiah actually saw?
   - Draw out: Jesus'. John says the enthroned LORD Isaiah saw was the Son. That's a massive claim about who Jesus is, right up there with "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58) and "I and the Father are one" (10:30). The prophet saw Christ on the throne. It's the capstone of the whole "who is Jesus" question the first half of the book has been asking.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does verse 41 tell us about who Jesus is?
   - The glorious LORD Isaiah saw enthroned in the temple was Jesus himself.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 21.

- Name the hard thing honestly. People saw Jesus do things only God can do, and they still walked away. Evidence has never been enough by itself. A hard heart can look at a raised man and plot another murder.
- Hold both truths gently. The Bible says these people would not believe, and it says they could not, because God gave them over. Both are true. You don't have to untangle it tonight. What you do with it is check your own heart: is it soft toward Jesus, or hard?
- Guard against pride, straight from the sermon. Don't imagine you'd have believed where they didn't. If your heart is soft toward Christ right now, that's a gift of grace, not something you're smarter for. The right response to this passage isn't superiority. It's "God, keep my heart soft."
- Point to the glory. Isaiah saw Jesus on the throne seven centuries early. The one they rejected is the LORD of glory. And God took even their rejection and used it to open the door to the whole world, including you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the LORD of glory Isaiah saw, in control even of the unbelief that sent him to the cross.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The right response to this passage isn't 'I'm glad I'm not like them.' It's 'God, keep my heart soft.' Write a version of that prayer in your own words."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 12:44-50 once before Thursday. Those are Jesus' last public words.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: Jesus' final public appeal, with the door still open.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%. This is the hardest theology in the unit, so give it room and let students wrestle honestly.
- Hold the judicial hardening and human responsibility together, as John does. They would not believe (12:37, real guilt), and they could not believe (12:39-40, God's judgment handing them over). Never fatalism ("so why bother"), and never a God who isn't sovereign over hard hearts.
- Guard against pride (sermon 21): the difference between belief and unbelief is grace, not merit. Land Move 5 on "keep my heart soft," never on "I'm glad I'm not them."
- Teach 12:41 as the deity capstone of the Book of Signs. The LORD Isaiah saw enthroned (Isaiah 6) was the Son.
- Handle the secret believers (12:42-43) gently, as a warning about fear of man, not a club against a frightened student.
- Carry the anti-Jewish guard: the believing crowds (12:11) and the many leaders who believed (12:42) are also Jewish. The hardening measures response to Christ, and it falls on any hard heart, including the churched student.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
