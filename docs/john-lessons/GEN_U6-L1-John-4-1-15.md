# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 6 Lesson 1 of 5: Jacob's well and living water (John 4:1-15)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U6 Living water |
| Lesson | 1 of 5 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Oct 7 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 4:1-15 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus goes out of his way to a thirsty outsider and offers her living water, the Spirit and the eternal life only he gives. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the giver of living water, greater than Jacob's well, who crosses every barrier to find one thirsty person. |
| Memory verse | No new verse yet this lesson. Keep John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, and 3:16 sharp. The unit's new verse (John 4:24) plants at L4, in the worship passage. |
| Resources | No paired sermon for John 4:1-42. External map: the route from Judea to Galilee through Samaria, showing Sychar and Jacob's well. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the woman was surprised Jesus spoke to her (4:9) and what barriers he crossed.
2. Students will contrast the well water and the living water Jesus offers (4:13-14).
3. Students will state what the living water is (the Spirit and the eternal life it brings, John 7:39).
4. Students will explain how Jesus is "greater than Jacob" (4:12).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): everyone is thirsty for something they keep going back to. Start there. Jesus meets a woman everyone avoided and offers her something that lasts.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): notice the barriers Jesus crosses on purpose (Jew and Samaritan, man and woman alone, clean and unclean).
- **Stretch** (disciple): living water as the Spirit (7:39), Jacob's well against the fountain of Jeremiah 2:13, the divine "had to" of 4:4.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Jesus, wearied as he was from his journey, was sitting beside the well" (John 4:6).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I actually thirsty for right now?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest thirst, even if you're not sure anyone's listening.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 4:1-15 aloud, slowly. One reads 1 through 9, the other 10 through 15.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 4, verses 1 through 15. Walk the newcomer to the page.
- Set the scene in one sentence: Jesus is tired and thirsty at a well at noon, and a Samaritan woman comes to draw water.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Look at verse 9. Why is the woman surprised Jesus even talks to her?
   - Cold-call check: he's a Jew asking a favor of a Samaritan woman. John adds, "Jews have no dealings with Samaritans." Jesus crosses that line first.
2. **Entry.** What are you thirsty for that you keep going back to, and it never lasts? Say it plainly.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 13 and 14 side by side. Write the difference between the water in the well and the water Jesus gives. What happens to the person who drinks Jesus' water?
   - Guide them: the well leaves you thirsty again, Jesus' water becomes "a spring of water welling up to eternal life." It's inside you and it lasts.
4. **Core.** In verse 12 she asks, "Are you greater than our father Jacob?" She means it as a challenge. How does the rest of the passage answer her question?
   - Land it: Jacob's well gives water you come back thirsty from. Jesus gives water that ends the thirst forever. He's greater than the well and greater than the man who dug it.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Jesus calls it "living water." Turn to John 7:39, where John tells us what the living water is. What is Jesus really offering her?
   - Draw out: the Holy Spirit, and the eternal life the Spirit brings. Connect to Jeremiah 2:13, God the "fountain of living waters" whom people trade for "broken cisterns that can hold no water."
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In verse 4 it says Jesus "had to pass through Samaria." Most Jews went around it. In one sentence, why did he have to go through?
   - He went to find her. Grace goes out of its way.

Circulate while groups work. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Start with how tired he is. Verse 6, the Giver of living water sits down thirsty and asks a woman for a drink. The fountain of life is thirsty. That's the God who came close in John 1:14, right here in one small human detail.
- Name the barriers he crosses. She's a Samaritan, a woman, alone at noon (probably to avoid the other women), and she has a past. Jesus crosses every one of those lines to talk with her. He came looking before she did.
- Offer the water plainly. Living water is the Holy Spirit and the life he gives, a spring inside you that never dries up. Everyone in the room is thirsty for something. Jesus offers the one drink that lasts.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the living water, greater than any well, and he goes out of his way to find one thirsty person.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The woman kept coming back to a well that never satisfied her for long. What's one 'well' you keep going back to, and what would it look like to bring that thirst to Jesus?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged, not what they decided.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 4:16-19 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation (Quiet-Time Journal and the Move 5 response).
- Sets up L2: Jesus is about to tell this woman he already knows her whole history.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- No paired sermon exists for John 4, so lean on the framing file for the theological read. Living water is the Spirit and the life it gives (7:39). Keep it there, and don't shrink it to a "Jesus satisfies you" slogan.
- Don't rush past verse 4's "had to." The necessity is the Father's purpose to find one woman, the same "must" language as 3:14.
- Watch the map: some students won't know Samaria from Galilee. Thirty seconds on the map earns the rest of the unit.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, so the Work block gets its full 25 minutes. If Move 3 runs long, trim the Deeper question first, keep the cold-call checks.
