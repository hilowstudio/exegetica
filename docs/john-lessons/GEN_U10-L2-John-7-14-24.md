| Big idea | Jesus teaches the Father's own message, and the test for recognizing God's truth is a heart willing to obey it. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the Sent One whose teaching is the Father's, and his truth draws a willing heart to himself. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state whose teaching Jesus claims to be giving (7:16).
2. Students will explain the test in 7:17 (the will to obey comes before the knowing).
3. Students will explain the Sabbath and circumcision argument and "judge with right judgment" (7:22-24).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the crowd is amazed Jesus knows so much without schooling.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you can decide a teaching is false just because you don't like it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): 7:17 as a real test, and the lesser-to-greater Sabbath argument.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God" (John 7:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Is there something God says that I don't want to hear?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the part of the Bible you most push back on, honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 7:14-24 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 7, verses 14 through 24.
- One-sentence setup: halfway through the feast, Jesus starts teaching openly in the temple, and people are stunned.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 15, what surprises the crowd about Jesus' teaching?
   - Cold-call check: he knows so much without having formally studied under a rabbi. Where did he get it?
2. **Entry.** In verse 16, whose teaching does Jesus say he's giving?
   - Not his own. It comes "from him who sent me," the Father.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 17 slowly: "If anyone's will is to do God's will, he will know whether the teaching is from God." Notice the order. What comes first, wanting to obey God or understanding whether it's true? Work this: why would being willing to obey come before knowing?
   - Guide them: Jesus says the willing heart is the one that recognizes God's truth. If you come to God ready to obey whatever he says, you'll see his teaching for what it is. If you come deciding you'll only accept what you already like, you'll always find a reason to reject the parts that cut against you.
4. **Core.** Some people build a version of God who agrees with everything they already think, and they reject anything that challenges them. How is that a way of never really hearing God at all?
   - Land it: a God who never disagrees with you is a God you made up. The real God tells you things you didn't want to hear, and a willing heart lets him. The test in verse 17 is really a test of the heart.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verses 22 and 23 Jesus points out that they circumcise a baby on the Sabbath to keep Moses' law. So why are they furious that he healed a man's whole body on the Sabbath? What's his argument?
   - Draw out: if it's fine to do a small work on the Sabbath to obey the law, it's more than fine to heal a whole person. They're straining out a rule and missing the mercy. That's why verse 24 says "judge with right judgment," not by appearances.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 17, what kind of person recognizes that Jesus' teaching is from God?
   - The person who's willing to do God's will.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on the first half of sermon 11.

- Name the claim. Jesus says his teaching isn't his own bright idea. It's the Father's own message, delivered by the One the Father sent. When Jesus speaks, God speaks.
- Turn verse 17 on the room, gently. You can't out-think your way to trusting Jesus while your heart is set on keeping the parts of your life he'd change. The willing heart is the one that comes to see he's true. If you're stuck, the honest prayer is "God, make me willing."
- Warn about the homemade God. It's easy to build a Jesus who agrees with everything you already believe and never asks you to change. That Jesus is just a mirror of you. The real Jesus loves you enough to tell you the truth.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus speaks the Father's own words, and a heart willing to obey is the heart that recognizes him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus says a willing heart is the one that recognizes God's truth. Is there something God says that you've been unwilling to hear? Write it honestly."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 7:25-36 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: the crowd argues about where Jesus is from and whether he's the Christ.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 11's first half: Jesus' teaching comes from the Father, and the test in 7:17 is a test of the will. Chrysostom and Augustine both read it this way: clear out the pride and the bent will, and the truth becomes recognizable.
- Watch the "homemade God" beat. Sermon 11's sharp application is that a person can shape a God who never disagrees with them. Press it warmly on the churched student.
- The Sabbath argument reaches back to the Bethesda healing (Unit 8, John 5). A quick reminder of that scene helps students track it.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
