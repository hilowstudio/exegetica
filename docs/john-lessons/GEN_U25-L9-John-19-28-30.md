**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will recite John 19:30 and explain "It is finished" (tetelestai) as completion and victory.
2. Students will explain "paid in full" as the meaning of the finished work.
3. Students will explain "he gave up his spirit" as Jesus dying in sovereign control (10:18).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus' last word was "done," not "I'm beaten."
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the work of saving you is finished; you don't add to it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): tetelestai as "paid in full"; "gave up his spirit" as a sovereign act.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the memory-verse plant and the climax of the unit. Give it the full period, and hold the reverence.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "It is finished" (John 19:30).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I always trying to be good enough at? What would it feel like to hear 'it's finished, you can stop earning it'?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:28-30 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 28 through 30.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus comes to his last moment, and speaks his final word.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 28, what does Jesus say, and what does John tell us it fulfilled? In verse 30, what does he say right at the end?
   - Cold-call check: "I thirst" (fulfilling Scripture, Psalm 69:21), and then "It is finished."
2. **Entry.** "I thirst" reminds us of something. Was Jesus really suffering physically, or just going through the motions?
   - Draw out: really suffering. He's fully human, dying of crucifixion, genuinely parched. The Word became flesh, and the flesh really hurt. His suffering was real, for us.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Now the big one. "It is finished" is one word in Greek: tetelestai. Here's the key: back then, merchants wrote that exact word across a bill when it was fully paid. It meant "paid in full." So when Jesus says "It is finished," what is he actually announcing?
   - Guide them: he's announcing that the work of saving us is complete, the debt is paid in full, and there is nothing left to add. It's a shout of victory. He's not saying "I'm finished, I'm beaten." He's saying "It's done. Paid. Complete. The thing I came to do is accomplished." The bill for your sin, stamped paid in full.
4. **Core.** Read the last line of verse 30: "he bowed his head and gave up his spirit." Compare that to how a person usually dies. Then look back at John 10:18, "no one takes it from me, I lay it down of my own accord." What's different about how Jesus dies?
   - Land it: he chooses the moment. Normally death takes you; here Jesus gives up his spirit, on purpose, when the work is done. He said in chapter 10 that no one could take his life, that he'd lay it down himself. And that's exactly what he does. He's Lord even of his own death. He didn't run out of life. He finished the work and handed his spirit over.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Put "It is finished" next to everything you've learned this year. If the debt is paid in full, and the work of salvation is complete, what does that mean for a person who's always trying to be good enough for God?
   - Draw out: it means the most important work in the universe was finished by someone else, perfectly, before you were born. You cannot add to a finished cross. You cannot earn what's already paid. All your striving to be good enough for God is answered by one word from the cross: finished. You don't repay a paid debt. You receive it, and you rest.
6. **Memory verse plant.** Have students say John 19:30 together, then in pairs. Make sure "It is finished" and "gave up his spirit" are solid. Say the word tetelestai out loud together once, and tell them what it meant on a receipt: paid in full.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is the center of the whole course. Let it land.

- Say the word over them. Tetelestai. It is finished. That's not the whimper of a beaten man. It's the shout of a victor. When Jesus said it, he meant the work of rescuing you was done, complete, accomplished. The bill for every sin you've ever committed or ever will, stamped in red: paid in full.
- Land the freedom. Some of you spend your whole life trying to be enough. Good enough, smart enough, holy enough, to finally be okay with God. Hear the cross answer you. It is finished. You can stop. The work you could never finish, he finished. You don't add to it. You don't earn it. You receive it.
- Land the sovereignty of his death. And notice how he dies. He doesn't just run out of life. He bows his head and gives up his spirit, on purpose, the moment the work is done. He's Lord even here. Nobody took his life. He laid it down for you, and then he laid it down.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus finished the work of saving you, paid your debt in full, and laid down his life himself. It is finished.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'It is finished.' The debt is paid in full. What have you been trying to earn that Jesus already finished for you?"
- Give it quiet, and let it be reverent. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Start memorizing John 19:30. Read John 19:31-37 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 19:30 enters the rolling memory quizzes.
- Sets up L10 (short Thursday): the pierced side, and John's eyewitness that Jesus really died.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This is the plant AND the climax of the unit. 19:30 is the fifteenth graded anchor. Give it the full period.
- Teach "It is finished" (tetelestai, perfect tense) as COMPLETION and VICTORY, the word stamped on a paid bill ("paid in full"). Guard HARD against hearing it as defeat or "I am finished."
- "He gave up his spirit" = Jesus dying in sovereign control (10:18, "no one takes it from me"). He chose the moment. Not life torn away; life laid down.
- The pastoral edge is the load-bearing point for a performance-driven room: a finished work is received, not repaid. This answers the legalist side door directly. Every prior anchor points here (3:16, 10:11).
- Keep it reverent and worshipful. This is the center of the course.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block for the plant.
