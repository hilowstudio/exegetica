# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 11: a note on the text (John 7:53-8:11)

| Field | Detail |

| Lesson | 1 of 1 |
| Date / Day type | Fri Nov 13 / Regular (50 min), light density |
| Passage | John 7:53-8:11 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The only man qualified to throw the stone lays it down, telling the truth about the woman's sin and refusing to condemn her. |
| Christ connection | Grace and truth meet in Jesus, who can refuse to condemn because he's walking toward the cross where the condemnation falls on him. |
| Memory verse | No new verse this unit, and no optional stretch verse (this passage is not in the earliest manuscripts, so it isn't handed out to memorize). Keep the six sharp: John 20:31, 1:14, 1:12, 3:16, 4:24, 6:35. The next new one is Unit 12 (John 8:12). |
| Resources | No paired sermon. This is a "note on the text" lesson: honest textual-criticism teaching plus the story's content. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain, plainly, why their Bible brackets and footnotes John 7:53-8:11, and why that builds trust.
2. Students will explain the trap the leaders set and name the injustice (the missing man, 8:4).
3. Students will explain the two things Jesus says to the woman (8:11) and why both matter.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a shamed woman is used as bait, and the one man with the right to judge her sets her free.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the men with the stones are the mirror. They walk out oldest first when the light hits their own record.
- **Stretch** (disciple): how we got the New Testament and why a marked, disputed passage strengthens our confidence in Scripture.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is a light-density lesson with two halves (the textual note and the story), so give each about equal time and keep both moving.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more" (John 8:11).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Is there something about me I'm afraid would get me condemned if people knew?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest fear, whatever it is.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (about 5 min)

- Have one student read John 7:53-8:11 aloud, slowly.
- Then have everyone find the same passage in their own ESV and read the note at the top silently ("The earliest manuscripts do not include 7:53-8:11") and notice the brackets around the story.
- First question of the day, cold and simple: "What did you notice at the very top of this story?" Let a student name the footnote. That hands you the first half.

## Move 3a: Work the text, the note (about 13 min, no lecture)

The first governed half is the textual question. Teach it as a trust-builder.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** What does the note at the top of John 8 say about this story?
   - Cold-call check: the earliest manuscripts don't include it. It's in brackets.
2. **Governed question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Your Bible tells you, right on the page, that the oldest copies of John don't have this story. Does that make you trust your Bible more or less, and why?
   - Guide them, don't lecture. Draw out the trust-building case:
     - We don't have John's original page. We have thousands of very old handwritten copies, and scholars compare them line by line.
     - When something shows up later or moves around, they flag it. They flagged this one. That's what careful looks like.
     - The flag is proof they're telling you the truth, not hiding anything.
     - No teaching of the Christian faith rests on this one paragraph. The whole rest of the New Testament, thousands of agreeing copies, is solid.
   - Land the half on trust: the note in your Bible is evidence the people who gave it to you were honest with you.
3. **For the group that goes deep (disciple tier).** Hand them three facts: a Christian writer named Papias knew this story around the year 130, older than the manuscripts of John that carry it. In some copies it shows up in a totally different place, even after Luke 21. And John 7:52 connects cleanly to John 8:12 without it. So the settled read is: very old, almost certainly a real event from Jesus' life, probably not part of what John originally wrote, safe and good to read.

## Move 3b: Work the text, the story (about 13 min, no lecture)

The second governed half is the content. One clean, light pass.

4. **Entry.** In verse 4 the leaders say the woman was "caught in the act of adultery." That takes two people. Who's missing? What does that tell you about these men?
   - Cold-call check: the man is gone. The Law they quote (both must die) requires the man too. They're breaking Moses' law to weaponize half of it. It's a rigged, unjust trap, and she's the bait.
5. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 7. Jesus says, "Let him who is without sin among you be the first to throw a stone." Then read verse 9. What happens, and in what order do they leave?
   - Guide them: they leave one by one, oldest first. Jesus made each accuser look at his own record. He didn't cancel the law. He made it impossible for these men, with dirty hands and a rigged case, to be the ones to carry it out.
6. **Core.** Read verses 10 and 11. Jesus says two things to the woman: "Neither do I condemn you" and "go, and from now on sin no more." Why does he say both? What would go wrong if he only said one?
   - Land it: only the first, and it sounds like her sin doesn't matter. Only the second, and it's crushing law with no mercy. Together, it's grace and truth. He tells the truth about her sin and refuses to condemn her. The "sin no more" is the road a freed woman walks once she's already pardoned.
7. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: who in that courtyard was actually qualified to throw a stone, and what did he do?
   - The one man without sin, Jesus, and he dropped it.

Circulate during both halves. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (about 6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the lesson. Framed fresh, no sermon.

- Name grace and truth in one person. Back in John 1:14, John said Jesus came "full of grace and truth." Here you watch it. Truth: he calls her sin what it is. Grace: he will not condemn her. Both from one mouth, in one breath, with no strain between them.
- Point to the clean hands. Every other person in that courtyard was disqualified by their own sin. Jesus wasn't. The one man who could have thrown the stone is the one who put it down and lifted her up. That's the gospel in a single move.
- Say the deepest thing for the disciple tier. Jesus can say "neither do I condemn you" because he's walking toward a cross where the condemnation she earned, and the condemnation you've earned, will fall on him instead. The stone he refuses to throw becomes the judgment he agrees to bear.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the sinless Judge who had every right to condemn came to save, and he says the same eight words over every guilty person who comes to him.

## Move 5: Respond (about 6 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Pick one prompt, and students may keep it private if they choose:
  - "Where in this story do you see yourself, the woman with no defense or the men holding stones, and why?"
  - "What's one thing you now know about how we got our Bible, and does it make you trust it more?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged, not what they decided.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 8:12-20 once before the next unit.
- To the gradebook: today's participation (Quiet-Time Journal and the Move 5 response). No new memory verse, no stretch verse.
- Sets up Unit 12: right after this, Jesus says "I am the light of the world," the next new memory verse (John 8:12).

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: light-density, two-part lesson. The textual note and the story each get about 13 minutes. Turn content into questions and keep both halves moving.
- The textual segment is a trust-builder, the same move Unit 8 made with the 5:4 note. End it on trust, never on a shrug. Get ahead of the leap from "this paragraph is disputed" to "so maybe none of the Bible is reliable." Say the opposite out loud: the flag in the margin is evidence of care, disputed passages are few and marked, and no doctrine turns on them.
- The single biggest pitfall on the content: the antinomian misread, hearing "neither do I condemn you" and stopping there. Always read 8:11 to its end. Augustine's line helps: the Lord condemned the sin and spared the woman, and he did not say "go and live as you please."
- Don't skip the missing man (8:4). It exposes the hypocrisy and injustice that are half the point.
- Handle it his way, no re-shaming. Jesus cleared the crowd and lowered his eyes before he spoke to her. Name the sin once, plainly, and move to the mercy. Watch any guilty student in the room.
- No stretch verse this unit, on purpose. Handing students a disputed line to memorize would undercut the honest lesson you just taught. Keep memory to a light review of the six anchors.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day at light density. If a section runs long, protect the Land.
