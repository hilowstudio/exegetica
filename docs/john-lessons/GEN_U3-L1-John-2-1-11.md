# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 3 Lesson 1 of 4: the wedding at Cana (John 2:1-11)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U3 Sign 1 & the temple |
| Lesson | 1 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Tue Sep 22 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 2:1-11 (ESV) |
| Big idea | At a wedding where the wine runs out, Jesus turns about 150 gallons of water into the best wine anyone has tasted, the first of his signs, and shows his glory. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the Bridegroom who has come, bringing the feast the prophets promised. The sign is a window: look at what he does and see who he is. |
| Memory verse | Review only (20:31, 1:14, 1:12). No new plant this unit. Offer John 2:11 as an optional off-graded stretch for the disciple tier (the unit's thesis: he "manifested his glory, and his disciples believed"). |
| Resources | No paired sermon covers Cana; fresh-build from this framing. Have a class Bible open to John 2. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will retell what happens at Cana and identify it as "the first of his signs" (2:11).
2. Students will explain what a "sign" is in John: a work that points past itself to reveal who Jesus is (2:11; 20:30-31).
3. Students will explain what the sign reveals about Jesus (his glory, the Bridegroom bringing abundance).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus went to a wedding, the wine ran out, and he made about 150 gallons of the best wine; that is a "sign," a clue to who he is. Leave able to find John 2 and say one sentence.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): press the six purification jars until the old religion of ceremonial washing runs dry and the new wine of Christ fills the room.
- **Stretch** (disciple): trace "my hour has not yet come" (2:4) forward to 12:23 and 17:1, and read Isaiah 25:6-9, the wine-feast on the mountain where God swallows up death forever.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the year's first sign, so plant the whole category of "sign" here, through questions.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "This, the first of his signs, Jesus did at Cana in Galilee, and manifested his glory" (John 2:11).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do you most want to see that Jesus is more than you assumed?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: honest curiosity is welcome.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 2:1-11 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 2, verses 1 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: John calls this "the first of his signs," so we are learning today what a sign is and how to read one.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Walk the scene. Where is Jesus, what runs out, and what does he do about it? How much wine, roughly, and what is it like (verse 10)?
   - Draw out: a wedding in Cana; the wine runs out; Jesus has six stone jars filled with water and it becomes wine, about 120 to 180 gallons, and the master says it is the best, saved for last.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Verse 11 does not call this a "miracle" or a "wonder." It calls it a "sign," and says Jesus "manifested his glory." What is the difference between a trick that makes you say "how did he do that" and a sign that makes you ask "who is this"?
   - Guide them: a sign points past the deed to the person doing it. John tells you at the end of the book why he wrote the signs down: "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ" (20:30-31). A sign is a window. You look through the water-into-wine and see who Jesus is.
3. **Core.** Look at the six stone jars in verse 6: what were they for? The water in them becomes the best wine, served last and in flood. What might John want you to see in the old washing-water becoming new wine?
   - Land it: the jars held water "for the Jewish rites of purification." The old religion of washing up gives way to the good wine of Christ, poured out in abundance. This is the feast the prophets promised (Isaiah 25:6, a banquet of well-aged wine on God's mountain). The Bridegroom has come, and the party has started.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verse 4 Jesus tells his mother, "My hour has not yet come." Hold that word "hour." Where else does it show up (12:23; 17:1)? What is "the hour" in John?
   - Draw out: in John, "the hour" is the cross, the moment the Son of Man is glorified. Even here, at a wedding, Jesus is moving on the Father's clock toward Calvary. His glory at Cana points to a deeper glory at the cross.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What does John call this event? What does a sign do?
   - The first of his signs; it manifests his glory and points to who Jesus is, so people will believe.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Name the Bridegroom. The wine at a wedding is joy, and it ran out, the way the joy of the old order had run thin. Jesus walks in and pours out the best, in flood. He is the Bridegroom the whole Bible has been waiting for, and he brings the feast in person.
- Point down the road. What you are watching is the front edge of a wedding that runs all the way to the cross, where the wine is his own blood, and on to the marriage supper of the Lamb.
- Christ connection to say out loud: look through the water-into-wine and you see the glory of the Word made flesh (1:14), the Bridegroom who came to bring joy that does not run out.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "A sign is a window onto who Jesus is. Looking through this one, what is one thing you would say Jesus is like?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 2:12-17 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Nothing from chapter 2 enters the memory quizzes; keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12.
- Sets up L2: Jesus walks into the temple at Passover and clears out the market.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%. This lesson plants the whole idea of a "sign," which pays off at every sign for the rest of the year.
- Grounding for your own prep: semeion is a mark by which a thing is known, the Old Testament oth (covenant sign); John files Jesus' works as signs, not spectacles (20:30-31). "Hour" (hora) is a fixed appointed time; the chain runs 2:4 to 7:30, 8:20, 12:23, 17:1, and in John it is the cross. Isaiah 25:6-9 is the wine-feast oracle, and the same passage says God "will swallow up death forever," which is why the chapter brackets "on the third day" (2:1) with "in three days" (2:19).
- Guard the alcohol tangent in one beat: Jesus made real wine, a lot of it, for a celebration; Scripture blesses wine as a gift and warns against drunkenness. Name the question if raised, then steer back to the glory revealed.
- Do not drain "sign" into "magic trick," and do not preach numerology on the six jars. Give the water-to-wine reading and stop.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
