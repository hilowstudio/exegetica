**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe who stood near the cross (19:25).
2. Students will explain what Jesus said to his mother and to the disciple (19:26-27).
3. Students will explain how Jesus honored his mother even while dying.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus thought of his mom even while dying.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus honored the command to care for parents to the very end.
- **Stretch** (disciple): a right reading of the text (Jesus caring for Mary), guarded against over-reading.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Woman, behold, your son!... Behold, your mother!" (John 19:26-27).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Who takes care of me? Who am I supposed to take care of?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:25-27 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 25 through 27.
- One-sentence setup: some of the women who loved Jesus, and the disciple John, stand near enough to the cross for Jesus to speak to them.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 25, who is standing near the cross?
   - Cold-call check: Jesus' mother Mary, her sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene (and, from verse 26, the disciple Jesus loved, John).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verses 26 and 27. Jesus is dying the most painful death there is. What does he stop to do? What does he say to his mother, and to John?
   - Guide them: he makes sure his mother will be cared for. He tells Mary, "behold your son" (meaning John will be like a son to you), and tells John, "behold your mother" (take care of her). And John takes Mary into his own home from that hour. Jesus, in agony, arranges for his mom.
3. **Core.** Think about what it takes to do that. Jesus is in unimaginable pain, carrying the weight of the whole world's sin, and he still notices his mother's face in the crowd and makes sure she's provided for. What does that show you about him?
   - Land it: it shows a love that doesn't shut down even under the worst suffering. Most of us, in a fraction of that pain, can think of nothing but ourselves. Jesus, dying, is still looking outward, still loving, still taking care of the people in front of him. His love for us never once turns inward, even on the cross.
4. **Core.** One of God's commands is "honor your father and mother." How is Jesus keeping that command, perfectly, right here at the end?
   - Draw out: he's making sure his widowed mother won't be left alone or uncared for. That's what honoring a parent looks like in practice: making provision for them. Jesus obeyed God's law perfectly his whole life, and he's still obeying it with his dying breath, seeing to his mother's future. His perfect obedience runs all the way to the cross.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Some people build big teachings about Mary out of these two verses. But read them plainly: what is actually happening here?
   - Draw out: a dying son is making sure his mother is taken care of, and handing her to a trusted friend. That's the plain sense, and it's beautiful on its own. The text gives us Jesus caring for Mary as his mother and John taking her in. We honor Mary as the mother of our Lord, and we let the verse say what it says: a son loving his mom to the end.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does Jesus do for his mother from the cross?
   - He makes sure she'll be cared for, giving her into the disciple John's keeping.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Slow down on the tenderness. In the middle of the worst agony a human being has ever endured, Jesus sees his mother's face and takes care of her. He's carrying the sin of the world, and he still has room in his heart to make sure his mom won't be alone. That's the kind of love that's dying for you.
- Land the perfect obedience. He kept God's law his whole life, including "honor your father and mother," and he kept it to his last breath, providing for Mary. That perfect obedience is part of the gift: he lived the life you couldn't, all the way to the end, so his righteousness could be yours.
- Make it practical, gently. And there's a plain example here for you. Jesus, in his hardest moment, thought of someone else and cared for them. When you're stressed or hurting, that's exactly when it's easiest to think only of yourself. Look who you can care for even then.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus loved and provided for his mother from the cross, keeping God's law perfectly and loving to the end.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus cared for someone else in his hardest moment. Who in your life needs you to think of them this week, even when you're stressed?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 19:28-30 once before Wednesday. The next lesson plants a new memory verse.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L9: Jesus' last words, "It is finished," and the new memory verse.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: Jesus caring for his mother in agony (a love that stays outward even under the worst suffering), and his perfect obedience to "honor your father and mother" to the end.
- GUARD against over-reading these two verses into Marian doctrine. Keep Calvin's plain sense: a dying son entrusting his mother's care to John. Honor Mary as the mother of the Lord, and let the text say what it says.
- Fresh-build; frame it richly.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
