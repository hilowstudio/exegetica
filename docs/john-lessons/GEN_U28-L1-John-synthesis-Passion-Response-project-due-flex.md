**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state that John's Gospel presents Jesus as the eternal Word made flesh, fully God (1:1, 1:14).
2. Students will recall the seven signs and what each showed about who Jesus is.
3. Students will explain that the signs were written so the reader would believe (20:30-31).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): John shows us who Jesus is through the things he did.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the signs aren't random miracles; each reveals the person.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the deity thread (1:1, 5:18, 8:58, 10:30, 14:9, 20:28) as the frame of the book.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is a synthesis. The students have studied all of this; they supply the material and you sort. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Note the moment: we've finished the Gospel, and now we gather the whole thing.
- Centering line on the board: "the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory" (John 1:14).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "After a whole year in John, what's the first thing that comes to mind when I hear the name Jesus?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Read the frame of the whole book back to back, so the room hears how John opens and why. Assign two readers.
  1. John 1:1-14 (the Prologue: who Jesus is)
  2. John 20:30-31 (why John wrote it all)
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 1 through 14; and chapter 20, verses 30 and 31.
- One-sentence setup: we've walked the whole Gospel; today we gather what it showed us about who Jesus is, starting where John starts.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, gather and sort)

This is synthesis. Pull the material out of the room. Do not re-teach the units. Two threads today: who John says Jesus is, and the seven signs.

### Thread A: the Word made flesh, and the deity of Christ (about 10 min)

1. **Gather (John groups).** From the Prologue you just heard, and from the whole year, what does John claim about who Jesus is? Write every answer on the board.
   - Expect: the Word, the Word was God (1:1), the Word became flesh (1:14), full of grace and truth, the light, the only Son, the one who makes the Father known.
2. **Sort: the deity thread.** All year, John kept showing Jesus claiming to be God. Where did you see it? Put the verses up.
   - Guide them to the thread: "the Word was God" (1:1); killed for "making himself equal with God" (5:18); "before Abraham was, I am" (8:58); "I and the Father are one" (10:30); "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9); and Thomas, "My Lord and my God!" (20:28).
   - The grounding hook: notice how the book opens and closes. It STARTS with "the Word was God" (1:1), and it ENDS with a disciple on his knees saying "my Lord and my God" (20:28). John frames the whole Gospel with the deity of Christ. Thomas says at the end exactly what John declared at the beginning.

### Thread B: the seven signs (about 12 min)

3. **Gather (John groups).** John chose seven signs to show who Jesus is. Name them, and for each, say what it showed. Fill the board.
   - The seven, with what each revealed:
     - Water into wine (2:1-11): the glory and the new joy Jesus brings.
     - The official's son healed from a distance (4:46-54): his word carries power anywhere.
     - The paralytic at Bethesda (5:1-15): the Son does the Father's own work.
     - Feeding the five thousand (6:1-15): he's the bread that never runs out.
     - Walking on water (6:16-21): the "I am" over the storm.
     - The man born blind (9): he's the light who gives sight.
     - Lazarus raised (11): he's the resurrection and the life.
4. **Sort: the signs point past themselves.** A sign points to something. What do all seven signs point to? Where do they land?
   - Land it: they all point to the person doing them. The signs were never about the miracle for its own sake. Each one is a window onto who Jesus is. And John tells you flat out why he wrote them: "so that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:31). Every sign was chosen to bring you to believe.

Circulate. Short probes. "Which chapter is that from?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the frame. Look at how John built this book. He doesn't ease you in. Verse 1: the Word was God. And nine months later, at the end, Thomas falls down and says the same thing: my Lord and my God. The whole Gospel is bracketed by who Jesus is. Everything in between is the proof.
- Land the signs. And the proof came in signs. Water into wine, blind eyes opened, a dead man walking out of a tomb. John picked these on purpose, each one a window onto the same person, so that when you look at what Jesus did, you'd see who he is.
- Point to the aim. And he told you why, plainly: so you would believe, and have life. That's the reason the whole book exists. Not to inform you about a fascinating man. To bring you face to face with God in the flesh, and to life.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the eternal Word became flesh and showed his glory in sign after sign, so that you would believe and live.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The book opens with 'the Word was God' and ends with 'my Lord and my God.' Where are you with that claim after a year in John?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged, never the belief itself.

## After the bell
