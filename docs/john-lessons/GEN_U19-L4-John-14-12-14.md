| Passage | John 14:12-14 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus promises his people will do "greater works" than he did, greater in reach through the Spirit, and that prayer in his name is answered for the Father's glory. |
| Christ connection | Jesus ascended to the Father, extending his work through the church by the Spirit and answering prayer for the Father's glory. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what "greater works" means (greater in reach, not bigger miracles) (14:12).
2. Students will explain "because I am going to the Father" as the reason those works are possible.
3. Students will explain what "ask in my name" means (14:13-14).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus sends his people out to do his work.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "greater works" is the church's worldwide reach, not a bigger-miracle contest.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "in my name" as praying Jesus' will for the Father's glory, guarded from magic-formula and prosperity readings.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep your sustained voice light. One clear question drives the block.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do, that the Father may be glorified in the Son" (John 14:13).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What have I been asking God for lately?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 14:12-14 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 12 through 14.
- One-sentence setup: same conversation, same night; Jesus keeps comforting them, now with two big promises.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: what "greater works" means, and what "ask in my name" means. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 12, what does Jesus say people who believe in him will do?
   - Cold-call check: the works he does, and even "greater works than these."
2. **The driving question (whole class, then small groups).** "Greater works than these." Bigger miracles than Jesus did? Read the reason in the same verse: "because I am going to the Father." What does that tell you "greater" means?
   - Guide them: not louder miracles. Greater in reach. Once Jesus goes to the Father and sends the Spirit, his followers carry the gospel to more people, in more places, across more centuries, than he reached walking around Galilee. Think of the church, worldwide, over 2,000 years. That's the "greater."
   - Sermon 24 handle: the flashy works, the bread and the healings, were signs pointing to who Jesus is. A bread factory can out-feed the five thousand. The great work is people brought to God, and the church has done that on a scale one man on foot never could.
3. **Core.** Read verses 13 and 14: "whatever you ask in my name, this I will do." Does that mean you get anything you want if you say the words "in Jesus' name"? Look at the rest of verse 13 for the goal.
   - Land it: "in my name" means asking the way Jesus would ask, for what he wants, on his authority, and the goal is stated right there: "that the Father may be glorified in the Son." It isn't a magic phrase or a wish machine. It's praying for what brings God glory.
4. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence each: what does "greater works" mean, and what does "ask in my name" mean?
   - Greater works: greater in reach, the church's worldwide gospel work through the Spirit. In my name: praying Jesus' will, for the Father's glory.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Lift the promise. Jesus is about to leave, and instead of the work ending, it's about to go global. He hands his mission to ordinary believers and sends his Spirit to power it. The greatest thing that's ever happened through people is people being brought to God, and he says his followers will do more of it than he did on foot.
- Guard the prayer promise honestly. "Ask in my name" isn't a cheat code. Tacking "in Jesus' name" onto the end of a selfish prayer doesn't bind God to it. Praying in his name means you want what he wants, and you're asking so the Father gets glory. That kind of prayer he answers.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus, gone to the Father, keeps working through his people by the Spirit and answers the prayers that seek his Father's glory.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "If praying 'in Jesus' name' means wanting what he wants, how would that change one thing you've been praying for?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 14:6. Read John 14:15-17 once before Friday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: "if you love me, you will keep my commandments," and the first promise of the Helper.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One idea, taught well: what the two big promises actually mean. Don't try to cover the whole chapter today.
- Guard both misreadings of 14:12: not a bigger-miracle contest (it's reach through the Spirit), and not name-it-claim-it (the works aim at the Father's glory, 14:13).
- Guard 14:13-14 hard against magic-formula and prosperity readings. The purpose clause is in the verse: "that the Father may be glorified in the Son."
- Derive the frame and the bread-factory illustration from sermon 24; cite it, don't read it aloud.
