# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 3 Lesson 2 of 4: Jesus clears the temple (John 2:12-17)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U3 Sign 1 & the temple |
| Lesson | 2 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Sep 23 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 2:12-17 (ESV) |
| Big idea | At Passover Jesus walks into the temple, finds a livestock market and money-changers filling the one court where outsiders could come near God, and drives it out. His disciples remember, "Zeal for your house will consume me." |
| Christ connection | Jesus loves his Father's house and the worship that happens there so much that the love will one day cost him his life. The whip is a sign of a love willing to be consumed. |
| Memory verse | Review only (20:31, 1:14, 1:12). No new plant this unit. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega 03, "Zeal for the Lord" (John 2:13-25), first part. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe what Jesus finds in the temple and what he does about it (2:14-16).
2. Students will explain why the marketplace was an offense (it filled the court where outsiders came near God, and it made worship a trade).
3. Students will explain "Zeal for your house will consume me" (2:17), especially the words "will consume me."

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus walked into the place where people came to meet God, found it turned into a flea market jamming the door, and cleared it, because his Father's house is for meeting God.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): slow down on "consume." Your zeal is not like Jesus' zeal unless it is willing to be consumed, to cost you.
- **Stretch** (disciple): read Psalm 69 in context, the righteous sufferer bearing reproach, and hear the whole psalm behind 2:17; notice the market sat in the one court a Gentile could use.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. Turn the content into questions and let students find it.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Zeal for your house will consume me" (John 2:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What is something you care about enough that it costs you? What does that tell you about what you love?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: honest is fine.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 2:12-17 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 2, verses 12 through 17.
- One-sentence setup: it is Passover, the temple is packed, and Jesus does something that looks nothing like the gentle-Jesus poster.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** What does Jesus find in the temple (verse 14), and what does he do (verses 15-16)? Read his exact words in verse 16.
   - Draw out: oxen, sheep, pigeons for sale, and money-changers. He makes a whip of cords and drives them out, and says, "Take these things away; do not make my Father's house a house of trade."
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Why is this an offense, and not just a mess? Think about whose house it is and who used that outer court.
   - Guide them: Jesus calls it "my Father's house." The market sat in the one court where non-Jews could come near God, so the trade blocked the very outsiders the temple was meant to welcome. And it turned sacrifice into a convenience purchase and skimmed a profit off pilgrims. Real worship was being crowded out and sold.
3. **Core.** The disciples remember a line from Psalm 69: "Zeal for your house will consume me." Which half of that sentence is the heavy half, and what does "consume" mean?
   - Land it: "consume" means to eat up, to devour. The heavy half is "will consume me." Jesus' zeal for true worship is the very zeal that will get him killed. This is not a man losing his temper. It is a love strong enough to be consumed.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Making a whip of cords takes time. What does that tell you about whether this is a hot-tempered outburst?
   - Draw out: he did not snap. He braided a whip, which means the zeal is deliberate, not a loss of control. Read Psalm 69 and you hear the whole song of the righteous sufferer humming behind this scene.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Why was the market an offense? What does "will consume me" point to?
   - It blocked the outsiders' court and sold worship; it points to the cross, where his zeal costs him his life.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Reframe the whip. This is not a permission slip for your anger. It is a window into a love so set on real worship that it will not leave the Father's house cheapened, and that same love walks toward a cross.
- Say the connection. Jesus cleared the stone court for one day so that the next day he could give up his own body and open the way to God forever. The whip clears a space; the cross opens the door.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus' zeal for true worship burns all the way to Calvary, and it is love, not temper.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus cared about real worship enough to be consumed by it. Where has your worship of God become a convenience or a habit you barely notice?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 2:18-22 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12.
- Sets up L3: the leaders demand a sign, and Jesus answers, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The load-bearing ruling is "will consume me" (Psalm 69:9 is the source). Teach the zeal, and guard hard against the tantrum reading. Use the detail that braiding a whip takes time, long enough to cool down, so the act is deliberate love, not a fit.
- The Gentile-court detail is good news for a mixed-faith room: Jesus is fighting for the outsider's access to God.
- Do not turn the hour into a harmonization seminar on whether John's cleansing is the same event as the Synoptics'. One clean beat: John arranges by theology. Whether a similar act happened twice is a fair question Scripture does not settle, and the point does not hang on it.
- Grounding for your own prep: Psalm 69 is the psalm of the righteous sufferer, an alien to his own brothers, bearing reproach for God; the whole psalm stands behind 2:17.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
