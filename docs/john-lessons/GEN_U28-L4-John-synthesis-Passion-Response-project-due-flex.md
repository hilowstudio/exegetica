**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain that the sixteen anchors together tell the whole story of Jesus in John.
2. Students will state the response the Gospel asks (believe and have life, 20:31; love and follow, 21:17).
3. Students will articulate, in their own words, who Jesus is after a year in John.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the whole year comes down to one person and one question.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the Gospel was written for a response, not just information.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the sixteen anchors as one connected portrait of Christ.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, and the last one. Keep your voice light and warm. Let the students speak, and land the whole year gently on Christ and his question. No pressure to profess.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Note it: this is our last class in John.
- Centering line on the board: "Simon, son of John, do you love me?" (John 21:17).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "A year ago, who was Jesus to me? Who is he now?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have students read the last two verses of the frame: John 20:31 (why the book was written) and John 21:17 (the risen Lord's question and charge).
- Navigation help: John, chapter 20, verse 31; and chapter 21, verse 17.
- One-sentence setup: on the last day, we gather the whole Christ and hear his question.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 18 min, the governed block, gather and land)

Single focus: the whole Christ, in the sixteen anchors, and his question.

1. **The sixteen as one portrait (whole class).** You've carried sixteen memory verses all year. Read them in order, out loud, as a class, and listen for the story they tell:
   - 20:31 (why the book exists), 1:14 (the Word became flesh), 1:12 (children of God), 3:16 (God so loved), 4:24 (worship in spirit and truth), 6:35 (bread), 8:12 (light), 10:11 (good shepherd), 11:25 (resurrection and life), 13:34-35 (love one another), 14:6 (the way), 15:5 (the vine), 16:33 (I have overcome the world), 17:3 (eternal life is knowing him), 19:30 (It is finished), 21:17 (feed my sheep).
2. **The one question that turns the list into a portrait.** Look at all sixteen. Are these sixteen separate sayings, or something else?
   - Land it: they're not sixteen sayings. They're one Christ, seen sixteen times. Read them in order and you get the whole story: God became flesh, loved the world, gave himself as bread and light and shepherd and life and way and vine, overcame the world, finished the work, and rose to restore and send. Sixteen verses, one person, the whole Gospel.
3. **The response (whole class).** John told you, back in 20:31, why he wrote all of this. What did he say the two goals were?
   - Draw out: that you would believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that by believing you would have life in his name. Believe, and have life. That's the whole point of the book.
4. **The last word (whole class).** And how does the book actually end? What are the risen Lord's last words to Peter, and the last words Jesus speaks in the whole Gospel (21:17, 21:19)?
   - Land it: "do you love me... feed my sheep," and "follow me." John ends the whole story of Jesus with a question and a call. After showing you everything about who Jesus is, the last thing he does is turn to a failure and ask, "do you love me," and say, "follow me." That's the response the whole book was written for. And the question comes to you, too.
5. **The final key question (each student, in writing).** You've watched this one Jesus for nine months. Who is he, and what is he asking of you? (This flows into Move 5.)

Circulate warmly. This is the last one; keep it personal and unhurried.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Close the whole year. Warm, hopeful, no pressure.

- Gather the year. Think about where we started, in September: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was God." And where we've come: a beach at dawn, the risen Lord cooking breakfast for the friend who denied him, asking "do you love me." In between, you watched God become a man, turn water to wine, open blind eyes, raise the dead, wash feet, die on a cross, and walk out of a grave. One person, the whole way through.
- Name the aim. And John told you why he showed you all of it: so that you would believe Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and have life in his name. That's the only reason this book exists. Not to make you experts. To bring you to Jesus, and to life.
- Ask his question, and leave them free. So here, on the last day, I'll ask you the question the whole book has been asking, the same one the risen Jesus asked Peter by the fire: do you love me. You don't have to answer out loud. You don't have to answer today. But hear it, because he's really asking. And whatever your answer is, know this: the door is still open (1:12), the invitation still stands, and the Lord who made breakfast for the friend who failed him is not done with anyone in this room.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the whole Christ of this Gospel, God in the flesh, crucified, risen, asks you the one question that holds everything: do you love me? Follow me.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "After a year in John, who is Jesus to you, and what would it look like to follow him?"
- Give it quiet, and let it be reflective. This is the last one. Credit honesty; never score the belief. Close with a short blessing over the class (opt-in).

## After the bell

- Homework: none. You've finished the Gospel of John.
- To the gradebook: today's participation, and the completed memory set of sixteen.
- What's next: any flex or graduation day, and a whole life of following the Jesus of this book.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus, and the FINAL teaching day of the whole course. Keep it warm, light, and unhurried. Let the students speak.
- The two capstone moves: the sixteen anchors read as one portrait of Christ (one person seen sixteen times, from 1:14 to 21:17), and the response the Gospel asks (believe and have life, 20:31; love and follow, 21:17).
- KEEP FAITH INVITATIONAL. This is the last impression. Set the whole Christ before the room, ask his question warmly, and leave every student free to answer in their own voice, on their own time. No pressure to profess. A room shown a beautiful Christ and left free remembers him.
- Land on the PERSON and his question, warm and hopeful, the tone of the beach at dawn (Unit 27). A warm word for the searching student: the invitation stands, the door of 1:12 is open, and the Spirit does what no lesson can.
- Review-only memory; the set of sixteen is complete.
