# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 1 Lesson 2 of 4: the Word, God and the maker of everything (John 1:1-5)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U1 The Word |
| Lesson | 2 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Fri Sep 11 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 1:1-5 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The one we are about to spend nine months reading about already was in the beginning, is himself God, made everything there is, and his light is already shining in the dark and winning. |
| Christ connection | Jesus is the eternal Word, God with God and God by nature, the maker of all things, and the light no darkness can put out. |
| Memory verse | Hold John 1:14, planted in L4. Preview it by pointing from verse 1 to verse 14: the one who "was God" (1:1) is the one who "became flesh" (1:14). Keep 20:31 warm. |
| Resources | No paired sermon covers 1:1-5 (sermon 01 pairs with L4). Have a class Bible open to John 1. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state from verse 1 the three things John says about the Word (he already existed, he was with God, he was God).
2. Students will explain from verse 3 why the Word cannot be "a god" or a created being.
3. Students will restate verse 5 in plain words and say what it means for a world that feels dark.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the Word is God speaking, and the speech turned out to be a person; the light in the darkness is a lamp in a blackout the dark cannot switch off.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): make verse 1 strange again by slowing it down, and take the "a god" question as a live thing a neighbor might ask at the door.
- **Stretch** (disciple): work the grammar of verse 1 and the logic of verse 3, and assemble the deity argument yourself, answering the "a god" objection straight from the text.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. Turn the content into questions and let students find it.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it" (John 1:5).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "The light is shining now, in a world that is genuinely dark, and John says the darkness has not overcome it. Where do you most want that to be true?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest about the dark.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 1:1-5 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 1 through 5.
- One-sentence setup: verse 1 makes three claims about the Word, in order. We take them one at a time.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 1, John says three things about the Word. Read them and list them in order.
   - Draw out: "in the beginning was the Word" (he already existed when the beginning began, so he was never made); "the Word was with God" (he is a distinct person, face to face with the Father); "the Word was God" (he is himself fully God).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Some people read verse 1 as "the Word was a god," a lesser being. Look at verse 3: how much was made through him? Quote the words. Can the one who made all created things be a created thing himself?
   - Guide them: "all things were made through him, and without him was not any thing made that was made." The maker of everything that was made is not one of the things that was made. He stands on the Creator's side of the line. That one verse settles the question.
3. **Core.** Give the "a god" objection one more angle, still from the text. If verse 1 alone left any doubt, how does John close his own frame? Look at verse 18 and jump ahead to 20:28.
   - Land it: verse 18 calls him "the only God, who is at the Father's side," and at the end Thomas says to Jesus, "My Lord and my God" (20:28). One clause at the start cannot outvote the book it opens. "The Word was God" describes his nature, the way "the wall is brick" tells you what the wall is made of.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 5 says the light shines in the darkness "and the darkness has not overcome it." The word behind "overcome" can mean both to put out and to grasp. Hold both. What does each say?
   - Draw out: the darkness could not snuff the light out, and it could not make sense of it either. Verses 10 and 11 show the second half coming true, when the world and his own did not know him. The light shines now, present tense, and the outcome is already settled.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What are the three things verse 1 says about the Word? Why does verse 3 rule out "a god"?
   - He existed before the beginning, he was with God, he was God; and the maker of all created things cannot be a created thing.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Say who this is. The person you are about to watch get tired, and weep, and bleed, and die, is the person who spoke the stars into place. He is not a bright creature. He is God.
- Say the hope of verse 5 out loud. He is the light, and John says the darkness has not overcome it, present tense, in a world you already know is dark. The light is not hoping to win. It is winning, and it has a name.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is God the Word, the maker of everything, and the light no darkness can put out.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it.' Is that easy or hard for you to believe right now? Write honestly, either way."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 1:6-13 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry.
- Sets up L3: a witness named John points to the light, the world turns him away, and those who receive him are born of God.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The deity ruling is the load-bearing point. Rule "the Word was God" as full, eternal, ontological deity, and answer the "a god" reading in three text-based moves: the grammar told as description, verse 3 (the maker is not a creature, the argument Irenaeus used against the Gnostics), and verse 18 with 20:28 closing the frame. Keep the confession that the Son is "very and eternal God, of one substance with the Father" in your background, off the student handout.
- One grammar note is the whole ration. Do not drown a fifteen-year-old in Greek.
- Teach the "a god" answer as warm, confident clarity for a friend at the door, never as a debate-club win.
- Guard the floor. If a searching student cannot say one plain sentence about who Jesus is by the bell, circle back before you close.
- Grounding for your own prep: the word behind "overcome" in verse 5 is katalambano, holding "put out" and "comprehend" together; John uses the same word at 12:35. "In the beginning" (1:1) deliberately echoes Genesis 1:1, the Word who made the first world.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
