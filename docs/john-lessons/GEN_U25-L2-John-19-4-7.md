**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "Behold the man!" and Pilate's intent (19:5).
2. Students will explain Pilate's repeated verdict of no guilt (19:4, 6).
3. Students will explain the charge in 19:7 and why it is true.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Pilate kept saying Jesus was innocent, and still sent him to die.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus is condemned for telling the truth about who he is.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "Behold the man" as unwitting testimony to the last Adam.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. Hold the room in reverence. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Behold the man!" (John 19:5).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When have I known the right thing and still done the wrong thing anyway?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:4-7 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 4 through 7.
- One-sentence setup: Pilate brings the bleeding Jesus out to the crowd, hoping they'll settle for this instead of death.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it. (Anti-Judaism guard active, see teacher notes.)

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 5, what does Pilate say as he brings Jesus out? What's he trying to get the crowd to do?
   - Cold-call check: "Behold the man!" He's trying to get them to pity Jesus and back off, hoping the beating is enough.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Count how many times in this passage Pilate says he finds no guilt in Jesus (verses 4 and 6). What does it tell you that the judge himself keeps declaring the accused innocent, and then hands him over anyway?
   - Guide them: it tells you Jesus is completely innocent, and everyone knows it, including the judge. This is not justice failing to find evidence. It's injustice steamrolling the evidence. Pilate has no case, says so repeatedly, and caves to the pressure. Jesus dies as a man the court itself declared not guilty.
3. **Core.** Read verse 7. What reason does the crowd finally give for why Jesus has to die?
   - Land it: "he has made himself the Son of God." They want him executed for claiming to be God's Son. Here's the thing: that claim is true. He IS the Son of God. So he's being condemned to death for telling the truth about who he is. The one real "crime" they can name is that he's God.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Pilate says "Behold the man" to shame Jesus, to say "look at this pitiful figure." But John wants you to hear it deeper. Who is "the man" Pilate is pointing at, really?
   - Draw out: the true Man, the second Adam. Where the first Adam sinned and brought the curse, this Man takes the curse and undoes it. Pilate points at a beaten prisoner and says "behold the man," and he's unknowingly pointing at the one true human being, the one who does what Adam failed to do. Pilate keeps saying more than he knows.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what is the "crime" Jesus is condemned for in verse 7, and is it true?
   - That he made himself the Son of God, which is true. He's condemned for telling the truth about who he is.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Land the innocence. Listen to the judge. "I find no guilt in him." Again. "No guilt in him." The man with the power to free Jesus says over and over that he's innocent, and sends him to the cross anyway. Jesus doesn't die because he did something wrong. He dies with a clean record, which is exactly what makes his death able to count for you.
- Land the real "crime." And what finally sticks? "He made himself the Son of God." He's killed for telling the truth about who he is. The world looked at God in the flesh and called it blasphemy.
- Turn "behold the man" around. Pilate meant "look at this broken nobody." But look again. That's the true Man, the second Adam, standing where you should stand, taking what you had coming. Behold the man who saves you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the innocent Son of God, condemned for being exactly who he is, so that the guilty could go free.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus was declared innocent and condemned anyway, in your place. What does his innocence make possible for you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 19:8-11 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: Pilate brags about his power, and Jesus tells him where it really comes from.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: the innocence motif (Pilate's repeated no-guilt verdict, the spotless one), and the true charge of 19:7 (condemned for the truth that he's the Son of God). For the disciple tier, "Behold the man" as unwitting testimony to the last Adam (Augustine, Cyril).
- ANTI-JUDAISM GUARD: "the Jews"/the crowd here is the specific hostile leaders. Keep the guilt on human sin and God's plan, never a people.
- Derive the frame from sermon 35; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
