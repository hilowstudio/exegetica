# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 2 Lesson 1 of 4: the witness who points away from himself (John 1:19-28)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U2 Witness & first disciples |
| Lesson | 1 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Sep 16 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 1:19-28 (ESV) |
| Big idea | The priests ask "Who are you?" and John keeps answering who he is not, so that a face nobody in the crowd recognizes yet becomes the whole point. |
| Christ connection | The Messiah is already standing in the crowd, unknown (1:26). The Baptist exists to turn heads toward him. |
| Memory verse | Preview John 1:12 only, planted in L3. Roll 1:14 in review: the Word made flesh is the very one now standing unknown among them. |
| Resources | No paired sermon covers 1:19-28; fresh-build from this framing. Have a class Bible open to John 1. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will list the three things John says he is not (the Christ, Elijah, the Prophet) and the one thing he claims to be (the voice of Isaiah 40:3).
2. Students will explain what "among you stands one you do not know" (1:26) means and connect it to the Gospel's purpose (20:31).
3. Students will describe the witness's posture: say what you saw, and point away from yourself to Jesus.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): John kept pointing away from himself to someone greater; leave able to say that in one sentence and find John 1:19 in the book.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the priests had the whole Bible and still missed the Messiah standing ten feet away; familiarity is how you hold Scripture and do not see him.
- **Stretch** (disciple): hear the fourfold "I am not" as the deliberate floor under Jesus' "I am" sayings later in the book, and notice that "I am the voice" applies the LORD's own highway text (Isaiah 40:3-5) to Jesus.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the least Christ-dense scene on its face, so play the drama and land it on Christ.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Among you stands one you do not know" (John 1:26).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where might God be present in your life in a way you have not noticed?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: it is okay to write that you are not sure he is there.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 1:19-28 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 19 through 28.
- One-sentence setup: the officials come to interrogate John the Baptist, and he keeps refusing to be the story.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** The priests ask "Who are you?" What three things does John say he is not (verses 20-21)? What one thing does he claim to be (verse 23)?
   - Draw out: not the Christ, not Elijah, not the Prophet. He is "the voice of one crying out in the wilderness, 'Make straight the way of the Lord'" (Isaiah 40:3). A voice carries a word and then fades; the word it carries is what matters.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Notice how many times John says "I am not." Why would a man work this hard to say he is not the important one?
   - Guide them: his whole job is to point away from himself to someone greater. He even says he is "not worthy to untie" the strap of that person's sandal, the task of a slave. The witness makes himself small so the one he points to fills the frame.
3. **Core.** Read verse 26 and 27. What does John say about the one the crowd is missing?
   - Land it: "among you stands one you do not know." The Messiah is already there, in the crowd, and the experts standing right in front of him do not recognize him. That is the Prologue coming true: "his own people did not receive him" (1:11).
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** John says "I am not" over and over. Hold that. Starting in chapter 6, Jesus will say "I am" over and over. What is John setting up?
   - Draw out: the witness says "I am not" so the Word can say "I am." John empties the frame so Jesus can fill it. That contrast runs all year.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What three things is John not? Who is standing in the crowd unrecognized?
   - Not the Christ, Elijah, or the Prophet; the Messiah himself.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Reduce the Baptist to one sentence. His whole life says, "He is here. Look at him." That is the posture this class asks of you and of me: point at Christ and get out of the way.
- Name the danger. Christ can be fully present and go unrecognized, even by people who know the Bible cold. The first motion of faith is simply to let a witness turn your head.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the one standing among them, unknown, is the Lamb of God they meet tomorrow.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The experts missed the Messiah standing right in front of them. What might you be missing about Jesus because he has become too familiar, or because you have not looked?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 1:29-34 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31 and 1:14.
- Sets up L2: John sees Jesus coming and says, "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world."

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Play the drama, or four denials read like a dull interview. A man refusing the spotlight so he can aim it at someone greater is the scene.
- Land the lesson on 1:26-27, on Christ present and unrecognized, so it does not end as a lesson about John.
- The disciple-tier spine is the "I am not" (ouk eimi) that sets up Jesus' "I am" (ego eimi) sayings from chapter 6 on. "I am the voice" applies Isaiah 40:3-5, comfort spoken to exiles, the highway of the LORD, to Jesus, an early deity note.
- Do not over-teach the Deuteronomy 18 "Prophet" expectation. One sentence, then move.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
