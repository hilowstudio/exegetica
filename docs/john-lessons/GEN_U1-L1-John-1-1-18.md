# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 1 Lesson 1 of 4: the whole doorway (John 1:1-18)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U1 The Word |
| Lesson | 1 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Sep 10 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus |
| Passage | John 1:1-18 (ESV), read whole as an orientation day |
| Big idea | Before Jesus does one thing, John tells you who he is, so read the whole doorway once and hear the shape: God became a man you could see, and his name is Jesus. |
| Christ connection | The Prologue names the main character of the next nine months. Every sign, every "I am," and the cross all belong to this one person, the eternal Word made flesh. |
| Memory verse | Hold John 1:14, the year's anchor, planted in L4. Read it aloud today and name it as the anchor; do not drill yet. Keep 20:31 warm as the course's aim, "that you may believe... and have life in his name." |
| Resources | No paired sermon for the whole-Prologue read (sermon 01 pairs with L4). Have class ESV Bibles with a table of contents for the find-the-passage drill. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will find John 1 in a physical Bible using the table of contents and read verses 1-18 aloud.
2. Students will find and list at least four names or titles John gives the main character in these verses.
3. Students will state in one plain sentence who John says Jesus is.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): physically find John 1 today, and leave able to say the one sentence: God became a man you could see, and his name is Jesus.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): hear familiar words fresh by reading the whole poem in one sweep, and see the Prologue as the map of the whole year.
- **Stretch** (disciple): mark where light, glory, and new birth first appear, and predict where each pays off later (8:12; 12:23; 3:3); hear the arc from eternity (1:1) to flesh (1:14) to the Son who makes the Father known (1:18).

---

**Talk budget:** short Thursday, and it is an orientation read, so keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 36 minutes. This is a single wide read, not verse-by-verse. Resist the urge to exposit; L2 through L4 do that, and their payoff depends on today staying a sweep.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5-6 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you, on day one of the unit. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "We start a whole year in one book today. What do you hope for, or wonder about? If you are not sure God is even there, you can write that too."
- On-ramp for the doubter: honest is welcome here.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (5 min)

- Help everyone find John first: the fourth book of the New Testament, after Matthew, Mark, and Luke. Big number is the chapter, small number is the verse. No one leaves lost in the Bible today.
- Have students read John 1:1-18 aloud, a few verses each, slowly, straight through. Do not stop to figure out every line. Listen for the shape.
- One-sentence setup: think of this as the doorway into the whole book. Before Jesus does anything, John tells you who he is.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 18 min, the governed block, no verse-by-verse yet)

Two tasks, both done from the text. Let students find it.

1. **Find the titles (any student can answer).** Read back through and underline every name or title given to the main character. Put them on the board as students call them out.
   - Draw them out: the Word (1:1), God (1:1), life (1:4), light (1:4-5), the true light (1:9), the only Son (1:14), Jesus Christ (1:17), the only God at the Father's side (1:18). Count them. Ask: what does it tell you that John stacks up this many before Jesus says a word?
2. **Hear the shape (small groups, note it in the Reading Journal).** The poem climbs. Where does it start, and where does it land? Have groups trace the movement.
   - Guide them: it starts in eternity, "in the beginning" (1:1), and climbs to a peak in verse 14, "the Word became flesh." It ends on the Son who makes the invisible God known (1:18). The whole thing moves from far away and long ago to a face you could see.
3. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** John opens with "In the beginning," the exact first words of Genesis 1:1. Hold that. Why might John want you to hear Genesis while you read about Jesus? We open it up next class.
4. **Cold-call comprehension check.** Where do we find John in the Bible? Name two titles for the main character. In one sentence, who does John say Jesus is?
   - Any student can answer: John, fourth book of the NT; the Word, the light (or others); God who became a man, and his name is Jesus.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and set the year.

- Say it plainly. You are about to spend a whole year watching one man. John wrote these eighteen verses to make sure that, before Jesus lifts a finger, you know who the man is: God, from the beginning, who came close enough to be seen.
- Name the wonder. Everything you will watch him do, the water into wine, the blind man seeing, the cross, you already know who is doing it. That is what a doorway is for.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the eternal Word made flesh is the one whose story we are about to read, so walk through the doorway and meet him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Out of everything John says about Jesus in these verses, what is one thing you want to understand better this year?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 1:1-5 once before next class. That is the whole assignment.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. No content graded on the orientation day.
- Sets up L2: we slow down on the first five verses, "the Word was God," and the light the darkness cannot put out.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: orientation day. This is a single wide read plus two find-it tasks, not exposition. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Protect the floor. If a student new to the Bible cannot find John 1 or say one plain sentence about Jesus by the bell, the day missed. Test for it in the cold-call.
- Hold the memory verse. 1:14 is the year's anchor; read it aloud and name it, but do not drill until L4. Keep 20:31 warm as the course's aim.
- Plant the Genesis echo lightly (the "in the beginning" of 1:1) and leave it; L2 opens it up. Do not exposit 1:1 today.
- Read it like news. The Prologue is the most dramatic claim in the book, so do not let a quiet reverence flatten it.
- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus.
