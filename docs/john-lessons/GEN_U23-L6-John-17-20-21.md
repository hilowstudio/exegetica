**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain who else Jesus prays for in 17:20 (all future believers).
2. Students will explain "that they may all be one" and its model (the Father and Son, 17:21).
3. Students will explain the purpose of the unity: so the world may believe (17:21).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus was praying for future Christians, including us.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Christian unity is meant to make the world believe.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the unity modeled on the Father-Son oneness (real, not just agreement).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "That they may all be one... so that the world may believe" (John 17:21).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Have I ever felt part of something bigger than myself? What was it like?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write it honestly.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 17:20-21 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 17, verses 20 through 21.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus lifts his eyes past the eleven men in the room and prays for everyone who will ever believe in him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 20, who does Jesus say he's praying for, besides the disciples in the room?
   - Cold-call check: "those who will believe in me through their word," future believers.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Think hard about verse 20. "Those who will believe in me through their word." The disciples told others, who told others, for 2,000 years, until it reached you. So who, sitting in that room the night before he died, was Jesus praying for?
   - Guide them: us. You. Jesus looked past the eleven and saw every Christian across two thousand years, including the students in this room, and prayed for them by name in his heart. The message reached you through a long chain of people passing it on, many of whom died for it. You were in this prayer.
3. **Core.** Read verse 21. What does Jesus ask for all these believers? What's the model, the "just as"?
   - Land it: "that they may all be one, just as you, Father, are in me, and I in you." Christian unity, modeled on the oneness of the Father and the Son. That's a deep, real oneness, because Jesus lives in his people and the Father lives in Jesus. It's more than a club of people who happen to agree.
4. **Core.** Still verse 21: WHY does Jesus want his people to be one? What's the goal at the end of the verse?
   - Draw out: "so that the world may believe that you have sent me." Christian unity is evidence to the watching world. When Christians actually love each other and are one, the world sees something real and believes. When they tear each other apart, the world sees a reason to walk away.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** This unity isn't everyone-agrees-on-everything, and it isn't papering over the gospel to get along. It's also not an excuse to write off other Christians. Where's the line? What holds real Christians together, and what's worth dividing over?
   - Draw out: real Christians are already one because the same Spirit lives in all who truly trust Jesus. The command is to live like it, working toward each other instead of avoiding conflict. What's worth dividing over is a false gospel or unrepented sin, the things that actually attack what saves. Getting along by pretending the gospel doesn't matter is a fake unity. Writing off true believers over secondary things is a fake division.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why does Jesus want his people to be one, according to verse 21?
   - So that the world may believe that the Father sent him.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Put them in the prayer. Read verse 20 slowly and let it land: "those who will believe in me through their word." That's you. The night before he died, in his last long prayer, Jesus was praying for the kids in this room. The gospel traveled two thousand years and crossed an ocean and walked into this classroom because he prayed and his people carried it, some of them to their deaths. You were on his mind.
- Land the unity and its point. And what did he pray for you? That you'd be one with every other believer, the way he and the Father are one, so a watching world would believe when it sees Christians actually love each other. The point of the unity is the world seeing Jesus in it. Your unity preaches.
- Make it honest. This isn't fake niceness that pretends nothing matters, and it isn't looking down on other Christians. It's real love for the people Jesus died for, worth fighting toward even when it's hard.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus prayed for you by name across two thousand years, and prayed that his people would be one so the world would believe.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus was praying for you the night before he died (verse 20). Sit with that, then write: what does it change to know you were on his mind?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 17:3. Read John 17:22-23 once before Tuesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L7: the glory Jesus shares with his people, and the unity that shows the world God's love.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The emotional payoff: 17:20 names the students (Jesus prayed for future believers). Make it vivid and personal (Adam's sermon 31: Jesus looking past the eleven to believers 2,000 years out). This is the biggest "land it on the student" moment in the unit.
- The unity (17:21): real, Spirit-wrought oneness modeled on the Father-Son oneness, aimed at the world's belief. Guard BOTH errors: false unity that papers over the gospel, and sectarian dismissal of true Christian unity. Divide only over a false gospel or unrepented sin.
- Derive the frame from sermon 31; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
