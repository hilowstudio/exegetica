# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 8 Lesson 2 of 5: the claim that got him killed (John 5:10-18)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U8 Sign 3 and the Son's authority |
| Lesson | 2 of 5 |
| Date / Day type | Wed Oct 21 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 5:10-18 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Jesus heals on the Sabbath on purpose, and when they come after him he claims God's own work as his own, making himself equal with God. |
| Christ connection | Jesus calls God his own Father and does the Father's work, the claim that forces a verdict about who he is. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why healing on the Sabbath drew persecution (5:16).
2. Students will explain what "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (5:17) claims, and why 5:18 calls it equality with God.
3. Students will explain what the "something worse" in 5:14 is, and what it is not.
4. Students will describe how the passage treats the healed man (grace, no faith-credentials).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a fight breaks out over a healed man carrying his mat, and it turns into a fight over who Jesus is.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the leaders guarded God's law carefully and missed God standing in front of them.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "equal with God" (isos, the Philippians 2:6 word); the Sabbath-working argument the leaders were making.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "My Father is working until now, and I am working" (John 5:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I keep rules carefully and maybe miss the point of them?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest question about rules and God.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 5:10-18 aloud, one reads 10 through 14, the other 15 through 18.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 5, verses 10 through 18.
- One-sentence setup: the man Jesus healed gets in trouble for carrying his mat on the Sabbath, and the fight quickly becomes about Jesus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 16, why are the leaders going after Jesus? What's their complaint?
   - Cold-call check: he was healing on the Sabbath. In their system, that broke the day of rest.
2. **Entry.** When have you seen someone follow the rule so closely they missed the whole point of it? Name it.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 17. Jesus says, "My Father is working until now, and I am working." Then read verse 18. What two things does John say made them want to kill him?
   - Guide them: he was healing on the Sabbath, and he was "calling God his own Father, making himself equal with God." The claim is bigger than breaking a rule. He's putting himself in God's place.
4. **Core.** The leaders believed God rested on the seventh day, yet God clearly keeps the world running every Sabbath (the sun comes up, the rain falls, hearts beat). So they figured God works on the Sabbath, and only God may. What is Jesus claiming when he takes the Sabbath as his own working day?
   - Land it: he's claiming to do what only God does. That's why verse 18 reads it as making himself "equal with God." They understood him correctly.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Go back to verse 14. Jesus tells the healed man, "Sin no more, that nothing worse may happen to you." Does that mean the man's thirty-eight years of sickness were punishment for his sin? Look at John 9:3 for help.
   - Draw out: no. In John 9:3 Jesus flatly rejects "he was sick because he or his parents sinned." The "something worse" than sickness is standing before God unforgiven. Suffering came into the world through sin in general, and this man's illness was not a scoreboard of his private guilt.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what did the leaders correctly understand Jesus to be claiming in verse 17?
   - That he does God's own work, making himself equal with God.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. This is where sermon 06 opens.

- Name the claim plainly. Jesus treats the healing as his Father's own work and does it right alongside him. The leaders heard that correctly, and it's why they wanted him dead. He was taking the place only God holds.
- Speak to the churched student. These were the careful ones, the rule-keepers, the Bible people. They guarded God's law with everything they had and missed God standing in the room. It's possible to be religious and precise and completely miss Jesus.
- Handle the sickness question with care. Don't let anyone leave thinking their illness is God's punishment. The "something worse" than a sick body is an unforgiven soul, and Jesus came to deal with exactly that.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus calls God his own Father and does the Father's work, and that claim leaves you no safe middle. You either want him gone or you worship him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The leaders knew the rules cold and missed Jesus completely. Where might you be doing the religious things and missing him? Write honestly."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 5:19-23 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L3: Jesus is about to unpack that claim, that the Son does exactly what the Father does.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Build on sermon 06's opening: the Sabbath-working argument and the "equal with God" claim (5:17-18). The word for "equal" is the same one Paul uses in Philippians 2:6.
- Guard 5:14 hard. The "something worse" is judgment. It doesn't mean a second illness. Use 9:3 to block the "your sickness is your sin" reading. This matters for any student carrying illness in their family.
- Handle the man's character with restraint. He never asks, doesn't know Jesus, and reports him to the leaders (5:15). John doesn't tell us his motive, so we don't either. The grace-first point stands: Jesus healed a man with nothing to offer.
- Don't turn the Sabbath fight into "rules are bad." The leaders were trying to honor God. Their problem was missing the Lord of the Sabbath in front of them.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
