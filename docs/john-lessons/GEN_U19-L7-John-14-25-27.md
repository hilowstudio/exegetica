**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the two things the Helper will do (14:26).
2. Students will explain how the Spirit's reminding grounds the apostles' witness.
3. Students will contrast Jesus' peace with the peace the world gives (14:27).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus gives his friends a peace the world can't hand out.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): 14:27 circles back to 14:1, the chapter's comfort bookend.
- **Stretch** (disciple): how 14:26 grounds why we can trust the Gospels.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Peace I leave with you; my peace I give to you" (John 14:27).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where do I go looking for peace? Does it last?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest about what doesn't actually settle you.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:25-27 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 25 through 27.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus is wrapping up this part of the talk, promising the Spirit's help and leaving them peace.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 26, who is the Helper, and who sends him?
   - Cold-call check: the Holy Spirit, whom the Father sends in Jesus' name.
2. **Entry.** Still in verse 26, what two things will the Helper do?
   - Draw out: teach them all things, and bring to their remembrance all that Jesus said.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus is talking to the eleven apostles, the men who will carry his message after he's gone. If the Spirit reminds them of everything Jesus said, what does that mean for the Gospels we read, like the book of John you're holding?
   - Guide them: their memory of Jesus wasn't left to fade or get fuzzy. The Spirit secured it. So the words of Jesus in the Gospels aren't a rough guess written down decades later. They're a Spirit-kept record. That's part of why we can trust them.
4. **Core.** Read verse 27. Jesus leaves them "peace," but says "not as the world gives." How is the peace the world gives different from the peace Jesus gives?
   - Land it: the world's peace depends on things going well, no problems, enough money, no threats. It disappears the moment life gets hard. Jesus gives peace to men who are about to watch him die. It's his own peace, and it holds even when everything's falling apart, because it rests on him and not on circumstances.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Look back at verse 1 of this chapter: "Let not your hearts be troubled." Now read the last line of verse 27: "Let not your hearts be troubled, neither let them be afraid." What has Jesus done by opening and closing on the same words?
   - Draw out: he's framed the whole chapter with comfort. Everything in between, the way, the Father, the Spirit, is meant to steady a troubled heart. The chapter is one long act of pastoral care, comfort to comfort.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what kind of peace does Jesus give, and how is it different from the world's?
   - His own peace, which holds in trouble, unlike the world's peace, which depends on things going well.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name what the Spirit guards. Jesus is about to be gone, and he promises the Spirit will keep his words alive and exact in the apostles. The reason you can open this Gospel and hear Jesus is that the Spirit made sure his words weren't lost. God protected the record of his own Son.
- Hand them the peace. The world's peace is a truce that lasts until the next bad news. Jesus gives his own peace to men whose world is about to cave in. It doesn't wait for calm weather. It holds in the storm, because it's anchored to him.
- Close the circle. He started the chapter saying "let not your hearts be troubled," and he ends it the same way. Everything he said in between was aimed at that one troubled heart. That's how he loves.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus keeps his words alive by the Spirit and leaves his people a peace the world can't take away.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus offers a peace that holds even when life falls apart. Where do you most need that kind of peace right now?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 14:6. Read John 14:28-31 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L8: the hard verse, "the Father is greater than I," and Jesus walking willingly toward the cross.
