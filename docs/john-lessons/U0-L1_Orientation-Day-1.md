# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 0 Lesson 1 of 2: entering John, day 1 (John 20:30-31)

| Passage | John 20:30-31 (ESV), the book's own purpose statement |
| Big idea | John wrote this Gospel for one reason: that you may believe Jesus is the Christ and have life in his name. That sentence governs our whole year. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state in their own words why John wrote (20:31).
2. Students will find the Gospel of John in their Bible.
3. Students will name that the book has two halves, the Signs and the Glory.
4. Students will name the daily rhythm of this class.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): no prior knowledge assumed; we physically find John together, and no one is put on the spot to know anything.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): what a "Gospel" is, and why there are four.
- **Stretch** (disciple): John's stated purpose as the interpretive key to everything that follows, and the word "believe" as a thread to watch all year.

---

**Talk budget:** first day earns a little more of your voice in the Land, but hold the working block to questions. Sustained teacher talk under about 20 minutes total, and no block past 8 to 10.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

Day 1 you host and model it fully, so the room learns the rhythm before any student leads.

- Settle (1 min): "Every day starts here, with seven quiet minutes before God, because time with him is meant to be simple." Read John 20:31 aloud once, slowly.
- Journal or sit (5 min): prompt, written in the Quiet-Time Journal: "A whole year in one book of the Bible. What do you hope for, wonder about, or worry about as we start? Be honest. If you are not sure God is even there, you can write that too." Phones away.
- Gather (1 min): no sharing today. Just close the journals. The point is the practice, not a performance.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (4 min)

- Help everyone find John first (the fourth book of the New Testament; give the page number on the board). This is the move that keeps the searching student from feeling lost on day one.
- A student reads John 20:30-31 aloud, slowly. Everyone has the actual sentence in front of them.

## Move 3: Work the text (22 min, questions, not lecture)

Seat and name the John groups (3 to 4, mixed) as you go; this is their first think-pair-share.

- Entry Q (all): "Why would someone write a whole book about one single person?" Turn-and-talk 60 seconds, then a few voices.
- Core Q (from 20:31, in groups): "John tells us exactly why he wrote. In his own words, what does he want to happen inside a person who reads this book?" Groups find the two things in the verse (believe; have life). One observation goes in the Reading Journal.
- Frame the year (brief, your voice, under 5 min): the book has two halves, the Book of Signs (chapters 1-12, Jesus shown through seven signs) and the Book of Glory (13-21, the upper room, the cross, the resurrection). Give the three questions we will ask of every passage: who is Jesus? what is believing? what does it ask of me?
- Deeper Q (D): "Watch the word believe this year. Do you think John means agreeing that something is true, or something more than that? Hold the question; we will keep testing it."
- Cold-call comprehension checks (named): where do we find John? What are the two halves called? Why did John write?

## Move 4: Land and minister (6 min, your protected pulpit)

Your first-day landing casts the whole year. Keep it plain and warm.

- This class treats the Bible as the living, breathing word of God, so we come expecting to meet a Person, and to read a Gospel for who he is.
- Speak the welcome over the room: whoever you are, wherever you are with God, deep faith or real doubt, you belong here, and honest questions are exactly where the best conversations begin.
- John's own invitation is open-handed: he wrote so that you may believe. That invitation stands over this room all year.

## Move 5: Respond (4 min, graded participation)

- First Reading Journal entry, in room: "John says he wrote so that you may believe. Where are you with that right now? Honest is welcome, and no one will grade what you believe."
- Collect nothing; this is theirs. You are watching for engagement, not answers.

## After the bell

- Homework (the only kind there is): read John 1:1-18 before next class. That is the whole assignment.
- Feeds the gradebook: participation only today (engagement, first Respond entry). No content graded on day one.
- Sets up L2: author, date, setting, and how to read a Gospel, and then Unit 1 on the Prologue, 1:1-18.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: resist casting vision for 50 minutes. The quiet time, the group work, and the two journal entries are what make day one theirs and not yours. The plan front-loads their output on purpose.
- Names matter today. Learn who is in each John group; you seated the room for the year.
- The student who could not find John this morning should leave able to find it. That small win is the day's quiet victory for the (S) tier.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, first day of school.
