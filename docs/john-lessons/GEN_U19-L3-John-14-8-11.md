**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Philip asks for and how Jesus answers (14:8-9).
2. Students will explain "whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (14:9).
3. Students will explain the backup Jesus offers: "believe on account of the works" (14:11).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): if you want to know what God is like, look at Jesus.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus corrects a scary or blurry picture of the Father.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the mutual indwelling (14:10-11) and the deity thread from 1:18 and 12:45.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:9).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I picture God, what's he like? Where'd that picture come from?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest picture, even if it's a scary or blank one.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 14:8-11 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 14, verses 8 through 11.
- One-sentence setup: Philip thinks one thing would settle everything, seeing the Father, and Jesus says he's been looking at him all along.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 8, what does Philip ask Jesus for? What does he think it would do for them?
   - Cold-call check: "Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us." He thinks seeing the Father would settle everything.
2. **Entry.** In verse 9, how does Jesus answer? Is he a little hurt?
   - Draw out: "Have I been with you so long, and you still do not know me, Philip? Whoever has seen me has seen the Father." He's a little grieved that Philip still doesn't get it.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Lots of people carry a scary, distant, or blurry picture of God the Father. Jesus says: want to know what the Father is actually like? Look at me. How does that change the picture for someone who's afraid of God?
   - Guide them: if you've seen Jesus, patient with failures, gentle with the lost, weeping at a grave, washing feet, then you've seen the Father's heart. God isn't different behind Jesus' back. Jesus is what God is like.
4. **Core.** Read verses 10 and 11. Jesus says he's "in the Father and the Father is in me." Then he offers a backup: if you can't take my word for it, "believe on account of the works." Why give a second way in?
   - Land it: he meets doubt where it is. If the claim is too big to swallow yet, start with what he did. The works point back to the person. He gives the struggling person a handhold.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Way back in chapter 1, John said "no one has ever seen God; the only God, who is at the Father's side, he has made him known" (1:18). How does 14:9 finish that thought?
   - Draw out: Jesus is the one who makes the invisible God known. Seeing him is the closest anyone gets to seeing the Father, because he's exactly like the Father. The deity thread runs the whole book.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 9, how do you see the Father?
   - By looking at Jesus. "Whoever has seen me has seen the Father."

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name the ache under Philip's question. Everybody wants to know if God is safe, if he's good, if he's for them. Philip says it out loud: just show us the Father and we'll be okay.
- Give Jesus' answer straight. You've already seen him. Look at Jesus and you're looking at God. The God behind the universe has the face of the man washing feet and weeping at Lazarus' grave.
- Offer the handhold. If that's still too big, Jesus doesn't shame you. He says start with the works: look at what I did, look at the cross and the empty tomb, and work back to who I am. Faith can start small.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the Father made visible, so the God you were afraid of turns out to have his face.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "If Jesus is exactly what God the Father is like, what does that change about how you picture God?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 14:6. Read John 14:12-14 once before Thursday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L4 (short Thursday): Jesus promises his people will do "greater works" and answers prayer in his name.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The heart of the lesson: Jesus is the exact revelation of the Father (the deity thread from 1:18 and 12:45). Land it pastorally for the kid with a scary picture of God.
- Don't drown the room in the "mutual indwelling" language. One clear idea: look at Jesus, see the Father. Save the deeper indwelling for the disciple tier.
- 14:11 models grace toward doubt: if the words are too big, start with the works. Keep that on-ramp open.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
