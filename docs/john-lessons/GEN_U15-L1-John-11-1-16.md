**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why Jesus delayed two days (11:4-6, 11:15).
2. Students will explain what Jesus means by Lazarus "fallen asleep" (11:11-14).
3. Students will describe Thomas's response (11:16).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus shows up late on purpose, and it's the hardest thing to understand.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): a delay that hurts can still be love, if it's after something bigger.
- **Stretch** (disciple): death as "sleep" for the believer; the delay aimed at resurrection over recovery.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus" (John 11:5).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Have I ever felt like God showed up late for me?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest hurt, whatever it is. This chapter is for exactly that.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 11:1-16 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: table of contents, turn to John, chapter 11, verses 1 through 16.
- Set the scene in one sentence: Jesus' close friend Lazarus is dying, and his sisters send for Jesus.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Read verses 5 and 6 slowly. What does the text say about Jesus' love for this family, and what does he do right after hearing Lazarus is sick?
   - Cold-call check: "Jesus loved" them, and "so" he "stayed two days longer" where he was. The waiting flows right out of the loving. That word "so" is the hard part.
2. **Entry.** If someone you loved was sick and you had the power to help, what would you do? So what's shocking about verse 6?
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Jesus waited on purpose, and the text says he loved them the whole time. Look at verse 4 and verse 15. What is Jesus after that's bigger than getting there in time to heal Lazarus?
   - Guide them: he's after the glory of God and their deeper faith. He's not going to prevent the death. He's going to defeat it. A quick healing would have been good. Raising the dead shows who he really is. Love that waits can be love aimed at something bigger than a quick fix.
4. **Core.** This is the tender part, so handle it gently. Some students have lost people, or feel like God ignored them when they prayed. What does this passage say, and just as importantly, what does it NOT say, about why God sometimes waits?
   - Land it carefully: it does say God can wait, on purpose, out of love, for a bigger good. It does not say God waited because someone lacked faith, prayed the wrong words, or wasn't good enough. Jesus loved this family fully the whole time he waited. And you'll see in a couple of lessons that he cried at the grave. The God who waits is not a cold God.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verses 11 through 14, Jesus calls Lazarus's death "sleep." Then he says plainly, "Lazarus has died." Why call death "sleep" if the man is really dead?
   - Draw out: for the one Jesus is going to wake, death is as reversible as sleep. It's not that Lazarus isn't really dead. It's that death has met someone who can wake the dead. The same word gets used for dead believers in 1 Thessalonians 4.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: why did Jesus wait two days before going to Lazarus?
   - So he could raise him from the dead and show God's glory, not just heal him.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermon 16, and go slow. Some students are carrying grief.

- Name the hard thing plainly. Jesus loved this family, and he still let Lazarus die. That's in the text, verses 5 and 6, right next to each other. God's love and God's timing can both be true even when the timing feels like absence.
- Guard the grieving student. Hear this clearly: God does not delay because you didn't have enough faith or didn't pray the right way. Jesus loved this family the whole time he waited. If God has felt slow to you, that slowness is not proof he doesn't care.
- Point ahead. Jesus wasn't going to spare Lazarus death. He was going to beat it. Sometimes God lets the hard thing happen because he's after a bigger rescue than the one you asked for. And in two lessons you'll watch him cry at the grave. This is not a cold God.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the Lord who loves you and whose timing you can trust, even when it looks like he's late.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Where have you felt like God was late? After today, what's one thing you can say to yourself about God's love and God's timing?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged. Be ready for real emotion here.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 11:17-27 once before Monday. That's the memory-verse lesson.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: Jesus meets grieving Martha and tells her he himself is the resurrection.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- This lesson carries the hardest pastoral point in the unit. Guard the delay-in-love hard. Never let a student hear that God waited because someone lacked faith. The tears and anger coming in L3 are the proof the one who delayed is not cold. Cross before crown: Jesus doesn't spare Lazarus death, he defeats it.
- Be ready for grief in the room. Some students have lost someone recently. Handle Move 4 and Move 5 gently, and don't rush.
- Death as "sleep" (11:11) is not soul-sleep. Jesus says plainly "Lazarus has died" (11:14). It means death is reversible for the one Jesus wakes.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
