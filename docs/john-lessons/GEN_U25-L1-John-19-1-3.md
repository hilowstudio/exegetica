**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe the flogging, crown of thorns, and mocking (19:1-3).
2. Students will explain how the mockery unknowingly proclaims Jesus as King.
3. Students will connect the thorns and the flogging to Jesus bearing the curse for us.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): they mocked Jesus as a king, and he really was one.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the crown of thorns is a true coronation.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the thorns as the Genesis 3 curse; the flogging as Isaiah 53.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is the crucifixion; hold the room in reverence. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Set a sober, reverent tone for the whole unit.
- Centering line on the board: "Hail, King of the Jews!" (John 19:3).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "We're at the cross now. What do I feel walking up to it?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write whatever's honest.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 19:1-3 aloud, slowly and reverently.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 1 through 3.
- One-sentence setup: after the trial, Pilate has Jesus flogged, and the soldiers turn his kingship into a cruel joke.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 2 and 3, what three things do the soldiers do to dress Jesus up and mock him?
   - Cold-call check: a crown of thorns, a purple robe, and the mocking cry "Hail, King of the Jews!" (while hitting him).
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** The soldiers are being cruel, dressing Jesus as a fake king to laugh at him. But look at what they actually put on him: a crown, a royal robe, and a king's title. What's the irony? What's true about their joke?
   - Guide them: he really is the King. They think they're mocking a pretender, and they're accidentally staging a coronation. The purple robe is the color of kings. The title is his real title. They're telling the truth without meaning to. The whole scene is Jesus being crowned as King, in the most backwards way possible.
3. **Core.** Look at the crown specifically. It's made of thorns. Where do thorns first show up in the Bible, and what do they represent (think of Genesis 3, after Adam and Eve sin)?
   - Land it: thorns come from the curse in Genesis 3. When sin entered the world, the ground was cursed to grow thorns. So the crown of thorns is the curse of sin, pressed down onto Jesus' head. He's literally wearing the curse, taking it on himself, for us. The King is crowned with our curse.
4. **Core.** This is a real flogging, a real body, real blood. The prophet Isaiah wrote about this 700 years before (Isaiah 53:5, "he was pierced for our transgressions... by his wounds we are healed"). Why does it matter that this suffering is real, and that it was for us?
   - Draw out: because a fake or symbolic suffering couldn't save anyone. Jesus really bled. He really felt every lash. And Isaiah says it was "for our transgressions," in our place. His real wounds are what heal us. To save real people from real sin, he took real pain.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Why would God let his Son be mocked and beaten like this? How is his lowest humiliation also, secretly, his coronation as King?
   - Draw out: because this is how he wins. He doesn't take his throne by force. He takes it by taking our curse. The mockery, the thorns, the blood, all of it is the King going to his throne by way of the cross. His humiliation is the road to his glory, exactly as the whole book has been saying (the "lifted up" of 12:32).
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how is the soldiers' mockery of Jesus actually true?
   - He really is the King, wearing the crown, the robe, and the curse, for us.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh, and hold the reverence.

- Slow the room down. We've arrived at the cross. Before we say anything clever, just look. The Son of God, whipped until his back is torn open, a crown of thorns crushed onto his head, soldiers laughing and hitting him. This really happened. Don't rush past it.
- Turn the mockery over. They think they're mocking a fake king. They're crowning the real one. And that crown is made of thorns, the curse of sin from the very beginning, and he's wearing it. On his head. For you. Your curse, pressed down on him.
- Land the love. He didn't have to become this touchable, this hurtable. He chose a body that could be beaten, so he could stand in your place and take what you had coming. Every lash, he took on purpose, out of love.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the King wears our curse and our wounds, crowned with thorns to carry us home.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "The King wore a crown of thorns, your curse, for you. Sit with that, then write: how do you respond to a King who did that?"
- Give it quiet, and let it be reverent. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Set the reverent, worshipful tone for the whole unit here. This is worship before it's instruction. The horror is real; look at it in order to worship, not to gawk.
- Two things to land: the mockery as a true coronation (he really is the King), and the thorns as the Genesis 3 curse pressed onto Christ. For the disciple tier, tie the flogging to Isaiah 53:5 (Adam's sermon 35: to die for us he first became fully human, "punchable").
