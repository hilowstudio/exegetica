**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "sanctify them in the truth" as being made holy through God's word (17:17).
2. Students will explain that Jesus sends his people as the Father sent him (17:18).
3. Students will explain "for their sake I consecrate myself" (17:19).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): God grows his people through his word.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): being made holy is God's work through the truth, not a grind-harder project.
- **Stretch** (disciple): sanctification grounded in Christ's self-consecration for the cross (grace-first).

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Sanctify them in the truth; your word is truth" (John 17:17).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I try to be a better person, how's it going on my own strength?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: be honest about the struggle.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 17:17-19 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 17, verses 17 through 19.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus asks the Father to make his people holy, and tells him how.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 17, what does Jesus ask the Father to do for his people?
   - Cold-call check: sanctify them, make them holy, set them apart for God.
2. **Entry.** Still in verse 17, HOW does Jesus say they'll be made holy? What's the tool?
   - Draw out: "in the truth; your word is truth." God makes his people holy through his word.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** "Sanctify" means to set apart and make holy. Notice who's doing the work in verse 17. Is this Jesus telling them to go clean themselves up by trying harder, or is it a prayer asking the FATHER to do something in them?
   - Guide them: it's a prayer. Jesus asks the FATHER to make them holy. Being made holy is God's work in you, through his word, not a self-improvement project you power on your own. You grow by God's word working on you, which is why reading it changes you. The truth shapes you.
4. **Core.** Read verse 19: "for their sake I consecrate myself." "Consecrate" means to set apart for a holy purpose. What is Jesus setting himself apart FOR here, and why?
   - Land it: he's setting himself apart for the cross. He's about to give himself up to die. And he says he does it "for their sake, that they also may be sanctified." So your becoming holy flows from what Jesus did. He set himself apart on the cross so you could be set apart to God. Your holiness starts with his sacrifice, not your effort.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Put verse 17 and verse 19 together. God makes you holy through his word (17), and it's grounded in Jesus setting himself apart at the cross (19). Where does the power for a holy life actually come from, and where does it not come from?
   - Draw out: it comes from Christ, received by faith, and applied by the Spirit through the word. It doesn't come from gritting your teeth and being disciplined enough. The order matters: Jesus made you his and set you apart first, and now he grows you. Grace first, effort flows out of it. (This is the pattern the course has taught all year: the tree is made good, then it bears fruit.)
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how does God make his people holy, according to verse 17?
   - Through the truth, his word.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Take the pressure off the self-improvement grind. A lot of you try to be a better person by sheer willpower, and you keep losing. Jesus doesn't pray "help them try harder." He asks the Father to make them holy, through the truth. Growing in holiness is something God does in you, not something you crank out alone.
- Point to the word. And notice the tool: "your word is truth." God shapes you through his word. Which means the Bible isn't homework. It's how God grows you. Time in it isn't a box to check. It's where the change happens.
- Ground it in the cross. And here's the deepest part. Jesus says he sets himself apart, for the cross, so that you can be set apart to God. Your holiness doesn't start with your effort. It starts with his sacrifice. He made you his first. The growing flows from that.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus consecrated himself at the cross so that his people could be made holy through the truth.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "God grows you through his word, on the ground of what Jesus did. What would change if you saw Bible reading as God shaping you instead of a chore?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep memorizing John 17:3. Read John 17:20-21 once before Monday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: Jesus stops praying for the eleven and starts praying for every Christian who will ever believe, including us.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Two things to land: sanctification by the word (17:17, God's work through the truth, not a willpower project), and its ground in Christ's self-consecration for the cross (17:19). Keep Marshall's grace-first order: holiness received before it's practiced (Adam's sermon 30: the grace that saved you is the grace that sanctifies you).
- Guard against turning "sanctify by the truth" into legalism or a to-do list. The power is in Christ, received by faith, applied by the Spirit through the word.
- Derive the frame from sermon 30; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
