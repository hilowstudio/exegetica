# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 18 Lesson 6 of 7: the denial foretold (John 13:36-38)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U18 The towel and the basin |
| Lesson | 6 of 7 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Jan 28 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus shape |
| Passage | John 13:36-38 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Peter promises to die for Jesus, and Jesus, loving him, tells him he will deny him instead, because only Jesus lays down his life. |
| Christ connection | Jesus alone lays down his life, and he is tender with the friend who will deny him. |
| Memory verse | John 13:34-35 was planted last lesson. Review it, do not re-plant. Keep the nine earlier anchors sharp too. |
| Resources | No paired sermon. Fresh-build. Reference: John 10:11, the Good Shepherd who lays down his life. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will describe Peter's promise (13:37) and Jesus' response (13:38).
2. Students will explain that laying down his life for the sheep is what the Good Shepherd does (10:11).
3. Students will explain how Jesus treats a man he knows will fail him.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Peter swears he'd die for Jesus, and Jesus says he'll deny him by morning.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): confidence in your own strength sets you up to fall.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Peter claims the shepherd's own work; only Jesus lays down his life.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "the rooster will not crow till you have denied me three times" (John 13:38).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where am I overconfident about my own strength?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 13:36-38 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 13, verses 36 through 38.
- One-sentence setup: Peter wants to go wherever Jesus is going, and makes a big promise.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verse 37, what does Peter promise to do for Jesus?
   - "I will lay down my life for you." He's sure of it.
2. **Read-check.** In verse 38, what does Jesus say will actually happen before the rooster crows?
   - Peter will deny even knowing Jesus, three times, that very night.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Peter says, "I will lay down my life for you." Now think back to John 10:11, "the good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep." Work this: Peter is promising to do the exact thing that belongs to Jesus alone. What does he get backwards, and who actually lays down his life for whom?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out: Peter has it upside down. He thinks he'll die for Jesus. But Jesus is the Shepherd who dies for the sheep, and Peter is a sheep. Peter, trusting his own strength, is about to fail badly. Jesus, who actually has the strength, is about to lay down his life for Peter. Peter's job tonight isn't to be a hero for Jesus. It's to be loved and saved by him.
   - For the disciple tier: notice Jesus doesn't cut Peter off. He tells him the hard truth, and he'd already said "you will follow afterward" (verse 36). The denial isn't the end of Peter. Jesus restores him later (John 21). Jesus loves people who fail.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the lesson. Framed fresh.

- Name Peter's mistake, because it's ours. Peter was sure he was strong enough to die for Jesus. He meant it. And he folded within hours. The moment you're most sure you'd never fail is usually the moment you're about to. Confidence in yourself is not the same as strength.
- Flip the direction. Peter said he'd lay down his life for Jesus. The truth is the other way: Jesus lays down his life for Peter. You don't save Jesus. He saves you. The pressure is off you to be the hero.
- Land on the tenderness. Here's the wonder: Jesus knows Peter will deny him that very night, and he keeps him. He'd already promised, "you will follow afterward." And after the resurrection, Jesus goes and restores him. Jesus loves people who let him down, which is everyone in this room. Failing him doesn't end you.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus alone lays down his life, and he loves and restores the very friend who denies him.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Peter was sure of his own strength and failed. Jesus loved and restored him anyway. Where are you overconfident, and what does it mean that Jesus keeps people who fail him?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Review John 13:34-35. Next class pulls the whole chapter together.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L7: we gather the whole chapter, the towel and the cross and the new command.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- The christological point is sharp: Peter claims to lay down his life, which is the Good Shepherd's own work (10:11). Only Jesus does it. Peter has the direction backwards.
- Handle the denial with grace, never shame. Jesus foretells it and still keeps Peter ("you will follow afterward," 13:36), and restores him in John 21. Teach it as grace for people who fail Jesus, which is everyone in the room. No student should leave feeling failure ends them.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes.
