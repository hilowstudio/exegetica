**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what "ask in my name" means (16:23-24).
2. Students will explain "the Father himself loves you" (16:27).
3. Students will explain "I came from the Father... and go to the Father" as the shape of the gospel (16:28).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): you can talk straight to God the Father.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the Father isn't angry behind Jesus' back; he loves you himself.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "ask in my name" guarded from magic-formula; 16:28 as incarnation-to-ascension.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "The Father himself loves you" (John 16:27).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When I imagine God the Father looking at me, what's his face doing?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest picture, even a cold or blank one.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 16:23-28 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 16, verses 23 through 28.
- One-sentence setup: Jesus opens up prayer to the Father and clears up a big misunderstanding about how God feels about them.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 24, what does Jesus tell them to do, and what will it lead to?
   - Cold-call check: ask (in his name), and they'll receive, so that their joy would be full.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** "Ask in my name" isn't a magic phrase you tack on to get whatever you want. Given what you learned in John 14, what does praying "in Jesus' name" actually mean?
   - Guide them: asking on the ground of who Jesus is, for the kind of things Jesus wants, aimed at the Father's glory. It's coming to God as one who belongs to Jesus, praying the way he'd pray. Not a cheat code, and not a promise of a comfortable life.
3. **Core.** Read verses 26 and 27 slowly. Jesus says he does NOT need to beg the Father to love them. Why? What does verse 27 say straight out?
   - Land it: "the Father himself loves you." Jesus isn't a middleman who has to calm down an angry God and talk him into caring about you. The Father already loves you, on his own, directly. Jesus opened the door, and behind it is a Father who's glad to see you.
4. **Core.** A lot of people secretly picture Jesus as a shield protecting them from a Father who's furious. How does verse 27 blow that picture up?
   - Draw out: it says the Father himself loves them. Same love, not a reluctant one Jesus wrings out of him. The Father and the Son are on the same side, and that side is for you. When you pray, you're not sneaking past an angry God. You're walking up to a Father who loves you.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 28: "I came from the Father and have come into the world, and now I am leaving the world and going to the Father." That one sentence holds the whole gospel. Trace it.
   - Draw out: the Son existed with the Father before the world, came from the Father into the world (the incarnation and his whole life), and is now going back to the Father (through the cross, resurrection, and ascension). Where he came from, why he came, where he's going, all in one line.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: according to verse 27, how does the Father feel about Jesus' people?
   - He loves them himself.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name the lie a lot of you carry. You picture Jesus as the nice one, warm and kind, and God the Father as the angry one in the back, arms crossed, disappointed. And you imagine Jesus standing in front of you like a shield, taking the heat so the Father won't get you.
- Then read them verse 27. "The Father himself loves you." Jesus says it flat out. He doesn't have to beg the Father to care about you. The Father already does, on his own, with the same love Jesus has. They are not divided about you.
- Change how they pray. So when you pray, you're not talking your way past an angry God. You're coming to a Father who loves you, whose door Jesus opened with his own blood. Ask. He wants to give.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus opened the way to a Father who already loves you, and he came from that Father and went back to him for you.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'The Father himself loves you.' If that's true, what changes about how you'd pray tonight?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 16:29-32 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L6: the disciples say they finally believe, and Jesus tells them they're about to scatter.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The heart of the lesson is 16:27, "the Father himself loves you." Adam's sermon 28 makes this the correction of the "Jesus is a heat shield between me and an angry Father" lie. Land it pastorally; a lot of students carry that picture.
- Keep "ask in my name" (16:23-24) guarded from magic-formula and prosperity, the same guard as U19 (14:13-14): praying on the ground of his person, for the Father's glory.
- 16:28 is the whole gospel in one line (incarnation to ascension); keep it for the disciple tier.
- Derive the frame from sermon 28; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
