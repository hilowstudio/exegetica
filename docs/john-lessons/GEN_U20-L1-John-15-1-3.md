**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus means by calling himself the "true vine" (15:1).
2. Students will identify the Old Testament vineyard background (Israel) and how Jesus fulfills it.
3. Students will explain why the disciples are "already clean" (15:3).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus draws a picture, a grapevine, to show how his people live.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "true vine" means Jesus does what Israel was supposed to and never did.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the prune-and-clean wordplay (15:2-3) and "already clean by the word," tied to 13:10.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. Keep your sustained voice light. One clear idea drives the block.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room fast. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser" (John 15:1).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where am I trying to grow something good on my own?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 15:1-3 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 15, verses 1 through 3.
- One-sentence setup: same night, same room, and Jesus draws a picture of a grapevine to teach them how his people actually live.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: what "true vine" means and why it matters. Ask these.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 1, what two things does Jesus call himself and the Father?
   - Cold-call check: he's the true vine; the Father is the vinedresser (the gardener who tends the vine).
2. **The driving question (whole class, then small groups).** Jesus says "true" vine. True compared to what? In the Old Testament, who was God's vineyard? (Point them to Isaiah 5 or Psalm 80.)
   - Guide them: Israel was God's vine or vineyard, planted to bear fruit for him, and it kept failing (Isaiah 5: he looked for good grapes and got wild ones). Jesus steps in as the true vine, the one who finally does what the vineyard was always meant to do. He's the true Israel.
   - Sermon 26 handle: Jesus has come to do perfectly what Israel failed to do.
3. **Core.** Read verses 2 and 3 slowly. Jesus says the Father "prunes" the fruitful branch, and then that the disciples are "already clean." Those two words come from the same root in Greek. What is it that both prunes and cleans them (verse 3)?
   - Land it: his word. "Already you are clean because of the word that I have spoken to you." They didn't scrub themselves clean. His word did it, and his word keeps trimming them to grow more. The tool is the word of Jesus.
4. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what does Jesus mean when he calls himself the "true" vine?
   - He's the real vine Israel only pictured, the one who bears the fruit the old vineyard never could.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Set up the picture. For a thousand years God had a vineyard, Israel, and he kept coming back looking for fruit and finding wild grapes. Then his own Son stands up and says: I'm the true vine. I'll grow what they never could.
- Land the cleanness. Before he asks the disciples to do a single thing, he tells them they're already clean, because of his word. That's the order every time with Jesus. He makes you his, then the fruit comes. You don't grow the fruit to earn the connection.
- Point ahead. Tomorrow he'll tell them how the branch bears fruit, and it's the most freeing thing in the chapter. Today, just get this: he's the vine, and being joined to him is everything.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the true vine, the true Israel, and his word makes his people clean before it ever asks them to grow.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "The disciples were clean because of Jesus' word, not their own effort. Where do you try to clean yourself up for God instead of letting him do it?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 15:4-5 once before Friday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L2: "abide in me," and the new memory verse, John 15:5.

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus. One idea taught well: Jesus is the true vine, the true Israel. Don't reach ahead into the abiding; that's L2.
- The Israel-vineyard background (Isaiah 5, Psalm 80) is the key move. Jesus fulfills it. Derive it from sermon 26; cite it, don't read it aloud.
- The kathairo (prune) / katharos (clean) wordplay in 15:2-3 is a stretch-tier gem: the word of Jesus both cleans and prunes. Tie "already clean" to 13:10 (clean by the word, not a rite).
- Keep the order grace-first: they're clean first (15:3), fruit comes later. This sets up the whole unit.
