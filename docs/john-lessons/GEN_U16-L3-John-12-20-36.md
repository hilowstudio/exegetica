# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 16 Lesson 3 of 5: the hour, the seed, and the cross (John 12:20-36)

| Big idea | The hour has come: Jesus is the grain of wheat who dies to bear a harvest, and lifted up on the cross he draws all people to himself. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain "the hour has come" (12:23) and what "glorified" means (12:33, the cross).
2. Students will explain the grain of wheat (12:24) and that Christ dies first.
3. Students will explain "now is my soul troubled" (12:27) and "lifted up... draw all people" (12:32).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): a seed has to be buried and die before it grows anything.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus was really afraid of the cross, and he went anyway.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "lifted up" as crucifixion and exaltation in one word; "all people" as every kind, drawn effectually.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. This is the densest day, so pace it as one arc.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "The hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified" (John 12:23).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What am I afraid of that I might have to face anyway?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest fear.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 12:20-36 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 12, verses 20 through 36.
- One-sentence setup: some Greeks (non-Jews) come looking for Jesus, and it sets off the biggest moment yet.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verses 20 and 21, who comes looking for Jesus? Why is it a big deal that non-Jews are showing up?
   - Cold-call check: Greeks, Gentiles. The world is starting to come to Jesus, exactly what the Pharisees just complained about. And it triggers what Jesus says next.
2. **Entry.** In verse 23 Jesus says, "The hour has come." The whole book has said his hour "had not yet come." What finally arrived?
   - The time for him to be glorified. And verse 33 tells us what that means: the kind of death he was going to die. The hour is the cross.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 24. What has to happen to a grain of wheat before it bears fruit? Jesus is talking about himself first. What is he saying about his own death?
   - Guide them: a seed stays alone unless it falls into the ground and dies, and then it grows a harvest. Jesus is the seed. He has to die, and his death is what grows the harvest of everyone who will believe. He goes first. The fruit is you.
4. **Core.** Read verse 27. Jesus says, "Now is my soul troubled." He even asks whether to pray "save me from this hour." What does this show about what the cross cost him, and what does he decide?
   - Land it: the cross genuinely scared him. He was troubled, shaken, afraid. And he says, "for this purpose I have come to this hour," and "Father, glorify your name." He faced the terror and went anyway, out of love. He wasn't a hero who felt nothing. He felt it all and obeyed.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 32: "when I am lifted up from the earth, I will draw all people to myself." "Lifted up" means the cross (verse 33). Does "all people" mean every single person gets saved? Look at how the whole book has talked about belief.
   - Draw out: "lifted up" means both raised up to die and raised up in glory, one word for both. "All people" means people of every kind, Jew and Greek, every nation, drawn to Christ. It's the harvest the Greeks just started. It doesn't mean every individual is saved, since the book keeps calling people to believe. It means the cross reaches the whole world.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what is Jesus the grain of wheat saying about his death?
   - He has to die, like a seed buried in the ground, so that a whole harvest of life can grow.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Build on sermons 19 and 20.

- Start with the seed. Jesus looked at his own death and called it a planting. A seed looks like it's being thrown away when it goes into the dark ground. Then it grows into a harvest. Jesus' death looked like the end, and it was the seed of life for the whole world. The fruit is every person who's ever come to him, including you.
- Sit on verse 27. Jesus was afraid. The cross scared him, and he said so. And he went anyway, because he came for this, and because he loved you. If you've ever thought Jesus can't understand being terrified, read this verse again.
- Land the cross. Lifted up on a cross, Jesus draws people from every nation to himself. The Greeks were the first drops of a worldwide harvest. When you look at the cross and something in you is drawn to him, that's this verse coming true in you.
- Offer the stretch verse. John 12:24 is worth carrying if a student wants it, off the quiz: "unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone, but if it dies, it bears much fruit."
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is the seed who died to grow a harvest, lifted up on the cross to draw a world to himself.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus faced the cross afraid, and went anyway, for you. What does it change to know he understands fear and still chose you?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 12:37-43 once before Wednesday.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. John 12:24 is an optional stretch, off the graded line.
- Sets up L4: after all the signs, most people still didn't believe, and John explains why.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%. This is the densest lesson, so pace it as one arc and let it land on two verses, 12:23 and 12:32.
- Build on sermon 19 (life through death, the grain of wheat) and sermon 20 (lifted up, the soul troubled). Guard the order: Christ is the seed and dies first, and the disciple's death to self (12:25) flows out of his death, never beside it.
- Keep the "now my soul is troubled" real (sermon 20): the cross genuinely scared Jesus. Keep both his real fear and his real obedience live.
- Guard "draw all people" (12:32) against universalism. It means people of every kind, drawn effectually, the reach of the gospel to all nations, never that every individual is saved.
- Offer 12:24 as the optional stretch here, off the graded line.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
