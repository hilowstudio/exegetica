**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will identify Golgotha and what happened there (19:17-18).
2. Students will explain why John gives the crucifixion so few words.
3. Students will connect the cross to "lifted up" (12:32-33).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus was crucified, between two others.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): the cross is the center of everything, and it asks to be beheld.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the cross as the hour of glory and the King's throne.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single focus. This is the holiest passage in the book. Keep your voice quiet and reverent, and give the room space to behold. Do not fill the silence.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away. Let it be quiet.
- Centering line on the board: "There they crucified him" (John 19:18).
- Students write one line in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "What do I want to say to Jesus, looking at the cross?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 19:17-18 aloud, slowly. Then read it once more, in silence after.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 19, verses 17 and 18.
- One-sentence setup: they lead Jesus out to a hill called the Skull, and there they crucify him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 18 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Single focus: behold the cross. Ask these, and leave room for silence.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 17, where do they take Jesus, and what does he carry? In verse 18, what happens there, and who else is crucified?
   - Cold-call check: to Golgotha, the place of a skull; he carries his own cross; there they crucify him, with two others, one on each side, Jesus in the middle.
2. **The one question for the day (whole class).** The most important event in the history of the world is right here in verse 18, and John gives it four words: "there they crucified him." No description of the nails, no dwelling on the agony. Why do you think John says so little?
   - Guide them, gently: some things are too holy and too heavy for a flood of words. John doesn't try to work up your emotions with gore. He states the fact plainly and lets it land with all its weight. The cross doesn't need dramatizing. It needs beholding. Sometimes the most reverent thing you can do is stop talking and look.
3. **Core.** Jesus is crucified "between" two others, in the middle. And all year Jesus has said he'd be "lifted up" (remember 12:32). What did being "lifted up" turn out to mean? And how is this hill, of all places, his throne?
   - Land it: "lifted up" meant lifted up on a cross to die, and lifted up so that he'd draw all people to himself. The cross is where he's enthroned. This bloody hill, with a criminal on each side, is the throne room. The King reigns from here, exactly as he said.
4. **Behold (a quiet minute).** Put the questions down for a moment. Read verse 18 one more time and just sit with it: the Son of God, crucified, for you. Let the room be silent.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: what happened at Golgotha, according to verse 18?
   - There they crucified Jesus, between two others.

Circulate quietly. Keep it reverent.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it quietly over the room.

- Keep it simple, like John does. There they crucified him. That's it. That's the sentence the whole Bible was moving toward. The Son of God, nailed to a Roman cross, on a hill shaped like a skull, between two criminals. Don't rush it. Just look.
- Name what you're seeing. This is the hour the whole book pointed to. What looks like the end of Jesus is his coronation. The cross is his throne. He's not losing. He's reigning, and he's reigning by dying for you.
- Invite worship. There isn't much to say today. There's Someone to behold. Look at what your King did, and let it make you quiet, and grateful, and his.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the King reigns from the cross, lifted up to draw you to himself.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt on the board: "There they crucified him. Write your honest response to the cross of Jesus."
- Give it quiet. Let the reverence hold. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

## Teacher notes

- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus, and the most sacred passage in the course. This lesson is worship. Keep your voice quiet, leave real silence, and resist filling it. Let the students behold.
- The whole design: John gives the crucifixion four words, so the lesson does too. Don't dramatize or add gore. Behold, don't gawk.
- Tie the cross to "lifted up" (12:32-33): the cross is the throne, the hour of glory. Keep it for the disciple tier without overloading the quiet.
