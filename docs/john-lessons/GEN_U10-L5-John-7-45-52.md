**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain why the officers did not arrest Jesus (7:45-46).
2. Students will describe Nicodemus's defense (7:50-51) and connect him to John 3.
3. Students will explain the leaders' error in "no prophet arises from Galilee" (7:52).

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the guards sent to grab Jesus come back empty, stunned by how he talks.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): speaking up for Jesus when the room is against him costs something.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Nicodemus's slow move toward the light; judging by appearances hardened into policy.

---

**Talk budget:** short day, single-focus. One key question worked in groups. Keep your sustained voice well under half the 36 minutes.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "No one ever spoke like this man!" (John 7:46).
- One quiet minute in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Where is it hard for me to be known as a Christian?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3 min)

- Have one student read John 7:45-52 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 7, verses 45 through 52.
- One-sentence setup: the temple guards were sent to arrest Jesus, and they come back with no prisoner and one sentence.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 16 min, one key question, no lecture)

Open with two quick read-checks, then work the key question.

1. **Read-check.** In verses 45 and 46, why didn't the officers arrest Jesus? What did they say?
   - They couldn't do it. "No one ever spoke like this man." His words stopped them.
2. **Read-check.** In verses 50 and 51, who speaks up, and what does he ask for?
   - Nicodemus. He asks that the law give Jesus a hearing before judging him. He's the same man who came to Jesus at night in chapter 3.
3. **Key question (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** The leaders are furious, the crowd is being called cursed, and everyone with power has decided against Jesus. Nicodemus speaks up anyway, carefully. Work this: what does it cost to say even a small true thing about Jesus when the whole room is against him? And why do the leaders miss Jesus completely?
   - Guide the groups, don't lecture. Draw out on the cost: Nicodemus risks his standing to ask for basic fairness. He isn't loud or fully out yet, and he's moving toward the light over time (he came at night in chapter 3, he speaks up here, and later he helps bury Jesus in chapter 19). Small honest steps count.
   - Draw out on the leaders: they judge by appearances (verse 52, "no prophet arises from Galilee"), the exact thing Jesus warned against back in verse 24. They never checked the facts. Their contempt for Galilee blinds them to who Jesus is.

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and land the unit. Build on the close of sermon 12.

- Start with the word that arrests the arresters. Armed guards came to grab Jesus, and they couldn't, because of how he spoke. His word has a power that stops people in their tracks. Whatever you thought you could do with Jesus, handle him, argue him away, fit him in a box, take your hands off and listen.
- Honor the small brave step. Nicodemus wasn't a hero yet. He asked one careful question when everyone else had made up their minds. That question was real courage, and it was one more step out of the dark. You don't have to have it all figured out to take a step toward Jesus in a room that doesn't want him.
- Warn about the settled verdict. The leaders judged Jesus by where he was from and never looked at the evidence. That's how people miss him: not because the case is weak, but because they decided in advance. Look honestly.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus' word disarms the men sent to seize him, and the honest heart keeps edging toward his light.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Nicodemus took a small, brave step toward Jesus when the room was against him. What's one small step you could take, even if you don't have it all figured out?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Keep reviewing your six memory verses.
- To the gradebook: today's participation. No new required memory verse this unit.
- Sets up Unit 11: back in the temple, Jesus faces a woman caught in adultery and the crowd holding stones.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: short day, single-focus. One key question, worked in groups. Keep it moving and protect Move 4.
- Build on the close of sermon 12: the word that arrests the arresters, and the box the leaders refuse to let Jesus out of.
- Handle Nicodemus with Unit 4's bookend arc: he came at night (chapter 3), he defends a fair hearing here (chapter 7), and he helps bury Jesus at cost (chapter 19). He's a man edging out of the dark over years. Don't call him a coward, and don't declare him fully converted.
- The sneer "no prophet arises from Galilee" (7:52) is the crowd's error from 7:24 hardened into policy. Note that they were factually wrong (Jonah was from Galilee), and their real problem was deciding before checking.
- Day-type note: Thursday 36 minutes. This closes Unit 10.
