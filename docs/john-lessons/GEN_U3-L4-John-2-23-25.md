# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 3 Lesson 4 of 4: the belief Jesus can read (John 2:23-25)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U3 Sign 1 & the temple |
| Lesson | 4 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Fri Sep 25 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 2:23-25 (ESV) |
| Big idea | Many "believed in his name when they saw the signs," and in the next breath "Jesus did not entrust himself to them," because "he knew what was in man." |
| Christ connection | Jesus knows every heart in that Passover crowd and every heart in this room, and he still holds himself out to be received. The cure for a shallow faith is coming to him with empty hands. |
| Memory verse | Review only (20:31, 1:14, 1:12). The next new anchor is John 3:16 in Unit 4. |
| Resources | Paired sermon: Adam Vega 03, "Zeal for the Lord" (John 2:13-25), last part. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will state what the crowd did (believed at the signs) and what Jesus did in response (did not entrust himself to them).
2. Students will explain the difference between being impressed by the signs and truly trusting Jesus.
3. Students will explain what "he knew what was in man" (2:25) claims about who Jesus is.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): there is a difference between being impressed by Jesus from a distance and actually receiving him; you come empty-handed, not with a résumé.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): you can believe the miracles happened, grow up on Jesus, and still never hand him your empty hands.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the deliberate wordplay in verses 23-24 (they "believed," he did not "entrust") and the divine heart-knowledge of verse 25, plus the seam John built into 3:1.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. This is a short passage on a full period, so gather the whole chapter first, then land on these three verses and open the door to Nicodemus.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "He himself knew what was in man" (John 2:25).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If someone could see everything in you, would that feel like a threat or a relief? Be honest."
- On-ramp for the doubter: honest is welcome, either way.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 2:23-25 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 2, verses 23 through 25.
- One-sentence setup: John says something startling. People believed, and Jesus did not trust their belief.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 23, what did many people do, and why? Then verse 24: what did Jesus do, or not do, in response?
   - Draw out: many "believed in his name when they saw the signs he was doing," and yet "Jesus on his part did not entrust himself to them."
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** John uses the same idea twice, on purpose: they believed, and Jesus did not entrust. What is the difference between being impressed by the miracles and actually trusting the Savior?
   - Guide them: a faith that only wants the wonders has not yet come to rest on the person. They trusted the show; he did not trust that kind of trust. Being amazed from a distance is not the same as receiving him (remember 1:12).
3. **Core.** Verse 25 says Jesus "needed no one to bear witness about man, for he himself knew what was in man." What is that claiming about who Jesus is?
   - Land it: he reads the heart the way only God reads it. He does not need anyone to tell him about you. He already knows. That is a claim about his deity, and it is quietly staggering.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read the very last line of chapter 2, then the first line of chapter 3. What has John set up?
   - Draw out: chapter 2 ends "he knew what was in man"; chapter 3 opens "there was a man of the Pharisees named Nicodemus," a religious insider impressed by the signs (3:2). John built the seam. The man 2:25 just described walks on stage in 3:1.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What is the difference between the crowd's belief and real trust? What does verse 25 say Jesus knows?
   - Being impressed by the signs versus receiving him; he knows what is in every person.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Lead with grace; this is the lesson to pastor most carefully.

- Say the relief first. Jesus reads every heart in this room, and knowing everything, he still stretches out his hands to be received. Being fully known by him is safe for the one who comes with nothing to offer.
- Name the empty hands. You cannot barter your way in with your good record, and you do not have to. Come with nothing, and be received. That is what "received him" in 1:12 means.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus knows you completely and still offers himself, so the man he meets after dark in 3:1 hears him say, "You must be born again."

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus knows everything in you and still offers himself to you. Write your honest reaction to being that known and still wanted."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 3:1-8 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12.
- Sets up Unit 4: Nicodemus comes to Jesus at night, and Jesus tells him he must be born again.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Govern this lesson most carefully, because the sermon lands with an adult edge that can wound an anxious fifteen-year-old. Do not make "Jesus did not entrust himself to them" a fear test. Lead with grace every time: the power is in Christ received, never in the intensity of a student's sincerity. Settle that Jesus knows them fully and still offers himself, and let real trust come as the fruit of a heart persuaded it is loved. Requiring a measured depth of conviction as the ticket to Christ is legalism guarding the door of grace.
- Keep the "depart from me, I never knew you" turn (Matthew 7:22-23) optional, for the disciple tier at most, and never the closer. The chapter's own last word is that Jesus holds himself out to be received, so end there.
- Do not flatten sign-faith into "miracles are bad." The signs are given so people may believe (20:31); belief simply has to grow past the spectacle to the Savior.
- Grounding for your own prep: the wordplay is deliberate, the same verb for their "believed" (episteusan, 2:23) and his not-entrusting (ouk episteuen, 2:24); keep it a disciple-tier note so it does not crowd the grace landing.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
