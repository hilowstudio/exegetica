**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain the Psalm 41:9 quotation (13:18) and why Jesus tells them in advance (13:19).
2. Students will describe the morsel given to Judas (13:26) and what it signals.
3. Students will explain "and it was night" (13:30) and how Jesus stays in control.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): Jesus is betrayed by one of his closest friends, and he knew it the whole time.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): Jesus foretells the betrayal so it will strengthen faith, not shatter it.
- **Stretch** (disciple): Jesus sovereign over his betrayal, fulfilling Scripture, timing Satan's move.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. No single block past 8 to 10 minutes. The Work block belongs to the students.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "I am telling you this now, before it takes place" (John 13:19).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "When someone let me down badly, where was God in it?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: write the honest hurt.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have two students read John 13:18-30 aloud, one reads 18 through 25, the other 26 through 30.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 13, verses 18 through 30.
- One-sentence setup: at the table, Jesus says one of the twelve is going to betray him.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 18, Jesus quotes Scripture about a friend turning on him ("he who ate my bread has lifted his heel against me," from Psalm 41:9). What does that tell you about how well Jesus saw this coming?
   - Cold-call check: he saw it clearly. It was even written ahead of time. The betrayal did not surprise him at all.
2. **Entry.** Read verse 19: "I am telling you this now, before it takes place, that when it does take place you may believe." Why would Jesus warn them ahead of time?
   - So that when the betrayal happens, instead of it destroying their faith, it confirms it. He called it in advance, so it becomes proof he knew all along.
3. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Read verse 26. Jesus dips a piece of bread and hands it to Judas. In that culture, handing a guest the dipped morsel was a gesture of honor, a mark of favor. Why is it so striking that Jesus gives that to the man he knows will sell him?
   - Guide them: he shows honor and kindness to the very man betraying him. He washed Judas's feet, and now he hands him a piece of bread like an honored friend, knowing exactly what Judas is about to do. That's love aimed at an enemy.
4. **Core.** Read verse 27: "Satan entered into him," and then Jesus says, "what you are going to do, do quickly." Who's giving the orders here? What does that tell you about Jesus' control over what's happening?
   - Land it: even with Satan at work in Judas, Jesus is the one setting the timing. "Do it quickly." Nobody takes Jesus by surprise or forces his hand. He walks toward the cross on his own schedule, in full control. The evil is real, and it never escapes his hands.
5. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Read verse 30: "he immediately went out. And it was night." John could have just said Judas left. Why add "and it was night"?
   - Draw out: it's more than the time. All through John, Jesus is the light, and people who reject him walk into darkness. Judas leaves the Light and steps into the night. It's the darkest moment, told in three words.
6. **Cold-call comprehension check.** In one sentence: how does Jesus stay in control of his own betrayal?
   - He foresaw it, foretold it from Scripture, and even set the timing, so nothing about it is outside his hands.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where's that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room. Framed fresh.

- Name the horror and the control together. Jesus is about to be sold out by a man who shared his table for three years. And Jesus is not a victim caught off guard. He saw it coming, said so ahead of time, and even told Judas when to go. The worst betrayal in history happened inside his complete control.
- Bring it to the student who's been betrayed. Almost everyone in the room has been let down or stabbed in the back by someone they trusted. Jesus knows exactly what that feels like. And he shows you that even the worst thing a person did to him did not fall outside God's plan to save. It became part of the road to the cross.
- Point to the love. He washed Judas's feet. He handed him bread in honor. He loved his betrayer right up to the door. That's the kind of love that's about to go to a cross for people who don't deserve it, which is all of us.
- Christ connection to say out loud: Jesus is in complete control even of his own betrayal, and he loved the man who sold him all the way out the door.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus knew he'd be betrayed and stayed in control, and even part of God's plan. When someone has let you down, what would it change to trust that God isn't caught off guard?"
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 13:31-35 once before Wednesday. That's the memory-verse lesson.
- To the gradebook: today's participation.
- Sets up L5: the moment Judas leaves, Jesus gives a new command to love.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The center is Christ's sovereignty over his own betrayal (Psalm 41:9 fulfilled, the timed word to Judas). Tie the foretelling to 13:19, where the betrayal becomes evidence for faith rather than a threat to it. This is the same comfort the course drew from 6:70 (Unit 9).
- The morsel (13:26) was a gesture of honor. Jesus shows favor to the man betraying him. Don't miss the love aimed at the enemy.
- "And it was night" (13:30) is a theological note: Judas leaves the Light and walks into darkness.
- Handle the betrayal with care for the student who's been betrayed. Jesus knows that pain, and he shows that even betrayal fell inside God's saving plan.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day, full Work block.
