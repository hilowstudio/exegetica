# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 2 Lesson 2 of 4: behold, the Lamb of God (John 1:29-34)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U2 Witness & first disciples |
| Lesson | 2 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Thu Sep 17 / Thursday (36 min), single-focus |
| Passage | John 1:29-34 (ESV) |
| Big idea | One sentence carries the whole Gospel. Before Jesus works a single sign, his death is announced over him: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." |
| Christ connection | Day one of the public ministry names the cross. This man dies as a sacrifice, and his death lifts sin off and carries it away. |
| Memory verse | Preview John 1:12 (the one you receive is this Lamb; the receiving arrives in L3). Roll 1:14 in review: the Word made flesh is the Lamb. Do not plant today. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will quote John the Baptist's words in 1:29 and identify Jesus as "the Lamb of God."
2. Students will explain what "takes away the sin of the world" means (a real removal, sin lifted off and carried away).
3. Students will explain how naming Jesus "the Lamb" on day one points to the cross.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): the Lamb of God is God's way of taking your sin and carrying it off, gone the way trash is carried out and gone.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "Lamb of God" has gone numb as a song lyric; sit in Isaiah 53 and the Passover until the word has blood in it again.
- **Stretch** (disciple): "takes away" is airo, to lift and carry off (1 John 3:5 uses it the same way); John frames Jesus as the Passover lamb and seals it at the cross (no bone broken, 19:36; Exodus 12:46); the Spirit "remains" (meno) on him.

---

**Talk budget:** short Thursday, single-focus. One image carries the whole 36 minutes, so slow down and keep your sustained voice under about 40%.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (5 min, ungraded)

- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!" (John 1:29).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "If someone could carry away the thing you most regret, and it was simply gone, what would that be worth?"

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 1:29-34 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 1, verses 29 through 34.
- One-sentence setup: John sees Jesus coming, and the first thing he says about him names how he will die.

## Move 3: Work the text (about 20 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** In verse 29, what does John call Jesus, and what does he say Jesus does?
   - Draw out: "the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world." A lamb is a sacrifice, an animal that dies in someone's place.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** Why a lamb? Think about Passover, when a lamb's blood spared God's people, and about Isaiah 53, the servant who bears the sin of many. What is John telling you about how Jesus saves?
   - Guide them: Jesus saves the way a sacrifice saves. He dies in our place. The Passover lamb's blood delivered; the servant of Isaiah 53 "bore the sin of many." Jesus is both, the Lamb who takes the punishment we earned.
3. **Core.** Look hard at the phrase "takes away the sin of the world." The word behind "takes away" means to lift up and carry off. What does that picture?
   - Land it: it is a real removal. Your sin is lifted off you and carried away, gone. Not covered over or ignored. Carried off, the way you take the trash out and it is gone for good.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** In verses 32-34 the Spirit comes down and "remains" on Jesus, and John says, "this is the Son of God." John also says twice, "I myself did not know him" (verses 31, 33). What does that last part tell you?
   - Draw out: God had to reveal the Son to John; recognizing Jesus was a gift given, not John's own cleverness. That is good news for anyone who thinks faith is only for naturally spiritual people. And the Spirit resting and remaining marks Jesus as the one who will pour out that same Spirit.
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What does John call Jesus in verse 29? What does "takes away" mean?
   - The Lamb of God; sin lifted off and carried away.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-6 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Say the whole direction of the book. Before Jesus has done one thing, his death is announced over him. The cross is not plan B. It is why he came, named on day one.
- Put it on the room, and lead with grace. "The sin of the world" includes yours, and the Lamb came to carry it from the very first day, before you had lifted a finger to deserve it. The gift stands before any response you could make.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the Lamb bears the world's sin away, and God himself provided the Lamb.

## Move 5: Respond (3-4 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "'The Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world.' Is that good news to you right now, hard to believe, or something you have never really considered? Write honestly."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 1:35-42 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31 and 1:14.
- Sets up L3: two of John's disciples hear him say it and follow Jesus, and we plant the memory verse, John 1:12.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: single-focus Thursday. One image, slowed way down. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- Make the Lamb cost something. For the churched student it is wallpaper; sit them in Isaiah 53 and Passover until the phrase has blood in it again.
- Grounding for your own prep: "takes away" is airo (lift and carry off; 1 John 3:5, "he appeared to take away sins," same verb). John's Passover framing is sealed at the crucifixion, dated to the lambs' killing (19:14) and no bone broken (19:36; Exodus 12:46). The Spirit "remains" (meno) on Jesus, and this One baptizes with the Spirit. One word note is the ration on a 36-minute day.
- Do not leave the cross abstract. Say plainly: this is the day his death is announced.
- Day-type note: Thursday, 36 minutes, single-focus.
