# Teacher lesson guide, Unit 4 Lesson 1 of 4: the night visit and the new birth (John 3:1-8)

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Unit | U4 The new birth |
| Lesson | 1 of 4 |
| Date / Day type | Mon Sep 28 / Regular (50 min) |
| Passage | John 3:1-8 (ESV) |
| Big idea | A successful religious man is told that all his religion cannot get him in: he needs a birth he cannot perform, and God performs it. |
| Christ connection | The kingdom Jesus opens is entered by a birth from above that Jesus alone brings, the new-covenant work of God arriving in person. |
| Memory verse | Say John 3:16 aloud once today (it plants in L3). Review 20:31 and 1:14, and especially 1:12-13, the Unit 1 thread this lesson pays off: the children of God are "born... of God." |
| Resources | Fresh-build (no Adam sermon covers John 3:1-21). Have a class Bible open to John 3, and a simple map of Jerusalem for the night-visit setting. |

**Content objectives (the floor quizzes and finals assess):**
1. Students will explain what Jesus tells Nicodemus he needs (to be born again / from above) and that he cannot perform it himself.
2. Students will explain "born of water and the Spirit" (3:5) as God's promised cleansing and new heart (Ezekiel 36:25-27).
3. Students will explain the wind-and-Spirit picture (3:8): the new birth is God's sovereign work, not something you command.

**Reach / Hold / Stretch:**
- **Reach** (new to the Bible): you cannot give yourself a fresh start with God; this is something done to you, and that can be a relief.
- **Hold** (churched, passive): "born again" is not tribal shorthand for a loud kind of Christian; it names what God does to every believer, including you.
- **Stretch** (disciple): the sovereignty of the Spirit as good news; Ezekiel 36 as the ancient promise arriving; the plural "you must" of 3:7 widening it to everyone.

---

**Talk budget:** keep your sustained voice under about 40% of the 50 minutes. Turn the content into questions and let students find it.

## Move 1: Quiet time with the Lord (7 min, ungraded)

- Host today: you or the rotating student. Prayer is opt-in.
- Settle the room. Phones away.
- Centering line on the board: "You must be born again" (John 3:7).
- Students write in the Quiet-Time Journal. Prompt: "Is there anything about yourself you have tried and failed to change? What would it be like if that change were God's job, not yours?"
- On-ramp for the doubter: honest is welcome.

## Move 2: Hear the Word (3-5 min)

- Have one student read John 3:1-8 aloud, slowly.
- Navigation help: John, chapter 3, verses 1 through 8.
- One-sentence setup: a religious insider comes to Jesus at night, and Jesus tells him the one thing all his religion cannot give him. (Recall 2:25, "he knew what was in man"; here is the man.)

## Move 3: Work the text (about 25 min, the governed block, no lecture)

Ask these. Let students find it.

1. **Entry (any student can answer).** Who is Nicodemus (verse 1), and what does Jesus tell him in verse 3 he must have to see the kingdom of God?
   - Draw out: a Pharisee, a ruler of the Jews, a top religious teacher. Jesus says, "unless one is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God." The most religious man in the room is told his religion cannot get him in.
2. **Core (small groups, observe in the Reading Journal).** The words for "born again" also mean "born from above." Nicodemus hears "again" and pictures climbing back into the womb (verse 4). Which meaning does Jesus press, and why does the mix-up matter?
   - Guide them: Jesus means "from above," a birth sourced in God. That is the point Nicodemus keeps missing. You did not arrange your first birth, and you cannot arrange this one either. It comes from God.
3. **Core.** Verse 5 says "born of water and the Spirit." Long before, God promised in Ezekiel 36 to "sprinkle clean water on you" and "put my Spirit within you." What is Jesus telling Nicodemus, "the teacher of Israel," he should already know?
   - Land it: God promised to wash people clean and put his own Spirit inside them, cleansing and a new heart, two sides of one work. Jesus tells Nicodemus that promise has to happen to him. It is one birth God gives, and Nicodemus of all people should have known the text.
4. **Deeper (high ceiling, for the disciple).** Verse 8: "the wind blows where it wishes." The same word means wind and Spirit. What does the picture say about how the new birth happens?
   - Draw out: you do not command the wind. You cannot make it blow, and you did not choose your first birth. The Spirit gives new life freely, on his own initiative. That is not scary; it means you are not left to save yourself. It pays off the Prologue: 1:12-13 already said God's children are "born... of God."
5. **Cold-call comprehension check.** What must Nicodemus have? Who does the new birth?
   - To be born again / from above; God, by his Spirit.

Circulate. Short probes. "Where is that in the verse?"

## Move 4: Land and minister (5-7 min, your protected pulpit)

Bring it to Christ and speak it over the room.

- Say the relief. You are as stuck as Nicodemus, and that is good news, because it moves the saving out of your hands and into God's. You do not have to work up a new birth.
- Point to the Giver. The wind you cannot command is the very Spirit who gives life, and Jesus is the one who brings this birth from above. Your job is to come to the Christ who gives it.
- Christ connection to say out loud: the new life you cannot make, Jesus gives, by the Spirit, from above.

## Move 5: Respond (3-5 min, graded participation)

- In-room written response in the Reading Journal. Prompt on the board: "Jesus tells a good religious man that his religion cannot save him; he needs God to give him new life. Is that good news or unsettling news to you right now? Write honestly."
- Give it quiet. Grade that they wrote and engaged.

## After the bell

- Homework: near zero. Read John 3:9-15 once before next class.
- To the gradebook: today's participation and the Respond entry. Keep reviewing 20:31, 1:14, 1:12.
- Sets up L2: Nicodemus asks "How can this be?" and Jesus answers with a snake on a pole.

## Teacher notes

- Governing reminder: governed Work block. Turn content into questions. Keep your sustained voice under 40%.
- The load-bearing ruling is regeneration as God's sovereign work. Teach "from above" as the primary sense, show the "again / from above" wordplay once as the reason Nicodemus is confused, then stop. Tie it to 1:13, the Unit 1 payoff.
- "Born of water and the Spirit" is ruled to Ezekiel 36:25-27 (clean water plus a new Spirit, one birth). Guard the baptismal-regeneration over-read kindly and briefly: the scene predates Christian baptism, and one preposition governs both nouns, so it is one birth. Keep it plain for the room.
- Do not reduce the new birth to a decision or a prayer to repeat. If it is God's sovereign work from above, a technique the student performs contradicts it. Call for faith; sell no method. And do not let "born again" become a label for a personality of Christian.
- Grounding for your own prep: pneuma is one word for wind and Spirit (3:8); the "Truly, truly" (amen amen) at 3:3, 5 marks bedrock; the plural "you must be born again" (3:7) widens it past Nicodemus to everyone.
- Day-type note: regular 50-minute day.
