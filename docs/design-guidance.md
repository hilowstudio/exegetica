# UI & Visual Design Guidance

A reference for designing any interface in this project: the web app, artifacts, dashboards, or documents. Order of use: **calibrate to the audience → apply the eight pillars → execute the craft layers (type, color, imagery) → run the pre-ship checklist.** Distilled from Taras Bakusevych's UI design principles.

## 0. Calibrate to the audience first (Empathy)

Beauty is not universal; a design only works when it fits its people. Set four dials before choosing a look, and read everything below through them:

- **Culture & society** — colors, symbols, and patterns carry different meanings; resonate respectfully and avoid missteps.
- **Industry & context of use** — match domain norms and the real conditions of use (one-handed in the field vs. a focused desktop session).
- **Demographics** — age, profession, and ability shift needs: young adults scan fast and expect polish; seniors need larger targets, clear affordances, and higher contrast; children need simplicity and forgiving interactions.
- **Tech-savviness** — meet the user's fluency; neither assume expertise nor condescend.

## The eight pillars

### 1. Empathy — design for them, not you
The lens for all the rest (see §0). There is no universal concept of beauty; understand the target audience first.

### 2. Layout — guide the eye
The layout is a visual composition that directs, delights, and engages.
- Use **negative space** deliberately; whitespace is structure, not waste.
- Establish a **clear hierarchy** with size, weight, color, and spacing — the most important thing reads first.
- Build on a **grid** for cohesion; set proportion with the **rule of thirds** or **golden ratio** for natural balance.
- Give each screen **one clear focal point** to orient the user immediately.
- Create **rhythm** (repetition, structured spacing) to carry the eye through.
- Match **scanning patterns**: F-pattern for text-dense screens (key info top and left), Z-pattern for sparse landing screens.

### 3. Essentialism — simplicity is the ultimate sophistication
Strip away the non-essential and focus on what matters to the user.
- **Reduce thoughtfully** — cut the non-essential, keep core functionality.
- **Organize so many feel fewer** — group and categorize; use tabs, menus, and disclosure.
- **Don't make users think** — predictable flows and conventional elements where expected; minimize cognitive load.
- **As little design as possible** — restrained colors, fonts, and graphics; let content lead.
- **Chunk big tasks** into steps with progress cues (bars, breadcrumbs).
- **Save the user time** — fast loads, smart defaults, autocomplete, predictive input. Speed feels like simplicity.

### 4. Guidance — lead the journey
Not just A to B; a path that feels natural, effortless, and engaging.
- **Onboarding** that shows value from the first interaction.
- **Intuitive step-by-step flow** that requires minimal effort.
- **Contextual help** (tooltips, inline hints) exactly when needed.
- **Progressive disclosure** — reveal only what is needed at each step.
- **Design for action** — prominent, accessible CTAs, buttons, and icons toward the desired next step.
- **Feedback for every action** — immediate visual or auditory acknowledgment of what happened and what comes next.

### 5. Aesthetics — the feel, not just the look
Aesthetics are emotional resonance and environment, not surface polish; a considered look builds trust and pleasure. Executed in the craft layers below.

### 6. Novelty — memorable yet comfortable
- **Original and unique** to stand out in a crowded field.
- **Leverage current technology** for cutting-edge experiences where it serves.
- **Most advanced yet acceptable** (MAYA) — push, but keep it usable and accessible.
- **Cross-pollinate** from art, architecture, nature, and other industries.
- **Follow trends judiciously** — stay aware, but keep your identity.
- **Novelty must serve the experience**, never adding complexity for its own sake. Balance intrigue with familiarity.

### 7. Consistency — familiarity builds trust
- Maintain a **design system** as the single source of truth.
- **Limit patterns** to a small, consistent set so the interface is predictable.
- Ensure **predictable behavior** — elements act the same everywhere.
- Use **standardized templates** for common page types.
- Keep **cross-device / cross-platform consistency**.
- Standardize **content** tone, style, and formatting.

### 8. Engagement — like a good conversation
Keep the user interested, respond to their actions, and encourage them to return.
- **Gamification** where it fits (points, badges, leaderboards) to motivate, not manipulate.
- **Personalization and customization** for individual relevance.
- **Storytelling** that guides and is memorable.
- **Visible progress** (bars, milestones) for a sense of accomplishment.
- **Variable rewards** (surprise and delight, used ethically).
- **Social features** (sharing, friendly competition, community).

## Craft layers (where aesthetics are executed)

### Typography
- **Hierarchy** via size, weight, and style — the most important content first.
- **Readability first**, especially for body text, across devices and screen sizes.
- **Reflect the brand mood** — professional, playful, or elegant.
- **Pair fonts** that complement each other; **limit** the number of families and styles.
- **Tune spacing** — kerning, word spacing, and line height for flow and readability.

### Color
- **Contrast is non-negotiable** — text against background, for readability and accessibility.
- **Consistent palette** tied to brand identity.
- **60-30-10** — dominant / secondary / accent for harmony.
- **Color psychology and culture** — emotions and meanings vary; choose for the audience.
- **Semantic status colors** — red for errors, green for success — kept separate from the brand accent.
- **Use color to guide action** — highlight key actions and links.

### Visual content
- **Content over UI styling** — let the visuals and content speak; don't over-decorate.
- **Purposeful imagery** — meaningful, custom or carefully curated, on-brand; avoid generic stock.
- **Concise text** — bullets and short descriptions over long paragraphs.
- **Micro-interactions and subtle animation** that enhance engagement without distracting (respect reduced-motion).
- **Video for dynamic storytelling** where static images can't carry the message.
- **High-quality product shots or renders** for commerce and product interfaces.

## Pre-ship checklist

- Who is this for (culture, context, demographics, tech level) — is every choice defensible for them?
- One clear focal point and hierarchy per screen; is the eye led effortlessly?
- Anything removable without loss? (Essentialism)
- Does the user always know where they are, what to do next, and what just happened? (Guidance + feedback)
- Consistent patterns, spacing, type, and color throughout and across devices?
- Contrast and accessibility pass; reduced-motion respected?
- Is novelty serving the experience rather than complicating it?
- Does it feel like *something* — an emotional register, not just neutral competence?
