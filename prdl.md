| ![](https://www.prdl.org/images/widget1l.gif)**About the Post-Reformation Digital Library**![](https://www.prdl.org/images/widget1r.gif)<br>**Purpose** \| [**Governance**](https://www.prdl.org/governance.php) \| [**Scholarship**](https://www.prdl.org/scholarship.php) \| [**Statistics**](https://www.prdl.org/stats.php) \| [**Contribute**](https://www.prdl.org/contribute.php) \| [**Contact**](https://www.prdl.org/contact.php)<br>Purpose<br>![T](https://www.prdl.org/images/letters/T.jpg)he Post-Reformation <br>Digital Library (PRDL) is a select database that organizes the vast array of publicly available digital sources <br>on the development of theology and philosophy during the early modern era (late 15th-18th c.). The PRDL spans <br>collections from major research libraries, independent scholarly initiatives, and corporate documentation projects. <br>With the proliferation of sources scattered across various [digital\<br>libraries](https://www.prdl.org/digital_libraries.php), it can be difficult for the individual <br>scholar to find or keep track of all the new content that is appearing daily. The PRDL is a response to the challenge <br>of curation arising from this situation, and is collaborative effort to organize this content for scholars of early <br>modern theology and philosophy. The PRDL does not directly host digital copies of sources, but rather assembles links <br>to publicly available sources from permanent collections.<br>The core of the PRDL project involves the organization of thousands of documents available in digital form from <br>select digital libraries from Europe and North America, which are making digitized forms of their holdings <br>available to the public. The project covers the work of thousands of authors from a wide variety of theological, <br>philosophical, and ecclesiastical traditions. The PRDL is a project of the [Junius Institute for Digital Reformation Research](http://www.juniusinstitute.org/). | |
